KCDATRUTH • 4 DA KULTURE • WEEK 2 SUNDAY RECAP

Week 2 was the first Sunday where the season started feeling less like an introduction and more like an argument. Some Week 1 statements held up. Some disappeared immediately. A few teams that looked dead seven days ago punched back. And by the time Sunday Night Football reached overtime, Kansas City and Indianapolis had turned the night into the game everybody was going to talk about Monday morning.

GAME OF THE DAY: CHIEFS 33, COLTS 30 — OT.

Patrick Mahomes looked much closer to the version of himself Kansas City expects to see. He finished 32-of-47 for 382 yards, three touchdowns and no interceptions, and the Chiefs needed almost all of it. Indianapolis made him work, answered Kansas City repeatedly and forced the game all the way to the final second of overtime.

Daniel Jones deserves his own paragraph because this was not empty production in a loss. He completed 22 of 31 passes for 210 yards with a touchdown and an interception, kept the Colts organized and gave Kansas City real problems. Jonathan Taylor added 92 rushing yards and two touchdowns. Indianapolis was not hanging around by accident. For long stretches, Jones looked completely comfortable playing on the same field as Mahomes.

Then Travis Kelce turned back the clock. Nine catches, 101 yards and a touchdown. Mahomes kept going back to him when the down mattered, and Kelce kept finding space. After all the conversations about age, mileage and what version of Kelce Kansas City would have this season, Week 2 looked like vintage No. 87.

Kenneth Walker III gave Kansas City exactly what it hoped it was buying when it added him: a real second dimension. He ran 24 times for 117 yards, then caught the 22-yard pass in overtime that moved Kansas City into Indianapolis territory on the final drive. Mahomes is still the engine. Walker gives the engine balance.

THE CHIEFS DIDN’T WIN BECAUSE THE COLTS FOLDED. THEY WON BECAUSE MAHOMES HAD TO KEEP ANSWERING — AND HE DID.

THE PLAY EVERYBODY WILL DEBATE.

Indianapolis opened overtime with the ball. Daniel Jones hit Laquon Treadwell downfield, Treadwell got up and had the ball punched free, and Kansas City recovered. The original ruling was a Chiefs fumble recovery. Replay overturned it, ruling Treadwell down by contact before the ball came loose.

The Colts kept possession and eventually tied the game at 30. That is why the ruling matters so much. If the original call stands, Kansas City gets the ball in immediate scoring position and the entire overtime changes. Instead, the game kept breathing. The reversal will be argued because the contact and timing were close enough to make the frame-by-frame replay feel different depending on what you were looking for.

Kansas City still had to finish it. Mahomes moved the Chiefs back down the field, Walker’s 22-yard catch changed the final drive, and Harrison Butker hit the 40-yard winner with one second remaining.

THE SUNDAY SCOREBOARD.

Panthers 34, Falcons 3

Carolina answered its Week 1 embarrassment in the loudest way possible. Bryce Young threw three touchdowns, the Panthers forced five turnovers and Atlanta fell to 0–2.

Saints 24, Ravens 17

New Orleans scored 15 in the fourth quarter and Tyler Shough stayed poised late. Baltimore went from a Week 1 statement to a Week 2 reminder that nothing carries over automatically.

Vikings 9, Bears 3

Minnesota won ugly with Carson Wentz starting and the defense controlling the game. Chicago’s Week 1 explosion disappeared in a divisional grind.

Bengals 20, Texans 6

Joe Burrow and Ja’Marr Chase did enough while Cincinnati’s defense kept Houston out of the end zone. The Bengals are 2–0; Houston is 0–2 and already searching for offensive answers.

Patriots 20, Steelers 3

New England made Pittsburgh’s offense look old and slow. A fourth-quarter fumble-return touchdown ended whatever suspense remained.

Packers 20, Jets 17 — OT

Green Bay erased a fourth-quarter deficit and survived an ugly, penalty-filled overtime game. Trey Smack’s 26-yard field goal finished it.

Browns 23, Buccaneers 19

Cleveland got the kind of answer it needed after Week 1. Deshaun Watson threw for 238 yards and two scores, including the go-ahead touchdown late in the fourth.

Eagles 24, Titans 20

Philadelphia needed a late Jalen Hurts drive to escape Nashville. DeVonta Smith finished with 117 receiving yards and a touchdown.

Broncos 20, Jaguars 13

Denver scored two fourth-quarter touchdowns and turned a 13–6 deficit into a response win. Bo Nix threw for 288 yards.

Raiders 26, Chargers 14

Las Vegas moved to 2–0 behind Kirk Cousins’ three touchdown passes. Tre Tucker went for 119 yards, while the Chargers fell to 0–2 after three turnovers.

Seahawks 31, Cardinals 7

Drew Lock threw three touchdowns, all to Jaxon Smith-Njigba, and Seattle’s defense held Arizona to 151 total yards.

Cowboys 37, Commanders 20

Dak Prescott threw four touchdowns and CeeDee Lamb went for 153 yards and two scores. Dallas answered its Week 1 loss with a complete offensive performance.

49ers 35, Dolphins 13

Brock Purdy was nearly flawless: 20-of-22, 287 yards, two passing touchdowns and a rushing score. San Francisco is 2–0 and looks like one of the league’s cleanest teams through two weeks.

Chiefs 33, Colts 30 — OT

Mahomes, Kelce, Walker, Daniel Jones, replay controversy and a final-second winner. Nothing else Sunday had more layers.

PLAYER OF THE DAY: PATRICK MAHOMES.

There were monster performances all over the league, but Mahomes gets the spot because of the degree of difficulty. Indianapolis scored 30, moved the ball, answered Kansas City and forced overtime. Mahomes responded with 382 yards, three touchdowns, zero interceptions and one last drive when Kansas City had no margin left.

STOCK DOWN.

Atlanta is 0–2 after committing five turnovers at home. The Chargers are 0–2 after another sloppy loss. Houston has scored six points in a game despite C.J. Stroud throwing for 353 yards. Baltimore’s Week 1 dominance did not survive its first real pushback. And Miami is 0–2 after San Francisco separated from it in one quarter.

INJURY FALLOUT.

Washington’s Jayden Daniels left the Cowboys game with a dislocated elbow. Chicago lost Caleb Williams to a hamstring injury. San Francisco’s receiver room took more hits with Demarcus Robinson suffering a high ankle sprain and Mike Evans leaving with a hip issue. Week 2 did not just move standings — it changed several depth charts before Monday even arrived.

POWER MOVEMENT.

Seven Sunday teams walked out 2–0: Kansas City, San Francisco, Seattle, Las Vegas, Cincinnati, Philadelphia and Minnesota. Two weeks is not enough to crown anybody, but it is enough to start separating teams that look organized from teams already chasing themselves.

QUESTIONS HEADING INTO MONDAY.

Can the Rams answer a 27–7 Week 1 loss without Myles Garrett? How much can Aaron Donald give them immediately? Can Jaxson Dart carry his Week 1 confidence into SoFi? And after Sunday changed the shape of the NFC and AFC, which team leaves Giants–Rams with the next real statement?

4DK VERDICT: WEEK 2 DIDN’T GIVE US FINAL ANSWERS. IT GAVE US BETTER QUESTIONS — AND KANSAS CITY FINISHED SUNDAY WITH THE LOUDEST ANSWER OF ALL.
