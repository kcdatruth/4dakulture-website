GAME 01 · FINAL

Indiana Pacers vs. Philadelphia 76ers

Indiana Pacers 124 · Philadelphia 76ers 131

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Indiana Pacers: Pascal Siakam 31 PTS; Quenton Jackson 8 AST

Philadelphia 76ers: Jaylen Brown 31 PTS; Tyrese Maxey 27 PTS, 6 AST; LeBron James 11 AST

THE 4DK DEEP DIVE

Philadelphia did not need a single offensive engine to survive a 124-point Indiana night. Brown provided the scoring punch, Maxey applied pressure as a secondary creator, and James gave the Sixers another passing hub. That variety is the real story: Indiana could not simply load up on one matchup and expect the possessions to stall.

THE TURNING POINT

The critical separation is the seven-point final margin in a game where Indiana still generated 124. Philadelphia won the offensive exchange rather than relying on a lockdown defensive result; the next film-room question is how its late possessions created enough quality shots to stay ahead.

WHAT IT MEANS

The Sixers move to 11–3, an early contender’s pace. Indiana falls to 5–9 despite Siakam’s 31: respectable individual production cannot disguise the need for better collective stops.

GAME 02 · FINAL

Los Angeles Lakers vs. Phoenix Suns

Los Angeles Lakers 131 · Phoenix Suns 123

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Los Angeles Lakers: Luka Dončić 43 PTS, 9 AST; Walker Kessler 14 PTS, 12 REB

Phoenix Suns: Collin Gillespie 26 PTS; Devin Booker 7 AST

THE 4DK DEEP DIVE

Dončić supplied the headline number, but 43 points and nine assists tell two stories at once. Phoenix had to account for his own scoring while respecting his ability to create for teammates. Kessler’s double-double added interior balance, keeping the Lakers from being entirely dependent on perimeter shot-making.

THE TURNING POINT

The Lakers scored 104 through three quarters, then survived a 43-point Phoenix fourth. That late Suns surge matters: Los Angeles built the cushion, but its closing defense gave Phoenix a path back into the contest.

WHAT IT MEANS

The 12–2 Lakers have the record of a genuine early Western Conference force. Phoenix at 5–7 can take something from its late scoring response, but must find defensive answers before the deficit becomes too large.

GAME 03 · FINAL

Charlotte Hornets vs. LA Clippers

Charlotte Hornets 105 · LA Clippers 113

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Charlotte Hornets: Coby White 21 PTS; Moussa Diabaté 12 REB

LA Clippers: Darius Garland 27 PTS, 6 REB, 7 AST; Brook Lopez 8 AST

THE 4DK DEEP DIVE

Garland’s 27 and seven assists put him at the center of the Clippers’ offensive organization. The important element is the balance between creating his own scoring and involving other players; Lopez’s eight assists show that the ball moved through more than the point guard. Charlotte had productive moments but lacked the same closing efficiency.

THE TURNING POINT

Los Angeles won the fourth quarter 29–22 after carrying only a three-point edge through three. That final-period margin is the decisive separation, not a wire-to-wire rout.

WHAT IT MEANS

The Clippers improve to 4–8, still a long way from where they want to be but in need of every stabilizing win. Charlotte drops to 4–9 and has to turn competitive stretches into a complete 48-minute performance.

GAME 04 · FINAL

San Antonio Spurs vs. Utah Jazz

San Antonio Spurs 110 · Utah Jazz 102

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

San Antonio Spurs: Dylan Harper 31 PTS, 9 REB; Victor Wembanyama 26 PTS, 11 REB, 6 AST, 5 BLK; Stephon Castle 9 AST

Utah Jazz: Mo Bamba 26 PTS; Keyonte George 8 AST

THE 4DK DEEP DIVE

Harper’s 31 points alongside Wembanyama’s 26, 11 and five blocks illustrates why San Antonio is so difficult to scheme against. The Spurs have both an emerging guard who can attack and a generational rim protector who changes what opponents dare attempt inside. Utah got 26 from Bamba but had to contend with that two-way pressure all night.

THE TURNING POINT

San Antonio outscored Utah 29–21 in the fourth after the teams entered the period tied at 81. The Spurs won the game when the possessions carried the most weight.

WHAT IT MEANS

At 12–2, San Antonio is no longer merely a promising young team; the results demand contender-level attention. Utah falls to 1–13, and even strong individual nights are not translating into sustained team success.

GAME 05 · FINAL

Dallas Mavericks vs. Boston Celtics

Dallas Mavericks 94 · Boston Celtics 131

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Dallas Mavericks: Zaccharie Risacher 18 PTS

Boston Celtics: Jayson Tatum 32 PTS, 6 REB, 6 AST; Payton Pritchard 6 AST

THE 4DK DEEP DIVE

Boston’s 42–9 first quarter turned a supposed measuring-stick game into a statement before Dallas could settle in. Tatum was efficient and decisive, but the larger point is collective readiness: Boston imposed pace and defensive pressure immediately. Dallas never established a stable offensive base after that opening avalanche.

THE TURNING POINT

The 33-point first-quarter gap was the game’s defining event. Boston also won the third 37–34, preventing Dallas from building any meaningful comeback momentum.

WHAT IT MEANS

The 11–2 Celtics are making the case that their ceiling remains elite. Dallas slides to 7–7, and a 37-point defeat is an unmistakable warning about defensive consistency and resilience against the East’s best.

GAME 06 · FINAL

New York Knicks vs. Toronto Raptors

New York Knicks 112 · Toronto Raptors 108

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

New York Knicks: Karl-Anthony Towns 28 PTS, 17 REB; Jalen Brunson 28 PTS, 9 AST

Toronto Raptors: Scottie Barnes 30 PTS, 9 REB; Collin Murray-Boyles 13 REB

THE 4DK DEEP DIVE

New York needed both its primary shot creator and its interior anchor. Brunson’s 28 and nine assists gave the Knicks direction in the half court, while Towns’ 28 and 17 rebounds ensured they could survive the possessions that did not produce clean first looks. Barnes answered with 30 for Toronto, making this a star-driven contest rather than a routine road win.

THE TURNING POINT

The game required overtime: New York won the extra period 17–13 after regulation ended 95–95. The Knicks’ ability to score in the additional five minutes is the concrete late-game difference.

WHAT IT MEANS

New York improves to 7–6 but still needs consistency to climb the East. Toronto falls to 5–8; Barnes’ production is encouraging, yet close losses continue to expose the need for more reliable finishing.

GAME 07 · FINAL

Houston Rockets vs. Washington Wizards

Houston Rockets 147 · Washington Wizards 119

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Houston Rockets: Amen Thompson 26 PTS, 7 REB, 7 AST; Kevin Durant 25 PTS; Alperen Şengün 19 PTS, 8 REB, 8 AST

Washington Wizards: Alex Sarr 25 PTS, 9 REB, 3 BLK; Trae Young 8 AST

THE 4DK DEEP DIVE

Houston’s 147 points reflect the kind of offensive ecosystem a contender wants: Thompson attacking and creating, Durant offering an elite scoring release valve, and Şengün functioning as a facilitator from the frontcourt. That distribution of responsibility is harder to neutralize than a single-player takeover. Washington’s Sarr offered an encouraging individual response, but the Wizards could not contain the Rockets’ varied attack.

THE TURNING POINT

Houston scored 73 in the first half and then exploded for 46 in the third quarter. The third-period surge extended the lead and removed most realistic comeback paths.

WHAT IT MEANS

The Rockets move to 11–3 and continue to strengthen their Western Conference standing. Washington falls to 4–10 and must address the defensive breakdowns that allow opponents to reach this kind of offensive volume.

Oklahoma City Thunder vs. Sacramento Kings

Oklahoma City Thunder 125 · Sacramento Kings 103
