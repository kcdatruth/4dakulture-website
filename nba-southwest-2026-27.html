THE SOUTHWEST IS BUILT AROUND THE NEXT ERA.

San Antonio is ready to chase June with Wemby. Dallas is turning the keys over to Cooper Flagg. Houston has enough talent to crash the West's elite. Memphis and New Orleans have different versions of the same question: which young players are actually part of the future?

By Kcdatruth • 4 Da Kulture • September 2026

The Southwest may have the widest range of timelines in basketball. One team has Finals expectations right now, another is building around the reigning Rookie of the Year, Houston could become dangerous immediately, and two franchises need development and clarity more than empty wins.

SAN ANTONIO SPURS

WEMBY IS READY FOR THE BIGGEST STAGE.

Finals expectations • WCF floor • Young core growing up

San Antonio is past the stage where development alone counts as a successful season. Wembanyama is already good enough to be the best player on a championship team, and after the Spurs' last postseason run the expectation is another trip deep into May — with the Finals firmly on the table.

The interesting roster question is De'Aaron Fox. If San Antonio is winning, there is no reason to move him just to clear the backcourt. The only trade worth considering would be a true win-now return, not another package of young players and picks. Stephon Castle still looks like a future starter, while Harper showed enough in the playoffs at age 20 to establish himself as another major piece.

The Spurs have talent everywhere. What the young core still needs is experience, physical growth and the ability to execute when every possession gets tighter. For Wemby specifically, getting stronger and handling the physicality of elite playoff bigs is the next layer.

Wemby is already good enough to anchor a champion. Now San Antonio has to prove the rest of the roster is ready to go with him.

DALLAS MAVERICKS

THE FLAGG ERA MAY ALREADY BE HERE.

35 wins • Development • Build the asset base

Dallas is still rebuilding, but the future already has a face. Cooper Flagg enters Year 2 after averaging 21.0 points, 6.7 rebounds and 4.5 assists as a 19-year-old Rookie of the Year. The hype followed him into the league and he has answered it — not just as a scorer, but with the all-around versatility that can define the next era of Mavericks basketball.

Jason Kidd gave Flagg point-guard responsibility early in his rookie year, similar to the developmental freedom Kidd once gave Giannis in Milwaukee. Now Kidd is out and former Michigan head coach Dusty May takes over, which makes Flagg's development process one of the biggest stories of the season. If Flagg climbs into the 24–25 point range while continuing to expand his passing, rebounding and defense, Dallas may be watching the beginning of a true franchise-player leap.

Kyrie Irving can help Flagg's efficiency and conserve his energy if he stays, but at 34 he no longer fits the timeline — especially after the Anthony Davis trade. Dallas should find the right trade partner if a deal brings meaningful future assets. Max Christie, still only 23 after showing clear improvement last season, has a chance to become a long-term piece. Rookie Morez Johnson Jr., the No. 9 pick after winning a national title at Michigan with May, brings instant familiarity with the new coach.

The rest of the roster should be evaluated through the same lens. Does Dereck Lively II finally stay healthy? Is PJ Washington starting to age out of the timeline? What can Naji Marshall return in a trade? Dallas has talent and movable assets, but not yet the makeup of a playoff contender.

Thirty-five wins is enough if Flagg takes another leap and Dallas turns older veterans into pieces that matter later.

HOUSTON ROCKETS

THE WILD CARD WITH A REAL CEILING.

55 wins • Top-4 seed • Second round / WCF upside

Houston has enough talent to compete with the league's elite. The question is whether health, consistency and point-guard play allow all of it to come together at once. Kevin Durant is 38, but if he can still score at a high level, the Rockets have one of the best late-game closers in basketball when the playoffs slow down.

The franchise future belongs to Amen Thompson and Alperen Sengun regardless of how long Durant stays. Amen, 23 and fresh off a max contract, needs to get to the 20-point mark while maintaining the defensive reputation that already makes him special. If the jumper becomes respectable, the ceiling changes. Sengun, 24, has to improve defensively and prove he can be the No. 1 option on a playoff contender after an underwhelming Lakers series in which Durant was available for only one game.

Fred VanVleet can still be valuable, but at 32 and coming off a major injury there is no guarantee he returns as the same player. That makes the Marcus Smart addition especially important as defensive toughness and point-guard insurance. Reed Sheppard is entering a make-or-break third season: 13.5 points per game across all 82 games was progress, but he still has to prove he can become Houston's long-term starter at the position.

Jabari Smith Jr. may be just as important to the post-Durant picture. He averaged a career-best 15.8 points last season and was one of Houston's bright spots against Los Angeles. If he becomes a more consistent rebounder and a high-volume stretch four, the Rockets may already have their future core in place with Amen, Sengun, Jabari and possibly Sheppard.

Fifty-five wins and a top-four seed are the regular-season benchmark. Win a playoff series, stay healthy, and Houston has enough talent — plus Durant to close games and an improved defense — to push all the way to the Western Conference Finals.

The young core determines the future. KD determines how dangerous Houston can be right now.

MEMPHIS GRIZZLIES

DEVELOPMENT COMES FIRST.

Young core • Cam Boozer watch • Long-term evaluation

Memphis does not need to fake urgency. This season should be about development, role clarity and figuring out which young players deserve to be part of the next serious Grizzlies team.

The most interesting name is Cam Boozer. His rookie development gives Memphis a fresh high-upside piece to evaluate, and the priority should be putting him in situations that reveal how much responsibility he can eventually handle rather than chasing short-term results.

The standings matter less than the answers Memphis gets by April. Which young players can create? Who can defend at a playoff level? Who fits next to Boozer long term? A developmental year only works if the organization comes out of it with a clearer hierarchy.

Do not rush the timeline. Find out what Cam Boozer and the young core can become.

NEW ORLEANS PELICANS

FREE TREY. LET MATHURIN COOK.

Zion clarity • Bigger roles • Future direction

New Orleans has spent too long living around the same unanswered question: is Zion Williamson actually going to be there for the next version of this franchise or not? Until that gets answered, everything else feels stuck between timelines.

Regardless of the Zion decision, Trey Murphy deserves more freedom. The Pelicans need to see what the offense looks like when his scoring and shot-making are treated like central features instead of secondary options. The same goes for Bennedict Mathurin: let him cook, give him real volume and find out whether his scoring can become one of the foundations of the next roster.

This should be a clarity season. If Zion is part of the future, build an identity that actually works around him. If he is not, stop delaying the pivot. Either way, Murphy and Mathurin should leave the year with bigger roles and a much clearer place in the franchise hierarchy.

The Pelicans need an answer on Zion — and they need to stop holding back the scorers who could define what comes next.
