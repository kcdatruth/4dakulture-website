The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

SGA has reached the point where 30 points feels routine. Last season he averaged 31.1 points on 55.3 percent shooting, won his second straight MVP and continued to play the kind of controlled, low-waste basketball that makes Oklahoma City almost impossible to speed up. He lives in the paint without relying on overpowering athleticism, gets to the line, changes pace and rarely gives a defender the same rhythm twice.

The biggest difference between Shai and many high-volume scorers is the efficiency of his decisions. He does not need ten threes a night to get to 30. He can score from the midrange, finish through contact, attack a mismatch and still keep his turnovers low enough that OKC’s defense is rarely scrambling after careless possessions. On the other end, his length and anticipation make him a real contributor rather than someone the Thunder need to hide.

Oklahoma City’s team structure also matters. Chet Holmgren protects the rim, Jalen Williams can take tough wing assignments and the roster is filled with shooting and athleticism. That means Shai can focus on being the best version of himself instead of solving every problem alone.

There is a completely legitimate case for No. 1. Back-to-back MVPs, elite efficiency and championship-level team success put him at the center of the league. The only reason 4DK gives Wemby the edge is the possibility that one player can impact the geometry of both ends of the floor in an even more extreme way.

If Shai wins a third straight MVP or takes OKC back to the Finals, nobody should be surprised. He is already in his prime, already proven and already operating with almost no obvious weakness.
