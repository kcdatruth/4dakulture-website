4DK NFL • WEEK 3 • SUNDAY RECAP

WHAT WE LEARNED
SUNDAY.

The Niners and Chiefs look like contenders. Vegas is 3–0. Marcus Mariota and Washington stun Seattle. And the Rams blow a 16–0 halftime lead in Denver.

Three weeks isn't enough to crown anybody, but it is enough to start recognizing patterns. Kansas City keeps winning in different ways. San Francisco keeps losing players and somehow keeps winning games. The Raiders are 3–0 after another fourth-quarter comeback. Washington knocked off Seattle without Jayden Daniels. And the Rams turned a dominant first half into one of the ugliest losses of the young season.

49ERS 36 • CARDINALS 30

SAN FRANCISCO LOOKS LIKE A CONTENDER.

SF 3–0PURDY 297 YDS • 4 TD • 0 INTKITTLE 6 REC • 82 YDS • 2 TDCMC 75 RUSH • TD

The most impressive thing about San Francisco is that this team still isn't close to whole. Brock Purdy averaged 11 yards per attempt and repeatedly answered Arizona when the Cardinals turned a comfortable lead into a fourth-quarter fight. George Kittle's two fourth-quarter touchdowns were the separator, while Christian McCaffrey remained the engine of the run game.

Arizona deserves credit for refusing to go away, but every time the pressure increased, San Francisco had an answer. That's the part that looks like a contender.

The question is health

That remains the cloud over everything. The 49ers have spent years watching championship-caliber rosters get worn down by December. At 3–0, the talent and eye test are there. The question is whether enough of this roster will still be standing when the games matter most.

RAIDERS 35 • SAINTS 27

VEGAS IS 3–0. ARE THEY LEGIT?

LV 3–0COUSINS 248 YDS • 3 TD • 0 INTBOWERS 10 REC • 116 YDS • TD4 NO TURNOVERS

It's officially time to take the Raiders seriously. New Orleans had Vegas down 27–16, and the Raiders responded by owning the fourth quarter. Kirk Cousins gave them steady veteran quarterback play, Brock Bowers immediately changed the passing attack, and the defense punished New Orleans every time it gave the ball away.

That doesn't mean we know their ceiling yet. Four Saints turnovers mattered, and Vegas still has to prove this against the league's best. But 3–0 is no accident when a team keeps showing it can survive adversity and close games.

COMMANDERS 33 • SEAHAWKS 31

MARIOTA JUST FLIPPED THE NFC CONVERSATION.

MARIOTA 183 YDS • 3 TD • 0 INTDARNOLD 379 YDS • 4 TDSEA 437 TOTAL YDSTURNOVERS: SEA 3 • WAS 0

Seattle had the bigger offensive numbers and still lost because Washington played the cleaner game. Marcus Mariota didn't need 400 yards; he needed to protect the football, hit timely throws and keep Washington functional without Jayden Daniels. He did exactly that.

Sam Darnold and Jaxon Smith-Njigba produced monster numbers, but the late pick-six changed everything. Seattle still fought back to 33–31, yet Washington finished the game when it had the chance. This looks more like a turnover-driven warning for Seattle than a reason to panic, but defending contenders can't give away three possessions and expect talent to erase it.

CHIEFS 24 • DOLPHINS 10

KC LOOKS BUILT FOR ANOTHER SUPER BOWL RUN.

KC 3–0MAHOMES 20/24 • 246 YDS • 2 TDWALKER 2 TOTAL TDRICE 7 REC • 88 YDS

Kansas City didn't need a Mahomes superhero game. That's what makes this version scary. Kenneth Walker gives the offense a legitimate running threat, Rashee Rice gives Mahomes explosiveness, Travis Kelce can still punish a defense in winning time, and Mahomes remains the ultimate answer when a game tightens.

Miami hung around into the fourth quarter, but Kansas City separated when it mattered. The Chiefs are showing they can win shootouts, survive overtime and control more conventional games. That's the profile of a team built for January.

SUNDAY NIGHT FOOTBALL • BRONCOS 30 • RAMS 26

THE RAMS BLEW A 16–0 HALFTIME LEAD. ARE THEY IN TROUBLE?

LA 482 YDS • DEN 257STAFFORD 390 YDS • 2 TD • 2 INTLA 13 PENALTIES • 91 YDSDEN 30 • LA 26

This might be the most troubling loss of Sunday because the Rams controlled so much of the game. They led 16–0 at halftime, moved the ball for 482 yards and held Denver to 257. And they still lost.

Denver scored 16 unanswered in the third quarter. Then Talanoa Hufanga's fourth-quarter pick-six gave the Broncos their first lead. Stafford answered with a 48-yard touchdown to Konata Mumpfield, but Bo Nix drove Denver back down the field and scored the go-ahead touchdown with under a minute left. Stafford still got LA to the Denver 14 before Hufanga intercepted him again to end it.

So, are the Rams in trouble?

At 1–2, they deserve scrutiny, not a funeral. The alarming part isn't simply the record; it's losing a game they statistically dominated. Thirteen penalties, two turnovers and poor late-game execution erased nearly 500 yards of offense. The question going forward is why a roster this talented isn't finishing games it controls.

BILLS 24 • CHARGERS 16

Buffalo finds another way.

James Cook powered the offense with 154 rushing yards and a touchdown. Josh Allen threw two interceptions, but Buffalo still moved to 3–0. Good teams don't always need their quarterback to be Superman.

COLTS 19 • TEXANS 17

Indy finally closes one.

After pushing Kansas City to overtime last week, Indianapolis got into the win column behind Daniel Jones. Houston falling to 0–3 turns its slow start into a real early-season problem.

JAGUARS 35 • PATRIOTS 6

Jacksonville makes a statement.

One of Sunday's most decisive results. A 29-point win after an uneven opening two weeks puts Jacksonville squarely on the Tuesday power-ranking radar.

GIANTS 12 • TITANS 7

Ugly still counts.

New York didn't need style points. The Giants moved to 2–1 while Tennessee dropped to 0–3 after producing only seven points.

STEELERS 30 • BENGALS 27

AFC North football.

Aaron Rodgers threw three touchdowns, Jaylen Warren topped 100 rushing yards and Pittsburgh survived Joe Burrow's three-touchdown response in a divisional fight.

BROWNS 21 • PANTHERS 18

Cleveland survives.

The Browns found enough late to escape a one-score game and move to 2–1. Carolina had its chances but couldn't finish.

LIONS 31 • JETS 24

Detroit's playmakers finish it.

Geno Smith threw for 321 yards and three touchdowns, but Detroit got another difference-making day from Jahmyr Gibbs and made the late plays New York couldn't.

VIKINGS 23 • BUCCANEERS 16

Minnesota is quietly 3–0.

The Vikings belong in Tuesday's undefeated-team conversation. Three weeks in, they keep stacking wins without needing the loudest headlines.

RAVENS 34 • COWBOYS 31

Baltimore gets the road response.

Lamar Jackson and Derrick Henry helped Baltimore survive in Dallas after last week's loss. Henry scored twice, and the Ravens made the winning plays in a tight fourth quarter.
