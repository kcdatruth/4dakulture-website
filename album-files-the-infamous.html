THE
INFAMOUS.

Two young MCs. One legendary sound. The making of a Queensbridge classic.

9.3 / 10 · HIP-HOP CLASSIC
MOBB DEEP · APRIL 25, 1995 · LOUD / RCA · QUEENSBRIDGE, NEW YORK
THE 4DK DEEP DIVE

TWO TWENTY-YEAR-OLDS. AN ENTIRE SOUND.

In 1995, hip-hop’s regional identities were flourishing. Nas, Biggie and Wu-Tang had brought renewed attention to New York; OutKast, UGK and 8Ball & MJG were shaping the South; and Tha Dogg Pound represented a different kind of West Coast chemistry. In the middle of that extraordinary moment, Prodigy and Havoc—both only 20 when this album arrived—delivered one of rap’s most fully realized second albums.

The Infamous sounds like its own environment. There is no attempt to sand away the paranoia, pressure or street-level storytelling. Instead, the duo turn those feelings into an aesthetic: sparse arrangements, unforgettable drums and verses that feel lived in. That maturity, at such a young age, remains staggering.

PRODUCER SPOTLIGHT

HAVOC DESERVES HIS FLOWERS.

When the great producers of the 1990s are discussed, DJ Premier, RZA, Dr. Dre and Pete Rock rightly enter the conversation. Havoc belongs there too. His ominous, economical production gave Mobb Deep a sound that was instantly recognizable. Even better, he was simultaneously delivering standout verses: his work on “Survival of the Fittest” and “Cradle to the Grave” makes the case on its own.

Q-Tip’s production contributions add welcome texture to the album, especially on “Give Up the Goods,” “Temperature’s Rising” and “Drink Away the Pain.” But Havoc’s overall sonic vision is the backbone. His later chemistry with Alchemist never erases how foundational his own production was to the Mobb Deep identity.

01. The Start of Your Ending (41st Side)

9.5 / 10

The title announces the album’s worldview before either MC finishes a verse. Havoc and Prodigy establish a raw mid-’90s East Coast atmosphere immediately: an introduction that sounds like Queensbridge and promises no compromises.

02. The Infamous Prelude

INTERLUDE · NOT RATED

A brief interlude that deepens the album’s street-level atmosphere.

03. Survival of the Fittest

9.8 / 10

One of Mobb Deep’s defining classics, powered by a haunting Havoc instrumental that belongs in any conversation about hip-hop’s greatest beats. Prodigy commands attention, but Havoc’s fiery verse is essential to the record’s force.

04. Eye for a Eye (Your Beef Is Mines)

10.0 / 10

Nas and Raekwon complement Mobb Deep perfectly. Four distinct voices share one grimy East Coast canvas without diluting the duo’s identity. The chemistry, menace and vivid writing make this a flawless collaboration.

05. Just Step Prelude

INTERLUDE · NOT RATED

A short connective piece that keeps the original album sequence intact.

06. Give Up the Goods (Just Step)

9.2 / 10

Big Noyd brings unmistakable Queensbridge energy to this Q-Tip-produced standout. The piano-led groove gives the record bounce without compromising its street character; another reminder of the album’s range.

07. Temperature’s Rising

9.5 / 10

A vivid account of loyalty and legal pressure, framed by Crystal Johnson’s soulful hook. The warmth of the production and singing only sharpens the gravity of the story.

08. Up North Trip

8.8 / 10

The duo examine incarceration and survival with the same cold-eyed perspective that anchors the album. A strong cut that contributes to its larger portrait, even if it falls below the highest peaks.

09. Trife Life

9.8 / 10

Our hidden gem. Prodigy’s storytelling is gripping, and the smooth, understated beat gives the narrative space to unfold. Its calm delivery makes the tension hit harder: storytelling rap at its finest.

10. Q.U. — Hectic

8.7 / 10

Rugged Queensbridge energy, aggressive performances and a sound that refuses to soften the album’s identity. A solid deep cut.

11. Right Back at You

8.4 / 10

Ghostface Killah and Raekwon deliver fiery guest verses alongside Big Noyd. The lyricism stands tall, but the instrumental is less compelling than the album’s most memorable production.

12. The Grave Prelude

INTERLUDE · NOT RATED

The last interlude provides a transition into the record’s closing run.

13. Cradle to the Grave

10.0 / 10

A personal favorite and a storytelling masterpiece. Havoc kills his verse; Prodigy’s final two verses are extraordinary. The simple, effective instrumental complements the narrative instead of fighting for attention.

14. Drink Away the Pain (Situations)

9.0 / 10

Q-Tip’s presence and production introduce a warmer sound, while imaginative brand-name metaphors explore relationships and personal strain. A creative change of pace that still fits the project.

15. Shook Ones, Pt. II

10.0 / 10

The record that helped make Mobb Deep a household name in hip-hop. Prodigy’s iconic verse, Havoc’s grimy production and the unforgettable video imagery—including the banging pots—form an enduring cultural landmark.

16. Party Over

8.7 / 10

Big Noyd returns for a raw, fitting finale. The album closes on its own terms: uncompromising and unmistakably Queensbridge.

THE FINAL 4DK VERDICT

AN UNDISPUTED QUEENSBRIDGE CLASSIC.

9.3 / 10

Three perfect 10s—“Eye for a Eye,” “Cradle to the Grave” and “Shook Ones, Pt. II”—tell only part of the story. “Trife Life” is the hidden gem, Havoc’s production is the album’s backbone, and Prodigy’s storytelling elevates even the quietest arrangements. The record is not flawless from start to finish, but its best moments helped define an era.

4DK standouts: Best song — “Shook Ones, Pt. II” · Best beat — “Survival of the Fittest” · Best storytelling — “Cradle to the Grave” · Hidden gem — “Trife Life” · Best collaboration — “Eye for a Eye.”

Final rating is the rounded average of 13 scored songs; the three interludes remain unrated.
