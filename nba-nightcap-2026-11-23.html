GAME 01 · FINAL · NOVEMBER 23
Memphis Grizzlies 117 — Charlotte Hornets 111

Jerome gives Memphis the answer in a tricky road test

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Ty Jerome: 29 PTS, 4 REB, 3 AST; Cam Spencer: 24 PTS, 5 AST. Charlotte: Christian Anderson 25 PTS, six threes.

THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

Memphis needed more than its headline talent to handle a Charlotte team willing to trade punches from deep. Jerome supplied the steadiness and shot-making that turned a potentially uncomfortable night into another win. Spencer’s 24 gave the Grizzlies a second offensive engine, an important distinction when opponents load up on the first action.

THE TURNING POINT

The Grizzlies outscored Charlotte 27–22 in the third after trailing 60–62 at halftime, flipping the balance before protecting the lead in the fourth.

WHAT IT MEANS

We see the shape of a resilient team: a 13–5 start is no accident if its supporting creators can keep the offense functional. Charlotte’s perimeter scoring showed fight, but converting that shooting into sustained stops is the next challenge.

GAME 02 · FINAL · NOVEMBER 23
Portland Trail Blazers 128 — Philadelphia 76ers 111

Avdija makes a statement in Philadelphia

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Deni Avdija: 31 PTS, 7 AST, five threes; Donovan Clingan: 15 PTS, 18 REB, 5 AST. Philadelphia: LeBron James 23 PTS, 6 REB.

THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

Portland did not merely sneak out of Philadelphia with a win; it imposed its offensive personality. Avdija’s 31 points and seven assists underline why he has become so much more than a complementary wing. He can initiate, punish a defense that goes under and keep teammates involved when the game tilts toward him. Clingan’s 18 rebounds provided the physical platform behind that perimeter attack.

THE TURNING POINT

Avdija’s five triples stretched the Sixers’ coverage, while Clingan’s work on the glass denied Philadelphia the kind of possession advantage that might have offset Portland’s scoring.

WHAT IT MEANS

This is a meaningful road result against a 14–4 Philadelphia group. We want to see Portland carry the same decision-making and interior discipline into its tougher Western Conference tests; Philadelphia must respond to being beaten in both rhythm and force.

GAME 03 · FINAL · NOVEMBER 23
Indiana Pacers 125 — Oklahoma City Thunder 120

Indiana finds an answer to Shai’s 38

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Pascal Siakam: 20 PTS, 8 REB, 5 AST; Kelly Oubre Jr.: 20 PTS, four threes. Oklahoma City: Shai Gilgeous-Alexander 38 PTS, 5 REB, 7 AST.

THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

The headline number belonged to Shai, but the collective win belonged to Indiana. The Pacers got balanced scoring from Siakam and Oubre rather than leaning on a single spectacular night. Against a Thunder team that can make every possession feel like a test, spreading responsibility matters: it gives the defense more than one player to erase.

THE TURNING POINT

Indiana held on through a 38-point Shai performance and closed a five-point win, ending its four-game skid rather than letting another tight finish slip away.

WHAT IT MEANS

For the Pacers, this is evidence of a viable offensive floor when multiple veterans contribute. For Oklahoma City, Shai’s excellence remains undeniable, but the broader supporting cast has to reduce how much rescue work he is asked to do.

GAME 04 · FINAL · NOVEMBER 23
Milwaukee Bucks 116 — Toronto Raptors 111

Herro and Turner finally break Milwaukee’s slide

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Tyler Herro: 32 PTS, 6 REB, 8 AST; Myles Turner: 27 PTS, 11 REB, 2 BLK. Toronto: Scottie Barnes 31 PTS, 5 REB, 8 AST, 4 BLK.

THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

Thirteen consecutive losses can make a team’s identity feel theoretical. Milwaukee finally got a tangible answer from the Herro–Turner partnership: perimeter creation paired with an interior scoring and rim-protection presence. Herro’s eight assists are as important as his 32 points because they show that the offense was not simply waiting for difficult jumpers.

THE TURNING POINT

Milwaukee survived Barnes’ all-around 31-point performance to close a five-point game, ending its 13-game losing streak.

WHAT IT MEANS

One win does not erase the problems behind a 13-game skid. But it offers a working blueprint: let Herro dictate tempo, use Turner as a two-way anchor, and demand the rest of the rotation defend with enough consistency to make that offense matter.

GAME 05 · FINAL · NOVEMBER 23
Minnesota Timberwolves 142 — Brooklyn Nets 116

McDaniels gives Minnesota another dimension

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Jaden McDaniels: 34 PTS, 10 REB, 6 AST; Ayo Dosunmu: 23 PTS, 5 AST, four threes. Brooklyn: Julius Randle 29 PTS, 4 REB, 4 AST.

THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

Minnesota’s 142-point outburst is the kind of result that forces opponents to reconsider where the pressure is coming from. McDaniels was not merely a defender who happened to score; 34 points, ten rebounds and six assists is a complete offensive imprint. Dosunmu’s 23 and four threes ensured Brooklyn could not load its coverage toward one hot hand.

THE TURNING POINT

The Wolves turned their shot-making into a 26-point margin, leaving Brooklyn unable to keep pace despite Randle’s 29.

WHAT IT MEANS

This is especially encouraging for a Minnesota group proving it can manufacture offense through multiple players. We still want to see that breadth translate against the league’s strongest defenses. Brooklyn must find a more reliable defensive baseline before its individual scoring nights can matter.

GAME 06 · FINAL · NOVEMBER 23
Chicago Bulls 122 — Cleveland Cavaliers 108

Chicago owns the fourth quarter

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Jordan Hawkins: 19 PTS, four threes; Tre Jones: 16 PTS, 9 AST, 2 STL. Cleveland: Donovan Mitchell 28 PTS, 5 AST.

THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

Chicago’s win was built on something more sustainable than a single 40-point explosion: distributed creation and enough late-game organization to withstand Cleveland’s push. Jones’ nine assists gave the Bulls direction, while Hawkins’ four threes made it costly for the Cavaliers to overhelp. Mitchell did his part as a scorer, but Cleveland never found enough collective answers.

THE TURNING POINT

After Cleveland threatened a comeback, Chicago delivered the stronger fourth quarter and finished with a 14-point cushion.

WHAT IT MEANS

For the Bulls, it is a useful example of how structure can beat superior individual firepower. Cleveland’s 108 points are not the only concern; the inability to dictate the final period is the bigger warning for a team with higher ambitions.

GAME 07 · FINAL · NOVEMBER 23
Phoenix Suns 123 — New Orleans Pelicans 119 (OT)

Phoenix wins the possession battle when it matters

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Jalen Green: 25 PTS; Devin Booker: 22 PTS, 8 AST; Miles Bridges: 14 PTS, 11 REB. New Orleans: Jordan Poole 28 PTS; Zion Williamson 25 PTS.

THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

New Orleans had the bursts and the shot-making to win this game. Poole’s explosive shooting and Zion’s pressure on the interior put Phoenix on the back foot. But the Suns kept finding a second answer: Green supplied the scoring, Booker managed the late possessions, and their supporting cast provided just enough timely plays.

THE TURNING POINT

Booker put Phoenix ahead late in regulation, Jeremiah Fears tied it to force overtime, and a 10–2 Suns run in the extra period opened a 119–113 lead. Phoenix then held off the final New Orleans push.

WHAT IT MEANS

Phoenix’s fourth straight win speaks to a team learning to close different kinds of games. New Orleans has to turn competitive stretches into 48-minute control; losing four straight and ten of eleven is a pattern, not an isolated late-game mistake.

GAME 08 · FINAL · NOVEMBER 23
Houston Rockets 116 — Los Angeles Clippers 104

Durant’s finishing kick separates Houston

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Kevin Durant: 32 PTS, 9 REB, 4 AST; Alperen Şengün: 20 PTS, 8 REB, 7 AST. Clippers: Darius Garland 26 PTS, 8 AST, four threes.

THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

Houston has a luxury most teams cannot replicate: when the game becomes a half-court shot-making contest, Durant can turn a narrow window into a decisive separation. His 32 points were complemented by Şengün’s 20–8–7 line, the connective passing and interior presence that keeps the Rockets from becoming a one-option offense.

THE TURNING POINT

Durant’s late three-point burst powered Houston’s closing stretch and turned the matchup into a 12-point victory.

WHAT IT MEANS

The Rockets’ offensive ceiling becomes more credible when Durant and Şengün are both threats to score and create. The Clippers got productive Garland minutes, but need more consistent resistance at the point of attack and stronger late-game answers.

GAME 09 · FINAL · NOVEMBER 23
San Antonio Spurs 123 — Sacramento Kings 107

Castle and Champagnie win without Wemby

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Stephon Castle: 28 PTS, 8 REB, 11 AST; Julian Champagnie: 22 PTS, six threes; Luke Kornet: 18 PTS, 11 REB. Sacramento: Domantas Sabonis 20 PTS, 12 REB, 8 AST.

THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

San Antonio entered without Victor Wembanyama and De’Aaron Fox, and Sacramento briefly made that absence look decisive. Instead, the Spurs demonstrated a deeper identity. Castle controlled the game as a scorer and distributor, while Champagnie gave the offense a jolt from outside. This is the kind of supporting-cast performance that makes a contender more than its superstar.

THE TURNING POINT

After trailing by 12 early, San Antonio scored 41 in the second quarter. Champagnie’s personal 14–4 surge flipped the game, and Sacramento never got closer than six after halftime.

WHAT IT MEANS

We cannot treat one Wembanyama-less win as proof the Spurs never need him. We can treat it as evidence that their structure and confidence travel beyond one player. Sacramento, fresh off a dramatic Boston win, must now prove it can sustain defensive intensity on consecutive nights.

GAME 10 · FINAL · NOVEMBER 23
Los Angeles Lakers 109 — Utah Jazz 94

Lakers settle the score with a fourth-quarter clamp

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Luka Dončić: 28 PTS, 4 REB, 8 AST; Jake LaRavia: 15 PTS, 10 REB. Utah: Kyle Filipowski 21 PTS, 12 REB.

THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

Utah had already shown it could disrupt Los Angeles, ending the Lakers’ nine-game winning streak earlier in the week. This rematch was not an effortless revenge game. The Jazz fought back from a halftime deficit and even led in the third, forcing Los Angeles to solve a real problem rather than coast on talent. Luka’s 28 and eight assists supplied the primary creation; the supporting defense supplied the finish.

THE TURNING POINT

Los Angeles opened the fourth quarter 9–0 and won the period 31–17, converting a one-point lead entering the quarter into a 15-point final margin.

WHAT IT MEANS

At 15–3, the Lakers are showing a useful ability to win without perfect shooting or Austin Reaves. Utah’s young players competed, but its 6-for-32 three-point shooting made sustained offense difficult once Los Angeles tightened its coverage.

4DK · PLAYER OF THE NIGHT

Stephon Castle · San Antonio Spurs

28 PTS · 11 AST · 8 REB

Our pick is Castle because of the context, not simply the box-score total. San Antonio scratched its two biggest names, fell behind by double digits and still won comfortably. Castle turned that uncertainty into command, controlling the offense while Champagnie supplied the outside firepower. Jaden McDaniels’ 34–10–6 and Deni Avdija’s 31-point road statement deserve serious recognition; Castle gets the nod for leading the Spurs through a difficult adjustment and an emphatic comeback.
