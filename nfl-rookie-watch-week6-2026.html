THE 4DK STANDARD

OPPORTUNITY IS NOT PRODUCTION.

We rank impact, responsibility and trajectory. A rookie gets credit for a role he actually plays—not simply his college tape or draft slot. Statistics that have been verified are labeled; when the individual line is not confirmed, the assessment remains clearly a scouting judgment.

OUR CURRENT BOARD

THE TEN TO KNOW

01
David Bailey
EDGE • New York Jets
The Jets’ 17–12 win over Cleveland belonged to the defense, and Bailey was its biggest difference-maker. Three sacks and repeated pressure changed the Browns’ protections. He has 26 pressures and five sacks through five weeks, according to the available game reporting. The top spot is earned, not based on draft hype.

3 sacks • 9 pressures • 7 tackles in Week 5

02
Sonny Styles
LB • Washington Commanders
The 27–27 tie made Washington’s late-game mistakes impossible to ignore, but Styles did more than accumulate tackles. His overtime forced fumble showed range and an instinct for the critical snap. He is one of the clearer every-down defensive prospects in this class.

13 tackles • forced fumble in overtime vs Giants

03
Carnell Tate
WR • Tennessee Titans
Tate’s prior breakout still matters, but a team that managed just three points against Houston in Week 5 cannot treat one productive afternoon as a fully formed trend. The next benchmark is staying dangerous when defenses plan around him.

9 catches • 145 yards in Week 4

04
Gavin Bartholomew
TE • Minnesota Vikings
T.J. Hockenson went down, and Bartholomew was asked to be useful in a 5–0 team’s offense. That matters. We are not awarding the spot for a flashy box score; we are measuring reliability, flexibility and the quarterback’s trust under pressure.

5 catches • 33 yards in Week 5

05
Willie Lampkin
OG • Philadelphia Eagles
Rookie offensive linemen rarely win social-media arguments, but a first start in a pressured situation deserves recognition. Philadelphia’s offense still lost in London, so this is about an individual stepping into responsibility, not inventing a team-wide turnaround.

First NFL start • one team sack allowed

06
Makai Lemon
WR • Philadelphia Eagles
We like the skill set, but a high selection is not automatically evidence of immediate star impact. Lemon needs the Eagles to feed him more consistently and show that his separation changes defensive attention.

Role and Week 5 volume remain under evaluation

07
Caleb Douglas
WR • Miami Dolphins
The opportunity is real on a team searching for offense. This ranking is a watch-list judgment, not a statistical claim; we want to track routes, targets and difficult catches instead of elevating a prospect solely because his club needs him.

Usage trend under evaluation

08
Jalon Daniels
QB • Tampa Bay Buccaneers
Wins carry weight for any young quarterback, particularly under prime-time pressure, but we are not treating one start as proof of long-term command. We want to see how Daniels reads blitz looks and handles second-half adjustments.

Week 5 win at Dallas

09
Anthony Hill Jr.
LB • Tennessee Titans
Hill remains a prospect whose speed and tackling range can change the Titans’ front. To rise, he needs repeated impact plays and a defense that keeps games competitive long enough for his skills to show.

Defensive snap and production review ongoing

10
Denzel Boston
WR • Cleveland Browns
Boston found the end zone in a losing effort, but Cleveland’s protection issues complicate any receiver evaluation. We want to see routes, separation and contested-catch execution more often, not only a highlight.

Week 5 touchdown against the Jets

THE BIGGER DEBATE

DEFENSE IS LEADING THIS CLASS.

DAVID BAILEY

THE HEADLINER

His three-sack Week 5 outing is one of the most compelling individual rookie performances of this young season. It also exposes why pass-rush pressure is so valuable: the offense changes before the sack happens.

CARNELL TATE

THE YEAR-LONG TEST

The Tennessee offense has not given Tate consistent support. A top receiver cannot be judged fairly without looking at quarterback play, protection and target quality, but the response in bad situations still matters.

JALON DANIELS

THE QUARTERBACK GAUNTLET

We like giving young quarterbacks credit for wins without assigning veterans’ command to them overnight. The next defenses will have more film, and that is when the development cycle truly starts.

THE NEXT WAVE

EVERY SNAP COUNTS

Offensive-line starts and replacement tight-end roles matter even when they are not trending. We will keep updating as the snap counts and clean statistical lines develop.
