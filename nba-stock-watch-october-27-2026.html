Who’s making noise, who’s under pressure, and which opening-week trends actually matter?

OCTOBER 27, 2026 • THROUGH GAMES OF OCTOBER 26

One week. That’s all we’ve had to evaluate the 2026–27 NBA season, and the league is already giving us plenty to discuss. Some contenders have come out swinging. Some young players look ready to accelerate their development. Others are discovering that offseason expectations mean nothing once the ball goes up.

We’re not handing out trophies in October. We’re separating real basketball trends from temporary hot streaks, and watching how injuries are already changing the equation.

STOCK UP / 01

LUKA DONČIĆ

LOS ANGELES LAKERS
52 points, 10 assists and eight threes against Utah

Luka's 52-point performance was more than an early scoring headline. Los Angeles trailed Utah by 18, and Dončić turned the game by dictating matchups, forcing defensive rotations and finding the right moments to attack. Austin Reaves added 29, illustrating why the Lakers' offense can survive even when the defense is loaded toward its lead creator. The concern isn't the ceiling; it's whether the defense and bench can support this kind of offensive brilliance over six months.

THE 4DK READ: The MVP conversation is already warranted, but championship talk requires a complete team.

STOCK UP / 02

HOUSTON ROCKETS

4–0 START
Şengün: 29 points, nine rebounds, nine assists and six steals vs. Atlanta

Houston's unbeaten start is built on more than one scorer getting hot. Kevin Durant supplies a dependable release valve, Şengün organizes possessions from the frontcourt, Amen Thompson creates pressure through athleticism and Fred VanVleet provides structure. Against Atlanta, Houston survived a back-and-forth game before separating with a 14–0 fourth-quarter run. That's a useful early signal: this group can execute when the game tightens.

THE 4DK READ: The Rockets look like a legitimate contender, provided their versatility holds up against elite defenses.

STOCK UP / 03

MEMPHIS GRIZZLIES

4–0 START
Ty Jerome named Western Conference Player of the Week

Ty Jerome's start has given Memphis a steady offensive hand. He scored 31 against Sacramento, then found crucial late buckets against Utah despite an inefficient shooting night. That second performance matters: the Grizzlies needed decisions and poise, not just another scoring explosion. Cameron Boozer's 16-point, 11-rebound game against Sacramento adds another intriguing layer to a team mixing veterans with emerging talent.

THE 4DK READ: Memphis has earned attention; Jerome's decision-making and Boozer's growth are the trends to follow.

STOCK UP / 04

CALEB WILSON

CHICAGO BULLS
67 points through his first three NBA games

Wilson's first three games were more than a nice rookie introduction. His scoring volume came with the physical activity and versatility that made him so interesting as a prospect. The Bulls have every reason to let him learn through meaningful minutes, even when the defensive rotations or offensive counters aren't polished. His production should not become a reason to rush comparisons; it should make the development plan even more deliberate.

THE 4DK READ: We see a potential centerpiece, not a finished product.

STOCK UP / CAUTION / 05

OKLAHOMA CITY THUNDER

4–0 START
Ajay Mitchell scored 30 against Phoenix

Oklahoma City stayed unbeaten even after the severe injuries to Jalen Williams and Cason Wallace. Ajay Mitchell's 30 against Phoenix showed how the Thunder's depth and player development can create short-term answers. But depth cannot automatically reproduce Williams' two-way creation or Wallace's perimeter defense. This is the distinction between surviving October and winning four playoff rounds.

THE 4DK READ: The Thunder's structure is impressive; their championship margin for error has narrowed.

STOCK DOWN / 06

MIAMI HEAT

0–4 START
Giannis recorded 29 points, 14 rebounds and 11 assists against Dallas

A Giannis triple-double still wasn't enough to secure Miami's first win. The Heat have offensive talent, but the pieces need clearer spacing, role definition and defensive consistency. A major roster change demands adjustment; it doesn't excuse every possession. The Eastern Conference will punish stretches of indecision while Miami works through those changes.

THE 4DK READ: No panic verdict after four games, but 0–4 is a genuine warning.

STOCK DOWN / 07

CHICAGO BULLS

DEFENSIVE EXECUTION
Lost 134–99 to Boston

Chicago's development project includes growing pains, but Boston exposed how quickly mistakes compound. Payton Pritchard scored 19 in the third quarter, and the Celtics turned defensive pressure and perimeter accuracy into a blowout. Young players need freedom to learn; they also need repeatable habits when opponents change coverages or accelerate the pace.

THE 4DK READ: Wilson's upside is real, but Chicago must establish defensive accountability.

STOCK DOWN / 08

PHOENIX SUNS

EARLY INCONSISTENCY
41-point loss to Portland before a bounce-back win

Phoenix has already shown two dramatically different versions of itself. Devin Booker and Jalen Green give the Suns shot creation, but shot creation isn't the same as a dependable offensive identity. The bigger question is whether Phoenix can defend consistently and find lineups that complement its stars rather than simply taking turns.

THE 4DK READ: One blowout doesn't define the season; recurring defensive problems might.

BREAKOUT RADAR / 09

YAXEL LENDEBORG

GOLDEN STATE WARRIORS
31 points, 12 rebounds, six assists against Denver

Lendeborg's explosion against Denver offered a compelling look at why Golden State values players who can think and react quickly. He contributed as a scorer, rebounder and passer while Stephen Curry delivered a perfect 8-for-8 shooting night. The key question is consistency: can Lendeborg bring the same versatility when defenses begin planning specifically for him?

THE 4DK READ: His basketball feel could make him a major long-term fit.

BREAKOUT RADAR / 10

COOPER FLAGG

DALLAS MAVERICKS
25 points, 10 assists, five rebounds, five steals vs. Miami

Flagg's performance against Miami was remarkable for its range of contributions. The assists and steals matter just as much as the points. He can influence the game without waiting for isolation possessions, and that makes him especially valuable alongside established creators. Dallas should encourage that all-around development rather than reducing his progress to nightly scoring totals.

THE 4DK READ: The next leap is becoming a dependable decision-maker every night.

4DK CONTENDER CHECK

THE FIRST WEEK GIVES US CLUES. NOT FINAL ANSWERS.

Houston and Oklahoma City have started 4–0. Luka has given Los Angeles an early MVP-level performance. Memphis looks more dangerous than many expected. But Miami is searching for its first win, Phoenix is searching for consistency, and injuries have already complicated the outlook for multiple contenders.

These are the stories we’ll keep measuring against the eye test and the results. The teams establishing good habits now are building something. The teams struggling still have time to respond.
