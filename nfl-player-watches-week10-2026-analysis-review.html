MVP WATCH

No. 1 • Buffalo Bills

Josh Allen

Buffalo's 7–1 start and another prime-time victory keep Allen atop the 4DK ballot. His candidacy is about control as much as fireworks: when defenses force patient football, the Bills still find a way to finish. The separation from the field depends on sustaining that efficiency against elite opponents.

No. 2 • San Francisco 49ers

Brock Purdy

Purdy's case rests on the 49ers' 7–1 record and an offense that has helped San Francisco recover from its first loss. He does not need to win every week through volume alone. The strongest argument is command of an attack that complements a turnover-hunting defense.

No. 3 • Detroit Lions

Jared Goff

Detroit's 6–2 record puts Goff firmly in the conversation, even after a defensive-led win in Miami. MVP value also shows up in avoiding mistakes and keeping a talented team on schedule. He needs more signature offensive performances to pressure the top two.

No. 4 • Jacksonville Jaguars

Trevor Lawrence

Jacksonville's 33–10 rout of Baltimore elevated Lawrence's profile. A 6–2 record and the team's commanding scoring differential provide real support, but voters will want to see that level sustained against contenders rather than treated as one exceptional night.

No. 5 • Seattle Seahawks

Jaxon Smith-Njigba

The highest-ranked non-quarterback on this ballot has a harder road to the trophy. Smith-Njigba's receiving impact must be judged against the positional bias that typically favors quarterbacks; his value lies in how much Seattle's offense depends on him.

No. 6 • Kansas City Chiefs

Patrick Mahomes

Kansas City is 6–2 and remains firmly in the AFC race. Mahomes has the platform to climb quickly, but the 4DK ballot rewards the season to date, not past trophies or reputation. His next statement opportunity will matter.

No. 7 • Cincinnati Bengals

Joe Burrow

Cincinnati's four-game winning streak and 34-point outing against Atlanta have moved Burrow's case forward. The Bengals are producing points and winning; the question is whether that surge continues against stronger defensive opposition.

No. 8 • Green Bay Packers

Jordan Love

A 45-point performance at New England puts Love's offense in the spotlight. Green Bay has the explosive ceiling to make this candidacy interesting, but a 6–3 record leaves less margin for error than the candidates above him.

No. 9 • Chicago Bears

Caleb Williams

Chicago is 7–2, yet four interceptions in the win over Tampa Bay illustrate why Williams is ninth rather than higher. His ability to recover and win matters; his decision-making must become cleaner before he can challenge the leaders.

No. 10 • Buffalo Bills

James Cook

Cook gives Buffalo a closing element that can turn competitive games into controlled wins. He is a genuine part of the Bills' identity, even if sharing the spotlight with Allen makes a running back MVP campaign especially difficult.

ROOKIE WATCH

No. 1 • New York Jets

David Bailey

Bailey remains the 4DK rookie leader on the strength of his disruptive defensive profile. On a struggling team, individual impact must be separated from the win-loss record; verified pressure and tackle-for-loss production will be central to the final case.

No. 2 • Washington Commanders

Sonny Styles

Styles stays second because versatility and consistency at linebacker are central to his evaluation. Washington's uneven season makes it especially important to examine the plays he creates independently of the final score.

No. 3 • New York Giants

Arvell Reese

Reese has the chance to influence games in several roles. The Giants' position atop the NFC East gives his rookie campaign a meaningful team context, but the final article will distinguish actual production from projection.

No. 4 • Cleveland Browns

Denzel Boston

Boston's place in the top four reflects the value 4DK sees in his receiving contribution. The evaluation should emphasize target quality, chain-moving catches and game situations—not raw totals without context.

No. 5 • Miami Dolphins

Jacob Rodriguez

Rodriguez is a bright spot on a one-win team. His ranking rewards individual defensive effort while recognizing that team record is an incomplete measure of a rookie linebacker's performance.

No. 6 • Tennessee Titans

Carnell Tate

Tate's offensive opportunity is part of a broader Tennessee rebuild. The 4DK lens asks whether he is creating reliable separation and becoming a foundational target, rather than judging him only by a difficult team season.

No. 7 • Kansas City Chiefs

Mansoor Delane

A young corner contributing to a 6–2 contender faces demanding assignments. Delane's case should be measured by coverage responsibility, ball production and the degree to which the Chiefs trust him in important situations.

No. 8 • Pittsburgh Steelers

Germie Bernard

Bernard remains eighth entering Pittsburgh's return from its bye. His position depends on the quality of his receiving contributions and whether he can build on them as the Steelers protect their division standing.

No. 9 • Dallas Cowboys

Caleb Downs

Downs rises into the ninth position in the locked 4DK order. The attraction is his ability to affect multiple phases of defense, from tackling to coverage and pressure. The final profile will verify each category before citing a statistical total.

No. 10 • Cleveland Browns

KC Concepcion

Concepcion completes the top ten. The Browns' close overtime loss to New Orleans illustrates the importance of offensive playmakers who can change field position. His final evaluation will prioritize verified receiving production and recent trend.
