GAME 01 · FINAL
Lakers 124, Hawks 114

Luka turns Atlanta’s late charge into another Lakers statement

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT
Luka Dončić: 32 PTS · 8 REB · 10 AST · 5 STL | Walker Kessler: 21 PTS · 10 REB | CJ McCollum: 21 PTS
The game & turning point

Los Angeles came in without an ill Austin Reaves and still built a 20-point second-quarter cushion. Dončić dictated the early passing angles, Kessler converted interior opportunities, and the Lakers scored 68 first-half points. Atlanta fought back behind its perimeter depth and got within 115-111 with 3:30 left. The difference was composure: a 6-0 Lakers response, with Luka attacking the rim and creating for teammates, shut down the comeback.

The 4DK read

The sixth consecutive win puts the Lakers at 10-2. The offense can survive an absent high-volume scorer when Luka manipulates help defenders and the center position finishes plays. Atlanta’s four-game streak ends, but its rally showed competitive depth; the defensive lapses that created the early hole remain the issue.

The bigger tactical story

The Lakers did not merely replace Reaves’s scoring with more Luka possessions. They leaned into the relationship between Dončić’s playmaking and Kessler’s finishing: when a defense has to account for the ball handler and a reliable target around the basket, its help decisions become more difficult. That is the framework behind the 68-point first half. Yet the late 115–111 score matters just as much as the early lead. A contender cannot assume a 20-point cushion will protect itself; its half-court defense and late-game shot selection still have to hold up when the opponent speeds the game up.

What comes next

At 10–2, Los Angeles has earned early credibility, but 4DK wants to see whether that closing composure travels into games against elite defenses. The six-game winning streak is a meaningful signal, not a finished verdict. Atlanta’s lesson is equally specific: its perimeter scoring can erase deficits quickly, but winning consistently requires preventing the deficit in the first place. The Hawks cannot make every night a rescue mission.

GAME 02 · FINAL
Hornets 124, Cavaliers 112

Charlotte finds its outside shot — then nearly lets it slip

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT
Grayson Allen: 27 PTS | Naz Reid: 25 PTS · 13 REB | Ryan Kalkbrenner: 15 REB | Meleek Thomas: 18 PTS
The game & turning point

Charlotte made 23 of 46 threes and led by as many as 33. That volume changed Cleveland’s defensive geometry: closeouts became urgent, and the Hornets could play with the advantage instead of creating against a set shell. But the Cavaliers kept coming, trimming the deficit to eight late. Charlotte’s fourth-quarter scoring dropped to 17, a reminder that hot shooting cannot substitute for sustained execution.

The 4DK read

The Hornets end a six-game skid and get a badly needed offensive confidence boost. Cleveland should take little comfort in the late rally: allowing 69 points in the first half and 107 through three quarters is not a viable foundation. The game was decided by Charlotte’s perimeter barrage long before the final buzzer.

The bigger tactical story

Twenty-three made threes on 46 attempts is a mathematical avalanche. Charlotte effectively turned every late closeout into a decision point: contest aggressively and risk an opening elsewhere, or stay home and concede another clean perimeter look. The 33-point lead shows how damaging that choice became for Cleveland. But the 17-point fourth quarter also exposes the volatility of an offense built around an extraordinary shooting night. Once the rhythm cooled, the Hornets struggled to control the finish.

What comes next

The encouraging Charlotte development is not that it can shoot 50 percent from deep every night; it is that multiple contributors, including Reid and Allen, can carry meaningful offensive responsibility. The challenge is to build reliable possessions when the threes stop falling. For Cleveland, the late comeback cannot erase the defensive failure that preceded it. A team with serious postseason ambitions must establish its resistance before the scoreboard forces desperation.

GAME 03 · FINAL
Pistons 122, Wizards 111

Detroit answers a superstar scoring night with collective offense

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT
Anthony Davis: 44 PTS · 13 REB | John Collins: 24 PTS · 10 REB | Daniss Jenkins: 22 PTS · 8 AST | AJ Dybantsa: 21 PTS
The game & turning point

Anthony Davis was almost unstoppable early, pouring in 30 first-half points, but Detroit resisted the temptation to turn the contest into a one-man scoring duel. The Pistons spread production across Collins, Jenkins and Duncan Robinson, then hit 63.2% from the field in the third quarter. That 35-29 period pushed the margin to 16 entering the fourth. Washington’s comeback never erased the deficit, despite Davis’s huge individual output.

The 4DK read

Detroit’s depth and balanced shot creation matter after a tough prior outing. Washington’s fourth consecutive loss underlines the difference between elite individual production and a functioning five-man offense. Davis supplied star power; Detroit supplied the more repeatable winning formula.

The bigger tactical story

Davis’s 30 first-half points put Detroit in a difficult spot: chase the individual scorer at the expense of everyone else, or keep its offensive structure intact and make Washington prove it could win through multiple options. The Pistons chose the second route. Collins’s work on the glass and Jenkins’s creation gave Detroit more ways to score, while the 35-point third quarter created the separation. This was a contest in which the most spectacular individual box score did not belong to the team with the most functional overall attack.

What comes next

Detroit should take confidence from a win in which it did not need one player to answer every Washington basket. That type of distribution is useful when a primary option is cold or an opponent loads up on one matchup. Washington has a more complicated question: how does it convert Davis’s dominance into team-wide efficiency and enough stops? Forty-four points and 13 rebounds are a headline. Four straight losses are the larger story.

GAME 04 · FINAL
Grizzlies 118, Raptors 102

Jerome and Boozer steady Memphis on the road

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT
Ty Jerome: 27 PTS | Cameron Boozer: 22 PTS · 7 REB · 8 AST | Jamal Shead: 20 PTS · 11 AST
The game & turning point

Memphis broke a three-game slide by recovering its half-court rhythm. Jerome hit five threes, while Boozer operated as both finisher and connector. A 12-0 third-quarter burst established control, but Toronto made another push and narrowed the gap to two in the fourth. Memphis responded with a Jerami Grant three and a 7-0 burst, then closed the door with defense that held Toronto scoreless for nearly five minutes.

The 4DK read

The Grizzlies move to 5-0 away from home — a meaningful early marker of composure. Boozer’s 22-7-8 is more important than the raw scoring total: his decision-making gives Memphis another way to manufacture efficient possessions. Toronto’s late drought exposes the risk of relying on short offensive bursts.

The bigger tactical story

Boozer’s eight assists reveal why this was more than a hot scoring night. When a frontcourt player can see the next pass and punish an overcommitment, a defense cannot simply send a second body and expect the possession to end. Jerome’s five threes supplied the spacing that made those decisions harder. Memphis also demonstrated an important distinction between creating a lead and protecting one: Toronto cut the margin to two, but the Grizzlies answered with a 7–0 response and a nearly five-minute defensive shutdown.

What comes next

The 5–0 road mark suggests Memphis has been able to carry its offensive habits into uncomfortable environments. That is especially important for a young roster trying to establish itself beyond highlight performances. Boozer’s 22–7–8 should strengthen his case as a serious rookie-impact player because it touched scoring, rebounding and organization. Toronto’s concern is its late-game offense: a comeback is not complete until a team can manufacture good looks after the opponent adjusts.

GAME 05 · FINAL
Mavericks 125, Pacers 122

Cooper Flagg delivers the night’s most explosive comeback

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT
Cooper Flagg: 43 PTS · 10 REB · 6 AST · 3 STL | Kyrie Irving: 35 PTS | Pascal Siakam: 29 PTS · 9 REB · 8 AST
The game & turning point

Indiana led 66-56 at the half after Pascal Siakam dominated the early offensive exchanges. Dallas needed a different level of aggression and got it from Flagg: 30 second-half points, relentless downhill pressure and timely creation. Irving supplied 35, including seven threes, and the two combined for 26 fourth-quarter points. The final minute remained chaotic. Indiana had a chance to tie, but Dallas survived missed free throws and a last-possession deflection by Caleb Martin.

The 4DK read

This is an early signature performance for Flagg: not simply 43 points, but a comeback with high-leverage possessions and six assists. Dallas completes a 3-0 homestand at 7-4. Indiana falls to 3-8 and must address how a 10-point halftime advantage became a 69-point second half for the opponent.

The bigger tactical story

Flagg’s second half changed the terms of the matchup. Indiana had been comfortable enough to score 66 before halftime, but Dallas flipped the pressure with a young forward who was not waiting for the offense to come to him. The six assists are central to the story: a 43-point scorer who still makes the next read is more difficult to contain than one who simply hunts shots. Irving’s seven threes gave Indiana another threat it could not abandon, and their combined fourth-quarter production turned the comeback into a sustained two-man attack.

What comes next

This is the kind of game that can alter how opponents prepare for Flagg. A 19-year-old producing 43 in a comeback forces defenses to consider him a primary problem, not just a promising piece. Dallas is 7–4 after a perfect homestand, though the missed free throws and frantic finish show there is still closing-game polish to develop. Indiana’s 3–8 start raises the opposite question: why did its defensive solutions disappear after a strong first half? A 69-point concession after intermission is too costly to dismiss.

GAME 06 · FINAL
Rockets 110, Nuggets 100

Houston wins the second punch against Denver

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT
Kevin Durant: 23 PTS · 12 REB · 8 AST | Alperen Şengün: 20 PTS · 11 REB · 8 AST | Jamal Murray: 38 PTS | Nikola Jokić: 25 PTS · 16 REB
The game & turning point

Houston established the first advantage with early three-point shooting and built a 19-point second-quarter lead. Denver responded with an 18-2 run, bringing the game level at 60 in the third. That was the real test: Houston did not panic. Marcus Smart and Tari Eason helped change the defensive pressure, and the Rockets finished the third up nine. Fourth-quarter threes from Reed Sheppard, Jabari Smith Jr. and Fred VanVleet restored separation.

The 4DK read

The Rockets snap a two-game skid against a genuine measuring-stick opponent. Durant and Şengün each approaching triple-doubles is a glimpse of the team’s dual-hub identity. Murray’s 38 and Jokić’s near triple-double kept Denver alive, but Houston’s defensive response after the 18-2 run decided the night.

The bigger tactical story

The key Houston possession was not a single highlight; it was the stretch after Denver’s 18–2 run tied the game. Many teams can look sharp while building a 19-point lead. Far fewer respond well when an opponent led by Jokić and Murray wipes that advantage away. Houston regained its footing through defensive activity and a broader offensive menu. Durant and Şengün were both near triple-doubles, while the later threes from Sheppard, Smith and VanVleet showed how the offense can punish attention paid to its central creators.

What comes next

4DK views this as a meaningful early measuring-stick win because Houston showed it could absorb a counterpunch rather than merely overwhelm a team from the opening tip. The dual-hub possibilities with Durant and Şengün are intriguing: one can initiate while the other becomes the release valve. Denver still got 38 from Murray and 25 with 16 boards from Jokić, so the defeat is less an indictment of its stars than a reminder that the supporting defensive possessions must survive when the game swings.

GAME 07 · FINAL
Spurs 122, Suns 96

Wembanyama turns Phoenix into a no-fly zone

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT
Victor Wembanyama: 40 PTS · 11 REB · 8 BLK | Dylan Harper: 17 PTS | Devin Booker: 32 PTS
The game & turning point

The game became a defensive demonstration. Wembanyama put up 22 points and five blocks before halftime; a 21-2 San Antonio run produced a 61-46 lead. Harper then scored 14 in the third quarter, helping the Spurs stretch the margin to 25 entering the fourth. Wembanyama needed only about 25 minutes to score 40 on 15-for-19 shooting, while the Suns were held under 40% from the field.

The 4DK read

San Antonio has won nine straight after opening 0-2. This is what separates a good young team from a potential contender: Wembanyama changes opponents’ decisions before they shoot, and the guards can punish the openings. Booker’s 32 was not enough to offset Phoenix’s lack of balanced production.

The bigger tactical story

Eight blocks in roughly 25 minutes is not simply rim protection; it is an offensive disruption mechanism for the other team. Wembanyama’s presence changes whether a driver commits to the basket, pulls up early or sends the ball back out. The box score captures eight rejections, but it cannot fully capture the possessions that never become clean attempts. On the other end, 40 points on 15-for-19 shooting made Phoenix pay for focusing so much energy on navigating his defense. Harper’s 14-point third quarter then turned San Antonio’s advantage into a rout.

What comes next

Nine straight wins after an 0–2 opening make San Antonio one of the most compelling early-season stories. The question is no longer whether Wembanyama can produce a monster line; it is how consistently the Spurs can pair his two-way dominance with reliable guard production. Phoenix got 32 from Booker and still lost by 26. That gap underscores how much more collective shot creation and defensive resistance the Suns need when facing a team with this level of interior control.

GAME 08 · FINAL
Warriors 138, Pelicans 87

Golden State’s 51-point demolition announces an eight-game streak

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT
Stephen Curry: 25 PTS | Brandin Podziemski: 25 PTS | Yaxel Lendeborg: 23 PTS | Derik Queen: 16 PTS
The game & turning point

Golden State overwhelmed New Orleans with both pace and precision. The Warriors scored 35, 37 and 40 points in the first three quarters and led 112-65 entering the fourth. They shot 54.9% overall and made 21 threes, with rookie Yaxel Lendeborg going 8-for-10 from the floor and a perfect 4-for-4 from deep. New Orleans never found a defensive counter and managed only 87 points.

The 4DK read

After an 0-3 start, Golden State has won eight straight, completing a 6-0 homestand. The 51-point margin is a warning that this is not only Curry’s offense: Podziemski and Lendeborg give the Warriors additional scoring layers. New Orleans must answer questions about defensive resistance and consistency after a third consecutive defeat.

The bigger tactical story

Golden State’s 35–37–40 scoring progression through three quarters is the signature of a team that never allowed its opponent to reset. Curry’s 25 mattered, but the more revealing numbers came from Podziemski matching him and Lendeborg adding 23 on 8-for-10 shooting. When the Warriors get that kind of efficient production beyond their centerpiece, defensive attention on Curry is no longer enough to disrupt the offense. Twenty-one made threes and a 112–65 lead entering the fourth speak to an advantage in execution, not just one exceptional run.

What comes next

An eight-game streak following an 0–3 start gives Golden State momentum, but 4DK is watching the sustainability of its secondary scoring more than the 51-point margin itself. Podziemski and Lendeborg do not have to reproduce these totals every night; they do have to keep defenses honest. For New Orleans, a third straight defeat capped by an 87-point output and a 51-point loss demands a response in defensive organization, transition effort and offensive shot quality. The score was extreme. The questions it raises are fundamental.
