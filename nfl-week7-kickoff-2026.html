NFL • WEEK 7 • OCTOBER 22–26, 2026

WEEK 7.
NO ROOM TO HIDE.

Maye vs. Caleb. The undefeated Niners. Walker’s Seattle homecoming. A weekend full of answers.

Six weeks have given us the contenders, surprises and warning signs. Now the schedule demands more. We break down the matchups, what matters tactically, and what each result could mean as the playoff picture begins to form.

4DK coverage standard: Every game deserves context, not just a score. This preview stays separate from our Week 6 recap and rankings archive.

TONIGHT • THURSDAY, OCTOBER 22 • 5:15 PM PT

PATRIOTS AT BEARS: MAYE VS. CALEB IS A REAL CONTENDER TEST

Both clubs enter 4–2, but their paths have been different. New England has won three straight behind Drake Maye’s improved decision-making; Chicago is coming off a win over Atlanta with Caleb Williams returning from injury. This is not just a quarterback showcase. It is a test of whether either team can control a game against another credible playoff challenger.

Maye has thrown 10 touchdowns against one interception over the last three weeks. Chicago must keep him from extending plays and generating explosive throws, while testing a Patriots offense missing multiple offensive linemen. New England has ruled out guards Alijah Vera-Tucker and Ben Brown, and tackle Morgan Moses is questionable. How the replacements handle interior pressure could dictate the entire evening.

Chicago leads the league in rushing at 160.7 yards per game, with D’Andre Swift and its physical approach helping keep the defense fresh. Kyle Monangai is out, which changes the rotation but not the basic identity. Caleb’s ability to punish single-high looks and make off-schedule throws will be critical if New England commits extra bodies to the run.

A.J. Brown’s three-catch, 27-yard return against the Jets left plenty to prove, particularly with Romeo Doubs sidelined. New England needs dependable receiving production. Chicago, meanwhile, must be disciplined against Maye despite its knack for generating takeaways.

4DK VERDICT: The decisive battle is the line of scrimmage and turnover margin. Maye has been hotter lately, but the Bears can shorten the game through their run game. We want to see which young quarterback remains patient when the easy answers disappear.

SUNDAY • OCTOBER 25 • 10:00 AM PT

49ERS AT FALCONS: CAN SAN FRANCISCO KEEP ITS PERFECT START?

The 49ers are coming off a 23–17 Monday night win over Washington, and the undefeated run now moves into another road test with less rest. Atlanta has enough offensive talent to punish a defense that gets sloppy, but the larger story is whether San Francisco can continue winning with control rather than surviving on late heroics.

Brock Purdy’s efficiency remains the centerpiece. The growing chemistry with Mike Evans adds a contested-catch option who can change third downs and red-zone possessions. We also want to see how the staff distributes touches to Christian McCaffrey: his versatility is elite, but keeping him fresh into December matters more than padding October volume.

Defensively, the 49ers need disciplined tackling and sound run fits against Bijan Robinson. Atlanta’s ability to force missed tackles and create manageable third downs could keep the game close. The Niners cannot give up explosive plays simply because they have been winning.

4DK VERDICT: This is a measuring-stick game for the Niners’ depth and maturity. If San Francisco wins cleanly on a short week, it strengthens the case that this team has a sustainable championship formula.

SUNDAY • OCTOBER 25 • 5:20 PM PT

CHIEFS AT SEAHAWKS: THE HOMECOMING — KENNETH WALKER RETURNS AS THE NFL’S LEADING RUSHER

4DK WEEK 7 COVER STORY: The reigning Super Bowl MVP comes back to Seattle with 665 rushing yards, a 5–0 record and a new role at the heart of Kansas City’s offense.

THE HOMECOMING IS BIGGER THAN A REVENGE GAME

Kenneth Walker III is coming back to Lumen Field with a championship ring, a Super Bowl MVP award and a new identity as the centerpiece of the Kansas City running game. Seattle drafted him in the second round in 2022, developed him alongside Zach Charbonnet and watched him become a playoff difference-maker in its championship run. Now the former Seahawk arrives wearing Chiefs red, and the numbers tell us this is not just a familiar face returning. He has become one of the defining players of the first six weeks.

THE ROAD FROM SEATTLE TO KANSAS CITY

For much of his Seattle tenure, Walker shared the backfield with Charbonnet. The split limited how often the offense could lean on him as an every-down centerpiece, even as his burst, vision and ability to escape first contact stood out. When Charbonnet was injured late last season, Walker took on a larger playoff workload and answered emphatically: 116 rushing yards and three touchdowns against San Francisco in the postseason opener, another score against the Rams, and 135 rushing yards against New England in the Super Bowl. That championship performance earned him Super Bowl MVP. Seattle got a title out of Walker; Kansas City acquired a player who had already proven he could carry the offense when the stakes were highest.

665 YARDS IN FIVE GAMES — AND HE HAS ALREADY HAD HIS BYE

Through Week 6, Walker leads the entire NFL with 665 rushing yards on 106 carries, an average of 6.3 yards per attempt and roughly 133 per game. He has six rushing touchdowns and four 100-yard rushing performances in five appearances. That last detail matters: Kansas City had its bye in Week 5, so Walker has played fewer games than many of his competitors. He is first in total rushing yards anyway. In Week 6 against the Chargers, he produced 128 yards and two scores on 19 carries as the Chiefs won 22–6. The production is not a one-week statistical explosion; it is a repeatable foundation for a 5–0 team.

THE GAME LOG EXPLAINS THE EXPLOSION

Walker opened his Chiefs career with 173 rushing yards against Denver, followed with 117 against Indianapolis, ran for 70 against Miami, exploded for 177 against Las Vegas and then added 128 against the Chargers. The 177-yard Raiders game included a 76-yard run, and the Denver opener included a 60-yarder. But the explosive plays only tell half the story. Kansas City has been able to hand him the ball repeatedly without sacrificing efficiency, which makes every play-action fake and every obvious passing down more dangerous for a defense.

WHY WALKER CHANGED THE CHIEFS

In previous versions of this Kansas City offense, opponents could devote enormous attention to Mahomes, the receivers and Travis Kelce, trusting that they could survive the rushing attack. Walker changes the arithmetic. Defenses now have to respect the possibility of a touchdown run from midfield, while also accounting for his ability to create yards after contact. Through five games, Kansas City credited him with 369 rushing yards after contact and a league-leading 183 rushing yards against stacked boxes. Even when opponents bring an extra defender near the line, he can win. That forces defensive coordinators to choose between protecting against the run and leaving more room for Mahomes to work.

THE NUMBERS BEHIND THE EYE TEST

Walker is not just benefiting from generous blocking or a handful of untouched runs. Next Gen Stats credited him with 277 rushing yards over expected through Week 6, more than 100 ahead of the next-closest back. His 13.2 rushing expected points added nearly doubled the runner-up figure. Those measurements reinforce what we see: he creates value beyond what the blocking and situation normally predict. It is early, but the combination of efficiency, volume and big-play production makes him a legitimate Offensive Player of the Year contender and gives him a place in the MVP discussion.

SEATTLE KNOWS HIM — BUT CAN IT CONTAIN HIM?

There is a difference between knowing how a runner wants to attack and actually bringing him down. Seattle’s linebackers and defensive staff know Walker’s patience, jump cuts and acceleration from their years together. They will want to force him sideways, close cutback lanes and tackle in layers rather than allowing one defender to be isolated in space. The first defender must slow him down; the second must finish. If Seattle overcommits extra bodies to stop him, Mahomes will have more favorable passing looks. If the Seahawks stay light in the box, Walker can dictate the game on the ground.

THE OTHER SIDE OF THE HOMECOMING

The Seahawks are 3–3 after winning the Super Bowl, and this game is about their identity as much as Walker’s return. Sam Darnold cannot give Kansas City short fields with forced throws. Jaxon Smith-Njigba remains the kind of explosive receiving threat who can change the game, but Seattle needs balance, clean protection and drives that end in points. The defense must avoid spending the night on the field while Walker controls the clock. A loss would intensify questions about whether the defending champions have found a sustainable offensive rhythm without their former playoff hero.

THE EMOTION — AND THE BUSINESS OF FOOTBALL

This is not a simple villain-and-revenge storyline. Walker helped deliver a championship to Seattle, and the city will remember what he did in that postseason. Players move, backfields change, contracts and roster construction force hard choices. Walker now has the chance to show Seattle the full extent of his value in an offense built to feature him. The Chiefs have a chance to demonstrate that his arrival is more than an exciting early-season story: it may be the roster move that makes their attack complete.

4DK VERDICT: THIS IS A STATEMENT GAME FOR BOTH TEAMS

We are watching whether Kansas City can impose its preferred tempo in one of the league’s toughest road environments. If Walker consistently gets four, five and six yards on early downs, the Chiefs can protect Mahomes, control possession and force Seattle to take chances. If the Seahawks keep Walker bottled up and Darnold protects the football, the game becomes far more uncomfortable for Kansas City. The biggest question is no longer whether Walker is good enough to be a lead back. He has answered that. It is whether the NFL’s leading rusher can walk back into his old stadium, beat a defense that knows him intimately and push a 5–0 Chiefs team another step toward a championship.

SUNDAY • OCTOBER 25 • 1:25 PM PT

RAMS AT RAIDERS: WHO RESPONDS TO ADVERSITY?

The Rams are coming off a 18–16 upset loss to Arizona. Las Vegas just lost a 33–30 thriller to Buffalo. Both teams have reasons to believe they can compete, but both must show they can finish the games that shape a season.

For Los Angeles, Matthew Stafford’s connection with Puka Nacua and the ability to keep its offense balanced are the foundation. The Rams must also avoid defensive lapses that allow opponents to control late-game possessions.

For Las Vegas, Kirk Cousins’ decision-making and the running game will be tested by a Rams defense that can generate pressure. The Raiders have shown more balance this season, but taking the next step means turning competitive performances into wins against strong opponents.

4DK VERDICT: We are watching the fourth quarter. Which team protects the football, adjusts to pressure and gets its playmakers the ball when the game is tight?

SUNDAY • OCTOBER 25 • 6:30 AM PT

STEELERS VS. SAINTS: THE NFL GOES TO PARIS

The first regular-season NFL game in France gives Week 7 an international spotlight. Pittsburgh enters off a 26–19 win over Tampa Bay, while New Orleans defeated the Giants 34–27.

Travel, preparation and early-game execution can matter more than usual in an international matchup. Both clubs must settle in quickly and avoid gifting the opponent short fields.

4DK VERDICT: The team that handles the unusual setting and protects the ball has a major edge.

COWBOYS AT EAGLES: AN NFC EAST SPOTLIGHT

Dallas comes in off a 23–13 loss to Green Bay, while Philadelphia was beaten 25–14 by Carolina. That puts immediate pressure on both teams to respond in a divisional rivalry.

The Cowboys must find more offensive consistency and protect the quarterback against Philadelphia’s pressure looks. The Eagles need better execution after a disappointing performance, particularly in situational football.

4DK VERDICT: The loser will have a lot to answer for. In a division game, effort alone is not enough: protection, third downs and finishing drives are the difference.

THE HOMECOMING IS BIGGER THAN REVENGE: WALKER, SEATTLE AND A CHANGED CHIEFS OFFENSE

Seattle drafted Kenneth Walker III 41st overall in 2022. Four seasons later, he returns to Lumen Field not as a visiting role player but as the reigning Super Bowl LX MVP and the centerpiece of an undefeated Kansas City rushing attack. His 135 rushing yards in Seattle’s championship victory helped define the Seahawks’ season; his decision to join the Chiefs in March made this reunion one of the league’s defining Week 7 stories.

Walker has 665 rushing yards on 106 carries through five games: 6.3 yards per attempt, six rushing touchdowns, 760 yards from scrimmage and eight total touchdowns. He has reached 100 rushing yards four times. His Week 6 performance against the Chargers — 128 yards and two scores on 19 attempts — was not an isolated explosion. It was the continuation of a pattern that has fundamentally altered how defenses have to play Kansas City.

The most revealing numbers are not simply his totals. Walker has produced 369 rushing yards after contact and 183 rushing yards with six scores against stacked boxes, according to the Chiefs’ Week 6 review. Teams know Kansas City can hand him the ball and still have not consistently stopped him. That makes the threat of the run real even when an opponent sells out to prevent it.

Our 4DK read: Mahomes has spent years forcing opponents to account for every blade of grass through the passing game. Walker now punishes those same opponents for light personnel, deep safeties and overaggressive pass-rush lanes. A defense that brings a safety down to meet Walker risks exposing itself to Mahomes. A defense that stays two-high risks surrendering the rushing game. That is what offensive balance actually looks like.

Seattle knows Walker’s patience, sudden acceleration and cutback ability better than anyone. But knowing his tendencies and tackling him are different problems. The Seahawks enter with 16 sacks and seven interceptions, evidence of a disruptive defense, yet the game may turn on whether the front can contain Walker without sacrificing coverage against Travis Kelce and Kansas City’s receivers.

For Seattle, the emotional element matters. Walker helped deliver the franchise a championship and now arrives with a chance to push the Chiefs to 6–0. Jaxon Smith-Njigba, his former teammate, leads the league with 717 receiving yards. The matchup is a fascinating contrast: Seattle’s premier pass catcher against the former Seahawk now leading the NFL on the ground.

4DK VERDICT: The story is not whether Walker gets cheered or booed. It is whether Seattle can stop the player who helped take it to the mountaintop — and whether the Chiefs have finally built an offense that can win without requiring Mahomes to carry every possession.

49ERS AT FALCONS: SIX WINS, BUT THE DETAILS STILL MATTER

San Francisco is 6–0 after surviving Washington 23–17 on Monday night. The Niners led 20–3 at halftime before the Commanders made it a one-score contest. Malik Mustapha’s late fourth-down breakup and a closing sack by Romello Height and Keion White preserved the win. That is a meaningful example of a defense finding its answers when the offense cannot put a game away.

Our 4DK concern is not the record. It is the accumulated workload and the question of how this roster looks in December. Brock Purdy has been among the league’s most efficient quarterbacks, but the offense cannot count on perfect execution every week, especially with receiver availability affecting the passing game. Mike Evans has given Purdy a true contested-catch option, and the next step is continuing to build that trust against tighter coverage.

Christian McCaffrey remains a game-changing weapon, but preserving him for January matters more than chasing maximum touches in October. Atlanta can test San Francisco’s discipline with its run game and play-action looks. The Falcons need to make the Niners defend the full width of the field and avoid letting Purdy operate on schedule.

4DK VERDICT: A 6–0 team can still have weaknesses worth addressing. We want to see San Francisco close more cleanly, manage McCaffrey’s workload and keep its defense fresh on the short turnaround.

PATRIOTS AT BEARS: THE MAYE–CALEB TEST GOES BEYOND HIGHLIGHTS

Both teams enter 4–2, and the matchup has become an early measuring stick for two young quarterbacks. Drake Maye’s ability to create outside structure meets a Chicago defense that can change the picture after the snap. Caleb Williams has the talent to punish undisciplined rush lanes, but the Bears need to build an offense that can stay efficient between the explosive plays.

New England’s protection is the pressure point. Guards Alijah Vera-Tucker and Ben Brown are out, and tackle Morgan Moses is questionable. Those absences can disrupt interior communication, the timing of route concepts and the depth of Maye’s drop. Chicago’s defense does not need a sack on every series to change this game; consistent early-down disruption could be enough.

Chicago is also without Kyle Monangai and Jack Sanborn. That changes personnel flexibility and makes offensive balance more important. Watch third down, red-zone finishing and how often each quarterback is forced to solve obvious passing situations.

4DK VERDICT: This is a contender test, not a referendum on one throw. The quarterback who handles protection breakdowns and late-down pressure better gives his team the edge.

RAMS AT RAIDERS: TWO TEAMS, TWO DIFFERENT TYPES OF PRESSURE

Los Angeles comes to Las Vegas at 2–4 after a narrow loss to Arizona. The Raiders are 3–3 following a 33–30 loss to Buffalo. Both teams have enough individual talent to be dangerous, but neither can afford to keep trading encouraging moments for damaging losses.

The Rams need their offense to create sustainable drives rather than rely exclusively on Matthew Stafford and Puka Nacua manufacturing difficult plays. Las Vegas has to show that its improved competitiveness can turn into fourth-quarter execution. A one-score loss is only useful if the lessons show up the following Sunday.

For the Raiders, the test is handling the Rams’ passing-game sophistication without abandoning the run defense. For Los Angeles, it is preventing Las Vegas from dictating the physical terms of the game. Turnovers and red-zone decisions will matter more than who wins the highlight reel.

4DK VERDICT: This is a response game. We are looking for the team that plays like it understands the urgency of Week 7.

CHIEFS–SEAHAWKS: WHAT WE ARE WATCHING ON FILM

First, Kansas City’s early-down personnel. Does Seattle match heavier formations with extra defenders in the box, or trust its front to handle Walker? Every adjustment has a cost against Mahomes.

Second, Walker’s runs after first contact. His ability to turn a blocked two-yard gain into seven or eight yards is what keeps Kansas City ahead of the chains and opens the full playbook.

Third, Sam Darnold’s ball security. Seattle has the explosive receiving threat in Jaxon Smith-Njigba, but giving Mahomes short fields is the fastest way to make the Seahawks chase a game they want to control.

Fourth, Seattle’s tackling angles on cutbacks. Walker’s acceleration punishes defenders who overrun their gaps. The Seahawks cannot let familiarity with his style become false confidence.

Finally, the emotional swing. The first big Walker run, the first big Seattle stop, and the response from Lumen Field will tell us a lot about how both teams handle the stage.

His impact is not theoretical — the Chiefs are undefeated, and their offensive identity is visibly different. Allen remains our top choice for his dual-threat value; Purdy’s efficiency and San Francisco’s 6–0 record keep him firmly in the conversation.

This week gives Walker a national stage against his former team and gives Purdy another road test. We will assess the complete Week 7 performances before making any ranking moves.
