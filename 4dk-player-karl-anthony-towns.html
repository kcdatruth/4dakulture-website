The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

Towns enters the season as a champion, and that matters because the biggest question around him was never whether he could put up numbers. It was whether his skill set could hold up as a major part of a team that survives four playoff rounds. New York answered that. He averaged 20.1 points and 11.9 rebounds last season, shot 36.8 percent from three and gave the Knicks a floor-spacing big who can force traditional centers away from the rim.

His offensive value is still unique. Few centers can trail a break into a confident three, attack a closeout and finish with touch, then punish a smaller switch on the next possession. Brunson benefits from the extra space, and New York’s wings benefit from the attention KAT demands.

The defense is where the ranking stops. Towns is improved and the Knicks can protect him structurally, but he is not the kind of back-line eraser that lets you play any scheme. Still, a 20-and-12 big who stretches the floor and just helped win a title belongs firmly in this tier.
