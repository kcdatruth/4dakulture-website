The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

Brunson is coming off the season that completed his rise from star to New York icon. He averaged 26 points and 6.8 assists, then finished the job in June as the engine of a Knicks championship and the Finals MVP. That postseason run changes how we evaluate him because the old question—can a smaller guard be the best player on a champion?—has already been answered.

His game is built for the half court. Brunson changes speeds, lives off two feet, uses strength to protect the ball and can create a quality shot without needing elite burst. Defenses can switch bigger wings onto him and he still finds a way to get to the elbow, draw contact or create separation with footwork. KAT’s spacing gives him room, while New York’s wings let him conserve some defensive energy.

There is a legitimate argument that the reigning Finals MVP should rank first. What keeps him at five is not a flaw; it is the ridiculous level of the four players ahead of him. Brunson does not have Wemby’s defensive reach, SGA’s two-way efficiency, Luka’s offensive volume or Jokić’s all-possession control. What he does have is proof. He has already carried a championship offense through the final possession of a season.

The question for 2026–27 is not whether Brunson belongs with the elite. It is whether he can repeat the physical and mental grind required to defend a title when every opponent spends the entire season preparing for New York.
