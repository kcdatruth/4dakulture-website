THE WIN THAT EXPOSED THE WORK LEFT TO DO

Green Bay won the game, but this was not a performance that should leave a contender comfortable. The Packers built a 27–10 advantage, controlled long stretches through Jordan Love's arm and repeatedly disrupted Bryce Young. Then the offense stalled. Three turnovers, inefficient rushing and an empty fourth quarter turned what should have been a routine finish into a game Carolina could still contest with five minutes remaining. The difference between a talented team and a finished product is often revealed in these moments: the Packers have enough playmakers to build a lead, but they still need the discipline and ground-game efficiency to protect it.

LOVE SET THE TERMS — UNTIL GREEN BAY STOPPED FINISHING

Love completed 30 of 41 passes for 288 yards, three touchdowns and one interception. His accuracy and willingness to distribute the ball gave Green Bay multiple answers when Carolina challenged the run. Tucker Kraft supplied a 12-yard touchdown, Matthew Golden scored from 24 yards and Josh Jacobs caught a four-yard scoring pass. The quarterback did not need a dominant rushing attack to create explosive opportunities. But Love's fourth-quarter interception and Green Bay's inability to sustain late possessions underscore the unfinished part of his team's profile. He was the best offensive player in this game, and he still left with areas to correct.

WATSON, GOLDEN AND KRAFT GIVE THIS OFFENSE OPTIONS

Christian Watson's eight catches for 113 yards mattered beyond the box score. His 53-yard reception tilted the field in Green Bay's favor, and his return after precautionary hospital tests for a chest injury gave the Packers a needed vertical threat. Golden finished with seven receptions for 79 yards and a touchdown, while Kraft added six for 53 and a score. That's the encouraging part for Green Bay: Love can win through different targets rather than force every drive through one star. The passing offense has enough variety to punish defensive adjustments; its consistency now has to match its upside.

THE GROUND GAME IS A WARNING SIGN

Josh Jacobs reached the end zone twice, once rushing and once receiving, but his rushing production tells a very different story: 21 carries, 39 yards. Against a Carolina defense that entered with serious vulnerabilities against the run, Green Bay averaged just 2.5 yards per carry as a team. Short-yardage inefficiency forced Love to carry more of the offense, and that imbalance became especially costly when the Packers needed to drain the clock. Touchdowns can disguise a problem for one night; they cannot solve it over a playoff run.

CAROLINA FOUGHT BACK, BUT THE PROTECTION FAILED

The Panthers did not disappear when they fell behind 27–10. Chuba Hubbard ran 22 times for 81 yards and a touchdown and added seven catches for 38 yards. Bryce Young completed 23 of 41 throws for 209 yards, one touchdown and one interception. His late fourth-down touchdown to Jalen Coker narrowed the deficit to eight, showing the offense could still respond under pressure. But Green Bay's six sacks repeatedly broke Carolina's rhythm. The comeback was real; so was the reason it never became a complete reversal. Too many possessions began with Young having to survive rather than dictate.

FOUR MOMENTS THAT DECIDED THURSDAY

First, Ty'Ron Hopper's interception of Young gave Green Bay a short field and helped set up Golden's touchdown before halftime. Second, Love's touchdown pass to Jacobs stretched the lead to 27–10 in the third quarter. Third, Carolina's fourth-down touchdown to Coker cut it to 27–19, but the failed two-point try kept the Panthers a full touchdown and conversion away. Finally, Carolina's ensuing possession ended on downs when a fourth-and-five throw fell incomplete. Green Bay had lost control of the tempo, but its defense delivered the decisive stop.

WHAT IT MEANS FOR THE NFC RACE

At 5–3, Green Bay has enough evidence to claim a place in the NFC playoff conversation, particularly with Love finding several reliable receivers and a front capable of changing games. But the Packers cannot expect six sacks and three passing touchdowns to rescue every sloppy performance. The 3–4 Panthers have reason to value their resistance, yet the offensive line and early-down execution must improve if close games are going to become wins. For our next Power 32, Green Bay's victory counts — and so do the flaws that nearly erased it.

4DK PLAYER OF THE GAME — JORDAN LOVE

Love's 288 passing yards and three touchdowns were the engine behind the Packers' 27 points. Watson's production and the defensive pressure deserve major credit, but Love repeatedly converted difficult passing situations into scoring opportunities. The interception and late stagnation keep this from being a spotless performance. That's exactly why the choice is meaningful: he was the difference-maker in a win that still demands a sharper finish.
