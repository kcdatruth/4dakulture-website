Game 1 / Final

Orlando Magic at Boston Celtics

Orlando Magic 115 · Boston Celtics 105

4DK Film & Feel

Orlando had to win the game twice. Boston emerged from the third quarter ahead 77–75 after a 36–25 period, and the Magic still answered with a 40-point fourth. That is the difference between merely having talent and having enough composure to close on the road.

Turning point

The fourth-quarter reversal was the story: Orlando turned a two-point deficit into a ten-point win. The Magic scored 54 points in the paint to Boston’s 32 and moved the ball for 31 assists, creating easier looks when Boston’s half-court defense tried to settle in.

What it means

Boston’s blowout win over Dallas a night earlier did not carry over. Tatum’s double-double was real production, but the Celtics need more consistent interior resistance; Orlando showed that a balanced attack can survive a momentum swing without Paolo Banchero.

Box Score Spotlight
Desmond Bane 24 PTS, 8 REB, 5 AST; Franz Wagner 21 PTS, 8 REB, 9 AST; Jayson Tatum 24 PTS, 12 REB, 4 AST
Game 2 / Final

Chicago Bulls at Cleveland Cavaliers

Chicago Bulls 115 · Cleveland Cavaliers 126

4DK Film & Feel

Cleveland did not need a perfect night from its backcourt because Donovan Mitchell made the decisive offensive difference. His 39 points on 16-of-25 shooting gave the Cavaliers an efficient anchor while Chicago kept pressing with 36 points in the fourth.

Turning point

The middle quarters flipped the game: Chicago led 31–25 after one, but Cleveland took the second 32–17 and protected the advantage. The Cavaliers controlled the glass 51–37 and won paint scoring 68–50, a physical advantage that mattered more than Chicago’s late burst.

What it means

Mitchell’s scoring is carrying serious weight, but Cleveland’s 21 turnovers still deserve attention. Chicago got 27 from Powell and 23 from Matas Buzelis, yet could not offset the rebounding and interior gap.

Box Score Spotlight
Donovan Mitchell 39 PTS, 4 REB, 4 AST; Jarrett Allen 13 PTS, 14 REB; Norman Powell 27 PTS, 6 AST
Game 3 / Final

Memphis Grizzlies at Washington Wizards

Memphis Grizzlies 136 · Washington Wizards 120

4DK Film & Feel

Memphis is making its early-season success look less like a hot streak and more like an identity. Cam Boozer delivered 29 points, seven rebounds and seven assists, and the Grizzlies played with an unselfishness that made Washington’s defense chase the ball all night.

Turning point

Washington led 69–59 at halftime. Memphis then detonated a 42–18 third quarter, a 24-point swing in one period. The Grizzlies finished with 42 assists, 72 paint points and a 48–31 rebounding edge—three numbers that explain why the game turned so sharply.

What it means

At 10–4, Memphis has earned the right to be discussed as a legitimate early Western Conference riser. Washington got 23 from Anthony Davis and 21 from AJ Dybantsa, but its inability to contain the third-quarter avalanche exposed the defensive work ahead.

Box Score Spotlight
Cam Boozer 29 PTS, 7 REB, 7 AST; Zach Edey 23 PTS, 10 REB; Anthony Davis 23 PTS
Game 4 / Final

Philadelphia 76ers at Brooklyn Nets

Philadelphia 76ers 135 · Brooklyn Nets 123

4DK Film & Feel

Philadelphia’s offensive ceiling is getting difficult to ignore. Joel Embiid scored 37, Jaylen Brown added a powerful 31-point, 13-rebound night, and Tyrese Maxey ran the offense with 24 points and nine assists. Three different kinds of pressure—post scoring, wing creation and speed—kept Brooklyn in rotation.

Turning point

The Sixers exploded for 45 points in the second quarter and led 78–59 at halftime. Brooklyn answered with 36 in the third, but Philadelphia’s cushion and multiple scoring options prevented the Nets from completing the comeback.

What it means

The 12–3 Sixers are building a real case near the top of the East. Brooklyn’s Julius Randle scored 33, but the Nets still surrendered 135; the bigger problem is the number of ways Philadelphia can attack even when the opponent has a productive scorer.

Box Score Spotlight
Joel Embiid 37 PTS, 5 REB; Jaylen Brown 31 PTS, 13 REB; Tyrese Maxey 24 PTS, 9 AST; Julius Randle 33 PTS
Game 5 / Final

Houston Rockets at Miami Heat

Houston Rockets 110 · Miami Heat 128

4DK Film & Feel

Miami gave Houston a sharp reminder that star power alone does not decide a game. The Heat were cleaner with the ball, more purposeful in their offensive execution and much better at turning good possessions into efficient shots. Giannis supplied 20 points, 13 boards and six assists without needing a 40-point showcase.

Turning point

Miami raced to a 29–15 first-quarter lead and scored 39 more in the third. The Heat committed just nine turnovers to Houston’s 14 and shot 50 percent to the Rockets’ 41.3 percent, sustaining the lead through execution rather than one isolated run.

What it means

This is a statement win for a Miami team trying to establish consistency. Houston’s Sengun and Durant combined for 45, but the Rockets must address defensive containment and spacing when opponents take away their preferred rhythm.

Box Score Spotlight
Giannis Antetokounmpo 20 PTS, 13 REB, 6 AST; Alperen Sengun 23 PTS, 7 REB; Kevin Durant 22 PTS
Game 6 / Final

Portland Trail Blazers at Minnesota Timberwolves

Portland Trail Blazers 98 · Minnesota Timberwolves 106

4DK Film & Feel

Minnesota keeps proving it can win without Anthony Edwards. Terrence Shannon Jr. seized the scoring responsibility with 34 points, while Rudy Gobert delivered the interior presence that helped the Wolves survive a rough shooting night. This was not a glamorous offensive performance; it was a resilience test against a Portland team that has earned respect.

Turning point

A 33–25 second quarter gave Minnesota the halftime lead, and the Wolves extended it with a 28–22 third. Portland held Minnesota to 19 in the fourth but never fully erased the deficit. Minnesota won the rebounding battle 53–49, and Shannon’s production created separation in a game where neither side shot 40 percent.

What it means

At 10–4 overall while navigating Edwards’ absence, the Wolves have moved from merely staying afloat to strengthening their contender argument. Portland got 27 from Avdija but Damian Lillard shot 2-of-15; that imbalance made the Blazers easier to defend.

Box Score Spotlight
Terrence Shannon Jr. 34 PTS, 6 AST; Rudy Gobert 18 PTS, 15 REB; Deni Avdija 27 PTS, 8 REB
Game 7 / Final

Golden State Warriors at New Orleans Pelicans

Golden State Warriors 94 · New Orleans Pelicans 115

4DK Film & Feel

The Warriors’ offensive problems without Stephen Curry were impossible to disguise. Yaxel Lendeborg fought for 28 points and eight rebounds, but Golden State fell into a hole too deep for one rookie’s effort to solve. New Orleans imposed its game immediately and never needed a frantic finish.

Turning point

The Pelicans dominated the first quarter 35–11 and led 61–36 at halftime. Golden State shot 33.3 percent overall and 23.9 percent from three. Jeremiah Fears added a 19-point, 10-rebound, 10-assist triple-double, giving New Orleans another creator alongside Saddiq Bey’s 23.

What it means

Lendeborg’s individual growth remains encouraging, but Golden State needs functional team offense around him until Curry returns. For New Orleans, the 21-point victory showed the upside of getting contributions across the roster instead of leaning on a single scorer.

Box Score Spotlight
Yaxel Lendeborg 28 PTS, 8 REB; Saddiq Bey 23 PTS; Jeremiah Fears 19 PTS, 10 REB, 10 AST
Game 8 / Final

Phoenix Suns at Denver Nuggets

Phoenix Suns 117 · Denver Nuggets 105

4DK Film & Feel

Phoenix walked into Denver and won a game that should command attention. The Suns did not have a 40-point hero; they got organized offense, balanced scoring and timely perimeter shooting. Devin Booker’s 20 points and eight assists mattered as much for his control of the game as for his scoring.

Turning point

Phoenix outscored Denver 36–29 in the second and 34–29 in the third to build an eight-point lead entering the fourth, then closed 27–23. The Suns hit 44.4 percent from three and outrebounded the Nuggets 51–35, a striking margin against a team anchored by Nikola Jokić.

What it means

Jokić and Murray each scored 28, but Denver cannot rely on its stars to overcome a 16-rebound disadvantage and poor perimeter defense. Phoenix earned a meaningful road win and a blueprint for competing against higher-ranked Western opponents.

Box Score Spotlight
Devin Booker 20 PTS, 8 AST; Nikola Jokić 28 PTS, 15 REB, 7 AST; Jamal Murray 28 PTS
4DK Player of the Night

Cam Boozer · Memphis

29 PTS · 7 REB · 7 AST

Washington had Memphis down ten at halftime. Boozer’s 29 points, seven rebounds and seven assists helped drive the Grizzlies’ 42–18 third-quarter response and a 136–120 road win. In a slate with multiple 30-point scorers, his all-around control in the biggest momentum reversal gets our vote.
