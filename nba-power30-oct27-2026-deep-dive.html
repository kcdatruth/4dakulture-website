Team of the Week: Memphis

Memphis is 4–0, and Ty Jerome has changed the conversation around this roster. We reward the wins without ignoring the small sample or the need to beat stronger opposition. The Grizzlies jump 21 places to No. 6.

Biggest Risers & Fallers

Memphis (+21), Detroit (+6), Houston (+5) and Golden State (+5) are the biggest upward stories. Miami (−17), New York (−9), Indiana (−6) and San Antonio (−5) face the sharpest early questions.

Oklahoma City Thunder

The undefeated start is real, but the roster's next test is less about talent than adaptation. Williams and Wallace being out removes secondary creation and a trusted perimeter defender. Ajay Mitchell's 30 against Phoenix was a welcome response, not a guaranteed nightly replacement. We want to see how Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and Chet Holmgren handle opponents that load up on the stars and make the replacement guards finish possessions.

#3 · Previous #5 · ↑2 · 3–1 through Oct. 26

Minnesota Timberwolves

A 3–1 start is a strong foundation, even after a painful overtime loss. Edwards' 47 in Cleveland was superstar-level shotmaking, and Gobert's presence still gives Minnesota an interior identity. But 125 points were not enough to secure the win, and integrating LaMelo Ball demands sharper late-game decision-making. The ceiling is contender-high. The concern is whether a deep rotation can become a dependable closing five.

#4 · Previous #10 · ↑6 · 3–0 through Oct. 26

Detroit Pistons

The 3–0 start has been built on more than hot shooting. Coming back from 22 down against Boston showed competitive resilience; holding Philadelphia to 93 showed defensive structure. Cade Cunningham controls tempo while Detroit's perimeter shooting has added a new dimension. The next step is proving that the three-point efficiency and fourth-quarter execution travel against another quality opponent.

#5 · Previous #4 · ↓1 · 3–1 through Oct. 26

Philadelphia 76ers

Philadelphia has star power, but its first losses should sharpen rather than erase the title conversation. Detroit held the Sixers to 93 points, illustrating what happens when rhythm disappears and opponents win the physical battle. Maxey's Player of the Week opening is a bright spot amid a crowded offensive hierarchy. We need to see cleaner late-clock possessions and defined defensive roles alongside LeBron James and Jaylen Brown.

#6 · Previous #27 · ↑21 · 4–0 through Oct. 26

Memphis Grizzlies

No team has changed the conversation faster than Memphis. Jerome's 4-for-15 shooting against Utah could have exposed a one-dimensional attack; instead he made late jumpers and distributed eight assists as Memphis reached 4–0. Cam Boozer adds a developing frontcourt dimension. The +21 leap is earned by results. We are reserving judgment on true contender status until the opponent quality rises.

#7 · Previous #11 · ↑4 · 3–0 through Oct. 26

Los Angeles Lakers

Three wins and a 52-point Luka Dončić masterpiece against Utah put Los Angeles firmly in the opening-week conversation. The Lakers recovered from a 47–29 first-quarter deficit against the Jazz, an important early sign of resilience. Luka's scoring and passing define the offense, but the group still has to demonstrate consistent stops and bench reliability. Our question is not whether Luka can carry a night. It's whether the Lakers can build a championship defense around him.

#8 · Previous #3 · ↓5 · 1–2 through Oct. 26

San Antonio Spurs

A slow start does not change Victor Wembanyama's impact, but the scoreboard still counts. San Antonio lost a tight 114–110 game to Houston and then answered Dallas with a 122–106 win. Those swings capture a team with immense defensive upside but uneven offensive rhythm. We are watching the spacing around Wembanyama and the decisions that determine whether a close game becomes a win.

#9 · Previous #8 · ↓1 · 2–1 through Oct. 26

Cleveland Cavaliers

Cleveland's overtime comeback over Minnesota was the sort of win that can reset a shaky opening week. Mitchell supplied 26 points and key overtime baskets; Mobley added 18 and 10, and rookie Meleek Thomas contributed 16 in his debut. The Cavaliers came back from 15 down rather than letting an Edwards scoring explosion bury them. We still want better first-half discipline. The comeback showed resilience, but spotting good teams double-digit leads is not a sustainable plan.

#10 · Previous #13 · ↑3 · 2–1 through Oct. 26

Boston Celtics

Boston has already shown both its vulnerabilities and its explosive response. After surrendering a 22-point lead to Detroit in the opener, Boston dismantled Chicago 134–99. Pritchard's seven threes and Tatum's all-around 23-point night illustrate the range of ways the offense can function. The priority is sustaining defensive focus for four quarters, not simply producing one devastating shooting run.

#11 · Previous #2 · ↓9 · 0–3 through Oct. 26

New York Knicks

The defending champions' 0–3 start deserves scrutiny, not excuses. The losses to Boston and Orlando highlight the early challenge: the Knicks have not consistently generated the late-game stops and half-court execution that defined their championship identity. Jalen Brunson's leadership matters now. We need to see whether New York responds with adjustments or continues relying on reputation.

#12 · Previous #17 · ↑5 · 1–3 through Oct. 26

Golden State Warriors

Golden State's first win came with a glimpse of its next offensive dimension. Yaxel Lendeborg's 31-12-6 line in Denver was not a routine rookie performance; his passing and finishing helped the Warriors generate offense without forcing everything through Curry. Steph was perfect from the field in a 24-point outing. The 1–3 record still matters. We need to see whether this chemistry holds up when defenses prepare specifically for Lendeborg.

#13 · Previous #9 · ↓4 · 1–2 through Oct. 26

Denver Nuggets

Denver's 1–2 start is a reminder that Nikola Jokić cannot solve every issue alone. The 134–114 home loss to Golden State exposed defensive problems against a rookie-led frontcourt attack. Jokić produced 19 points and 11 assists, but the Nuggets could not match the Warriors' scoring pace. The offense will create good looks. We are measuring whether Denver can defend consistently enough to turn that creation into wins.

#14 · Previous #12 · ↓2 · 2–1 through Oct. 26

Toronto Raptors

Toronto's early wins keep it in the middle of a crowded Eastern Conference race. The Raptors beat Washington before falling 117–94 to Minnesota. Kawhi Leonard's health is a major variable for this roster, and the Wolves game illustrated how quickly the offense can bog down when physical defenders dictate pace. We are watching lineup continuity and secondary creation, not assuming a healthy star will fix every possession.

#15 · Previous #18 · ↑3 · 2–0 through Oct. 26

Portland Trail Blazers

An unbeaten opening is encouraging, but the sample is still tiny. Portland beat Sacramento 128–120 and has shown it can score in a high-tempo setting. The next challenge is defending disciplined opponents and finding repeatable half-court answers when transition chances disappear. We like the early confidence; we want evidence it survives a difficult road stretch.

#16 · Previous #14 · ↓2 · 1–2 through Oct. 26

Orlando Magic

Orlando has already delivered a meaningful road result. The 111–102 win in New York showed that the Magic can make a more celebrated opponent uncomfortable through physicality and defensive pressure. Their offensive ceiling still depends on spacing and avoiding stagnant possessions. We are watching whether Orlando can turn one strong defensive night into an identity.

#17 · Previous #19 · ↑2 · 2–2 through Oct. 26

Dallas Mavericks

Dallas has been more competitive than its position suggests. Cooper Flagg's 29 points, 11 assists, five steals and three blocks against Miami showed remarkable two-way influence in a 113–109 road win. His emergence gives Dallas a young player who can affect every phase of a game. The question is how consistently the Mavericks can surround that production with spacing and disciplined team defense.

#18 · Previous #21 · ↑3 · 2–2 through Oct. 26

Washington Wizards

Washington's talent is more interesting than its early results. Anthony Davis scored 29 and grabbed 10 boards while Trae Young produced 22 and 10 assists against Charlotte, yet the Wizards still lost 110–103 because Brandon Miller erupted for 39. The ceiling depends on defense and role balance: elite individual offense cannot cover repeated perimeter breakdowns.

#19 · Previous #16 · ↓3 · 2–2 through Oct. 26

Atlanta Hawks

Atlanta's start has offered glimpses of offense without enough reliable stops. The Hawks surrendered 120 in Houston and could not withstand the Rockets' decisive 14–0 fourth-quarter run. The 128–81 win in Charlotte earlier showed the upside, but the contrast is stark. We are tracking whether the Hawks can defend quality creators in the final six minutes of close games.

#20 · Previous #20 · —0 · 1–2 through Oct. 26

Phoenix Suns

Phoenix is still searching for a dependable rhythm around Devin Booker. A 131–122 victory over Golden State showed the Suns can score, but Oklahoma City put up 125 against them the following game. The offense needs better complementary creation while the defense must finish possessions. The middle of the West is unforgiving; isolated shotmaking will not be enough.

#21 · Previous #15 · ↓6 · 2–2 through Oct. 26

Indiana Pacers

Indiana's early swings reveal a team that has not found a stable closing identity. Haliburton's 22 points and nine assists were not enough against Milwaukee, where the Pacers managed just 17 fourth-quarter points. A 136–109 win over Brooklyn showed how dangerous they are when pace and ball movement click. The gap between those performances is what we need to understand before moving them back up.

#22 · Previous #22 · —0 · 1–3 through Oct. 26

Chicago Bulls

Chicago is developing talent while taking some difficult early lessons. Caleb Wilson's 17 points and two blocks in Boston were a useful sign, but the Bulls lost 134–99 after Pritchard ignited a huge Celtics run. Young players need responsibility, and Chicago also needs defensive accountability. We are measuring Wilson's growth alongside the team's ability to avoid extended scoring droughts.

#23 · Previous #6 · ↓17 · 0–4 through Oct. 26

Miami Heat

Miami's 0–4 start has put immediate pressure on its new identity. Giannis recorded 29 points, 14 rebounds and 11 assists against Dallas, but the Heat lost 113–109. The problem is not whether Giannis is producing; it is whether the team can space the floor and defend the decisive possessions around him. A superstar can change the ceiling, but the group has to establish winning habits.

#24 · Previous #23 · ↓1 · 1–3 through Oct. 26

Utah is competitive in stretches while still defining its young core. Keyonte George put up 26 and six assists against Memphis, yet the Jazz fell 110–105. Darryn Peterson's development alongside George, Lauri Markkanen, Ace Bailey and Isaiah Collier remains a central storyline. We care more about quality possessions and defensive growth than one early result, but the late-game execution must improve.

#25 · Previous #25 · —0 · 3–1 through Oct. 26

Charlotte Hornets

Charlotte's 3–1 start deserves more respect than the preseason projections gave it. Brandon Miller's career-high 39 against Washington was a star-level shotmaking display, and Charlotte finished the job 110–103. The 47-point loss to Atlanta shows how uneven the floor still can be. We want to know whether Miller can keep producing when opponents send more attention his way.

#26 · Previous #26 · —0 · 0–3 through Oct. 26

New Orleans has shown fight without turning it into wins. The 141–135 loss in Denver demonstrated offensive potential, but allowing 141 points is not a sustainable formula. The young pieces can score; the challenge is communication, rebounding and resistance at the rim. We are looking for a complete defensive performance before rewarding the upside.

#27 · Previous #29 · ↑2 · 1–3 through Oct. 26

Milwaukee finally has a result to build on. Tyler Herro's 23 points, 10 rebounds and seven assists helped the Bucks beat Indiana 110–102 after an 0–3 start. The win showed how organized guard play and a strong finish can change the feel of a season. One win does not fix the defense or the rotation, but it provides a practical blueprint.

#28 · Previous #24 · ↓4 · 1–2 through Oct. 26

Both games showed that hanging around against quality opponents is not the same as finishing them. Rookie Keaton Wagler is part of a changing rotation. We are watching shot creation, injury availability and whether the second unit can keep games within reach.

#29 · Previous #28 · ↓1 · 0–3 through Oct. 26

Sacramento Kings

Sacramento's winless start has left little room for optimism. Memphis beat the Kings 123–112 and Portland scored 128 in another defeat. Darius Acuff Jr.'s early scoring flashes matter, but the Kings must build a defensive foundation around their developing guard. The franchise needs a sustainable identity rather than another isolated big rookie night.

#30 · Previous #30 · —0 · 0–3 through Oct. 26

Brooklyn Nets

Brooklyn is at the bottom because the opening results have not shown enough progress. The Nets lost 125–121 to Cleveland and then 136–109 to Indiana. Competitive stretches exist, but the defense has been unable to string together stops against established offenses. We are looking for individual growth and better possession-by-possession discipline before moving them upward.
