MVP Watch

#1 · —

Joe Burrow

Cincinnati's 22–20 loss to Washington complicates the team argument, not the individual one. Burrow threw for 300 yards and two touchdowns, yet the Bengals could not protect a late lead. His case at No. 1 rests on the responsibility he carries for sustaining Cincinnati's offense when the defense offers little margin. We are evaluating quarterback value, not simply rewarding the highest-ranked team. His next step is to make the decisive possessions impossible to waste, while recognizing that a quarterback cannot defend the final drive.

#2 · —

Brock Purdy

San Francisco's 35–10 dismantling of Minnesota is a strong counterargument to keeping Purdy second. He delivered 255 passing yards and three touchdowns in a game where the 49ers dictated the pace against a seven-win opponent. Purdy's timing, accuracy and ability to exploit spacing are central to that efficiency. The unresolved question is whether he can create enough when an elite defense disrupts the first structure of the play. For now, Burrow's larger offensive burden keeps this race razor-close.

#3 · ↑3

Patrick Mahomes

Mahomes' 324-yard, three-touchdown performance in Kansas City's 34–7 win over Arizona moves him into third. The Chiefs did not merely survive; they controlled the game through the passing attack. Mahomes remains dangerous because his creativity changes what defenses must prepare for, but the MVP case strengthens most when that improvisation is paired with efficient, on-schedule production. Buffalo is the next measuring stick for this rise.

#4 · —

Jared Goff

Detroit's 31–21 victory over Tampa Bay keeps Goff fourth. His case is built on repeatable execution: quick identification of leverage, timing with his receivers and a willingness to distribute rather than force spectacular throws. Jahmyr Gibbs' rushing threat creates favorable looks, but Goff still must deliver the correct answer when the defense closes the easy windows. Chicago on Thanksgiving will test that relationship between quarterback command and supporting cast.

#5 · ↓2

Josh Allen

Allen's Bills improved to 9–1 with a 26–9 win over Miami. His MVP case combines creation as a runner, arm talent and the ability to rescue broken plays. Sliding to fifth is a reflection of the extraordinary production around him in this week's ballot, not an indictment of Buffalo. The standard for climbing back is sharper passing efficiency and sustained scoring against top competition.

#6 · ↓1

Jaxon Smith-Njigba

Seattle's bye does not erase the receiver's impact. Smith-Njigba is the passing game's central gravity: defenses must account for him before the snap, and the Seahawks need to punish the coverage compromises that creates. As a non-quarterback MVP candidate, he faces a higher burden of proof because his opportunities depend on protection and quarterback delivery. The 4DK argument is that changing the geometry of the defense is itself a form of value.

#7 · ↑1

Jahmyr Gibbs

Gibbs rises to seventh because Detroit's offense changes when his speed forces defenders to widen their fits and hesitate before committing to coverage. He is not merely a runner accumulating yards; he helps create space for Goff's timing throws and forces opponents to defend multiple threats with the same personnel. The MVP ceiling depends on how often Detroit turns those mismatches into decisive production against playoff-level fronts.

#8 · ↓1

Trevor Lawrence

Jacksonville's 23–18 loss to the Giants weakens the immediate case for Lawrence despite a strong season record. His arm talent allows the Jaguars to threaten deep and outside the numbers, but quarterback value also shows up in red-zone decisions, third downs and the ability to recover when the initial plan fails. No. 8 acknowledges his central role while demanding more efficient scoring against defenses that can tighten coverage.

#9 · —

Jordan Love

Green Bay did not play in Week 11, so Love remains ninth on the strength of the established résumé. His arm talent lets the Packers challenge tight windows and attack vertically, but the next level requires fewer possessions derailed by pressure or inconsistent early-down gains. The Rams game is a useful test of whether Green Bay can make the offense less dependent on high-difficulty throws.

#10 · —

James Cook

Cook stays tenth because his rushing threat changes Buffalo's offensive math. Defenses that devote resources to Allen cannot ignore a back capable of turning a small crease into a field-position swing. His case differs from the quarterbacks above him: it is about efficiency, explosive gains and the leverage created for the rest of the offense. The challenge is demonstrating the same influence when opponents specifically design to remove the run.

4DK Film & Context: The Week That Changed the Board

NO. 1 · SAN FRANCISCO

The case for a new No. 1

San Francisco's 35–10 win over Minnesota is the kind of result that makes a power ranking argument without needing a complicated tiebreaker. Brock Purdy threw for 255 yards, while the offense demonstrated it could generate explosive plays against a seven-win opponent. This was not merely beating an inferior team; it was taking a fellow NFC contender out of its preferred game script. Buffalo remains an elite No. 2 after a 26–9 win over Miami. The difference is the quality of San Francisco's statement, not a collapse in Buffalo's standard.

The next challenge is Seattle, whose defense will try to force Purdy to hold the ball and work away from preferred timing throws. A top ranking is earned weekly, not inherited.

THE MVP TENSION

Burrow's production vs. Purdy's control

Joe Burrow's 300 passing yards and two touchdowns were not enough in a 22–20 loss to Washington. That does not erase the weight he carries in Cincinnati's offense, but it does sharpen the argument for Purdy, whose 49ers won by 25. Our No. 1 and No. 2 MVP spots remain a deliberate close call. Burrow's case rests on high-volume responsibility and creation; Purdy's on efficient direction of a balanced, top-ranked contender. Mahomes' 324 passing yards in Kansas City's 34–7 win over Arizona strengthen his No. 3 claim. These are different kinds of value and deserve to be evaluated separately.

ROOKIE WATCH · NEW ENTRY

Antonio Williams made the final drive possible

Washington rookie Antonio Williams caught nine passes for 122 yards against Cincinnati, but the final possession explains why he enters the 4DK Top 10. After the Bengals took a 20–19 lead with 46 seconds left, Jayden Daniels found Williams for 22 yards and then 42 more, putting the ball at the Cincinnati eight. Rookie kicker Drew Stevens finished the comeback with a 26-yard field goal as time expired. This was not empty volume: Williams delivered consecutive contested, high-leverage gains in the game's decisive moments.

Williams was a third-round Washington selection in 2026. The broader evaluation still needs route-level film review, separation metrics and season-long target efficiency before the final scouting verdict.

BIGGEST RISER · PHILADELPHIA ↑4

Winning ugly is still winning

Philadelphia's 19–16 victory over Pittsburgh does not suddenly make the Eagles an elite offense. It does show that they can execute well enough to beat a team that entered the week far higher on our board. Pittsburgh's four-place slide reflects a missed opportunity against a struggling opponent. Philadelphia's four-place rise rewards a tangible result without pretending one win rewrites the season.

4DK Deep Dive · Giants 23, Jaguars 18

DEFENSE, SITUATIONAL FOOTBALL, AND THE LIMITS OF YARDAGE

New York won the possessions that mattered

Jacksonville produced 354 net yards to New York's 238, but the Giants turned two Trevor Lawrence interceptions and three sacks into a disruptive afternoon. The contrast matters: a defense can concede movement between the twenties while denying the finishing plays that decide the scoreboard. Lawrence completed 25 of 47 passes for 301 gross passing yards, one touchdown and two interceptions. The Jaguars' passing volume was substantial; their efficiency in the red zone was not.

The Giants' Abdul Carter recorded two sacks, giving New York a way to affect Lawrence without relying on a constant stream of blitzes. That disruption changed Jacksonville's down-and-distance math. The Jaguars were only five of fifteen on third down and committed thirteen penalties for 107 yards, the kind of self-inflicted damage that converts promising drives into field-goal attempts or stalled possessions.

New York's offense was hardly flawless. J.J. McCarthy finished 18 of 38 for 173 yards and a touchdown, while the team ran for 77 yards. Yet the Giants carried a 20–6 halftime lead and protected the ball well enough to survive Jacksonville's fourth-quarter push. The winning identity was defense, timely points and an ability to close the final possession, not offensive dominance.

4DK verdict: The Giants' climb to No. 12 is earned by beating a division leader, but their offensive efficiency still limits how high we can place them. Jacksonville stays No. 8 because the season résumé matters, yet this loss exposed an urgent problem: talent and yardage do not replace red-zone execution and disciplined football. These are two teams with real playoff stakes and very different questions to answer next week.

Week 11 · The 4DK Team Deep Dives

Game-specific evidence and editorial implications, separated from our full-season scouting evaluations. Statistics below are Week 11 single-game figures, not season averages.

Detroit · No. 3 | Lions 31, Buccaneers 21

Detroit's offense did not need to rely on a single star to win. Jared Goff passed for 279 yards, while Jahmyr Gibbs ran for 98 and scored twice in the fourth quarter. Those late rushing touchdowns matter because they show how Detroit can finish with physicality after forcing a defense to respect the passing game for three quarters. Goff's touchdown connections with Amon-Ra St. Brown and Jameson Williams demonstrated two different stress points: timing and space underneath, plus the threat of vertical speed. Tampa Bay did not turn the ball over, so this was not a game the Lions simply stole through giveaways. Detroit generated four sacks of Baker Mayfield and turned sustained pressure into a ten-point victory. The 4DK question entering the Bears matchup is whether the Lions can keep their pass protection and run-game efficiency together against a defense capable of taking away the first read.

Kansas City · No. 4 | Chiefs 34, Cardinals 7

Patrick Mahomes' 324 passing yards against Arizona made this more than a routine win over a struggling opponent. Kansas City established command early and gave its defense a margin that changed Arizona's play-calling. The Chiefs' 34–7 result also reinforces why their record belongs near the top of the AFC, but the quality of opponent still matters in our ballot. The real test comes in Buffalo: against the Bills, Mahomes will need to sustain that production without counting on repeated short fields or an opponent forced into obvious passing situations. Kansas City remains fourth, one spot behind Detroit, because our ranking measures both ceiling and the total résumé rather than automatically placing a quarterback's team above everyone else.

Buffalo · No. 2 | Bills 26, Dolphins 9

Josh Allen led the Week 11 passing leaders for Buffalo with 304 yards, and James Cook added 89 rushing yards. That balance is what makes the Bills difficult to defend: loading the box against Allen's mobility leaves space for the passing game, while dropping extra defenders invites Cook to stay ahead of the chains. Miami managed only nine points, giving Buffalo a victory in which the defense carried its share of the burden. We moved the Bills down one spot despite a 9–1 record because San Francisco's decisive win over Minnesota supplied a stronger statement against a quality opponent this week. The Thanksgiving meeting with Kansas City is the ideal opportunity to reclaim No. 1.

Dallas · No. 16 | Cowboys 51, Titans 30

Dallas produced 51 points, and Dak Prescott's 325 passing yards and CeeDee Lamb's 132 receiving yards explain much of the offensive explosion. The Cowboys showed the high-end passing-game potential that has been missing too often from their overall résumé. Still, conceding 30 points to a one-win Tennessee team should prevent anyone from treating this as a complete transformation. Dallas rises because the offensive ceiling is real; it remains in the middle of our rankings because its defense has not yet demonstrated the consistency required of a contender. Philadelphia's upset of Pittsburgh sets up a divisional game that will reveal whether Dallas can pair explosive scoring with better control.

Carolina · No. 18 | Panthers 35, Ravens 33

Carolina's two-point win over Baltimore was one of Week 11's most consequential results for the playoff picture. Chuba Hubbard led the game's rushers with 120 yards, providing a stabilizing element in a contest that otherwise invited both offenses to trade scores. The Panthers are now 5–5 and lead a struggling NFC South, which makes their trajectory more significant than the raw record suggests. Their ability to win a close game matters, but allowing 33 points also underlines how thin the margin remains. The 4DK test is whether Carolina can build a repeatable identity around its run game and situational execution rather than needing another shootout to survive.

Houston · No. 20 | Texans 26, Colts 3

Houston's Thursday-night win was built on defensive control. Indianapolis scored only three points, while David Montgomery gave the Texans 107 rushing yards and C.J. Stroud added 194 through the air. The formula matters because it reduces the pressure on Stroud to manufacture explosive plays on every possession. At 4–6, Houston still has ground to make up in the AFC South, and a single dominant performance against a struggling opponent cannot erase the season's unevenness. But this was a credible model for how the Texans can compete: stay ahead of schedule on the ground, avoid offensive desperation and force opponents to sustain long drives against their defense.

Denver · No. 5 | Broncos 38, Raiders 24

Denver's 38 points against Las Vegas were supported by 111 rushing yards from Coleman, the game's leading rusher. The Broncos' 8–2 record is now impossible to dismiss as a short-lived surge. What matters for the next step is whether their offensive balance survives when opponents can tackle in space and prevent easy gains on early downs. The Raiders still put up 24 points, so Denver cannot confuse a comfortable final margin with a flawless defensive afternoon. Pittsburgh offers a more meaningful test of how the Broncos handle physical play and high-leverage possessions. Their rise to fifth rewards results while leaving room to evaluate how sustainable the formula really is.

Philadelphia · No. 27 | Eagles 19, Steelers 16

Philadelphia's win over Pittsburgh was not an offensive showcase; it was a needed proof of competitive resilience. Saquon Barkley led the game with 75 rushing yards, and the Eagles held a Steelers team led by Aaron Rodgers to 16 points. A three-point win does not suddenly fix a season that began with repeated losses, but beating a team above .500 changes the conversation. The Eagles rise four places because they finally delivered a result that can anchor a credible turnaround argument. The next challenge is Dallas, whose passing game just exploded for 51 points. Philadelphia needs another disciplined defensive performance and a more consistent offensive rhythm before we can consider a larger leap.

Washington · No. 19 | Commanders 22, Bengals 20

Washington's win over Cincinnati carried more weight than the final two-point margin. Joe Burrow passed for 300 yards, yet the Commanders still found the late possessions necessary to finish the upset. Rookie Antonio Williams' 122 receiving yards were the most in the game, and his late gains helped create the winning field-goal opportunity. This is why Washington rises two spots: it beat a high-ranked opponent with its season at a crossroads. The concern is that allowing Burrow to move the ball so effectively can be costly against another efficient quarterback. The opportunity is that Washington may have found an additional explosive receiving option capable of changing its late-game playbook.

San Francisco · No. 1 | 49ers 35, Vikings 10

San Francisco's win over Minnesota was a statement against a seven-win opponent, not merely a routine victory. Brock Purdy threw for 255 yards, and the 49ers limited the Vikings to ten points. The final margin reflects control across phases, which is the central reason we moved San Francisco ahead of Buffalo despite both teams sitting at 9–1. The test of a No. 1 team is not whether it can beat inferior opposition but whether its plan holds against credible playoff competition. San Francisco answered that question emphatically this week. Seattle is next, bringing a disciplined defense and a receiver capable of punishing even brief coverage mistakes.

4DK Team Deep Dives · Second Wave

Ten additional team evaluations, grounded in Week 11 results. Passing, rushing and receiving totals cited here are single-game figures, not season averages.

Chicago · No. 6 | Bears 21, Saints 13

Chicago's eight wins are no longer a novelty. The Bears beat New Orleans 21–13 without needing a shootout, an important counterpoint to the idea that their rise is entirely about Caleb Williams producing highlight throws. The offense scored enough to control the game while the defense kept New Orleans to 13. That is a meaningful blueprint for winning in late November. Still, the Saints entered with a losing record, so the result reinforces Chicago's consistency rather than proving it has already overtaken the NFC's elite. Thanksgiving at Detroit is the clearest measuring stick on the schedule: a direct comparison of two 8–2 rivals and a test of whether Chicago can keep the Lions from dictating tempo.

Cincinnati · No. 7 | Bengals 20, Commanders 22

Cincinnati led 20–19 with 46 seconds left after Evan McPherson hit a 56-yard field goal, yet still lost on Washington's final possession. Joe Burrow completed 28 of 40 passes for 300 yards and two touchdowns; the Bengals' passing game moved the ball even while Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins were held to modest receiving totals. Tight end Mike Gesicki led Cincinnati with five catches for 77 yards. Boye Mafe's three first-half sacks showed that the defense could disrupt Washington, but that pressure did not translate into a final stop. Falling four spots is a response to the loss and the fragility of the closing sequence, not a dismissal of Burrow's MVP-level season. Cincinnati must become a team that finishes the game as well as its quarterback creates chances to win it.

Jacksonville · No. 8 | Jaguars 18, Giants 23

The Jaguars' 7–3 record still commands respect, but the 23–18 loss to New York exposed the difference between gaining yards and producing points. Trevor Lawrence threw for 301 yards, and Brian Thomas Jr.'s offense had opportunities to move the chains, yet Jacksonville could not consistently finish drives against a Giants defense that affected the quarterback. The offense needs cleaner situational football when opponents create negative plays. We keep Jacksonville in the top eight because its larger body of work remains stronger than that of most teams beneath it, but this was a reminder that playoff defenses will not allow every promising possession to become a touchdown.

Minnesota · No. 9 | Vikings 10, 49ers 35

Minnesota entered the Mexico City matchup at 7–2 and left with a 25-point defeat that changes the conversation around its ceiling. The Vikings managed only ten points against San Francisco, and their defense could not stop Brock Purdy from controlling the passing game. One loss does not erase the season, especially against our new No. 1 team, but the margin matters. A contender must have a reliable adjustment when the opponent wins early downs and removes its preferred offensive rhythm. Minnesota drops two places because the performance revealed more unanswered questions than a close road loss would have. The upcoming Atlanta matchup is a chance to show that this was a bad night, not a broader offensive limitation.

Pittsburgh · No. 15 | Steelers 16, Eagles 19

Pittsburgh's 19–16 defeat to Philadelphia is damaging because the opponent entered the week with only two wins. Aaron Rodgers led the game with 227 passing yards, but the Steelers could not convert that production into enough scoring. The defense allowed just 19 points, a total that should normally give a well-rounded team a strong chance to win.
