LUKA’S 52.
SUNDAY SHAKES THE LEAGUE.

Seven games. Luka takes over in Utah. Detroit makes a statement in Philadelphia. Orlando shocks the defending champs. We break down every result and what comes next.

LAKERS 132 · JAZZ 123

LUKA TAKES OVER. THE LAKERS STILL HAVE WORK TO DO.

Utah hit Los Angeles with 47 first-quarter points and built an 18-point lead. Luka answered with a 52-point offensive masterpiece, including eight threes, and Reaves gave him the secondary scoring the Lakers needed. The 45-point second quarter flipped the game.

Walker Kessler’s return to Utah was quiet (two points, seven boards), so the bigger lesson is not simply that Los Angeles can outscore an opponent: it cannot make a habit of digging holes against explosive starts. Lauri Markkanen scored 28, Keyonte George nearly posted a triple-double (24/9/10), and Utah showed enough shot-making to make this uncomfortable. The Lakers are 3–0; the defensive standard still has to rise.

PACERS 136 · NETS 109

HALIBURTON TAKES CONTROL. INDIANA RUNS AWAY.

Indiana took a two-point deficit after one quarter and turned it into a decisive rout, putting up 41 in the second and 37 in the third. Haliburton scored 20 of his 24 after halftime, a reminder of how quickly the Pacers can turn a game into a track meet when their floor general controls tempo.

Siakam's all-around contribution kept the offense balanced. Brooklyn got 17 from Nolan Traoré, but the Nets' defensive resistance disappeared as Indiana's passing and transition pressure built. The Pacers look far more dangerous when Haliburton is dictating the terms.

TIMBERWOLVES 117 · RAPTORS 94

MINNESOTA WINS WITH DEPTH, NOT JUST STAR POWER.

Minnesota's new-look depth is translating into early wins. Edwards supplied the top-end scoring, while LaMelo's eight assists gave the offense a different playmaking gear.

The 23-point margin matters because this wasn't a night where Minnesota needed one superstar to score 40. Toronto was without Kawhi Leonard because of left-knee swelling, and Ja'Kobe Walter supplied 22 points, but the Raptors couldn't match Minnesota's sustained two-way pressure. For a Wolves team with title ambitions, comfortable wins built on balance are every bit as encouraging as highlight nights.

MAGIC 111 · KNICKS 102

ORLANDO CLOSES. NEW YORK'S 0–3 START GETS LOUDER.

Orlando saved its best basketball for the fourth quarter, outscoring New York 40–27 to steal a road win at Madison Square Garden. Wagner's near triple-double gave the Magic creation, rebounding and late-game control; Bane added four threes and 22 points.

Jalen Brunson had 28 for the Knicks, but the defending champions fell to 0–3. There is no need for October panic, yet a fourth-quarter defensive breakdown at home is precisely the kind of problem a championship-caliber team must address before it becomes a pattern.

THUNDER 108 · CLIPPERS 101

OKC STAYS UNBEATEN. THE INJURIES CHANGE THE STORY.

The Thunder stayed unbeaten at 3–0, but the result came at a steep potential cost. Jalen Williams was stretchered off with a left-leg injury late in the fourth, while Cason Wallace (shoulder) and Jaylin Williams (wrist) also exited.

Oklahoma City won the third quarter 34–30 and fourth 31–25 after trailing at halftime. Darius Garland posted 16 points and 11 assists for Los Angeles. This is the clearest example of why the regular season is about survival as well as wins: the immediate priority is the health of the Thunder's rotation, not the standings.

PISTONS 106 · 76ERS 93

CADE CONTROLS THE GAME. DETROIT MAKES A STATEMENT.

Detroit delivered a road statement in a meeting of unbeaten teams. Cunningham controlled the game without needing a 40-point scoring explosion, and Robinson's five threes punished defensive gaps.

The Pistons won despite missing Jalen Duren and Ausar Thompson, with Daniss Jenkins and John Collins contributing valuable minutes. Philadelphia was without Joel Embiid and VJ Edgecombe; Tyrese Maxey scored 29, but 18 came in the fourth when Detroit had already dictated the contest. Detroit's depth and Cade's command are the early-season stories worth watching.

GRIZZLIES 123 · KINGS 112

MEMPHIS CLOSES STRONG. BOOZER KEEPS GROWING.

Memphis finished the game with authority, using a late 11–0 burst to break Sacramento's resistance. Jerome's 31 points carried the offense, and rookie Cameron Boozer posted another double-double, reinforcing why the Grizzlies can build a serious frontcourt identity around him.

Cedric Coward added 15 and 11, while Zach Edey rested. Sacramento got 37 from Zach LaVine and 22 from rookie Darius Acuff Jr., but its defense couldn't close possessions when it mattered. The Kings are winless; Memphis is showing that its supporting cast can win without depending on one name.

A 52-point night in a comeback victory is our defining individual performance. Luka controlled the scoring load and playmaking responsibilities when the Lakers needed both. The 3–0 start is real, but the early defensive lapses are real, too.

4DK ROOKIE SPOTLIGHT

Another double-double for the Memphis rookie, who is beginning to show how his rebounding and interior presence can anchor a new chapter for the Grizzlies.
