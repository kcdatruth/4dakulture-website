4 DA KULTURE / NFL WEEK 9 / THURSDAY NIGHT FOOTBALL / NOVEMBER 5, 2026

JACKSONVILLE MAKES A STATEMENT.
BALTIMORE IS RUNNING OUT OF ANSWERS.

The Jaguars handled the rain, the road and a desperate opponent. The Ravens’ quarterback crisis has turned a four-game skid into a full-scale test of their season.

FINAL • JACKSONVILLEJAGUARS 336–2
FINAL • BALTIMORERAVENS 104–5
M&T Bank Stadium • Baltimore

There are wins that require a team to survive, and wins that reveal what a team has become. Jacksonville’s 33–10 dismantling of Baltimore belongs in the second category. On a rainy Thursday night, the Jaguars did not need a flawless offensive performance. They needed command: of the clock, the field position, the explosive moments and an opponent with almost no margin for error.

Baltimore entered without Lamar Jackson and Tyler Huntley. Undrafted rookie Joe Fagnano made his first NFL start, and Jacksonville treated that reality as a tactical opportunity, not an excuse to take the night lightly. The result lifted the Jaguars to 6–2 and pushed the Ravens to 4–5 after four consecutive defeats.

01 / The first punch set the tone

Jacksonville opened with a seven-play, 77-yard touchdown drive. Rookie Bhayshul Tuten finished it from one yard out, establishing an early message: the Jaguars intended to dictate terms even in difficult conditions. Baltimore answered with Jake Moody’s 53-yard field goal, but Jacksonville restored a seven-point cushion with a 36-yard Cam Little kick. The Ravens could not string together the kind of possessions required to protect an inexperienced quarterback. Their three second-quarter drives ended in punts. The scoreboard still suggested a game at 10–3, but the contest was already tilting toward Jacksonville.

02 / Fifty yards that changed the night

With less than a minute remaining before halftime, Trevor Lawrence found Parker Washington for a 50-yard touchdown. Washington beat man coverage, secured the ball after a juggle and finished the play in the end zone. That strike made it 17–3 and gave Jacksonville a two-touchdown lead Baltimore’s depleted passing attack was poorly equipped to erase. Lawrence finished 16-of-26 for 242 yards, one touchdown and no interceptions. He did not need to dominate every snap; he identified the opportunities that mattered and made them count.

03 / The second-half defensive verdict

Jacksonville did not allow the game to become chaotic after halftime. Cam Little added field goals from 45 yards in the third quarter and 47 and 33 in the fourth. Defensively, the Jaguars made Baltimore earn every inch. Montaric Brown and Jourdan Lewis intercepted Joe Fagnano, while Jalen McLeod recovered a fumble. The Ravens managed only 227 total yards. McLeod’s recovery set up Chris Rodriguez Jr.’s two-yard touchdown run, making it 33–3. Justice Hill’s 65-yard touchdown run on the next Baltimore offensive play was spectacular but isolated. One explosive rush could not disguise the absence of a consistent passing threat.

04 / Baltimore’s quarterback emergency is now a team problem

Joe Fagnano’s first NFL start was a brutal assignment: bad weather, an aggressive defense and a team already pressing to end a losing streak. He completed 10 of 18 passes for 60 yards and threw two interceptions. Baltimore tried direct snaps to Derrick Henry and unconventional rushing looks, but the adjustments could not create sustained offense. Henry carried 17 times for 57 yards and moved into seventh place on the NFL’s all-time rushing list with 13,707 career yards, passing Jerome Bettis and LaDainian Tomlinson. That achievement deserves recognition even in defeat. Safety Kyle Hamilton also exited with a hip injury. Baltimore has been outscored 84–10 in the second halves of its four straight losses. That is not just an injury story; it is a team struggling to make corrections while its options narrow.

05 / The AFC playoff race

Jacksonville sits at 6–2 with the AFC South lead and five victories in six games. This should not be mistaken for definitive proof against a healthy contender; Baltimore was badly undermanned. But good teams are supposed to beat compromised opponents without giving them life, and the Jaguars did exactly that. Baltimore’s situation is less forgiving. At 4–5, the Ravens are below .500. Every week without their top quarterbacks raises the difficulty of keeping pace in the AFC North and wild-card race. They cannot wait indefinitely for health to solve execution problems.

06 / The 4DK final word

Jacksonville played like a team that understands the difference between making noise and building a résumé. Baltimore played like a team whose injuries have exposed deeper weaknesses. The Jaguars leave Thursday night with momentum and credibility. The Ravens leave with another loss — and fewer answers than they had a week ago.

4DK VERDICT: JACKSONVILLE — CONTROLLED ROAD STATEMENT. BALTIMORE — URGENT RESET REQUIRED.
