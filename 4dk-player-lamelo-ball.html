The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

LaMelo is where the list starts changing gears. At No. 25, we are no longer talking only about talent; we are talking about whether a player can help shape the identity of a contender. His size, vision and passing creativity remain elite, and the fact that he finally played 72 games last season matters after years of availability concerns. He can hit pull-up threes, manipulate weak-side defenders and throw passes most guards do not even see.

The move to Minnesota changes the evaluation. He no longer needs to carry an offense every possession because Anthony Edwards is the clear downhill scoring force. That should let LaMelo lean into what he does best: organize pace, hit Ant early, find shooters before the defense is set and punish teams that load up on the primary scorer. The challenge is efficiency. He shot only 40.7 percent from the field last season, and a contender cannot afford too many empty, high-difficulty possessions.

Defensively, his size gives him a chance to survive if the engagement is there. If Minnesota can get the creative version of LaMelo without the loose possessions, this could become one of the most interesting backcourts in basketball.
