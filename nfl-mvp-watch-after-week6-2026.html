4 DA KULTURE / NFL DESK / OCTOBER 20, 2026

TOP 10
MVP WATCH

After Week 6: Allen takes the throne. Walker makes a historic running back argument. Purdy keeps winning. And the rest of the field has work to do.

Our rankings balance individual impact, performance, winning, supporting cast and the difficulty of a player's role. This is our editorial board—not a prediction of award voting.

01

Josh Allen

Buffalo Bills

The complete offensive engine

Allen takes our top spot because he changes the geometry of a defense even on snaps when he does not run. Against Las Vegas, Buffalo won 33–30 and Allen completed 23 of 33 passes for 255 yards and two touchdowns. The Raiders kept him largely contained as a runner—five carries for one yard—but the threat still created openings. On a third-and-eight near the goal line, defenders reacted to his escape, and Allen found Keon Coleman for a touchdown. That is value beyond rushing totals: coverage has to account for the quarterback after the play breaks down. Buffalo scored 20 points after halftime, and Allen protected the ball. The counterargument is that he has thrown more interceptions than Purdy or Goff this season. For us, the combination of high-leverage creation, winning and the amount of defensive attention he commands gives him the narrow edge.

02

Kenneth Walker III

Kansas City Chiefs

A running back forcing his way into a quarterback conversation

Walker has turned Kansas City's ground game from a complement into a defining advantage. His 128 rushing yards and two touchdowns in the 22–6 win over the Chargers pushed him to a league-leading 665 rushing yards through five games, at 6.3 yards per carry. His acceleration makes ordinary blocking look exceptional; his ability to turn modest creases into explosive gains changes how opponents set the front. With Patrick Mahomes still directing the passing game, defenses cannot simply overload the box. The MVP hurdle is real: voters usually favor quarterbacks, and Walker shares credit with a tremendous coach and passer. But if we are evaluating who has most transformed his team's week-to-week offensive identity, Walker belongs this high. His next challenge is maintaining the production as opponents devote more resources to stopping him.

03

Brock Purdy

San Francisco 49ers

The undefeated quarterback still has a powerful case

San Francisco is 6–0, and Purdy remains the steady hand behind one of football's most adaptable offenses. His value is not just in distributing the ball to Christian McCaffrey and the receiving corps. He anticipates windows, keeps the offense on schedule and can improvise when the original design fails. Earlier this season he navigated Seattle's pressure and helped produce late scoring drives; through six games he has 14 passing touchdowns against just two interceptions. The argument for No. 1 is obvious: efficiency, situational execution and the league's cleanest record. We place him third only because Allen has delivered more unmistakable individual rescue plays, while Walker's production has radically reshaped Kansas City's offense. Injuries around Purdy make his ability to keep the 49ers moving even more important going forward.

04

Patrick Mahomes

Kansas City Chiefs

Commanding the game without carrying every snap

The Chiefs are 5–0, and Mahomes remains the player opponents fear most in the passing game. He completed 23 of 30 passes for 272 yards in the 22–6 win over the Chargers, a performance defined by control rather than desperation. Walker's rushing success is a benefit, not an indictment: Mahomes can exploit lighter coverage, manage protections and choose when to attack downfield. The offense also has to navigate injuries at tackle, which makes his pocket command and quick decisions especially valuable. His MVP argument would become stronger if Kansas City keeps winning while the passing attack becomes more consistently explosive. For now, we give Walker the higher spot because his production has been the headline change in the Chiefs' identity.

05

Trevor Lawrence

Jacksonville Jaguars

Production meets the test of closing games

Lawrence has put himself in the conversation with a productive opening stretch and a Jacksonville team that has shown it can threaten quality opponents. The difficult part of his case is the volatility. In Week 6 the Jaguars lost 34–31 to Houston in London, a reminder that strong passing numbers mean less if the offense cannot finish drives or protect a lead. Lawrence has the arm talent to stress the entire field, and his mobility can keep defenses honest. But an MVP candidate has to turn those gifts into consistent, winning fourth-quarter football. We keep him in the top five because of the offensive responsibility he carries, while making the distinction between a high ceiling and a fully convincing MVP résumé.

06

Jared Goff

Detroit Lions

The efficiency argument is getting harder to ignore

Goff's early-season passing profile is one of the cleanest in the league: 1,485 yards, 12 passing touchdowns and no interceptions through Detroit's first five games. He has operated the Lions' offense with rhythm, anticipation and a willingness to take the right throw rather than force a highlight. His chemistry with Detroit's skill players allows the offense to attack every level of the field. The debate is how much credit belongs to Goff versus the line, the scheme and Jahmyr Gibbs. We think that can become a lazy criticism: executing an offense at a high level is a skill, especially when defenses know what is coming. Detroit's Week 6 bye means he had no fresh performance to move him higher this week. A strong response against top competition would push him back toward the front.

07

Drake Maye

New England Patriots

A young quarterback changing expectations

Maye belongs in this conversation because New England is winning meaningful games and its offense increasingly looks capable of sustaining drives rather than surviving on isolated plays. The Patriots beat the Jets 26–19 in Week 6 and extended their winning streak to three. Maye's best trait is the combination of pocket mobility and aggressive but controlled passing: he can make a difficult throw without turning every possession into a gamble. We still want to see the same level of play against better defenses, especially when the run game is not creating favorable downs. His placement is a recognition of New England's rise, not a claim that he has already matched the production of the names above him.

08

Bryce Young

Carolina Panthers

Carolina’s turnaround has a quarterback at its center

Young rises to No. 8 after leading Carolina past Philadelphia 25–14. That is the kind of result that changes the way we evaluate a quarterback on a developing team. He has to manage protections, create timing throws and keep the offense composed even when a defense expects the Panthers to struggle. The important distinction is that one win does not erase inconsistency; we still need Carolina to show it can move the ball reliably against different defensive looks. But MVP Watch is not simply a list of the league's prettiest stat lines. It also measures whose play is lifting a team beyond expectations. Young's progress has made the Panthers a much more serious weekly opponent, and that deserves a place on our board.

09

Jahmyr Gibbs

Detroit Lions

Explosiveness that changes every defensive call

Gibbs gives Detroit an entirely different kind of MVP case from Goff. He threatens the edge, punishes light boxes and forces linebackers to defend in space as a receiver. Entering Detroit's Week 6 bye, he had built a résumé of explosive all-purpose production, including a 207-scrimmage-yard, two-touchdown performance against Arizona. The bye means there is no new Week 6 tape to elevate him, and sharing an offense with a highly productive quarterback naturally splits the credit. Still, when Gibbs gets into the second level, a routine play can become a touchdown. His next big opportunity is proving that impact against the league's best defensive fronts.

10

Jaxon Smith-Njigba

Seattle Seahawks

The receiving threat defenses cannot overlook

JSN takes the final spot because his value is visible even when Seattle is struggling. In the Week 5 loss to San Francisco he produced 102 receiving yards while no other Seahawks player reached 25, a striking picture of how heavily the passing attack leans on him. He also had a costly late drop, which belongs in an honest evaluation. Seattle's 3–3 record and the difficulty of winning MVP as a wide receiver keep him at No. 10 rather than higher. But separation, route detail and the ability to produce while drawing the defense's best coverage are meaningful forms of value. We are rewarding the responsibility he carries without pretending the team results are stronger than they are.
