Anthony Edwards. Jalen Williams. Paolo Banchero. Kawhi Leonard. Jaren Jackson Jr. The season has barely started — and the contender picture is already changing.

October is supposed to be the month when we learn what these teams can become. Instead, some of the league’s biggest names are already headed for the training room. These injuries don’t all carry the same severity or timeline, but they share one consequence: they force contenders and developing teams to answer questions they never planned to face this early.

We’re looking beyond the medical bulletins. Who takes the shots? Who absorbs the toughest defensive assignments? And which teams have enough structure to survive until their stars return?

INJURY FILE / 01

ANTHONY EDWARDS

MINNESOTA TIMBERWOLVES
Right knee • Arthroscopic surgery • Reported absence: up to two months

Edwards' timing could hardly be worse for Minnesota. Four games into the season, he was averaging 33.3 points, 4.8 rebounds and 3.5 assists, then scored 47 in a loss to Cleveland. That's not simply high-volume scoring. It's the sort of pressure on a defense that determines the spacing and shot quality for everybody else.

LaMelo Ball now becomes the central organizer for long stretches. He can make the game easier for cutters and shooters, but his style is not a one-for-one replacement for Ant's downhill force and late-clock shot creation. The Wolves need Jaden McDaniels, Ayo Dosunmu, Terrence Shannon Jr. and Jonathan Kuminga to turn opportunities into consistent production while Rudy Gobert anchors the other end.

THE 4DK READ: Minnesota shouldn't chase one hero to replace Edwards. The best path is collective offense, disciplined defense and enough wins to keep the West within reach. A two-month estimate is not a guaranteed return date.

INJURY FILE / 02

JALEN WILLIAMS

OKLAHOMA CITY THUNDER
Broken left fibula and dislocated left ankle • Surgery required • No return timetable

This is the injury that changes the ceiling conversation for the reigning contender. Williams gives Oklahoma City a second creator who can score, pass, defend wings and keep the offense from becoming predictable. Without him, opponents can send more attention toward Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and ask everyone else to beat them.

The seriousness of the injury comes first. Surgery and rehabilitation will determine the timeline; there is no announced return date. The Thunder's depth remains real, but depth and replacement value are different things when a two-way star goes down.

THE 4DK READ: Oklahoma City can still win games behind Shai and Chet Holmgren. Whether it can reproduce its full postseason versatility without Williams is a much bigger question.

INJURY FILE / 03

CASON WALLACE

OKLAHOMA CITY THUNDER
Torn left shoulder labrum • Surgery required • No firm return timetable

Wallace's injury compounds the Williams loss. His ball pressure, quick hands and ability to defend without monopolizing possessions give Oklahoma City flexibility across lineups. The Thunder are not just missing two names; they're missing two important pieces of the defensive identity that allows their offense to play freely.

The next wave of guards will get more minutes and harder assignments. That's an opportunity, but it also changes the workload for everyone around them.

THE 4DK READ: The Thunder's regular-season floor may remain high. Their margin against elite playoff backcourts just got thinner.

INJURY FILE / 04

PAOLO BANCHERO

ORLANDO MAGIC
Low-grade right high ankle sprain • Multiple weeks possible • No official return date

Orlando's offense has a clear center of gravity: Paolo. Before the injury he was averaging 24.5 points, 7.5 assists and 6.5 rebounds while shooting 70 percent. Those assists matter as much as the scoring. Banchero doesn't just finish possessions; he creates advantages that make the next pass possible.

Franz Wagner and Desmond Bane now have to manufacture more offense without the same easy matchups. Orlando's defense can keep it in games, but close fourth quarters will reveal how much its half-court creation has progressed.

THE 4DK READ: This stretch is a test of Orlando's system, not just its depth chart. The Magic need multiple playmakers, not a temporary Paolo impersonator.

INJURY FILE / 05

KAWHI LEONARD

TORONTO RAPTORS
Left knee swelling • At least one week out • Imaging found no acute injury

Toronto's reunion with Leonard carries enormous upside and a familiar uncertainty. When healthy, he can control the pace from the midrange, punish mismatches and elevate a team's defensive attention to detail. But availability has been the defining complication of his later career.

The positive detail is that testing found no acute injury. The Raptors still need to manage the swelling and rehabilitation before they know when he can play. Scottie Barnes and the rest of the rotation must build continuity without treating every game as a waiting room.

THE 4DK READ: The question isn't whether Kawhi can still influence a big game. It's whether Toronto can build a stable season around a star whose availability remains uncertain.

INJURY FILE / 06

JAREN JACKSON JR.

UTAH JAZZ
Torn right MCL • Out at least six weeks before reevaluation

Utah loses the defender who can erase mistakes behind its perimeter players. Jackson's shot blocking and mobility make him more than a traditional rim protector; his presence changes where opponents are willing to attack. The Jazz also lose a versatile frontcourt scorer at a time when the rotation was still settling.

With Lauri Markkanen also dealing with a right wrist sprain, Utah's younger players face an accelerated learning curve. Kyle Filipowski and the remaining bigs can gain meaningful reps, but asking them to replace Jackson's defensive instincts overnight is unrealistic.

THE 4DK READ: This is an evaluation stretch for Utah, not a reason to rush an injured player. Jackson's six-week mark is a reevaluation point, not a promised return.

INJURY FILE / 07

JALEN DUREN

DETROIT PISTONS
Right hamstring strain • Out at least two weeks • Reevaluation expected in early November

Detroit entered the season with something it hasn't had in a long time: real expectations. After winning 60 games and finishing atop the East last season, the Pistons are no longer just a young team hoping to arrive. They're being judged by how far they can go. That's what makes Jalen Duren's early injury such an important story.

Duren injured his right hamstring during the October 23 win over Miami. The Pistons said he would be reevaluated in two weeks — a checkpoint, not a guaranteed date for his return. He earned All-Star and All-NBA Third Team recognition last season after averaging 19.5 points and 10.5 rebounds on 65 percent shooting. Those numbers explain his value, but they don't fully capture what he does for Detroit's identity.

When Cade Cunningham gets downhill, Duren gives him a powerful target around the rim. His screening, lob threat, offensive rebounding and second-chance finishing force defenses to account for the paint on every possession. On the other end, he supplies size and physicality that help Detroit control the glass. Losing him changes the geometry of the floor for Cade and asks the Pistons to manufacture those easy interior opportunities another way.

Paul Reed stepped into the spotlight against Miami with 19 points and 11 rebounds, giving Detroit a reminder that its depth is real. John Collins can offer different frontcourt looks, and the coaching staff has options for smaller groups. But the longer Duren is out, the more Detroit must prove that its physical identity survives without the big man who helps define it.

THE 4DK READ: The Pistons have enough talent to stay dangerous, especially with Cunningham directing the offense. But Duren is not an interchangeable center. His absence tests Detroit's rebounding, interior finishing and ability to impose its will on good teams. For a group trying to turn a breakout season into sustained contention, the first priority is getting its young cornerstone healthy — not rushing a hamstring recovery.

THE BIGGER PICTURE

THE TITLE ISN’T WON IN OCTOBER.
BUT THE ROAD CAN CHANGE HERE.

Minnesota needs a new offensive balance. Orlando must prove its creators can function without Paolo. Toronto has to manage the uncertainty around Kawhi. Utah must develop its frontcourt without Jackson. Detroit must keep its physical edge without Duren. And Oklahoma City faces the most complicated problem of all: replacing two players whose two-way versatility made the whole machine work.

That doesn’t mean every team is finished. It means health, depth and adaptation have moved from supporting storylines to the center of the season. We can debate who has the best roster on paper. The teams that can actually put their best players on the floor — and survive when they can’t — will shape the race.

4DK FINAL WORD: Nobody wants the opening weeks defined by injuries. But this is where coaching, development and roster construction get tested. The contenders we believed in before opening night now have to show us what they’re made of.
