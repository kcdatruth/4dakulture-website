ORIGINAL 1996 ALBUM ARTWORK · NAS
The 4DK deep dive

The poet, the hustler and the world between them.

Following Illmatic was never going to be a simple assignment. Nas had already established himself as a writer capable of making Queensbridge feel both intimately observed and mythically significant. It Was Written does not try to repeat that debut. Instead, it expands the scale: sharper suits, more elaborate criminal narratives, polished singles and a narrator increasingly fascinated by the possibilities and costs of power.

The transformation into Nas Escobar is sometimes mistaken for a surrender to commercial pressure. But the best songs expose how little of Nas’ essential craft changed. His images remain precise, his voice alert to contradiction and his storytelling rooted in the neighborhood even when the music suggests a world of wealth. The contrast between aspiration and danger becomes the album’s central engine.

The 4DK listening discussion found two perfect 10s: “The Message,” which announces this evolution without hesitation, and “If I Ruled the World,” where Lauryn Hill helps turn a fantasy of freedom into a generational anthem. “Affirmative Action” came within a tenth of perfection, while “I Gave You Power” and “Shootouts” demonstrate the depth of Nas’ conceptual and cinematic writing.

Chapter 01 · The sound

Polished does not mean powerless.

Trackmasters bring recognizable melodic foundations and a crossover-ready sheen to records like “Street Dreams” and “If I Ruled the World.” But the album never depends on one production language. DJ Premier supplies the bleak imagination of “I Gave You Power,” Havoc introduces Queensbridge darkness, Live Squad contributes shadowy atmosphere, and Dr. Dre brings a hard West Coast funk sensibility to “Nas Is Coming.”

The variety is not simply a bid for different audiences. It mirrors the competing identities inside the record. There is the street observer, the sophisticated hustler, the storyteller, the philosopher and the dreamer. Some songs feel like private reflections, others like widescreen crime dramas, and the biggest single imagines a world beyond the limits that shaped the narrator.

Chapter 02 · Historical perspective

A sophomore album in an extraordinary year.

In 1996, New York rap was being transformed by cinematic hustler narratives, competitive lyricism and a rapidly expanding commercial audience. Nas’ second album belongs in that conversation not merely because of its singles but because it is an unusually coherent portrait of ambition. The Firm’s early identity takes shape on “Affirmative Action”; the darker Queensbridge tradition remains alive in the Mobb Deep collaboration; and “If I Ruled the World” demonstrates that an expansive hit can still carry a meaningful social imagination.

The album is best understood on its own terms rather than as an attempt to surpass Illmatic by imitation. Its greatness is in the tension between polish and danger, fantasy and experience, and the poet and the persona. That tension gives the strongest songs their staying power.

The complete track-by-track

Fourteen records. Every score earned.

Track 01 / 14 · THE OPENING DECLARATION

The Message

Produced by Trackmasters
10.0 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

Nas opens with the confidence of an artist who has no intention of repeating his debut. The reflective guitar melody softens the surface, but his rhymes carry confrontation, coded rivalry and a new appetite for cinematic detail. The voice is recognizably the Queensbridge poet; the frame around it has become wider.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

The interpolation of Sting’s “Shape of My Heart” is haunting rather than comforting. Its delicate tone allows Nas to make threat and memory coexist, demonstrating that sophistication in production need not mean softness in writing.

The 4DK verdict

A perfect opening statement. The record establishes Nas Escobar without surrendering the extraordinary detail that made Nas a landmark storyteller.

Track 02 / 14 · THE HUSTLER’S IMAGINATION

Street Dreams

Produced by Trackmasters
9.7 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

The dream of money is not presented merely as a shopping list. Nas makes wealth an escape route from limitation, even while the street economy used to pursue it creates new traps. That tension is the point: the fantasy is irresistible precisely because the reality is so difficult.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

The Eurythmics “Sweet Dreams” interpolation provides an immediately familiar, funk-driven foundation. Nas moves with an effortless conversational flow. The polished sound invites a broad audience, but the details of his world remain intact.

The 4DK verdict

A genuine gem whose musical identity stands beyond comparisons to the other lavish street records of 1996. Upgraded to 9.7 for its enduring replay value.

Track 03 / 14 · A WEAPON GIVEN A CONSCIENCE

I Gave You Power

Produced by DJ Premier
9.7 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

Nas makes a handgun the narrator, sustaining the conceit across a story of possession, fear and recurring violence. The brilliance is not simply that the gun speaks; it recognizes the damage done through it while having no meaningful say in its own use. That contradiction turns a familiar symbol of dominance into an object trapped by human decisions.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

Premier’s spare, shadowy arrangement leaves every narrative turn exposed. There is no celebratory release in the music; the beat keeps returning to the same uneasy atmosphere, just as the cycle in the lyrics keeps repeating.

The 4DK verdict

A hidden-gem classic and one of the most inventive extended metaphors in Nas’ catalog. The concept is genius because the emotion serves the story.

Track 04 / 14 · THE DANGER INSIDE THE CIRCLE

Watch Dem Niggas

Produced by Trackmasters
9.1 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

The warning is pointed inward. Nas is less worried about an obvious rival than the familiar face whose loyalties shift when money and status enter the room. The record understands that proximity is not the same as trust, and that success can make old relationships harder to read.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

The George Clinton-derived funk gives the song a luxurious bounce that contrasts with its distrustful message. The groove is a major part of its appeal: relaxed on the surface, alert underneath.

The 4DK verdict

A dope beat carrying practical street wisdom. The theme deepens the album’s portrait of success as something that attracts both admiration and danger.

Track 05 / 14 · THE POET UNDER THE PERSONA

Take It in Blood

Produced by Live Squad
9.2 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

Nas packs the verses with memory, dense images and philosophical turns, sounding like an observer who can no longer separate the world he escaped from the world he inhabits. There is no need for a single linear plot; the song works through the accumulation of sharp impressions.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

The hypnotic, subdued instrumental feels closer to underground New York than the album’s crossover singles. Its atmosphere is nocturnal and almost dreamlike, leaving the writing in the foreground.

The 4DK verdict

An essential deep cut that shows the Nas Escobar concept never replaced the poet who made Illmatic extraordinary.

Track 06 / 14 · QUEENSBRIDGE MEETS THE WEST

Nas Is Coming

Produced by Dr. Dre
8.7 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

In an era when regional conflict was shaping hip-hop’s public image, Nas and Dr. Dre made a record together. Nas meets the production with aggression and technical confidence, refusing to let a legendary producer become the only attraction.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

Dre’s hard, spacious funk is the defining musical feature. The low-end pressure and disciplined drums create a different environment from Nas’ usual East Coast collaborators, and the beat hits with unmistakable authority.

The 4DK verdict

A strong cross-coast collaboration with a hard Dre beat. Its historical significance is considerable even if the song does not quite reach either artist’s greatest work.

Track 07 / 14 · THE FIRM BEFORE THE FIRM

Affirmative Action

Produced by Dave Atkinson
9.9 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

Nas, AZ, Cormega and Foxy Brown create more than a standard posse cut: the voices sound like characters inside the same organized-crime narrative. AZ brings elegance, Cormega adds grit, Foxy commands attention, and Nas anchors the world. Their contrasting deliveries make the collective feel complete.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

The dramatic production makes the record sound like the opening sequence of a crime epic. It is polished without losing tension, giving every MC room to occupy a distinct role.

The 4DK verdict

A near-perfect mafioso classic and the foundation of The Firm’s mythology. One of the album’s defining records and a 4DK favorite.

Track 08 / 14 · WHEN FAMILIARITY BECOMES A TRAP

The Set Up

Produced by Havoc
8.8 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

Betrayal is not an abstract possibility in Nas’ universe; it is a constant calculation. The story turns on misplaced confidence and the speed with which ordinary interactions can become dangerous. The writing keeps the album’s recurring theme of suspicion alive.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

Havoc’s murky Queensbridge palette supplies the record with genuine unease. Its grit provides a welcome contrast to the album’s smoother passages, emphasizing that the luxury never eliminates the risk.

The 4DK verdict

A solid, ominous chapter in the album’s street narrative, even if its strongest moments do not match the most ambitious songs here.

Track 09 / 14 · COMPASSION AND ITS LIMITS

Black Girl Lost

Produced by L.E.S.
8.5 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

Nas turns toward relationships, identity and the pressure of social expectations. His attempt to understand a young woman’s experiences is thoughtful in places, but some of the framing carries judgments that deserve scrutiny. The song is most interesting when heard with both its sympathy and its contradictions intact.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

The mellow R&B arrangement gives Jo-Jo Hailey of Jodeci room to provide an expressive hook. That soulful voice is central to the song’s emotional atmosphere and distinguishes it from the harder tracks around it.

The 4DK verdict

A reflective change of pace with a memorable vocal contribution, though its perspective is less fully realized than the album’s strongest narratives.

Track 10 / 14 · QUEENSBRIDGE AFTER DARK

Suspect

Produced by L.E.S.
9.4 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

The mood is immediate: tension, suspicion and the knowledge that violence can arrive without warning. Nas sounds fully at home in this raw setting, using clipped warnings and street-specific detail to make the threat feel close rather than theatrical.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

The hard, understated production is built to reinforce the atmosphere. It does not chase crossover polish; its strength is in how directly the beat supports Nas’ uncompromising performance.

The 4DK verdict

A forceful deep cut that captures the street-centered side of the record. The hook’s warning stays with the listener long after the beat ends.

Track 11 / 14 · THE STREET AS CINEMA

Shootouts

Produced by Trackmasters
9.7 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

Nas stages conflict through character, movement and consequence. Instead of relying on general claims about danger, he gives listeners enough detail to imagine the room, the people and the decisions that lead to confrontation. His control of pacing makes the story feel like a compact crime film.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

The instrumental is fire without demanding attention away from the plot. Its restraint makes the tension more effective: the beat holds steady while Nas moves the narrative toward its most dangerous moments.

The 4DK verdict

A hidden-gem classic. Nas is in his bag here, showing why his storytelling belongs among hip-hop’s finest.

Track 12 / 14 · QUEENSBRIDGE ROYALTY

Live Nigga Rap

Produced by Havoc
9.0 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

Nas joins Mobb Deep for a meeting of distinct but complementary approaches to the same environment. Prodigy’s cold intensity, Havoc’s direct aggression and Nas’ layered imagery make the chemistry feel organic rather than assembled for a feature credit.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

Havoc’s dark, minimal production provides the common language. The instrumental hits hard and leaves the MCs nowhere to hide, echoing the sound that made Queensbridge a defining center of East Coast street rap.

The 4DK verdict

A strong collaboration with genuine neighborhood chemistry and a rugged atmosphere.

Track 13 / 14 · A DREAM BIGGER THAN THE BLOCK

If I Ruled the World (Imagine That)

Produced by Trackmasters
10.0 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

Nas imagines freedom from incarceration, scarcity and limits imposed by circumstance, while still acknowledging the lure of luxury. The fantasy contains contradictions, but that makes it human: he wants opportunity, security, recognition and room to breathe.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

The Kurtis Blow concept and Whodini-derived melodic feel become a warm, expansive anthem. Lauryn Hill is the perfect voice for the hook, supplying both hope and a melancholy sense of what the world still lacks.

The 4DK verdict

A flawless generational record, among Nas’ most consequential and recognizable hits. Its influence, impact and Lauryn’s performance make the perfect 10 unavoidable.

Track 14 / 14 · THE DARKNESS AFTER THE DREAM

Silent Murder

Produced by Live Squad
8.7 / 10

The 4DK deep dive

After the optimism of “If I Ruled the World,” this bonus cut returns to the distrust and violence running through the album. The contrast is revealing: the fantasy of a better world does not erase the world Nas knows.

Production, performance and the bigger picture

The restrained, ominous production makes the track feel like an epilogue from the shadows. It lacks the sweeping melodic release of the preceding anthem and instead closes on tension.

The 4DK verdict

A worthwhile deep cut and a darker alternative ending, rated 8.7 as the album’s final reviewed track.

The final 4DK verdict

It Was Written is a definitive hip-hop classic.

Nas did not recreate Illmatic; he enlarged the universe around it. It Was Written combines intricate street poetry, unforgettable hooks, unsettling conceptual writing and some of the most cinematic collaborations of its decade. Its strongest records do not merely sound impressive in isolation. Together, they trace an artist wrestling with the allure of success and the dangers that follow it.

Our perfect scores for “The Message” and “If I Ruled the World” capture the project’s extraordinary range. One is an uncompromising statement of lyrical authority; the other opens the door to hope on a scale that reaches far beyond Queensbridge. “Affirmative Action” introduces the original Firm chemistry, “I Gave You Power” turns a handgun into a narrator, and “Shootouts” proves Nas can build a crime film from verses alone.

Fourteen rated tracks, including “Silent Murder,” produce an exact arithmetic mean of 9.3. The verdict is clear: this is not a great album in spite of its evolution from Illmatic. Its evolution is one of the reasons it remains essential.
