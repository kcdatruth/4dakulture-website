AFTER WEEK 6 · 2026 SEASON
San Francisco keeps the throne. Kansas City is coming. Cleveland rises, Carolina earns its respect, and Philadelphia faces a season-changing injury crisis.

Editorial rankings as of October 20, 2026. Previous editions remain separate.

01

San Francisco 49ers

6-0
An unbeaten standard, and a warning

The 49ers survived Washington 23–17 to reach 6–0, but the fourth-quarter scoring drought was a reminder that the margin can narrow quickly. Brock Purdy has operated a talented attack with composure, and Christian McCaffrey remains the foundation of its versatility. Mike Evans has given the receiving corps a contested-catch presence and a dependable option in tight windows. His renewed Achilles trouble is now a major concern, especially with other pass catchers unavailable. The next test is whether San Francisco can preserve its offensive identity without asking McCaffrey to carry an unsustainable workload. This is our best team today, but health could decide whether it remains the best in January.

02

Kansas City Chiefs

5-0
The Walker effect

Kansas City beat the Chargers 22–6, and Kenneth Walker III was again the centerpiece: 128 rushing yards and two touchdowns. Through five Chiefs games he leads the NFL with 665 rushing yards, averaging 6.3 per carry. That kind of efficiency gives Patrick Mahomes a different menu: lighter boxes, manageable third downs and fewer possessions where he must manufacture everything himself. The defense also dominated the Chargers, generating four sacks. This is no longer a Mahomes-or-bust offense; the rushing attack has made the Chiefs harder to defend and more capable of controlling the game. They are within striking distance of our No. 1 spot.

03

Buffalo Bills

5-1
Allen has Buffalo believing

Buffalo followed its overtime win over the Rams with a 33–30 escape against Las Vegas. Josh Allen continues to be the difference between a good offense and one that can steal wins when the plan breaks down. James Cook supplies the finishing power, and the Bills have repeatedly answered in pressure moments. The concern is the other side of the ball: giving up 30 to the Raiders after allowing 34 against the Rams is a warning about explosive plays and late-game control. Buffalo looks like a genuine championship contender, but Allen cannot be expected to erase defensive breakdowns every week.

04

Minnesota Vikings

5-0
Undefeated, with room to grow

Minnesota entered its bye 5–0 and returns with a strong case to climb higher. Its defense has set the tone, keeping opponents uncomfortable and protecting an offense that has not always been explosive. The Vikings deserve full credit for finishing every game on their schedule, yet our ranking leaves them behind Buffalo because we are judging the whole performance profile rather than record alone. The next stretch will reveal whether their offense can generate enough consistent scoring to support a serious title run.

05

Jacksonville Jaguars

4-2
A stumble, not a collapse

Jacksonville lost 34–31 to Houston in London after entering Week 6 at 4–1. That result exposed how quickly a game can slip away when the defense gives up explosive production and the offense is forced to chase. The Jaguars have earned a Top 5 position with their overall start, but the loss means they need a response rather than excuses. Their path forward depends on better situational defense, reducing empty possessions and showing that complementary football can travel beyond the opening month.

06

New England Patriots

4-2
A legitimate rebound

New England moved to 4–2 with a 26–19 win over the Jets. Drake Maye is giving the Patriots a steadier direction, while the defense continues to make opponents work for points. The win was not an offensive masterpiece, and that is precisely the issue separating this team from the elite: it can control stretches of a game without always putting it away. Still, the Patriots are no longer just an interesting rebuild. Their record and recent results demand respect, and the next challenge is turning competitive football into repeatable, convincing wins.

07

Chicago Bears

4-2
The offense is gaining shape

Chicago handled Atlanta 31–23 and improved to 4–2. Caleb Williams and Ben Johnson have an opportunity to build on a performance that showed how dangerous the Bears can be when they get ahead and dictate terms. The early scoring created the separation; Atlanta’s late push showed why closing games cleanly still matters. Chicago’s ranking reflects a rising ceiling, not a finished product. The Bears must prove that their offensive rhythm holds up against stronger defenses and that their own defense can prevent opponents from climbing back into games.

08

Baltimore Ravens

4-2
A tough loss changes the conversation

Baltimore fell 13–10 to Cleveland after being shut out over the final three quarters. The Ravens still have the talent and defensive physicality to threaten anyone, but that kind of offensive stagnation cannot be waved away. They are 4–2, not in crisis, yet their Week 6 loss creates a real question about adjustments when an opponent disrupts their first plan. We keep Baltimore in the Top 10 on its overall résumé, while making clear that its next offensive response matters.

09

Green Bay Packers

4-2
Winning with a clearer identity

Green Bay beat Dallas 23–13 to reach 4–2. Jordan Love’s offense did enough to create separation, while the defense helped make the Cowboys’ passing numbers feel less meaningful. The Packers are building momentum, but the question is whether their offense can become consistently dangerous rather than simply efficient enough. Their recent run earns a Top 10 place; beating stronger opponents and avoiding long quiet stretches would strengthen the argument for a climb.

10

Denver Broncos

4-2
Results over aesthetics

Denver edged Seattle 17–14 in overtime and now sits 4–2. Bo Nix and the offense have been uneven, and the running game has not always supplied an easy escape hatch. Yet Denver has beaten quality opposition, including Seattle and Jacksonville, and its ability to survive difficult games deserves more weight than style points. The Broncos enter our Top 10 because they are getting it done against credible teams. The next step is finding enough offensive consistency that every victory does not require a defensive rescue.

11

Cleveland Browns

4-2
The résumé is getting impossible to ignore

Cleveland’s 13–10 victory over Baltimore followed its earlier win against Pittsburgh, giving the Browns meaningful head-to-head arguments over two AFC North rivals. The defense is making games uncomfortable, and that toughness is a real identity rather than a one-week surprise. The offense still needs more sustained production, particularly when opponents force longer drives. But the Browns are 4–2, have won the kinds of games that matter in division races, and deserve No. 11 ahead of Pittsburgh.

12

Pittsburgh Steelers

4-2
A comeback does not erase the questions

Pittsburgh rallied past Tampa Bay 26–19 in overtime, a win that showed resilience after a sluggish opening three quarters. Aaron Rodgers and the offense found answers late, but needing overtime against a one-win team makes it hard to place the Steelers higher. Cleveland already owns the head-to-head result, and Denver’s overall résumé gets the edge. Pittsburgh remains a credible playoff team; the challenge is producing cleaner starts and relying less on late-game escapes.

13

Detroit Lions

3-2
A bye-week reset

Detroit entered the Week 6 bye at 3–2. The Lions remain capable of explosive offense and physical football, but their first five games did not quite justify keeping them above teams that have collected bigger wins. This is a placement about evidence, not a dismissal of their ceiling. Coming out of the bye, Detroit needs to sharpen execution and prove that its offensive talent can translate into four-quarter control against the league’s best.

14

Cincinnati Bengals

3-2
Potential without a new result

Cincinnati was idle in Week 6 at 3–2. The Bengals have enough offensive talent to change a game quickly, but we need more consistency before placing them among the teams at the very top. The bye offered a chance to clean up protection, situational execution and defensive communication. Their next stretch will tell us whether the early unevenness was temporary or part of their identity.

15

Carolina Panthers

3-2
The Panthers have earned this jump

Carolina went into Philadelphia and won 25–14, improving to 3–2. This was not a meaningless upset: the Panthers controlled enough of the game to make a struggling Eagles team pay for its mistakes. We are moving them to No. 15 because their recent play deserves to be judged on its own merits rather than through preseason expectations. The next question is sustainability—can Carolina reproduce that intensity when opponents stop treating it as a surprise?

16

New York Giants

3-2-1
Opportunity left on the field

The Giants lost 34–27 to New Orleans and fell to 3–2–1. They have been competitive, but a home loss to a struggling opponent shows the limits of their current margin for error. New York has to convert competitive stretches into full games, particularly on defense and in late possessions. The record keeps the Giants in the middle tier, but their next response will determine whether they are a playoff threat or simply a difficult out.

17

Seattle Seahawks

3-3
Defending champions at a crossroads

Seattle is 3–3 after a 17–14 overtime loss in Denver, its second consecutive defeat following a loss to San Francisco. Those are formidable opponents, so the record needs context. Still, a defending champion cannot live indefinitely on last season’s credentials. The Seahawks need to restore offensive rhythm and avoid leaving their defense to protect narrow margins. We moved Seattle above Las Vegas because its losses have come against stronger opposition, but the next few games must show a real correction.

18

Houston Texans

2-4
A win that can change momentum

Houston beat Jacksonville 34–31 in London, a valuable result for a team that had opened 1–4. The Texans showed they can create points against a division leader and compete in a high-pressure setting. One win does not wipe away their slow start, however. The key now is turning offensive production into a consistent weekly standard and getting enough stops that every victory does not require a shootout. Houston has a path back into the race if it builds on this rather than treating it as a one-off.

19

Las Vegas Raiders

3-3
A hot start has turned into a slide

Las Vegas opened 3–0 but has now lost three straight, most recently 33–30 to Buffalo. The Raiders showed fight against an elite opponent and generated enough offense to stay in the game, but close losses still count. The slide raises concerns about defensive consistency, protection and the ability to finish against quality teams. Ashton Jeanty’s role and the offensive line’s ability to create dependable rushing opportunities deserve close attention. The Raiders are not buried at 3–3, but they have to halt the skid before the early optimism disappears.

20

Atlanta Falcons

2-4
Too much catch-up football

Atlanta fell 31–23 to Chicago after digging a substantial early hole. The Falcons found some offense late, but chasing a game is not the same as controlling it. Their 2–4 record reflects inconsistency in both execution and game management. Atlanta has enough playmaking potential to challenge teams, but it must start faster, protect the ball and get defensive stops before the fourth quarter. The next stretch is about showing progress, not just flashes.

21

New York Jets

2-4
Competitive, but not finishing

The Jets lost 26–19 to New England and sit 2–4. They have kept several games within reach, but their offense still struggles to generate enough sustained scoring to reward the defense. Close losses can be encouraging for a young team, but the standings do not give partial credit. The Jets need more explosive plays and cleaner red-zone execution to turn respectable performances into wins.

22

Arizona Cardinals

2-4
The upset earns a promotion

Arizona went into Los Angeles and beat the Rams 18–16. The Cardinals did not produce a scoring explosion, but they made enough plays in a tight division game to earn a tangible move up our board. At 2–4, the season is still an uphill climb. Yet a head-to-head victory over a talented rival matters, and it is why we put Arizona directly ahead of the Rams. The challenge is turning that defensive toughness and late-game composure into a run.

23

Los Angeles Rams

2-4
The talent is not matching the record

The Rams lost 18–16 to Arizona and dropped to 2–4, a troubling position for a roster with serious expectations. A close loss to a division opponent is especially damaging because it undermines both confidence and tiebreaker positioning. Injuries add another layer of concern, but the offense still needs to convert talent into points and the coaching staff must find more reliable answers. We drop Los Angeles to No. 23 until it shows the results to justify climbing.

24

Dallas Cowboys

2-4
The offense has gone quiet

Dallas lost 23–13 in Green Bay, and the offense again failed to sustain scoring drives. Dak Prescott can move the ball, but yards without touchdowns are not enough. The Cowboys have also struggled to establish the run, leaving too much pressure on the passing game and the defense. Their 2–4 start puts them in danger of falling out of the race early. Dallas needs a clearer identity and more productive early downs, not just another promise that the talent will eventually click.

25

Indianapolis Colts

2-4
A damaging home defeat

Indianapolis lost 26–20 at home to previously winless Tennessee. That is the kind of result that changes the tone of a season. The Colts led early but could not put the game away, and the late collapse exposed weaknesses in finishing drives and protecting leads. At 2–4, Indianapolis has to stabilize its defense and find a more dependable four-quarter offensive plan. This is a team that cannot afford many more missed opportunities.

26

Washington Commanders

1-4-1
A loss that still revealed fight

Washington pushed unbeaten San Francisco before falling 23–17 on Monday night. The Commanders are 1–4–1, so this ranking is not a reward for their record. It reflects a competitive performance against our No. 1 team and signs that they can still make opponents uncomfortable. The next step is converting that fight into actual wins, particularly by reducing early deficits and generating more consistent offensive possessions. We move them to No. 26, but the standings remain unforgiving.

27

New Orleans Saints

2-4
A badly needed offensive response

New Orleans beat the Giants 34–27, a welcome result after a difficult start. The Saints finally showed the kind of scoring punch that can relieve pressure on the rest of the roster. Their 2–4 record still reflects deeper concerns about week-to-week consistency and defensive reliability. The win should be treated as a building block, not proof that every problem has been solved. More efficient offense and better late-game control are essential.

28

Philadelphia Eagles

2-4
An injury crisis changes the outlook

Philadelphia fell 25–14 to Carolina and sits 2–4. More important than the score is the quarterback situation: Jalen Hurts was placed on injured reserve after a blood-clot diagnosis, and subsequent reporting indicates he is expected to miss the remainder of the season. His health comes first. On the field, Tanner McKee faces an enormous challenge, while injuries affecting the supporting cast—including concerns around Saquon Barkley and DeVonta Smith—further reduce the margin for error. The Eagles are not ranked No. 28 because their roster suddenly lacks talent; they are here because the results and availability outlook have changed dramatically.

29

Tampa Bay Buccaneers

1-5
A close loss is still a loss

Tampa Bay took Pittsburgh to overtime but lost 26–19 and fell to 1–5. The Buccaneers showed competitiveness and briefly controlled the game, but could not close it. The offense needs to sustain pressure for four quarters, and the defense has to prevent late momentum swings. There is fight here, but not enough execution. At this stage, Tampa Bay needs wins rather than moral victories.

30

Tennessee Titans

1-5
Finally on the board

Tennessee earned its first victory, 26–20 over Indianapolis, with a 20-point fourth quarter. That finish showed resilience and gave a struggling roster something concrete to build on. One comeback does not change the broader picture of a 1–5 start, but it can improve belief and provide a template for better late-game execution. The Titans need to show the same urgency from the opening kickoff, not just when they are desperate.

31

Miami Dolphins

0-5
The search for a first win continues

Miami entered its Week 6 bye at 0–5. The break offered a chance to reset, but the Dolphins remain in a difficult position with little evidence of a consistent identity. The immediate priorities are cleaner offensive execution, fewer negative plays and better defensive discipline. Miami needs to show signs of sustainable progress before any talk of a turnaround carries weight.

32

Los Angeles Chargers

0-6
A brutal start gets worse

The Chargers fell to 0–6 after a 22–6 loss in Kansas City. Justin Herbert faced constant pressure, the offense failed to finish drives and the defense could not contain Kenneth Walker. Even when Los Angeles reached scoring territory, penalties and execution mistakes erased opportunities. This team has talent, but the winless record reflects serious problems in protection, situational football and consistency. The priority now is protecting Herbert and finding an offensive foundation before the season slips further away.
