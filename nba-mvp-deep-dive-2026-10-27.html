MVP Watch
Top 10

October 27, 2026. Rankings locked by 4DK. This page is a review draft and has not replaced the live site.

#1 • Lakers

Luka Dončić

Luka remains first because 4DK weighs the entire offensive burden, not just a nightly scoring headline. His change of pace forces defenders to commit before the pass arrives, creating advantages for cutters and weak-side shooters. The Lakers’ 3–0 start strengthens his case. The question we will track is whether the team can sustain that control against elite defensive pressure without letting Luka shoulder every late-clock possession.

#2 • Timberwolves

Anthony Edwards

Edwards supplied 47 points in Minnesota’s 127–125 overtime loss to Cleveland. Eleven of those points came in the extra period, a clear reminder that he can generate offense when opponents know the ball is coming his way. We are not moving him down because of one close defeat. What matters next is how Edwards and LaMelo Ball divide late-game creation, and whether Minnesota converts Gobert’s interior work into enough defensive stops to win tight games.

Verification: verified Oct 26 scoring and game result

#3 • Thunder

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander

Oklahoma City opened 4–0, including a 125–114 win over Phoenix in which SGA scored 26. Jalen Williams’ fractured fibula and dislocated ankle and Cason Wallace’s torn labrum will test the Thunder’s depth and change the type of creation SGA must provide. Ajay Mitchell’s 30 against Phoenix was encouraging, but an MVP season requires sustaining elite efficiency while absorbing more attention. Our third-place position reflects both the winning and the challenge ahead.

Verification: verified Oct 26 team result and injury details

#4 • Pistons

Cade Cunningham

Cade has Detroit positioned among the early East contenders, and his value goes well beyond a box-score scoring total. He controls matchups with size, patience and passing angles that let Detroit dictate pace. The comeback against Boston is a useful early test of poise, but our next update will evaluate how often Detroit’s half-court offense creates efficient looks when the first action stalls. Fourth is an aggressive ranking, intentionally rewarding the Pistons’ early response rather than relying on preseason reputation.

Verification: editorial; full game-log verification pending

#5 • Grizzlies

Ty Jerome

The Western Conference Player of the Week averaged 34.7 points and 6.0 assists on 56.3% shooting over Memphis’s first three games. He then shot 4-for-15 against Utah, yet his late jumpers and eight assists helped secure a 110–105 win and a 4–0 start. That contrast is why we have him fifth: the breakout is real as an early-season event, but we need to see whether his shotmaking holds up against better defenses and scouting adjustments. We are rewarding the work, not predicting he will score 35 a night all year.

Verification: verified NBA Week 1 award and Oct 26 game

#6 • Nuggets

Nikola Jokić

Denver’s 134–114 home loss to Golden State was a reminder that even the best offensive organizer cannot compensate for every defensive lapse. Jokić had 19 points, seven rebounds and 11 assists, yet the Nuggets surrendered 74 second-half points. His ability to read the floor still gives Denver a championship-caliber offensive foundation. Sixth reflects the early team results; it is not a claim that five players have suddenly become better all-around basketball players.

Verification: verified Oct 26 box-score totals and game result

#7 • Spurs

Victor Wembanyama

Wembanyama’s two-way ceiling remains unique: he can change shot selection without blocking a shot and stretch defenses with his size and skill. Our seventh spot is a present-tense MVP evaluation, not a ceiling projection. We need to measure his efficiency, minutes and San Antonio’s actual defensive performance as the schedule develops. The path upward is straightforward: dominant production that consistently turns into wins.

Verification: editorial; full game-log verification pending

#8 • 76ers

Tyrese Maxey

Maxey earned Eastern Conference Player of the Week after averaging 28.8 points, 4.0 rebounds, 4.8 assists, 2.0 steals and 1.0 blocks across Philadelphia’s 3–1 first week. That production matters because it combines scoring with disruption on the other end. With LeBron James and Jaylen Brown in the 4DK roster context, we are watching whether Maxey can remain aggressive while sharing touches. Eighth rewards an excellent opening week while leaving room for a larger sample.

Verification: verified NBA and Philadelphia 76ers Week 1 award report

#9 • Celtics

Jayson Tatum

Boston’s 134–99 win over Chicago gave us a glimpse of the Celtics’ ceiling when the perimeter offense flows. Tatum recorded 23 points, seven rebounds and six assists, while Payton Pritchard hit seven threes. Tatum’s MVP case depends on being both the primary problem-solver and the player who unlocks teammates when defenses load up. Ninth reflects the current early-season picture; the next step is proving that Boston can repeat this offensive balance against stronger resistance.

Verification: verified Oct 26 result and totals

#10 • Heat

Giannis Antetokounmpo

Giannis put up 29 points, 14 rebounds and 11 assists against Dallas, but Miami lost 113–109 to fall to 0–4. We cannot pretend that triple-double is anything less than elite individual production, nor can we pretend a winless start is irrelevant to an MVP board. The Heat need cleaner spacing and more reliable closing possessions around him. Tenth is a warning about team results, not a dismissal of his impact.

Verification: verified Oct 26 result and totals
