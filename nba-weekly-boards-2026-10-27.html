Rookie Watch Top 10

#1 / Warriors

Yaxel Lendeborg

2026–27 regular season through Oct 26: 21.8 PPG · 9.3 RPG · 4.8 APG · 33.3 MPG

Four regular-season games give us more than a single highlight: 12 points in the opener, 18 and 13 rebounds against Memphis, 26 and nine against Phoenix, then 31 and 12 in Denver. His six assists in both the Memphis and Denver games demonstrate connective playmaking, not just scoring. The counterpoint is Golden State’s 1–3 start and his 1-for-8 three-point shooting against Denver. Our No. 1 ranking rewards his overall production while recognizing that shot selection and team defense remain works in progress.

#2 / Bulls

Caleb Wilson

2026–27 regular season through Oct 26: 21.0 PPG · 7.3 RPG · 1.8 APG · 34.0 MPG

Wilson’s long-term projection is why we have been watching him closely. We see elements of Kevin Garnett’s rangy frame and motor, Rasheed Wallace’s multi-positional tools, and Jermaine O’Neal’s two-way instincts. That is a stylistic comparison, not a claim that he has already reached their level. His 17 points, five rebounds and two blocks in the 134–99 loss to Boston showed individual flashes against a much more cohesive opponent. Chicago must give him opportunities to make reads through mistakes, especially against physical frontcourts.

#3 / Grizzlies

Cam Boozer

2026–27 regular season through Oct 26: 15.3 PPG · 10.5 RPG · 4.8 APG · 33.0 MPG

Boozer is contributing to a 4–0 Memphis team without needing the offense built around him. His 13 points in the 110–105 win over Utah illustrate how he can fit beside the guards and still supply reliable finishing. We want to track his defensive positioning, rebounding in traffic and how his touches change against stronger competition. Being third on our board is a recognition of his role in winning, not a prediction that his usage will stay limited.

#4 / Kings

Darius Acuff Jr.

2026–27 regular season through Oct 26: 22.3 PPG · 2.7 RPG · 4.7 APG · 36.0 MPG

Acuff has already delivered a 30-point game early in his career, an important indicator of the scoring responsibility Sacramento is willing to hand him. We like his ability to create pressure off the dribble and manufacture a shot late in the clock. The next test is whether he can combine those scoring bursts with efficient playmaking and defensive consistency while the Kings search for their first win.

#5 / Jazz

Darryn Peterson

2026–27 regular season through Oct 26: 11.5 PPG · 4.3 RPG · 2.5 APG · 29.3 MPG

Peterson is entering a Utah backcourt with Keyonte George and Isaiah Collier, while Lauri Markkanen and Ace Bailey demand their own touches. He contributed 14 points and seven rebounds against Memphis. His value is not simply whether he leads the Jazz in scoring right away; it is how quickly he learns to create good looks without freezing the rest of the offense. We want to see his off-ball movement, defensive effort and late-clock decisions.

#6 / Clippers

Keaton Wagler

2026–27 regular season through Oct 26: 13.0 PPG · 4.3 RPG · 4.0 APG · 26.0 MPG

Opportunity matters in a rookie race. With the Clippers working through their early-season rotation, Wagler has a chance to earn a larger role. We will evaluate the quality of his possessions rather than assume that more minutes automatically equal progress: can he keep the ball moving, defend his matchup and create a dependable scoring action when the offense stalls?

#7 / Nuggets

Trevon Brazile

2026–27 regular season through Oct 26: 9.0 PPG · 7.3 RPG · 0.0 APG · 21.0 MPG

Brazile is with Denver, where frontcourt minutes must be earned around Nikola Jokić and a veteran offensive structure. His athleticism can give the Nuggets vertical spacing and energy, but we need to see reliable screening, defensive rotations and rebounding against NBA size. His ranking is a developmental assessment, not a claim that he has already secured a permanent rotation role.

#8 / Bucks

Brayden Burries

2026–27 regular season through Oct 26: 7.3 PPG · 3.3 RPG · 1.5 APG · 14.3 MPG

Burries is with Milwaukee. The opportunity is to bring pace and perimeter energy to a club looking for steadier guard play. We will track whether his decision-making keeps up with his aggression, especially when he faces pressure at the point of attack. He belongs on the board because of the early promise, while his offensive efficiency and defensive consistency still need a larger sample.

#9 / Cavaliers

Meleek Thomas

2026–27 regular season through Oct 26: 16.0 PPG · 2.0 RPG · 0.0 APG · 19.0 MPG

Thomas made his NBA debut in Cleveland’s 127–125 overtime win over Minnesota and scored 16 points off the bench. That matters because his first opportunity came in a competitive game, not empty minutes. We are encouraged by his catch-and-shoot confidence and willingness to play within the Cavaliers’ veteran offense, but one game is too small a sample to make claims about season-long efficiency or a guaranteed role.
