4DK playoff-impact desk

Jacksonville: Bates adds range and takeaway potential behind a heavier defensive front, while Davis and Godchaux provide two different interior answers. This is a direct attempt to make Jacksonville's defense less matchup-dependent in January. The opportunity cost is meaningful: the draft capital invested reduces the margin for error if the current window closes early.

Buffalo: Hooker's five interceptions through nine weeks are an immediate reason to respect the addition, but the larger value is schematic flexibility. Buffalo can disguise its coverage rotations and ask less of any single safety. A 2027 second-rounder is a substantial price for an in-season move, even with a fifth-round pick coming back.

Chicago and San Francisco: Simmons offers Chicago disruptive interior play; Buckner gives the 49ers an established lineman who can help dictate protections. Both deals address the kind of line-of-scrimmage battles that decide playoff games, although availability, health and integration must still be evaluated separately.

Detroit and Baltimore: Cisco adds another coverage option to the Lions' secondary. Brissett gives Baltimore quarterback insurance, but a veteran backup does not by itself resolve the offense's broader injury or continuity questions.

THE 4DK PLAYOFF LENS

San Francisco — Keenan Allen: A veteran route runner offers Purdy an underneath answer against pressure; the low acquisition cost limits downside.

Minnesota — Kirk Cousins: The reunion prioritizes immediate quarterback stability in a crowded NFC North; fit and readiness matter more than nostalgia.

Kansas City — Josh Downs + 2027 seventh: Downs brings separation and a chain-moving target for Mahomes; a mid-round double-pick cost raises expectations for instant integration.

Denver — Daiyan Henley: Henley adds pursuit speed and divisional familiarity, but a new linebacker must master Denver's fits before the playoff push.

Baltimore — Ryan Neuzil: Baltimore's center exchange addresses continuity in the protection calls; a new starter's chemistry cannot be assumed.

Seattle — Quenton Nelson: An established interior blocker could transform Seattle's short-yardage floor and pocket integrity; integration is the immediate test.

Houston — Marvin Harrison Jr. + 2027 sixth: Harrison raises the receiving corps' theoretical ceiling; Houston still needs timing and usage to turn talent into efficient drives.

Dallas — Joey Porter Jr.: Porter's press coverage can change how Dallas allocates safety help, though a premium future second is a real price for a 4–5 club.

Baltimore — Cam Jurgens + 2027 seventh: The earlier Jurgens move underscores Baltimore's investment in protection and its continuing offensive-line adjustment.

New York Giants — J.J. McCarthy: An earlier quarterback acquisition reshaped the Giants' options and makes development as important as the immediate win column.

Not a completed trade: The proposed Cincinnati–Philadelphia Arnold Ebiketie deal was canceled. It must not be included as an acquisition or counted in team trade totals.
