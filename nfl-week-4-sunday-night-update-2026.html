4DK NFL • WEEK 4 • SUNDAY NIGHT UPDATE

The League Is Starting To Show Its Hand.

San Francisco, Kansas City and Minnesota are 4–0. Carolina’s offense exploded on Sunday night. Dallas stole one in Houston. The Chargers are 0–4. And Kenneth Walker III just made the RB1 debate real — in a Chiefs uniform.

THROUGH SNF • OCT. 4, 2026 • MNF PENDING
THE SUNDAY READ

Week 4 Changed The Board.

Four weeks is still early, but it is no longer nothing. The unbeaten teams have had enough time to show repeatable habits, the winless teams have had enough time for flaws to become patterns, and the individual leaderboards are finally gaining enough sample size to mean something. That is why this is the first full 4DK Sunday-night package of the season: every completed Week 4 game, our updated hierarchy, the MVP and rookie races, the standings and a league-leaders snapshot all in one place.

Kenneth Walker’s case is real — and it belongs in Kansas City.

Walker’s 177-yard, two-touchdown demolition of Las Vegas moved him to 537 rushing yards through Sunday. That leads the league. We are not declaring the all-around RB debate over after four games, but if the question is who has the strongest rushing case for No. 1 right now, Walker is at the front of it.

ALL 15 COMPLETED GAMES

Every Game. No Drive-By Recaps.

Thursday through Sunday night. Atlanta–New Orleans remains for Monday.

01

Browns 27, Steelers 24

CLE 3–1 • PIT 2–2

Cleveland survived the kind of finish that can flip a division race. Pittsburgh erased a 21–10 fourth-quarter deficit, Aaron Rodgers hit Pat Freiermuth for a touchdown and ran in the two-point conversion to make it 24–24, but Andre Szmyt answered with a 56-yard field goal with 15 seconds left. Rodgers then threw his second interception of the night on Pittsburgh’s final snap. The box score was nearly even — Cleveland 372 total yards, Pittsburgh 361, with two turnovers apiece — but the Browns owned the last decisive possession. Deshaun Watson finished 24-of-33 for 268 yards, a touchdown and a pick; Denzel Boston’s 89 yards gave Cleveland another explosive outlet. Rodgers threw for 299 yards and three scores, but the two interceptions mattered more than the volume. At 3–1, Cleveland has already banked two AFC North wins’ worth of credibility; Pittsburgh at 2–2 is still dangerous, but its week-to-week volatility is becoming part of the story.

02

Colts 30, Commanders 13

IND 2–2 • WAS 1–3 • London

Indianapolis won this game without needing Daniel Jones to carry it. Jones threw for only 143 yards and had two turnovers, but Jonathan Taylor gave the Colts the stabilizer they needed with 95 rushing yards and two touchdowns. Washington actually outgained Indy 317–257, yet ten penalties for 84 yards, a turnover and an early knee injury to Marcus Mariota kept sabotaging any rhythm. Seventh-round rookie Athan Kaliakmanis was forced into meaningful work and went 15-of-33 for 186 yards, a touchdown and an interception. The Colts’ formula was simple: stay patient, win the field-position game, lean on Taylor and let Washington beat itself. At 2–2, Indianapolis is back in the AFC South mix; at 1–3, the Commanders now have both a record problem and a quarterback-health problem to monitor.

03

Ravens 24, Titans 18

BAL 3–1 • TEN 0–4

Baltimore controlled most of the afternoon and then made the ending more uncomfortable than it needed to be. Lamar Jackson was razor efficient — 15-of-20, 222 yards, two touchdowns, no interceptions — and Zay Flowers produced 118 yards and a score. Derrick Henry added 73 rushing yards and a touchdown. Tennessee’s most encouraging development was rookie Carnell Tate, who erupted for nine catches and 145 yards and looked every bit like the high-end target the Titans drafted fourth overall. Cam Ward threw for 222 yards with a touchdown and an interception, but Tennessee again spent too much of the game chasing. Baltimore’s 13 penalties for 131 yards are the warning sign, and Jackson leaving with an ankle issue is the bigger Week 5 concern. The Ravens are 3–1, but their ceiling still depends on cleaning up the self-inflicted damage and keeping Lamar healthy.

04

Patriots 29, Bills 26

NE 2–2 • BUF 3–1

This was New England’s best win of the season and Drake Maye’s first real signature moment of 2026. Buffalo took a 26–21 lead with 4:33 left when Josh Allen found Keon Coleman for a 34-yard touchdown. Maye answered with a 23-yard touchdown to Efton Chism III with 1:55 remaining, then hit Rhamondre Stevenson on the two-point conversion. Maye finished with 269 passing yards, three touchdowns and one interception while also leading New England with 54 rushing yards. The Patriots held the ball for 33:41 and finished with 405 total yards to Buffalo’s 340. Allen was solid — 253 passing yards, one passing touchdown, one interception and a rushing score — and Coleman went for 116, but Buffalo never fully controlled the game. The Bills remain 3–1 and still belong near the AFC’s top tier; New England at 2–2 just proved it can beat a contender in a one-possession finish.

05

Bears 23, Jets 12

CHI 3–1 • NYJ 1–3

Chicago didn’t win with fireworks; it won by physically owning the game. The Bears ran 89 offensive plays to the Jets’ 32, held the ball for 42:45 and outgained New York 492–157. Kyle Monangai was the engine with 30 carries for 146 yards and two touchdowns, while Tyson Bagent gave Chicago 268 passing yards. Geno Smith completed only eight passes, and even a late 58-yard touchdown to Isaiah Williams couldn’t hide how little possession the Jets had. The Bears are now 3–1 because they have shown they can win more than one way. This was the bully-ball version: keep the opposing offense on the sideline, keep pounding the run game and make every Jets possession feel like it has to be perfect.

06

Jaguars 22, Bengals 17

JAX 3–1 • CIN 2–2

Cincinnati won the yardage battle 437–323 and Joe Burrow threw for 428 yards. Jacksonville still won because Trevor Lawrence was much cleaner and the turnover margin swung the afternoon. Lawrence completed 18 of 23 for 227 yards, a touchdown and no interceptions. Burrow threw two picks, and Jacksonville turned a 13–10 third-quarter lead into a 22–10 cushion when Chris Rodriguez Jr. broke a 25-yard touchdown run with 6:42 left. Cincinnati got back within five and had a final possession, but the Jaguars closed it. Tee Higgins’ 157 yards were wasted in the loss. Jacksonville being 3–1 is no fluke at this point: the Jags are getting efficient quarterback play, timely takeaways and enough run-game balance to survive games where the opponent posts the bigger raw yardage total.

07

Cowboys 34, Texans 30

DAL 2–2 • HOU 0–4

Houston led 16–6 at halftime and 23–13 in the third quarter. Dallas still found a way to steal it, and CeeDee Lamb was the center of everything: 17 catches, 189 yards and the game-winning 12-yard touchdown with 43 seconds remaining. Dak Prescott went 32-of-45 for 335 yards, a touchdown and no interceptions, while Javonte Williams scored three rushing touchdowns. Houston’s offense was good enough to win — C.J. Stroud threw for 347 yards and two touchdowns with no picks, Nico Collins went 7-for-118 with both scores — but the Texans again failed to finish. Thirteen penalties for 90 yards repeatedly put them behind the sticks, and the defense gave up 28 second-half points. At 0–4, Houston has moved beyond the ‘bad break’ stage. The talent is visible, but the inability to close games is now the defining fact of the season. Dallas, meanwhile, gets back to 2–2 with a comeback that can change the emotional direction of its month.

08

Giants 36, Cardinals 24

NYG 3–1 • ARI 1–3

The Giants continue to bank wins while their quarterback situation evolves. Jameis Winston gave them the full Jameis experience — 250 yards, three touchdowns and two interceptions — but New York got enough explosive plays from Malik Nabers and the defense to finish Arizona. Nabers caught six passes for 112 yards and a touchdown. Rookie linebacker Arvell Reese delivered one of the best rookie defensive games of Week 4 with nine solo tackles, an interception and three passes defended. Then Deonte Banks slammed the door with a 98-yard pick-six with 11 seconds left. Arizona’s Jacoby Brissett threw three interceptions, and that was ultimately too much to overcome. The Giants are 3–1 and sitting on top of the NFC East through Sunday; however it looks, that record has to be respected.

09

Rams 24, Eagles 20

LAR 2–2 • PHI 2–2

This was the comeback of the day. Philadelphia led 20–10 at halftime and didn’t score again. The Rams entered the fourth quarter still down ten, then Kyren Williams scored twice in the final 4:02, including the winning seven-yard run with 1:17 left. The return of Puka Nacua changed the texture of the offense immediately: nine catches, 125 yards and a two-yard rushing touchdown. Matthew Stafford threw two interceptions, but Los Angeles still rolled up 415 yards while holding Philadelphia to only 172. Jalen Hurts threw for just 93 yards, and the Eagles’ final possession ended under relentless pressure from Byron Young. For LA, this is exactly why Puka’s return mattered — not because he fixes every issue, but because he gives Stafford a chain-moving, coverage-bending target that keeps the offense alive when the game gets ugly. For Philly, 2–2 is less alarming than the offensive profile: 172 yards and a scoreless second half is the part that needs an answer.

10

Packers 17, Buccaneers 14

GB 2–2 • TB 0–4

Green Bay didn’t dominate, but it did enough to avoid falling below .500. Jordan Love went 22-of-30 for 197 yards with two touchdowns and one interception, and the Packers protected a 17–7 lead just well enough after Tampa made its fourth-quarter push. Jalon Daniels gave the Buccaneers 55 rushing yards in addition to 148 through the air, but two interceptions kept Tampa from turning a competitive afternoon into its first win. The Bucs are 0–4, and the frustrating part is how many of these games have remained within reach. Green Bay at 2–2 still has plenty to clean up, but winning a close road game while the offense is not at peak form keeps the season on stable ground.

11

Vikings 15, Dolphins 10

MIN 4–0 • MIA 0–4

Minnesota got to 4–0 without scoring an offensive touchdown. That sounds ugly until you look at how thoroughly the Vikings controlled the game: 39:11 of possession, 72 plays to Miami’s 37, 336 total yards to 198 and zero turnovers. Kyler Murray completed 27 of 37 for 247 yards, T.J. Hockenson caught 13 passes for 119, and Aaron Jones ran for 94. Will Reichard supplied all 15 points with five field goals. Miami’s lone touchdown came from Ollie Gordon II, who ripped off 100 yards on only nine carries, but the Dolphins simply didn’t possess the ball enough to sustain offense. Minnesota’s 4–0 start is gaining substance because it has now shown it can win even when the touchdown column is empty. Miami at 0–4 is heading the opposite direction.

12

Chiefs 30, Raiders 27

KC 4–0 • LV 3–1

This was the AFC West measuring stick, and Kansas City escaped Las Vegas because it has a weapon it didn’t have a year ago: Kenneth Walker III. Walker exploded for 177 yards and two touchdowns on 22 carries, including a 76-yard burst that changed the fourth quarter. With KC trailing 19–17, Walker scored from two yards out and then from 22 yards out just seconds later, turning a deficit into a 30–19 lead. Patrick Mahomes was only 15-of-30, but he threw for 225 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions; Tyquan Thornton supplied 111 yards and both receiving scores. Vegas fought back behind Kirk Cousins’ 365 yards and Brock Bowers’ late touchdown, but Xavier Nwankpa intercepted Cousins with 10 seconds left. The Raiders are still a real 3–1 team — 437 yards and 35:42 of possession against KC is nothing to dismiss — but Kansas City is 4–0 because it keeps winning the high-leverage snaps. And the Walker question is officially real: through Sunday he leads the NFL with 537 rushing yards. On pure rushing production and explosiveness, he has the strongest RB1 case right now; the best all-around back in football is still a wider debate.

13

Seahawks 30, Chargers 23

SEA 3–1 • LAC 0–4

The Chargers outgained Seattle 301–286 and still lost because the rest of the game was a disaster. Los Angeles committed four turnovers and 16 penalties for 130 yards. Justin Herbert threw two interceptions and, with a chance to complete the comeback, was strip-sacked by Uchenna Nwosu with one minute left. Seattle didn’t need huge volume from Sam Darnold — 168 yards and two touchdowns — because Emanuel Wilson and Rashid Shaheed supplied enough on the ground and the Seahawks kept cashing in L.A.’s mistakes. The Chargers are 0–4, and yes, Jim Harbaugh is under real pressure in the football sense: the season is already in emergency mode. But four games is too early to turn that into unsupported job-security talk. The accurate issue is execution: turnovers, penalties and situational breakdowns have made a talented roster winless.

14

49ers 24, Broncos 14

SF 4–0 • DEN 2–2

San Francisco keeps looking like the cleanest team in football. The 49ers committed zero turnovers and only one penalty for five yards, then answered Denver’s late push with the decisive explosive play. Brock Purdy finished 19-of-30 for 218 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions. Christian McCaffrey scored on the ground, Deebo Samuel scored through the air, Mike Evans led the team with 76 receiving yards, and George Kittle broke the game open with a 56-yard touchdown with 2:25 left. Denver had cut the margin to 17–14 and had momentum; Purdy and Kittle ended that conversation immediately. San Francisco is 4–0 not because every game has been a blowout, but because the Niners keep winning the details: ball security, discipline, timely explosives and late-game execution.

15

Panthers 32, Lions 26

CAR 2–2 • DET 2–2 • Sunday Night Football

Carolina’s offense announced itself on national television. Bryce Young went 29-of-41 for 329 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions. Chuba Hubbard ran for 122 yards and two scores. And Tetairoa McMillan — a second-year receiver, not a rookie — authored the night’s biggest individual performance: 14 catches, 192 yards and two touchdowns. Detroit still produced 452 yards and Jared Goff threw for 412, but Carolina was more balanced, just as explosive and never turned the ball over. McMillan’s two touchdowns came from 32 and 10 yards, while Hubbard gave Carolina the physical counterpunch Detroit couldn’t consistently stop. The Panthers are only 2–2, but through Sunday they sit atop the NFC South and now have a real offensive identity to build on. This wasn’t a fluky defensive-score win; Carolina put up 445 yards and beat Detroit straight up on offense.
