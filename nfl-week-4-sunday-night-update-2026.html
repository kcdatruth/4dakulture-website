FINAL • OCT. 5, 2026 • ALL 16 GAMES COMPLETE
MONDAY NIGHT FINAL • ATLANTA 45, NEW ORLEANS 24

Bijan Ran New Orleans Out The Building.

This was a sorry-ass football game if you were waiting for drama. Atlanta took the air out of the Superdome almost immediately, and New Orleans never found a real answer for the one thing everybody knew was coming: the Falcons were going to run the football.

Bijan Robinson set the tone on Atlanta’s third offensive play with a 59-yard touchdown and never really gave the Saints a chance to settle in. He finished with 145 yards and two touchdowns on 19 carries. Brian Robinson Jr. added 62 yards and three more rushing scores. Add it up and Atlanta ran for 205 yards, scored five times on the ground and averaged enough per carry to make the Saints’ front look helpless for long stretches.

The bigger issue for New Orleans was not just the yardage — it was the lack of resistance. Atlanta kept getting clean first-level movement, Bijan kept reaching space before contact, and once the Falcons got ahead the Saints had to defend the run while also worrying about Penix hitting play-action shots behind it. That is a miserable way to play defense.

Michael Penix Jr. did exactly what Atlanta needed: 15-of-20, 223 yards, one touchdown, zero interceptions. He was efficient, aggressive when the shot was there and never forced himself into the story. The 31-yard fourth-quarter touchdown to Jahan Dotson was the perfect example. Penix did not need 40 attempts because the run game was destroying New Orleans. That is a good thing, not a limitation.

Tyler Shough’s final line — 30-of-48, 286 yards, one touchdown, zero interceptions — looks cleaner than the game felt. He was not the reason New Orleans lost, but a lot of the production came with the Saints already chasing a multi-score deficit, and the only touchdown pass came with 1:19 left. At 5.96 yards per attempt, this was volume more than control. Chris Olave gave him a real target with eight catches for 116 yards, but the offense never forced Atlanta out of its preferred game.

The quarterback takeaway is simple: Penix looked like a quarterback playing inside a functioning offense. Shough looked like a quarterback trying to survive the game script after his defense had already lost control of the night.

THE WEEK 4 VERDICT

What The First Month Told Us.

Four weeks is enough to stop hiding behind preseason expectations. The 49ers, Chiefs and Vikings have built real unbeaten résumés. Vegas lost but still looks legitimate. Cleveland, Chicago, Jacksonville and Seattle are forcing themselves into the upper half of the league. The Chargers, Texans, Titans, Bucs and Dolphins have officially reached the point where “it’s early” cannot be the whole explanation anymore.

Atlanta is suddenly one of the most interesting 2–2 teams in football.

Back-to-back dominant wins have changed the temperature. Bijan is rolling, Penix is back, and the Falcons get Baltimore on Sunday night in Week 5. That is a real measuring stick.

ALL 16 WEEK 4 GAMES

Every Game. Nothing Deleted.

The Sunday analysis stays here. Monday gets added to it.

01

Browns 27, Steelers 24

CLE 3–1 • PIT 2–2

Cleveland survived a fourth-quarter Pittsburgh rally and got a 56-yard winner from Andre Szmyt. Deshaun Watson finished with 268 yards while the Browns defense intercepted Aaron Rodgers twice. Cleveland is 3–1 because it keeps finding late answers; Pittsburgh is 2–2 because the volatility keeps showing up.

02

Colts 30, Commanders 13

IND 2–2 • WAS 1–3 • LONDON

Indianapolis leaned on Jonathan Taylor, field position and Washington mistakes. Taylor ran for 95 yards and two touchdowns while the Commanders committed ten penalties and lost Marcus Mariota to a knee injury. Indy is back to .500; Washington leaves London with both a record problem and a quarterback-health question.

03

Ravens 24, Titans 18

BAL 3–1 • TEN 0–4

Lamar Jackson was brutally efficient: 15-of-20, 222 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions. Carnell Tate’s 145-yard breakout was the bright spot for Tennessee, but the Titans spent too much of the afternoon chasing the game. Baltimore is 3–1, with Lamar’s ankle now the Week 5 concern.

04

Patriots 29, Bills 26

NE 2–2 • BUF 3–1

Drake Maye delivered New England’s first real signature win of the season, answering Josh Allen late and giving Buffalo its first loss. The Bills still have a contender ceiling, but the Patriots proved they can beat a good team in winning time.

05

Bears 23, Jets 12

CHI 3–1 • NYJ 1–3

Chicago owned the football game physically: 89 offensive plays, almost 43 minutes of possession and 492 total yards. Kyle Monangai ran for 146 yards and two scores. The Bears are building an identity that travels; the Jets barely had the football.

06

Jaguars 22, Bengals 17

JAX 3–1 • CIN 2–2

Joe Burrow threw for 428 yards, but Jacksonville won the turnover battle and Trevor Lawrence played the cleaner game. The Jaguars are 3–1 because the quarterback play, takeaways and situational football are starting to match the talent.

07

Cowboys 34, Texans 30

DAL 2–2 • HOU 0–4

Houston led by ten at halftime and still let it get away. CeeDee Lamb caught 17 passes for 189 yards and the late winner. C.J. Stroud played well, but 13 Houston penalties and a defense that surrendered 28 second-half points turned another winnable game into 0–4.

08

Giants 36, Cardinals 24

NYG 3–1 • ARI 1–3

The Giants keep banking wins while the quarterback picture evolves. Malik Nabers topped 100 yards, rookie linebacker Arvell Reese had an interception and three pass breakups, and Deonte Banks ended it with a 98-yard pick-six. Ugly or not, 3–1 has to be respected.

09

Rams 24, Eagles 20

LAR 2–2 • PHI 2–2

Philadelphia led 20–10 at halftime and never scored again. Kyren Williams scored twice in the final 4:02 and Puka Nacua’s return immediately changed the Rams offense. The bigger concern is Philly’s offensive profile: 172 total yards and a scoreless second half.

10

Packers 17, Buccaneers 14

GB 2–2 • TB 0–4

Green Bay did enough to survive a close road game without playing its cleanest football. Tampa is 0–4, and the frustrating part is how many of these games have stayed within reach. The Bucs are running out of time to call these encouraging losses.

11

Vikings 15, Dolphins 10

MIN 4–0 • MIA 0–4

Minnesota reached 4–0 without an offensive touchdown. The Vikings held the ball for nearly 40 minutes, committed no turnovers and let Will Reichard kick them home. That kind of floor is exactly why we take this start seriously.

12

Chiefs 30, Raiders 27

KC 4–0 • LV 3–1

Kansas City survived the AFC West measuring stick because Kenneth Walker III changed the game. Walker ran for 177 yards and two touchdowns, while Vegas still produced 437 yards and pushed KC to the final seconds. The Raiders lost without losing our respect.

13

Seahawks 30, Chargers 23

SEA 3–1 • LAC 0–4

The Chargers outgained Seattle and still lost because four turnovers and 16 penalties buried them. At 0–4, this is no longer a slow start. Seattle is 3–1 because it keeps capitalizing when opponents hand it opportunities.

14

49ers 24, Broncos 14

SF 4–0 • DEN 2–2

San Francisco committed zero turnovers and one penalty, then answered Denver’s late push with the decisive Purdy-to-Kittle strike. The 49ers are 4–0 because they keep winning the details that decide close games.

15

Panthers 32, Lions 26

CAR 2–2 • DET 2–2 • SNF

Carolina’s offense announced itself. Bryce Young threw for 329 yards and two touchdowns, Chuba Hubbard ran for 122 and two scores, and Tetairoa McMillan went off for 192 yards and two touchdowns. Carolina is only 2–2, but that offense finally looked like something with a real identity.

16

Falcons 45, Saints 24

ATL 2–2 • NO 1–3 • MONDAY NIGHT FOOTBALL

Atlanta finished Week 4 with the most physically dominant run-game performance of the week. Bijan Robinson ran for 145 yards and two scores, Brian Robinson Jr. added three more rushing touchdowns, and Michael Penix Jr. completed 75 percent of his passes without a turnover. New Orleans could not stop the run, could not create explosive offensive answers early enough and spent most of the night playing from behind. The Saints’ 320 total yards were not meaningless, but they were mostly accumulated while the game state belonged completely to Atlanta.
