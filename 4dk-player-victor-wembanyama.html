The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

Wemby is No. 1 because the combination of present production and future ceiling has already become impossible to separate. This is not a projection based on highlights. Last season he averaged 25 points, 11.5 rebounds and more than three blocks per game, won Defensive Player of the Year and carried San Antonio deep enough into the postseason to earn Western Conference Finals MVP. At 22, he was already impacting games like a player opponents had to redesign their offense around.

Defensively, there is nobody else in the league who changes shot selection the same way. Drivers can beat the first defender and still pull the ball back because Wemby is standing near the rim. Guards who normally love floaters release them earlier. Centers rush hooks. Shooters hesitate on pull-ups because his recovery range erases space that exists against every other big. San Antonio can play aggressive perimeter defense because mistakes behind the ball are not always fatal.

Offensively, he is still unfinished—and that is the scary part. He shot 34.9 percent from three, can attack a closeout, finish lobs from impossible angles, post smaller defenders and increasingly create his own shot from the perimeter. The next step is not adding another trick. It is making the existing package more efficient: fewer loose possessions, better reads against doubles and more consistent dominance inside when teams try to guard him with size.

The Spurs have also built a real ecosystem around him. De’Aaron Fox adds downhill speed, Stephon Castle gives them another oversized creator and San Antonio can now win possessions without asking Wemby to manufacture everything. That should preserve his energy for defense and late-game offense.

Jokić is a historically great offensive center. Luka can lead the league in scoring while creating ten assists. SGA has won back-to-back MVPs. Brunson just won Finals MVP. Ranking Wemby above all of them is aggressive—but that is the point of an entering-the-season list. Right now, no player offers a more extreme combination of rim protection, scoring upside, rebounding, size, mobility and the ability to alter what the other team is willing to attempt.

The future of the league is not approaching anymore. It is already here. Entering 2026–27, 4DK is putting Victor Wembanyama at No. 1.
