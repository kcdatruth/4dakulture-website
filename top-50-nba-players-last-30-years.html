This is not an all-time NBA ranking. That changes everything.

Michael Jordan does not get credit for the first three-peat. Karl Malone does not get his entire 1980s and early-90s résumé. Hakeem, Ewing, David Robinson and Scottie Pippen are judged only for what falls inside the window.

THE 4DK RULE

We judged 1996 through 2026 using peak dominance, longevity, championships, playoff performance, individual accolades, two-way impact, era context, availability, whether production translated to winning, role on contenders and influence on the game itself. No single category could carry a player.

Era matters. Some players worked in slower, more physical defensive environments. Others put up historic offense in an era with more spacing and freedom. Team quality matters too: huge numbers on a mediocre team are not automatically equal to huge numbers driving a contender.

Availability mattered. Derrick Rose, Tracy McGrady, Kawhi Leonard, Anthony Davis and Kevin Durant all lost ground because of injuries or missed seasons. Cultural impact mattered too. Kobe, Iverson, Steph, Shaq, Vince and T-Mac became your favorite player’s favorite player.

THE 4DK VERDICT

THE WINDOW CHANGES THE ARGUMENT.

The point was never to copy an all-time list and chop off the old names. A 30-year window changes how every résumé looks. Jordan loses half his greatness from consideration. Malone, Payton, Kidd and Shaq lose early years. Younger stars have monster peaks but less time. The final order is the balance of what happened, when it happened, how long it lasted, how much it led to winning and how much it changed basketball.
