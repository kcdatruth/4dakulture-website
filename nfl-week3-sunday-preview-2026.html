WHAT SUNDAY IS REALLY ABOUT.

The schedule tells you who plays. These are the threads that make Week 3 feel like Week 3.

BUFFALO 2–0 • L.A. 0–2THE BILLS CAN CREATE DISTANCE.

Buffalo has started like an AFC heavyweight. The Chargers arrive at 0–2 and short-handed up front, putting even more pressure on Justin Herbert to keep pace with Josh Allen.

KANSAS CITY • NEW IDENTITYHOW MUCH KENNETH WALKER IS TOO MUCH?

Walker enters Sunday with 290 rushing yards, 369 scrimmage yards and 56 touches through two games. Kansas City finally has the violent run-game element it wanted — now the Chiefs are talking about keeping him fresh.

SEATTLE • WASHINGTONONE QB RETURNS. ANOTHER IS OUT.

Sam Darnold is back for 2–0 Seattle after missing Week 2. Washington has to turn to Marcus Mariota with Jayden Daniels out because of another left-elbow dislocation.

SAN FRANCISCO • HEALTH TESTTHE 49ERS ARE 2–0. NOW BOSA IS OUT.

San Francisco has looked complete through two weeks, but Nick Bosa's calf injury will cost him multiple games. Week 3 becomes the first real look at how the defense adjusts without its premier edge threat.

LAS VEGAS • 2–0THE RAIDERS KEEP STACKING WINS.

Las Vegas has opened 2–0 without Brock Bowers. The star tight end is questionable in New Orleans and could give Kirk Cousins another major piece as the Raiders try to make the fast start harder to dismiss.

RIO DE JANEIRO • 4:25 ETTHE BIGGEST STAGE OF THE AFTERNOON.

Baltimore and Dallas meet in the NFL's first regular-season game in Rio. Derrick Henry meets a depleted Dallas defense while Dak Prescott arrives after becoming the Cowboys' career touchdown-pass leader.

SUNDAY NIGHT FOOTBALLSTAFFORD + ADAMS TAKE THE SHOW TO DENVER.

Matthew Stafford just threw four touchdowns and Davante Adams went for 195 yards and two scores. Puka Nacua is doubtful again, so Denver gets the hottest veteran QB-WR connection in the league under the lights.

THE EARLY WINDOW

FOX • ORCHARD PARK

CHARGERS (0–2) @ BILLS (2–0)

1:00 ET

The records create the tension before the ball is even kicked. Buffalo can get to 3–0 and keep building early AFC separation. Los Angeles is trying to keep September from turning into a hole. The Chargers' offensive line is the matchup: Kayode Awosika and Trey Pipkins are out, which means Herbert may have to speed up his internal clock against a defense that can make obvious passing downs ugly. Buffalo has its own uncertainty at receiver, but Josh Allen and James Cook give the Bills multiple ways to control the game.

PLAYERS TO WATCHJosh Allen • James Cook • Justin Herbert • Ladd McConkey
INJURY WATCHLAC: Brenen Thompson, Charlie Kolar, Kayode Awosika, Trey Pipkins, Dalvin Tomlinson and Elijah Molden OUT. BUF: Keon Coleman, DJ Moore, Ar'maj Reed-Adams and Ed Oliver QUESTIONABLE.
4DK ANGLE: Buffalo can win this game without making Allen play superhero. The Chargers probably cannot say the same about Herbert.
FOX • CLEVELAND

PANTHERS (1–1) @ BROWNS (1–1)

1:00 ET

Carolina's 34–3 response against Atlanta changed the tone after Week 1. Bryce Young now gets the harder assignment: prove the performance travels. Cleveland is 1–1 and built to make games physical, but the Browns could be missing pieces in the secondary. If Carolina gets Jalen Coker and Xavier Legette on the field, this becomes a better test of whether Young is settling into a sustainable rhythm or simply had one clean afternoon.

PLAYERS TO WATCHBryce Young • Jalen Coker if active • Deshaun Watson • Grant Delpit if active
INJURY WATCHCAR: Claudin Cherelus, Devin Lloyd and Nick Scott OUT; Jalen Coker and Xavier Legette QUESTIONABLE. CLE: Teven Jenkins OUT; Tyson Campbell and Grant Delpit QUESTIONABLE.
FOX • DETROIT

JETS (1–1) @ LIONS (1–1)

1:00 ET

Both teams are coming off losses and both need to re-establish what they want to be. Detroit wants tempo, explosives and pressure on the scoreboard. New York's path is much more controlled, especially with Minkah Fitzpatrick out. If the Jets cannot limit Detroit's early-down efficiency, this can turn into the exact kind of game they do not want — one where their offense has to chase points instead of controlling possession.

PLAYERS TO WATCHJared Goff • Jahmyr Gibbs • Adonai Mitchell if active • New York's defensive front
INJURY WATCHNYJ: Kene Nwangwu, Mason Taylor, Francisco Mauigoa and Minkah Fitzpatrick OUT; Adonai Mitchell and Joseph Ossai QUESTIONABLE. DET: Ben Bartch and Thomas Harper OUT.
CBS • INDIANAPOLIS

TEXANS (0–2) @ COLTS (0–2)

This is already a pressure game. Houston managed only six points against Cincinnati and now loses Nico Collins, while Indianapolis took Kansas City all the way to overtime before falling to 0–2. For the Colts, the path is obvious: let Jonathan Taylor dictate the physical terms and force Houston to prove it can create offense without its No. 1 receiver. For C.J. Stroud, the challenge is creating answers before obvious passing downs arrive.

CBS • JACKSONVILLE

PATRIOTS (1–1) @ JAGUARS (1–1)

1:00 ET

New England just held Pittsburgh to three points. Jacksonville is trying to rebound after a loss to Denver. That sets up a clean quarterback test: can Drake Maye protect the football on the road, and can Trevor Lawrence avoid letting New England's defense turn patience into frustration? The Jaguars need a better early-down game so Lawrence is not constantly asked to solve third-and-long.

PLAYERS TO WATCHDrake Maye • Rhamondre Stevenson • Trevor Lawrence • Brian Thomas Jr.
INJURY WATCHNE: Dre'Mont Jones OUT; Eli Raridon and Craig Woodson QUESTIONABLE. JAX: Albert Regis DOUBTFUL.
CBS • MIAMI

CHIEFS (2–0) @ DOLPHINS (0–2)

1:00 ET

Kansas City finally looks like it has a backfield that can force defenses to respect the run without asking Patrick Mahomes to manufacture everything. Kenneth Walker III enters Week 3 with 290 rushing yards and 369 scrimmage yards on 56 touches, including 178 scrimmage yards in the overtime win over Indianapolis. The new question is workload. Andy Reid and his staff have already talked about getting rookie Emmett Johnson more work so Walker is not burned out before the games get bigger. Miami, meanwhile, is 0–2 and has to find a way to keep the Chiefs from owning both tempo and possession.

PLAYERS TO WATCHPatrick Mahomes • Kenneth Walker III • Travis Kelce • Emmett Johnson
INJURY WATCHKC: Josh Simmons OUT; Chamarri Conner DOUBTFUL. MIA: Caleb Douglas and Robert Beal Jr. OUT; Jaylen Wright DOUBTFUL; Ryan Miller and Julius Brents QUESTIONABLE.
4DK ANGLE: The most important Chiefs development might not be another Mahomes explosion. It may be proving they can reduce Walker's touches and still keep the same offensive identity.

1:00 ET

The Giants' season changed in Los Angeles. Jaxson Dart's knee injury requires season-ending surgery, so Jameis Winston takes over an offense that looked completely different before Dart went down. Tennessee is 0–2, which turns this into an immediate test of New York's emotional reset. Can the Giants still play controlled offense around Malik Nabers and their run game, or does the quarterback change push too much responsibility onto the defense?

PLAYERS TO WATCHJameis Winston • Malik Nabers • Cam Ward • Tyjae Spears if active
INJURY WATCHTEN: Tyjae Spears and Cordale Flott QUESTIONABLE. NYG: Brian Burns, Deonte Banks and Tyler Nubin QUESTIONABLE.

BENGALS (2–0) @ STEELERS (1–1)

The AFC North does not need much help creating stakes. Cincinnati is 2–0 and comes in with Joe Burrow and Ja'Marr Chase already looking settled. Pittsburgh just scored three points against New England and needs this game to become ugly, physical and uncomfortable. The Steelers' backfield is also compromised with Rico Dowdle out and Jaylen Warren questionable, which puts more pressure on Aaron Rodgers to avoid obvious passing situations against a defense playing with confidence.

SEAHAWKS (2–0) @ COMMANDERS (0–2)

Seattle does not need Darnold to rescue a broken team; Washington may need Mariota to stabilize one before 0–2 becomes something worse.

FOX • SANTA CLARA

CARDINALS (1–1) @ 49ERS (2–0)

4:05 ET

San Francisco's biggest opponent keeps being health. The 49ers are 2–0 and coming off a 35–13 win over Miami, but Nick Bosa injured his calf in practice and is expected to miss multiple weeks. That matters beyond one pass rusher: the defensive front was already thin, so Arizona gets a real opportunity to test San Francisco's depth and run fits. Offensively, the Niners still have the Purdy-McCaffrey structure to control the game, but the defense has to show it can generate pressure without leaning on Bosa.

PLAYERS TO WATCHBrock Purdy • Christian McCaffrey • Jeremiyah Love • Mike Evans if active
INJURY WATCHARI: Dadrion Taylor-Demerson OUT; Roy Lopez and Max Melton QUESTIONABLE. SF: Demarcus Robinson, Nick Bosa, Romello Height and James Thompson Jr. OUT; Mike Evans QUESTIONABLE.
4DK ANGLE: San Francisco has looked like one of football's best teams through two weeks. Week 3 starts answering the season-long question: what does this roster look like when the injuries start stacking?

VIKINGS (2–0) @ BUCCANEERS (0–2)

4:05 ET

Minnesota has quietly put together one of the cleanest starts in the NFC, while Tampa is already fighting to keep 0–2 from becoming a season-defining hole. The Vikings can play this game on their terms if they stay balanced and make Tampa string together long drives. The Buccaneers' defense will be without Rueben Bain Jr. and Josiah Trotter, which only increases the importance of creating negative plays elsewhere.

RAIDERS (2–0) @ SAINTS (1–1)

4:25 ET

Vegas is 2–0 without its best offensive player. Kirk Cousins has kept the offense organized, the defense has created pressure, and now Brock Bowers is questionable after missing the first two games following knee surgery. New Orleans has already shown it can drag teams into uncomfortable fourth quarters, so this is a better measuring stick for the Raiders than the record alone suggests. If Bowers returns, Las Vegas finally gets to show what the complete version of its offense might look like.

CBS • MARACANÃ • RIO DE JANEIRO

RAVENS (1–1) VS. COWBOYS (1–1)

4:25 ET

This is the game that makes the Sunday slate feel international. Baltimore and Dallas bring two of the league's biggest brands into the NFL's first regular-season game in Rio de Janeiro. Derrick Henry enters with 212 rushing yards and four rushing touchdowns through two weeks, and Dallas will try to stop him without four defensive players already ruled out. On the other side, Dak Prescott arrives after throwing four touchdowns against Washington and becoming the Cowboys' career leader in touchdown passes. Baltimore is without Ronnie Stanley, while Zay Flowers is questionable, so both teams have real matchup problems underneath the spectacle.

PLAYERS TO WATCHLamar Jackson • Derrick Henry • Dak Prescott • CeeDee Lamb • Zay Flowers if active
INJURY WATCHBAL: Ronnie Stanley OUT; Zay Flowers QUESTIONABLE. DAL: DeMarvion Overshown, Cobie Durant, Malik Hooker and P.J. Locke OUT.
4DK ANGLE: The travel and the history make the backdrop different. The football question is simple: can Dallas survive Baltimore's physicality, and can the Ravens protect Lamar without Stanley?

SUNDAY NIGHT FOOTBALL

NBC • DENVER

RAMS (1–1) @ BRONCOS (1–1)

8:20 ET

The Rams walked out of Monday night looking like the team people expected after their opening loss. Matthew Stafford threw for 327 yards and four touchdowns against the Giants, while Davante Adams caught eight balls for 195 yards and two scores. Puka Nacua is doubtful again, so the Stafford-Adams connection becomes even more central in Denver. The Broncos are 1–1, playing at altitude and built to make quarterbacks work for clean pockets. It is also the first Stafford-Bo Nix head-to-head meeting and a matchup between two teams that reached their conference championship games last season.

PLAYERS TO WATCHMatthew Stafford • Davante Adams • Bo Nix • J.K. Dobbins
INJURY WATCHLAR: Puka Nacua and Kamren Kinchens DOUBTFUL; Colby Parkinson QUESTIONABLE. DEN: Jonah Coleman OUT; Marvin Mims Jr. QUESTIONABLE.
4DK ANGLE: If Stafford and Adams carry Monday's timing into Denver, the Broncos have to create pressure without letting the coverage structure collapse. This is a real Sunday-night measuring stick for both teams.
