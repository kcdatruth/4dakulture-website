The AFC Has No Easy Out.

Denver sits on top of our regular-season board, Mahomes is still lurking, Buffalo's window remains open and almost every division has a quarterback carrying real pressure into 2026.

The AFC still feels like the deeper conference. Patrick Mahomes, Josh Allen, Joe Burrow and Lamar Jackson keep their teams in the championship conversation almost automatically, while C.J. Stroud, Drake Maye and Trevor Lawrence are trying to force their way into that same tier.

But this season is about more than quarterbacks. New coaches are taking over contenders, veteran starters are fighting to hold off younger replacements, and several teams have enough talent to win 11 or 12 games while still feeling one bad month away from disappointment.

Seven playoff spots. At least ten teams that can talk themselves into January. No easy Sundays.
Power. Potential. Pressure.

AFC West

DEN 13–4 • KC 12–5 • LAC 9–8 • LV 6–11
Denver Broncos

Can They Finish What They Started?

13–4

Denver enters 2026 looking less like an upstart and more like a team that belongs near the top of the AFC. Bo Nix returns from the broken ankle that ended his postseason, and the Broncos gave him another major weapon by adding Jaylen Waddle next to Courtland Sutton.

The offense also has a new voice with Davis Webb taking over play-calling duties, while the defense still has elite-level pieces such as Pat Surtain II and Talanoa Hufanga. The schedule is rough early, but this roster is built to absorb it.

4DK Verdict: Best regular-season record in the AFC.
Kansas City Chiefs

Mahomes Is Still Mahomes.

12–5

The comeback starts with Patrick Mahomes' knee. If he is healthy, Kansas City immediately becomes dangerous again. Eric Bieniemy is back, Kenneth Walker III upgrades the backfield, and Rashee Rice and Xavier Worthy give Mahomes enough speed and playmaking to make the offense feel explosive again.

The defense is where the turnover is real. Trent McDuffie and Jaylen Watson are gone, L'Jarius Sneed returns, and young pieces such as Mansoor Delane, Peter Woods and R Mason Thomas are being asked to grow up fast. Chris Jones remains the veteran centerpiece, but there is more pressure on the next wave now.

4DK Verdict: As long as Mahomes is in the conference, nobody is ever truly out of it.
Los Angeles Chargers

Talent Says Elite. Results Say Not Yet.

9–8

The Chargers have the talent of a top-tier team. Justin Herbert is still there. Derwin James is still there. Jim Harbaugh gives them a high floor, and Mike McDaniel's arrival should make the offense more creative.

But Herbert is 28, entering his seventh season and still looking for his first playoff win. He also has to clean up the tendency to hold the ball too long. Getting Rashawn Slater and Joe Alt back healthy should help, but the bigger issue is the same one that has followed this team for years: inconsistency.

4DK Verdict: Too talented to be bad. Too inconsistent to trust.
Las Vegas Raiders

How Long Until The Kid Gets The Keys?

6–11

Kirk Cousins should open the season as the bridge to No. 1 pick Fernando Mendoza. If Vegas starts 0–4, I expect Mendoza to be starting by Week 5. If the Raiders remain competitive, letting him watch a 38-year-old veteran for another month or two makes sense.

The player I am watching just as closely is Ashton Jeanty. If he stays healthy and improves behind a better line and steadier quarterback play, the Raiders could have the beginning of something special with Jeanty, Mendoza and Brock Bowers.

4DK Verdict: Slightly better, still rebuilding.
Contenders. New Chapters. Big Expectations.

AFC East

BUF 12–5 • NE 11–6 • MIA 8–9 • NYJ 5–12
Buffalo Bills

The Window Always Has A Crack.

12–5

As long as Josh Allen is in Buffalo, the championship window never fully closes. At 30, though, Allen has to be smarter about the wear and tear of a 17-game season and Buffalo has to stop asking him to carry everything.

James Cook can help after leading the league with 1,621 rushing yards in 2025, and the addition of D.J. Moore gives Allen another proven target. This is also Year 1 of the Joe Brady era after Sean McDermott, making the coaching transition one of the biggest stories on a roster built to win now. Keon Coleman is entering an important third season and needs to become more consistent.

4DK Verdict: Still a real Super Bowl contender.
New England Patriots

They Already Proved They Belong.

11–6

When you just made a Super Bowl, you are already a serious playoff threat. The A.J. Brown trade raises the ceiling of the offense and gives Drake Maye the kind of No. 1 receiver who can accelerate his development.

The keys are health and chemistry. How quickly can Maye and Brown click? Can Maye take another step? And after Christian Gonzalez landed a record-setting extension, can he continue playing like one of the league's premier corners? Mike Vrabel makes you believe this team will stay in the fight.

4DK Verdict: 11 wins and dangerous again in January.
Miami Dolphins

Now Malik Willis Has To Do It For 17.

8–9

Malik Willis built a cult following in Green Bay by flashing real improvement behind Jordan Love. Now, at 27, he finally enters a season as the unquestioned No. 1 quarterback.

Miami has also torn up the old offensive identity around him. Tua Tagovailoa, Tyreek Hill and Jaylen Waddle are gone, while De'Von Achane becomes even more important as the stabilizing playmaker in the backfield. Willis does not need to look elite for a month. He has to prove he can lead a team for an entire season.

4DK Verdict: Fascinating quarterback experiment, but still below .500.
New York Jets

Still Rebuilding. Still Searching.

5–12

The Jets are still rebuilding, but the young offensive pieces are worth watching. Kenyon Sadiq and Omar Cooper Jr. join Garrett Wilson, Breece Hall and Braelon Allen in a skill group that has more upside than the record prediction suggests.

Geno Smith gets the first chance to stabilize the position, but if New York starts slowly, Cade Klubnik could be on the field before October is over. Aaron Glenn is also trying to rebuild a defense that needed major changes after last season.

4DK Verdict: Eight wins would be success. I only see five.
Elite Offenses. Defensive Questions. Pressure.

AFC North

CIN 11–6 • BAL 10–7 • PIT 9–8 • CLE 4–13
Cincinnati Bengals

The Offense Is Proven. Fix The Defense.

11–6

I trust Joe Burrow and Ja'Marr Chase. There is no question what Burrow, Chase and Tee Higgins can be together. They have made this one of the league's elite passing offenses for years.

The question has always been the defense. Cincinnati attacked that weakness by adding Dexter Lawrence II, Jonathan Allen, Boye Mafe, Bryan Cook and Kyle Dugger. Burrow's health also matters after another injury-shortened season. If the defense becomes merely good and Burrow stays upright, this team can win the conference.

4DK Verdict: Championship offense. Defense determines everything.
Baltimore Ravens

Lamar Keeps The Door Open.

10–7

As long as Lamar Jackson is there, Baltimore always has a chance. But the window closes a little more every year they fail to get over the hump.

Derrick Henry is still one of the best backs in football after 1,595 rushing yards and 16 touchdowns, while Zay Flowers looks like a real deep threat after an 86-catch, 1,211-yard season. The issue has been inconsistent defense and blown leads. Now first-year head coach Jesse Minter inherits that pressure, with Trey Hendrickson added to help restore the pass rush.

4DK Verdict: Still dangerous. Still carrying January questions.
Pittsburgh Steelers

Can Rodgers Find The Fountain Of Youth?

9–8

Pittsburgh feels mediocre. Aaron Rodgers is 42 and, in my view, should have retired two years ago. The defense will keep the Steelers competitive, but the offense still has a hard ceiling unless Rodgers turns back the clock.

Mike Tomlin is gone and Mike McCarthy is in, reuniting with the quarterback he coached for years in Green Bay. Michael Pittman Jr. gives Rodgers another established target. The question is whether that combination can squeeze one more meaningful run out of an aging quarterback.

4DK Verdict: The defense keeps them around .500. The offense keeps them from the elite.
Cleveland Browns

The QB Controversy Is The Season.

4–13

Deshaun Watson opens as the starter despite barely being football-relevant since Houston. Shedeur Sanders is clearly the fan favorite, and I expect the switch around Week 6.

The more encouraging story is the young defense. Cleveland lost Myles Garrett but got Jared Verse back, giving the Browns a former Defensive Rookie of the Year and two-time Pro Bowler to build around with Mason Graham and Carson Schwesinger. Todd Monken takes over as head coach, while Judkins and Fannin Jr. are two offensive pieces not to sleep on.

4DK Verdict: Bad record, important development year.
Young Stars. Big Questions. High Ceilings.

AFC South

HOU 11–6 • JAX 10–7 • IND 9–8 • TEN 5–12
Houston Texans

The Defense Is Ready. Is C.J.?

11–6

Houston might have the best defense in football. Talent is everywhere, and Will Anderson Jr. has already played himself into the conversation among the NFL's best edge rushers.

I like the David Montgomery pickup at running back because it gives Houston another way to take pressure off C.J. Stroud. The offensive line has also been reshuffled again, which makes Stroud's accuracy and command even more important. He has to show he can go from a good young quarterback to an elite one.

4DK Verdict: Super Bowl defense. Stroud decides the ceiling.
Jacksonville Jaguars

Trevor Lawrence Has To Take The Leap.

10–7

Jacksonville's entire ceiling still comes back to Trevor Lawrence. At some point the flashes have to become an elite full season.

Travis Hunter remains one of the league's most fascinating players because Jacksonville still has to decide how much two-way football is sustainable. The backfield also changes after Travis Etienne's departure, and the defense has to prove last season was not a fluke.

4DK Verdict: Playoff talent. First team out of our AFC field.
Indianapolis Colts

Daniel Jones Decides The Season.

9–8

Jonathan Taylor may make a real run at 1,800 rushing yards. That gives Indianapolis a weekly identity no matter what happens at quarterback.

But Daniel Jones is the difference between this team sneaking into the playoffs and flaming out. He is returning from an Achilles injury, and Anthony Richardson is still waiting behind him. If Indianapolis starts slowly, don't be surprised if Richardson gets the job back.

4DK Verdict: Taylor raises the floor. Quarterback play decides the rest.
Tennessee Titans

Cam Ward. Cam Ward. Cam Ward.

5–12

This season is about one thing: Cam Ward's development. Tennessee brought in Robert Saleh as head coach and Brian Daboll to run the offense, giving Ward a new structure in Year 2.

I still see a 5–12 team, but the record is not the only way to judge this season. If Ward can keep Tennessee competitive, show clear progression and somehow push this team toward eight wins, that is a win for the franchise.

4DK Verdict: Find out whether Ward is the franchise quarterback. Everything else is secondary.

That does not mean Denver is automatically the conference champion. Kansas City at 12–5 would be the most dangerous wild card in football, Buffalo still has Allen, Cincinnati still has Burrow, and Houston may have the defense nobody wants to see.

Final Thoughts

The AFC is deep enough that a 10-win Jacksonville team misses our projected field. Denver leads the regular-season board, but Buffalo, Kansas City, Cincinnati and Houston all have legitimate conference-winning talent. New England has already shown it can get to February, while Baltimore remains dangerous because Lamar Jackson changes every matchup.

Behind them, the Chargers are entering a pressure year, Indianapolis lives on a quarterback swing, Miami is testing whether Malik Willis can truly be a full-time starter, and Las Vegas, New York, Tennessee and Cleveland are still trying to identify what the next version of their franchise looks like.

Predictions are easy. Perspective is everything. Now we get to see who was right.
