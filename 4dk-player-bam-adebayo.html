The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

Bam remains one of the NBA’s great defensive problem-solvers. Miami can switch him, play drop, put him on a star wing, let him cover two players in pick-and-roll or ask him to protect the rim late. That versatility is why his impact is larger than a traditional center box score.

Offensively, last season was uneven. He averaged 20.1 points and 10 rebounds, but 44.2 percent from the field is low for a center and the three-point experiment did not create enough spacing to justify all the misses. With Giannis now in Miami, Bam’s offensive role has to become clearer. The two-big pairing can be devastating in transition and defensively, but the half-court geometry is the question. Bam will need to screen, make quick passes, punish short rolls and hit enough open jumpers that teams cannot simply wall off the paint.

If Miami solves the spacing, Bam’s defensive value next to Giannis could make the Heat brutal to score against. His offensive ceiling is why he stops at 17; his defensive floor is why he stays this high.
