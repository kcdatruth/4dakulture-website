Seven Games. One Overtime Thriller. A Western Conference Warning.

Curry delivers 41 in overtime. Atlanta climbs out of a 17-point hole. The Thunder overwhelm Washington. Booker finishes Houston. Wembanyama keeps San Antonio rolling. Every game, every consequence — only on 4dakulturemedia.com.

4DK Player of the Night

Stephen Curry — 41 points

Six rebounds. Six assists. A 126–119 overtime win over Orlando. On a night filled with dominant team performances, Curry delivered the signature individual performance in the tightest spotlight. His three-point play with 1:38 remaining in overtime gave Golden State the lead for good. His next driving basket pushed him to 41, while Yaxel Lendeborg supplied 23 points and three blocks. That combination of late-game star creation and two-way rookie support gives Curry the edge over Wembanyama, Brunson and Gilgeous-Alexander.

GOLDEN STATE 126 • ORLANDO 119 (OT)

CURRY TAKES OVER WHEN THE LIGHTS GET BRIGHTEST

Stephen Curry: 41 PTS, 6 REB, 6 AST | Franz Wagner: 30 PTS, 6 REB, 11 AST

The 4DK Breakdown

Golden State's seventh straight win was not a wire-to-wire display of control. Orlando outscored the Warriors 37–20 in the third quarter, then put up 32 more in the fourth to force overtime. That matters because the Magic were not simply hanging around on a hot shooting night: Franz Wagner was operating as a scorer and distributor, producing 30 points and 11 assists, while Wendell Carter Jr. supplied 19 points and 11 rebounds. Orlando found enough different routes to offense to keep Golden State reacting deep into the night.

But the Warriors still had the ultimate late-game problem solver. Curry's 41 points gave them a source of shot creation when the game tightened and the ordinary flow of offense became harder to sustain. Yaxel Lendeborg's 23 points, eight rebounds and three blocks were just as revealing for the team's bigger picture. This was not a case of Curry dragging an empty roster over the line. The rookie provided two-way support, and Draymond Green's 13 assists helped organize the attack around their scoring threats.

There is a difference between surviving because a star catches fire and surviving because the roster has enough connected pieces to capitalize on that fire. Golden State showed both. Orlando, meanwhile, has a real offensive foundation in Wagner's creation, but allowing 16 points in the five-minute extra period turned a competitive effort into a fourth consecutive defeat.

Turning Point

The game was still up for grabs in overtime. Curry's three-point play with 1:38 remaining put Golden State ahead for good; a driving basket 44 seconds later pushed his total to 41. Lendeborg then finished the job with a dunk and two free throws. The Warriors won the extra period 16–9. Those are the possessions that explain the final margin far better than simply saying Golden State executed better.

What It Means

Seven consecutive victories after an 0–3 start put the Warriors firmly on the early Western Conference radar. The larger development is the combination of Curry's late-game brilliance and Lendeborg's rapidly expanding role. Orlando's fourth straight loss demands a different kind of evaluation: the Magic proved they can create offense against a dangerous opponent, but their defense still has to finish the job.

ATLANTA 124 • BROOKLYN 121

THE HAWKS ERASE SEVENTEEN AND FINISH THE JOB

Jalen Johnson: 30 PTS, 7 REB, 6 AST | Day’Ron Sharpe: 12 PTS, 10 REB, 13 AST

The 4DK Breakdown

Brooklyn scored 37 in the opening quarter and stretched its advantage to 17. Atlanta could have spent the rest of the evening trading baskets and hoping the deficit shrank on its own. Instead, the Hawks built their comeback through a better fourth quarter, outscoring the Nets 36–26 and turning a game they had chased into a finish they could control.

Jalen Johnson's return from a three-game absence changed the offensive balance. His 30 points, seven rebounds and six assists were not empty volume; 12 of those points came in the fourth quarter. Nickeil Alexander-Walker and Aaron Wiggins each added 20, with Wiggins also scoring 12 in the final period. That spread of contributions is why Atlanta could keep attacking even as Brooklyn produced answers.

The Nets deserve more than a passing mention. Michael Porter Jr. supplied 25 points and nine rebounds, Julius Randle had 19 points and eight assists, and Day'Ron Sharpe's 12-point, 10-rebound, 13-assist triple-double gave Brooklyn an unusual passing presence. Mikel Brown Jr. also scored 13 of his 18 points in the fourth. Brooklyn did not collapse because it lacked offensive ideas; it lost the late-possession battle against a team that finally found defensive resistance.

Turning Point

Alexander-Walker's two free throws with 1:26 remaining gave Atlanta its first lead. That was the decisive reversal: the Hawks had spent almost the entire night behind, yet they entered the final stretch with the advantage. Johnson's fourth-quarter scoring and Wiggins' contributions created the platform for that moment.

What It Means

Four consecutive Atlanta wins are a genuine momentum marker, particularly with Johnson immediately returning to a leading role. But the opening-quarter defense cannot become a habit. Brooklyn's challenge is sharper: a creative night featuring Sharpe's triple-double and multiple scoring options still ended in a loss. The Nets need closing execution to match their playmaking imagination.

OKLAHOMA CITY 114 • WASHINGTON 75

THUNDER DEFENSE TURNS THE GAME INTO A ROUT

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander: 30 PTS, 9 AST | Alex Sarr: 15 PTS, 14 REB

The 4DK Breakdown

Holding an NBA team to 75 points is not merely a good defensive night; it is a statement about how thoroughly a defense can dictate the terms of a game. Washington scored 19, 17, 23 and 16 points by quarter. Oklahoma City never needed an offensive explosion to make the result lopsided, because its defense kept the Wizards from stringing together enough productive possessions to change the pressure.

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander's 30 points and nine assists gave the Thunder a stable offensive center. Around him, the depth mattered: Jared McCain scored 19 off the bench, rookie Aday Mara delivered 18 points on 8-of-10 shooting in just his second start, and Isaiah Hartenstein pulled down 14 rebounds. The Thunder were able to score through different players without sacrificing their defensive identity.

Washington's rookie AJ Dybantsa led the Wizards with 19 points, but Oklahoma City's defense kept Washington from establishing consistent offense. Those individual efforts could not solve the larger problem: the Wizards struggled to generate sustained offense against a team that repeatedly forced them to play from disadvantage. Trae Young's four points on 1-of-6 shooting were another illustration of how difficult the night became for Washington's creators.

Turning Point

Oklahoma City won the second quarter 32–17 and the third 33–23. By the end of three periods, the Thunder had piled up 90 points while Washington had only 59. The separation was built across multiple quarters rather than one dramatic final-minute sequence.

What It Means

The Thunder's fourth consecutive victory moved them to 8–2 and reinforced the case that their defense travels into any matchup, regardless of who is available. Washington has to treat this as a structural offensive warning, not simply a cold shooting night. A team that cannot create quality possessions against pressure will struggle to survive even when its individual talents have respectable box scores.

NEW YORK 109 • CLEVELAND 97

BRUNSON AND TOWNS MAKE THEIR CASE

Jalen Brunson: 31 PTS, 9 AST | Karl-Anthony Towns: 29 PTS, 13 REB | Donovan Mitchell: 37 PTS

The 4DK Breakdown

The Knicks did not need perfect perimeter shooting to beat Cleveland. They needed the right players to control the game's most important stretches. Jalen Brunson scored 31 with nine assists, Karl-Anthony Towns delivered 29 and 13 rebounds, and the pair gave New York two distinct pressure points: Brunson manipulating the perimeter and Towns providing size, rebounding and scoring inside the arc.

Brunson's 18 consecutive New York points in the second quarter were the clearest example of star shot creation changing the rhythm of a game. New York carried a 63–53 lead into halftime, then held Cleveland to just 17 points in the third quarter. That defensive stretch matters as much as the scoring highlights. It forced the Cavaliers to chase a margin they could never fully erase.

Donovan Mitchell's 37 points included 21 in the fourth quarter, a remarkable individual response. But the Cavaliers never closed within eight during that late push. Evan Mobley's 12 points, nine rebounds and five assists offered some support, yet Cleveland lacked the sustained second offensive engine New York had in Towns. The difference was not simply who had the hottest scorer; it was which team could answer one star with another.

Turning Point

Brunson's second-quarter scoring barrage built the foundation, and the Knicks' 25–17 third quarter extended it. Mitchell's fourth-quarter eruption tested that cushion, but New York's earlier work meant it could withstand the push without surrendering control.

What It Means

Three straight wins and a 5–4 record moved New York above .500. More importantly, the Brunson–Towns partnership showed how it can withstand an elite opposing scorer. Cleveland fell to 5–5 and needs to find more reliable complementary offense when Mitchell faces a team with multiple dependable creators.

DENVER 114 • INDIANA 102

JOKIĆ CONDUCTS THE SHOW IN MEXICO CITY

Nikola Jokić: 19 PTS, 13 REB, 8 AST | Jamal Murray: 16 PTS, 8 AST

The 4DK Breakdown

Nikola Jokić did not need a 35-point masterpiece to control the game in Mexico City. His 19 points, 13 rebounds and eight assists were the organizing force behind a Denver attack that took care of the basketball and kept Indiana defending multiple options. Cameron Johnson added 18 on efficient 8-of-12 shooting, and Jamal Murray contributed 16 points and eight assists. The Nuggets' strength was the connection between their creators, not one scorer's takeover.

Denver committed only three turnovers, an extraordinary figure for a game played at NBA speed. That ball security meant Indiana had fewer opportunities to manufacture easy transition points. With Jokić directing traffic and Murray helping initiate offense, the Nuggets could build an advantage through possession quality rather than relying on rushed attempts.

Pascal Siakam scored 23 for Indiana and Larry Nance Jr. hit five threes for 15 points, but those performances were largely responses to a game Denver had already tilted. Indiana played without Tyrese Haliburton, which reduced the number of ways it could pressure the Nuggets' defense. The Pacers showed second-half resistance, yet the first-half hole was too deep.

Turning Point

Denver's 15–0 run in the second quarter built a 49–28 advantage. The Nuggets led 64–41 at halftime. That was the real separation: Indiana had to spend the rest of the game recovering from a stretch in which Denver scored repeatedly without giving anything back.

What It Means

Denver won for the fourth time in five games and demonstrated that its offense can dominate without an overwhelming individual scoring line. Indiana has lost six of seven and must find ways to protect the ball and disrupt elite passing teams, especially while missing a primary creator.

PHOENIX 102 • HOUSTON 99

BOOKER WINS THE POSSESSION-BY-POSSESSION BATTLE

Devin Booker: 24 PTS | Kevin Durant: 21 PTS, 10 REB

The 4DK Breakdown

Phoenix beat Houston in a game where neither offense consistently found easy answers. That made Devin Booker's shot creation especially valuable. He finished with 24 points, but the story was the timing of his scoring: nine consecutive Suns points late in the fourth quarter when the outcome was still balanced on a few possessions.

Houston's Kevin Durant finished with 21 points and 10 rebounds, but shot 5-of-14 overall and 1-of-7 from three. Amen Thompson added 17 points, eight rebounds and four blocks, while Fred VanVleet scored 16. Those contributions kept the Rockets competitive, yet Houston shot just 38.7 percent from the floor and 10-of-35 from deep. Its offense generated enough chances to stay close, but not enough efficient finishes to seize control.

Booker had help, too. Jalen Green added 16 points and six assists, while Miles Bridges produced 13 points, 10 rebounds and seven assists. That distribution mattered because it kept Phoenix from asking Booker to create every possession. The Suns' late-game hierarchy was clear: others helped keep the game within reach, then Booker took the hardest shots when the clock demanded an answer.

Turning Point

With the score tied 93–93 and 4:24 remaining, Booker hit a 17-footer. He followed with a step-back jumper, a three and a driving layup, scoring nine straight Phoenix points to build a 102–95 lead with 2:30 left. Houston made the finish uncomfortable, but that personal scoring burst created the cushion Phoenix needed.

What It Means

This was a meaningful result for a Phoenix team trying to build confidence against the West's stronger opponents. Houston's 7–3 record remained impressive, but the loss exposed a question contenders must answer: who creates efficient offense when the first action stalls and the game slows to a possession-by-possession test?

SAN ANTONIO 125 • PORTLAND 103

WEMBANYAMA MAKES AN EIGHTH STRAIGHT WIN LOOK ROUTINE

Victor Wembanyama: 29 PTS, 7 REB, 3 AST | Deni Avdija: 15 PTS

The 4DK Breakdown

San Antonio's eighth consecutive win was a display of collective control, not merely Victor Wembanyama's brilliance. He scored 29 points and grabbed seven rebounds before sitting the entire fourth quarter, as did the other four Spurs starters. That detail tells the story: the contest was effectively decided before the closing period began.

The Spurs never trailed. They outrebounded Portland 51–38 and converted 28 of 30 free throws, combining physical advantages with disciplined execution. Devin Vassell added 15 points, Stephon Castle supplied 14 points, seven rebounds and five assists, and the bench provided further support through Julian Champagnie and Dylan Harper. Wembanyama remained the central matchup problem, but the surrounding production prevented Portland from loading all its attention onto him.

Deni Avdija led the Trail Blazers with 15 points, while Scoot Henderson added 13 and Damian Lillard had 12 points and seven assists. Portland entered with an encouraging start to the season, yet this matchup exposed how much harder life becomes when the opponent wins the glass, gets to the line and scores across multiple positions. San Antonio did not just have the best player on the floor; it won the underlying team battles.

Turning Point

San Antonio outscored Portland 45–27 in the third quarter and at one point led 109–74, a 35-point cushion. The Spurs' starters could rest for the entire fourth because the third-quarter surge had already removed the suspense.

What It Means

At 8–2, the Spurs belong in the early conversation with the West's strongest teams. Their eighth straight victory was not dependent on a miraculous closing stretch; it came from controlling the game from the opening minutes. Portland must respond by finding more resistance on the glass and more defensive answers against teams with elite size and coordinated scoring.

The Night in Perspective

Golden State and San Antonio left Saturday with winning streaks worth watching for very different reasons: the Warriors survived a close-game examination, while the Spurs imposed their will from start to finish. Oklahoma City looked every bit as dangerous defensively as its reputation suggests. Phoenix and Atlanta showed that close wins can carry as much meaning as blowouts. Seven games, seven different ways to make a statement.
