4DK THURSDAY NIGHT RECAP • WEEK 5

DALLAS LET
ONE GET AWAY.

Tampa Bay came to Arlington 0–4, without Baker Mayfield, starting an undrafted rookie quarterback — and left with a 24–16 win. Jalon Daniels got his first NFL victory. Bucky Irving ran wild. Dallas dropped the kind of home game playoff teams are supposed to put away.

THE STORY

Dallas did not lose because Tampa Bay played a perfect game. The Buccaneers committed 14 penalties for 92 yards. Dallas lost because Tampa controlled the game physically, protected the football and punished every Cowboys mistake.

THIS IS THE KIND OF GAME DALLAS CANNOT GIVE AWAY.

At home. Prime time. Tampa Bay entered 0–4. Baker Mayfield was out. Jalon Daniels was making only his second NFL start. Dallas had just gone into Houston and won a game it had to fight from behind to steal.

This was supposed to be the easier part.

Instead, the Cowboys leave Week 5 at 2–3 because they lost the exact kind of game that becomes painful in December when everybody starts counting tiebreakers and asking where the ninth or tenth win was supposed to come from.

DALLAS LOST THIS GAME THE WAY BAD TEAMS LOSE WINNABLE GAMES.

Tampa Bay ran it 39 times. Dallas ran it 13. The Buccaneers held the football for 39:21. Dallas had it for only 20:39. Tampa finished with 419 total yards and zero turnovers.

Dallas turned it over three times.

That is not some bad-luck formula. That is a team getting dictated to. Dak Prescott threw for 316 yards. George Pickens went for 130. Ryan Flournoy had 90. But Dallas never controlled the rhythm.

BUCKY IRVING RAN THROUGH THEM.

Irving carried 21 times for a career-high 165 yards, averaged nearly eight yards per carry and broke a 72-yard run that completely changed the feel of the game. He scored a rushing touchdown and caught Daniels' touchdown pass too.

The impressive part was not just the explosive run. It was how Tampa kept going back to him even though Dallas knew the rookie quarterback was not going to be asked to carry the offense with 40 throws.

4DK GAME BALLBucky Irving: 21 carries, 165 rushing yards, one rushing TD and one receiving TD. Tampa protected Jalon Daniels by giving Dallas a run game it could not stop.

JALON DANIELS GOT HIS FIRST WIN — AND HE EARNED IT.

Daniels finished 19-of-25 for 189 yards, one touchdown and no interceptions. He added 23 yards with his legs. He did not chase throws that were not there. He did not hand Dallas the short field it needed.

His first start against Green Bay had rookie mistakes. Thursday looked different. The game looked slower for him.

Daniels did not outduel Dak statistically. He outplayed him in the category that mattered most: he never gave Dallas the ball.

DAK'S NUMBERS LOOK BETTER THAN DALLAS' OFFENSE FELT.

Dak threw for 316 yards, but the two interceptions are the headline because this was a game where Dallas could not afford empty possessions. The final one, picked off by Alex Anzalone with 1:23 left, ended the last real comeback chance.

Pickens was excellent: nine catches, 130 yards and the fourth-quarter touchdown. Flournoy gave Dallas another vertical spark. Javonte Williams scored early. The issue was not whether Dallas had weapons. The issue was whether the offense could stay balanced and avoid making Tampa's job easier.

TAMPA DIDN'T PLAY CLEAN. IT PLAYED WINNING FOOTBALL.

Tampa committed 14 penalties for 92 yards. On most nights, especially with a rookie quarterback, that is how an 0–4 team becomes 0–5.

But the Bucs overcame it because the rest of the formula was strong: no turnovers, a dominant run game, long possessions and three takeaways.

The third quarter was the difference. Tampa outscored Dallas 17–0, with Emeka Egbuka scoring on a 14-yard run, Irving finishing another drive from one yard out and Chase McLaughlin adding a field goal after KaVontae Turpin's fumble.

WHAT HAPPENS WHEN BAKER MAYFIELD COMES BACK?

Baker dislocated the thumb on his right throwing hand late in Tampa Bay's Week 3 loss to Minnesota. Testing showed no fracture and no tendon damage, and the original timetable was a minimum of three weeks.

He has now missed Weeks 4 and 5. Week 6 against Pittsburgh sits at the very front edge of that timeline, with a road game at Carolina following in Week 7. There is still no official return date.

Daniels winning changes the pressure level. Tampa does not have to rush Baker back just because the season started 0–4. If the grip and ball control are not fully back, the rookie just proved he can keep the offense functional when the run game travels.

TAMPA BAY IS 1–4. IS THERE ACTUALLY A PATH?

Yes — because the NFC South has not run away from them.

Carolina and Atlanta entered the rest of Week 5 at 2–2. New Orleans is 1–3. Tampa is 1–4. That is still a bad record, but it is not a division where the leader is already 5–0.

The upcoming schedule gives Tampa direct opportunities to change the conversation: Pittsburgh at home, then Carolina and Atlanta in Weeks 7 and 8. If Baker gets back during that stretch and Irving keeps giving the offense this kind of floor, Thursday may be the game that kept the season from dying.

IS DALLAS EVEN A PLAYOFF TEAM?

Right now, we would call Dallas a fringe playoff team with playoff-level offensive talent — not a team that has earned contender status.

The Cowboys are 2–3. The Giants entered the rest of Week 5 at 3–1. Philadelphia is 2–2. Dallas still has plenty of season left, but the next two games are at Green Bay and at Philadelphia.

That is why this Tampa loss matters. You can survive losing to a good team. What gets teams in trouble is dropping the home game against an 0–4 opponent immediately before the schedule gets harder.

IS THIS JUST ANOTHER BAD LOSS — OR IS THIS WHO DALLAS REALLY IS?

Our answer is: both can be true.

This was absolutely a bad loss. Dallas was at home against a winless team starting a rookie backup quarterback. But the way the Cowboys lost also fits the flaws we have already seen.

They can throw the football. Dak can move it. Pickens can win outside. There are explosive plays in this offense. But Dallas has not consistently dictated games. The defense can be moved. The run game can disappear. And when the turnovers show up, the Cowboys do not have enough margin to survive them.

If those same problems show up in Green Bay and Philadelphia, this Tampa loss stops looking like a random Thursday stumble and starts looking like the clearest snapshot of what Dallas actually is.

4DK FINAL WORD

TAMPA SAVED ITS SEASON. DALLAS CREATED A PROBLEM.

For Tampa Bay, this is exactly the kind of win a struggling team needs. Jalon Daniels got his first NFL victory. Bucky Irving was the best player on the field. The defense took the ball away three times. And the Bucs finally have something positive to carry into the Baker Mayfield decision.

For Dallas, there is no clean way to dress this up.

The Cowboys were at home, against an 0–4 team, with the opposing starting quarterback out — and still lost the possession battle by almost 19 minutes, turned it over three times and got run on all night.

One loss does not eliminate Dallas from anything. But playoff teams have to bank the games they are supposed to win. The Cowboys just gave one of those away.
