Milwaukee Bucks

Milwaukee’s slow fall from grace started after its Finals run in 2021. The Bucks would finish with 51 wins in the 2021–22 season, but they were bounced in seven games in the second round by the eventual Eastern Conference champion Boston Celtics.

That loss did not immediately signal the end. Milwaukee still had a championship core, a generational franchise player and every reason to believe there was more basketball left in its window. But the years that followed turned what looked like a temporary setback into the beginning of a steady decline.

Five years after the championship, Giannis is gone and Milwaukee is starting over.

Last offseason, Dame would leave Milwaukee, leaving Giannis as the lone star on a team destined for a rebuild. The Bucks suffered a seven-game losing streak early in the season, and then the rumors started.

Trade rumors really heated up around the deadline. With injuries piling up, limited draft capital and tension growing between Giannis and management, Milwaukee’s franchise cornerstone would play only 36 games.

The Bucks eventually suffered their worst season in more than a decade, finishing 32–50. Doc Rivers was fired, the organization was forced to confront where it really stood, and in July Milwaukee finally moved its disgruntled franchise cornerstone to the Miami Heat.

The 2021 title will always matter. Nothing about the collapse that followed erases what that team accomplished. But Milwaukee’s story is now about how quickly a championship window can close—and how difficult it is to recover when the decisions, injuries and roster limitations all start hitting at once.

The Fall, in Four Chapters

2021
Milwaukee reaches the top and wins the NBA championship.
2022
A 51-win season ends in a seven-game second-round loss to Boston.
2025–26
The roster unravels, Milwaukee slides to 32–50 and the Giannis rumors become impossible to ignore.
July 2026
The era ends with Giannis being moved to Miami and Milwaukee beginning a new chapter.
