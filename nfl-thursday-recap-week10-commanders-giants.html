One more chance was all Jayden needed.

Washington kept its season alive—and the Giants let a chance to take control of the NFC East slip away.

WASHINGTON 24 · NY GIANTS 21FINAL · METLIFE STADIUM · WAS 3–5–1 / NYG 5–3–1

The Giants had the lead. Their defense delivered consecutive interceptions. Their offense had two opportunities to start putting this game away.

And somehow, Washington still got the football back with a chance to win.

Jayden Daniels found Stefon Diggs for a 32-yard touchdown with 57 seconds remaining, turning a potential season-damaging defeat into a desperately needed divisional victory. Washington improved to 3–5–1, while New York dropped to 5–3–1. The Giants remain atop the NFC East, but this was an opportunity to create separation that they cannot get back.

Our 4DK read: Washington earned credit for surviving its mistakes. New York deserves scrutiny for failing to punish them.

Daniels’ night was complicated. His two interceptions gave the Giants chances to close the door, and those mistakes belong in the evaluation alongside the winning touchdown. But Washington’s defense forced consecutive three-and-outs after the turnovers, keeping a four-point deficit from becoming something worse. The offense then delivered a three-play, 71-yard touchdown drive that took just 49 seconds.

That is complementary football at its most necessary. A quarterback cannot rescue a game if his defense never gives him another possession. Washington’s response matters because a struggling team can let one mistake become three: a turnover, a defensive breakdown, then another rushed offensive possession. The Commanders interrupted that sequence.

Diggs supplied the finishing play, getting open on third-and-four and turning Daniels’ throw into the decisive touchdown. For 4DK, that is the value of a veteran receiving option in a pressure situation. The offense needs somebody who can turn a manageable down into more than merely another set of downs. Washington needed the end zone, and the connection delivered it.

New York’s frustration should run deeper than the final defensive breakdown. Isaiah Likely’s 66-yard touchdown reception had put the Giants ahead 21–17. Then Jevón Holland and Ar’Darius Washington intercepted Daniels on consecutive possessions. Neither takeaway produced a first down for New York’s offense.

Those possessions were opportunities to win the game without another explosive play. Move the chains. Force Washington to spend time and timeouts. Give the defense a longer break. Even when an offense cannot score, it can change the opponent’s remaining options.

Instead, the Giants kept asking their defense for another stop.

The broader numbers make that failure more painful. New York outgained Washington 399–340 and averaged 6.2 yards per play to Washington’s 5.1. But the Giants converted just four of 12 third downs, compared with seven of 14 for the Commanders. Both teams committed two turnovers.

That distinction is the center of this recap. Producing yards and controlling a game are different accomplishments. New York generated enough offense to win, but too little of it arrived when another first down could have changed the finish.

J.J. McCarthy’s production and Likely’s big night give the Giants positives to carry forward. Cam Skattebo also supplied two rushing touchdowns. But New York’s final chance ended when Rasul Douglas intercepted McCarthy. The Giants cannot erase the earlier opportunities by reducing this loss to one final throw.

4DK PLAYER OF THE NIGHT: STEFON DIGGS. Likely had the larger receiving total, but Diggs delivered the play that decided the game.

Washington should celebrate this win without confusing it with proof that every problem is solved. The late turnovers remain a concern. What changed Thursday was the response: the Commanders found enough defensive resistance and offensive execution to survive them.

For the Giants, this is a finishing problem that demands an answer. A division leader has to convert favorable situations into victories, especially when its defense hands the offense repeated opportunities.

Washington left MetLife with another chance at its season. New York left knowing it had multiple chances to deny them.
