4DK NFL • WEEK 5 • MONDAY NIGHT FOOTBALL • OCTOBER 12, 2026

Allen Gets the Last Word. The Rams Run Out of Mistakes to Make.

Stafford and Allen traded late-game answers, Puka put on a receiving clinic, and a blocked overtime field goal nearly flipped the ending. Buffalo escaped with the win. Los Angeles left with a much bigger problem than one loss.

BUFFALO BILLS 37 — LOS ANGELES RAMS 34 (OT)

A Game That Refused to End Quietly

Some games deliver a great finish without telling us much about either team. This wasn't one of them. Seven lead changes in regulation and a frantic fourth quarter showed exactly why these quarterbacks command respect—and why both coaching staffs will have plenty to clean up. Buffalo led 13–7 at halftime, but that modest advantage offered almost no warning of the chaos ahead. The teams traded control throughout the second half until the Bills went ahead 34–27 with 1:39 left. Stafford answered with a touchdown drive in just 94 seconds. Overtime was the only reasonable conclusion to a game where neither offense wanted the last word to belong to the other.

Josh Allen: The Difference Between Flash and Finish

Allen accounted for 331 total yards and two touchdowns, but the raw production isn't what we'll remember first. It was the response. The Bills' offense had stretches when it looked uncomfortable and disjointed. When the game demanded a late answer, Allen created one. That's the distinction between putting up MVP-caliber numbers and showing MVP-caliber command. He didn't play a spotless game. He played a winning one, against a dangerous opponent, in a game that kept offering Buffalo opportunities to unravel.

For our contender evaluation, this is meaningful evidence, not a championship certificate. Buffalo showed it can take a punch from an explosive offense and still execute in the biggest moments. But the penalties, missed opportunities, and defensive concessions also showed why this team isn't entitled to a spot above the league's other heavyweights. Against the best teams in January, resilience matters; so does not needing to rescue yourself from avoidable mistakes.

Stafford and Puka: A Losing Performance Worth Respecting

Matthew Stafford threw for 383 yards and three touchdowns. Puka Nacua caught 17 passes for 138 yards and a touchdown. That's not a quarterback and receiver getting empty numbers while trailing by multiple scores; that's a pairing keeping a team alive against a legitimate AFC threat. Stafford's command late in regulation, especially with the clock working against him, was elite. Puka was the security blanket, the chain mover, and the matchup problem Buffalo never completely solved.

Seventeen catches tell us more than one highlight ever could. It means Los Angeles repeatedly trusted Nacua to win, and he repeatedly delivered. When we talk about the game's best receivers, this is the type of performance that belongs in the conversation. The frustrating part for the Rams is that their stars supplied enough to win. The operation around them did not.

James Cook's Night: Production and Possession

Cook rushed for 111 yards and two touchdowns. Those are winning numbers—until the turnovers enter the picture. His two fumbles, including the tense overtime moment, nearly turned a standout rushing night into the central reason Buffalo lost. One disputed fumble sequence also drew attention when an initial ruling favoring Los Angeles was changed. The takeaway isn't that Cook should suddenly be viewed as unreliable; it's that ball security is the price of admission in close postseason-style games.

Buffalo needs his burst and balance because it can't ask Allen to carry every down. But the Bills also need Cook to understand that a four-yard gain ending with possession intact can be more valuable than a spectacular run that puts the ball at risk. The two truths can coexist: Cook was productive, and the mistakes were unacceptable.

The Blocked Kick—and the Kick That Counted

Overtime supplied one more twist. Harrison Mevis lined up for a potential Rams winner, but Greg Rousseau blocked the attempt. A game Los Angeles seemed ready to steal flipped again in a heartbeat. Buffalo got its chance, and Tyler Bass finished the job with a 45-yard field goal. That's the difference between a heroic comeback and a painful near-miss. One special-teams snap became the hinge of the night.

We can't discuss the blocked kick as just a Buffalo highlight, either. For Los Angeles, it adds to the uncomfortable pattern of losing the margins. The Rams have enough talent to trade scores with almost anybody. They need to become the team that closes games when the window opens.

Too Many Flags, Too Many Self-Inflicted Problems

Both teams gave away rhythm with penalties, and Buffalo alone was flagged 13 times. Some erased positive plays; others extended drives that appeared over. One sequence saw Buffalo's touchdown celebration complicated by a failed two-point attempt, a false start, and a missed extra point. These weren't harmless technicalities. They affected field position, momentum, scoring opportunities, and the number of chances each opponent received.

At the highest level, a penalty is not merely lost yardage. It's a change in game script. When the quarterbacks are playing this well, giving either offense an extra down can be the difference between winning and losing. Both staffs have to address discipline before the calendar turns toward the division stretch.

Are the Rams in Real Trouble at 2–3?

Yes—in the standings, absolutely. Los Angeles is three games behind undefeated San Francisco after five weeks. That's a meaningful hole, especially in a division where head-to-head results and tiebreakers can determine the playoff picture. But we're not confusing record with capability. A team that gets this much from Stafford and Nacua is not automatically finished. The Rams can still threaten opponents on any Sunday.

The danger is the accumulation. Close losses still count as losses. Explosive offense doesn't erase poor situational execution. If Los Angeles keeps needing its quarterback to manufacture miracle drives while special teams and penalties give points away, the playoff path gets narrow in a hurry. The wild-card route may become more realistic than chasing the 49ers, but that's a path earned by stacking wins—not by collecting impressive losses.

Where Does Buffalo Stack Up With the Elite?

At 4–1, Buffalo belongs in the contender discussion. Allen is the kind of quarterback who can win a game that looks lost, and this victory offered fresh proof. The question isn't whether the Bills have the ceiling. It's whether their floor is stable enough to survive a postseason gauntlet. Kansas City, San Francisco, and the other top-tier teams will punish 13-penalty outings and loose football more severely than some regular-season opponents.

What we saw was a team with championship-level quarterback play and unfinished championship-level habits. That's a good place to be in October—as long as Buffalo treats the win as a warning as much as a celebration.

The 4DK Verdict

Buffalo proved it can finish. Los Angeles proved it can fight. Only one of those qualities moved the standings in the right direction.

Allen gets the last word, and he earned it. Stafford and Puka deserve their flowers. Cook has to protect the ball. Both teams need to clean up the flags. And for the Rams, the next game already carries extra weight. When you're 2–3 with an unbeaten division leader pulling away, the margin for another thrilling loss is practically gone.
