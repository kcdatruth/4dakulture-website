THE CLOSEST
HE GOT.

Denver was not supposed to be the West finalist. Then Melo elevated, Chauncey organized everything, and the Nuggets pushed Kobe and the Lakers harder than most expected.

01
THE SURPRISE

DENVER WAS NOT THE OBVIOUS TEAM TO COME OUT OF THE WEST.

The Lakers were the class of the conference. Denver reaching the Western Conference Finals at all felt like a breakthrough.

Melo had averaged 22.8 points in the regular season. In the playoffs he jumped to 27.2, exactly the type of elevation you want from a star when the games get heavier.

There was even a plausible alternate path if Houston had finished its seven-game upset bid against Los Angeles in the second round. That Rockets team pushed the Lakers without Tracy McGrady and eventually without Yao Ming. The West was not inevitable — but the Lakers survived it.

The regular season introduced the contender. The playoffs introduced playoff Melo.
02
THE RUN

CP3. DIRK. THEN KOBE.

Denver handled Chris Paul's Hornets in five games, then beat Dirk Nowitzki's Mavericks in five. By the time the Lakers series arrived, this was no longer a cute story.

For 4DK, 2009 is Carmelo's signature Denver playoff moment. It was the first postseason where the full version showed up: scoring, physicality, confidence, defense and real leadership.

2009 was Melo’s coming-out playoff run.
03
THE LAKERS

LOS ANGELES HAD TOO MANY LAYERS.

Kobe Bryant was still Kobe Bryant. Pau Gasol gave the Lakers size and skill. Lamar Odom gave them another big playmaker. Andrew Bynum added more size, and Trevor Ariza made winning plays while taking difficult defensive assignments.

Denver absolutely held its own. Outside the Game 6 blowout, the series was competitive, physical and full of possessions that could have swung the tone.

The late Ariza steal in Denver is one of the plays 4DK remembers as a series-changing moment. So is Melo's Game 1 duel with Kobe — an 'I'm here too' performance that felt like a respect-earning night.

Melo did not look out of place next to Kobe. He looked like he belonged on that stage.
04
WHAT IT PROVED

COULD MELO BE THE NO. 1 ON A CHAMPION?

4DK's answer is yes — but not by himself.

The 2009 run is the strongest evidence that Melo had enough scoring and star power to lead a championship-level offense. What Denver lacked was another prime co-star close to his age who could share the heaviest creation burden.

That is the difference between saying a player was incapable and saying the roster needed one more level. The 2009 Nuggets got close enough to make the second argument more convincing.

2009 was proof of concept: Carmelo Anthony could lead a contender.
