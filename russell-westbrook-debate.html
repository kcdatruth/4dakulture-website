Russell Westbrook Was Never Supposed to Be This Great

You either loved him or hated him. But after retiring in August, Russell Westbrook leaves as one of the greatest point guards of his era — flaws, fire, triple-doubles and all.
By Kcdatruth • 4 Da Kulture • September 7, 2026
4 Da KultureNBA Legacy
Russell Westbrook

Point God Debate

You either love him or hate him, but after retiring in August, Russell Westbrook will go down as one of the greatest point gods of his era.

Russ wasn’t a perfect player. He committed a lot of turnovers. He never developed a consistent jumper. He never won a ring. But don’t get it twisted — Russell Westbrook was a SUPERSTAR.

He might be one of the most critiqued NBA players ever, but you can’t deny the talent, the production or the accolades. And if you really think about his journey, Russ overachieved. That’s not an exaggeration.

Russ went from an afterthought to a top-five pick — then spent the next 18 seasons making people argue about him.

From UCLA Afterthought to No. 4 Pick

Rewind 20 years to 2006, Westbrook’s high school class. Casuals forget he entered UCLA as an afterthought in a crowded backcourt that featured future NBA vets Darren Collison and Arron Afflalo. He didn’t play a lot on that 2007 Final Four team, but he was a rotation piece. Those late-2000s Bruins teams were loaded, reaching three straight Final Fours from 2006 through 2008.

Going into his sophomore season, Russ was still an afterthought. Collison was still there. Freshman phenom Kevin Love had arrived. Luc Richard Mbah a Moute was an established upperclassman. Westbrook started that season on basically nobody’s draft board.

Then his game started standing out.

He didn’t put up superstar numbers — 12.7 points, 3.9 rebounds and 4.3 assists — but the more you watched him, the more you paid attention. The speed, the defense, the aggression and the athleticism jumped off the screen. His stock exploded after winning Pac-10 Defensive Player of the Year and another deep tournament run that ended in the Final Four against Derrick Rose and Memphis.

Westbrook went from afterthought to top-five pick in a year. Oklahoma City took him No. 4 in the 2008 Draft, one pick before Kevin Love, and plenty of critics thought the Thunder reached.

OKC Grew Up Fast

Alongside Kevin Durant — and James Harden in the early years — Russ just kept getting better. As a rookie, he established the dog mentality and athleticism that would define his career. There weren’t many 6-foot-3 point guards anybody would rather have coming downhill.

People forget how fast OKC became real. By Year 2, the Thunder were already in the playoffs with one of the youngest cores in the league. Russ and KD were 21. Two years later, at 23, Westbrook was starting in the NBA Finals.

He averaged 27.0 points, 6.6 assists and 6.4 rebounds in the 2012 Finals against LeBron James and Miami. The Thunder lost in five, and Russ never made it back to the Finals. But by then he had already reached elite point guard status.

The wild part? We still hadn’t seen his best.

2015: The Superstar Breakout

The true ascension into superstardom came in 2015. Oklahoma City didn’t even make the playoffs, but Kevin Durant was limited to 27 games by foot issues and Russ finally got an extended run showing the world what he could do as a true No. 1 option.

He averaged 28.1 points, 8.6 assists and 7.3 rebounds, won the scoring title and piled up 11 triple-doubles. This was Russ with the volume turned all the way up: 40-point nights, triple-doubles, talking shit, attacking the rim like the basket had personally offended him.

It’s also worth remembering what was happening around him. Golden State was ascending and Steph Curry was becoming Steph. If Curry doesn’t become what he became and OKC finds a way to win a ring before KD leaves in 2016, the way people talk about Russell Westbrook today is probably completely different.

2017: Fully Unleashed

Then came MVP Russ.

Fully unleashed. Full green light. No KD. No safety net.

Those numbers are still hard to fathom. He became the first player since Oscar Robertson to average a triple-double for a full season and broke the single-season triple-double record.

Then he proceeded to average a triple-double the next two seasons too, with Paul George alongside him. Something that once sounded impossible started feeling normal because Russ kept doing it.

Houston, Washington and One More Reminder

In 2019, after 11 years with the franchise, Oklahoma City sent Westbrook to Houston for another future Hall of Famer and fellow 2026 retiree Chris Paul.

Because of COVID, a lot of people only remember that 2019–20 Houston team for how it ended in the bubble. But the Russ-Harden partnership was putting up ridiculous numbers. While Harden was lapping the league in scoring at 34.3 points per game, Westbrook quietly averaged 27.2 points, 7.9 rebounds and 7.0 assists at age 31.

Then he went to Washington for a year and averaged a triple-double for the fourth time: 22.2 points, 11.5 rebounds and 11.7 assists. Doing that at 32 might be almost as impressive as the first one.

Don’t Let the Last Chapter Erase the Book

A lot of casuals mostly remember post-COVID Russ now: the underwhelming Lakers tenure, the fallout around the LeBron pairing, the Clippers run that let him stay home, the Denver sixth-man year, and a final season in Sacramento that barely felt like part of the national conversation.

But that version of Russ shouldn’t erase what came before it.

Russell Westbrook was a SUPERSTAR.

If you isolate just his Oklahoma City years, there’s a real debate about how his résumé stacks up against almost any point guard in NBA history. He never cheated the game. He gave us posters. He gave us triple-doubles. He gave us chaos. And every single night, he gave us everything he had.

So Where Does Russ Really Rank?

Westbrook finished his career averaging 20.9 points, 8.0 assists and 6.9 rebounds. Maybe it’s time to stop calling him an afterthought or even just an overachiever.

He’s an NBA Top 75 player. He’s a future Hall of Famer. He’s a top-10 point guard all time.

And depending on how you value peak, production, pressure and pure impact — maybe even top five. 💯💯
