4DK NBA • 2026–27 Division Outlook

THE PACIFIC IS ABOUT WHAT COMES NEXT.

Luka owns Los Angeles now. Phoenix is one bad first half away from a franchise decision. Golden State rides out the Steph era. The Clippers and Kings are both staring at transition years for completely different reasons.

By Kcdatruth • 4 Da Kulture • September 2026

The Pacific has one team beginning a new superstar era, two teams trying to squeeze value out of the present, and two others deciding how aggressively to pivot toward the future. More than any other division, 2026–27 could change the direction of multiple franchises before the trade deadline.

LOS ANGELES LAKERS

THIS IS LUKA'S TEAM NOW.

50 wins • Top-5 West • Deep-run upside

The Lakers are not rebuilding after LeBron. This is a full Luka Doncic takeover. If Luka stays healthy, the expectation is an MVP-level season — something in the neighborhood of 33 points, 9 rebounds and 8 assists while controlling everything the Lakers do offensively.

Austin Reaves is the clear No. 2. If he stays healthy, around 24 points, 6 rebounds and 5 assists is realistic, and that means the Lakers' offense should be fine even without LeBron. The front office reshaped the supporting cast instead of trying to replace LeBron with one player: Walker Kessler to anchor the defense, Collin Sexton as a microwave scorer, Quentin Grimes as a 3-and-D swingman, Sandro Mamukelashvili as a skilled big after a career year in Toronto, and first-rounder Cameron Carr as another developmental piece.

The Achilles heel is defense. Losing Marcus Smart matters because his two-way impact helped Los Angeles get through the first round last season. Grimes is good but not elite yet. Matisse Thybulle has an All-Defense résumé but has not looked like that version in years. Jarred Vanderbilt can still be elite against bigs, guards and wings, but his offensive limitations can become a playoff problem.

If the Lakers win 50 games, finish top five and get to the second round, that is a strong first full season of the Luka era. If they get to 55 wins and a top-three seed while Luka's numbers hold, he should be right in the MVP race. With the right matchup, a nuclear Luka series and just enough defense, this team can go much deeper.

Full Luka takeover. MVP expectations. Elite offense. Defense decides how far they go.

LOS ANGELES CLIPPERS

THE KAWHI ERA IS OVER. NOW WHAT?

Retool • Evaluate the veterans • Find the next direction

Kawhi Leonard's move back to Toronto closed the book on the Clippers' previous identity. That does not mean they have to tear everything down immediately, but it does mean 2026–27 should be treated as a transition season instead of pretending the same championship window is still open.

The priority should be clarity. Which veterans still make sense beyond this season? Which contracts can be turned into younger players or draft value? And which players on the current roster can actually carry larger roles once the old hierarchy is gone?

If the Clippers stay competitive, fine. But the standings should not force them into short-term moves that delay the inevitable. This year is about leaving the season with a cleaner timeline, more flexibility and a better idea of who belongs in the next version of the franchise.

The Clippers do not need to replace Kawhi in one move. They need to decide what the post-Kawhi franchise is supposed to be.

PHOENIX SUNS

COMPETE NOW. THINK ABOUT THE FUTURE.

44 wins • Playoffs • Deadline crossroads

Phoenix still has enough talent to make the playoffs. Devin Booker gives the Suns a real offensive floor, and if Jalen Green stays healthy and gets over 20 points per game while adding 5–6 assists, he can become the secondary creator Booker needs next to him.

The Suns also need last season's versions of Dillon Brooks and Gillespie. Miles Bridges gives them another combo forward who can score in bunches, while Luke Kennard gives them elite shooting off the bench. That is enough to stay competitive in the West, but it does not give Phoenix the top-end talent of a true contender.

That is why the deadline matters. If Phoenix is at or below .500, the front office should seriously consider what Booker can bring back in young talent and draft capital rather than forcing another low-seed playoff chase. The goal is 44 wins and the postseason, but the bigger priority is not getting trapped in the middle.

Make the playoffs if you can — but do not sacrifice the future just to stay mediocre.

SACRAMENTO KINGS

THE REAL STORY IS THE EXIT PLAN.

Sabonis / LaVine decisions • Acuff development • Asset recovery

Sacramento's season is less about chasing a specific seed and more about when the roster finally turns the page. The two biggest veteran questions are Domantas Sabonis and Zach LaVine: when do they get moved, where do they go, and how much future value can the Kings get back?

The development story is Acuff. Sacramento needs to give him enough responsibility to find out what kind of lead guard or creator he can become while the front office clears space around the younger timeline. Every veteran move should be judged by whether it creates picks, flexibility or cleaner opportunities for the players the Kings actually want to build with.

This is a transition year. If Sacramento can move at least one major veteran for real value and Acuff leaves the season looking like a legitimate long-term building block, the year will have done its job regardless of the final record.

For Sacramento, 2026–27 is about turning veterans into future value and finding out how real Acuff is.

GOLDEN STATE WARRIORS

ONE MORE RIDE WITH STEPH.

45 wins • Playoffs are the goal

Golden State is riding the rest of the Steph Curry wave out. At this stage, a playoff berth is the goal and anything beyond that is a bonus. Steph should still give them around 28 a night, so the real question is who comes with him.

Kristaps Porzingis staying healthy would change the ceiling, but there is no reason to trust that happening over a full season. Jimmy Butler returning is the bigger swing factor. If Jimmy comes back and looks like himself, Golden State has a real path into the Play-In or even the 7-seed range.

De'Anthony Melton and Brandin Podziemski already score in double figures, and one or both need to raise that production. Williams and Richard are other options to absorb a bigger scoring load. The Warriors do not need another superstar season from somebody else; they need enough secondary offense that Steph is not carrying every drought by himself.

The season is not about proving Steph can still play. It is about giving him one more meaningful postseason shot while he still can.

45 wins. Make the playoffs. Let Steph have one more real run.
