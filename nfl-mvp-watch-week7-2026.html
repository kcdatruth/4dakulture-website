01

Josh Allen

Buffalo Bills • 5–1
PREVIOUS #1 • — HOLD
THE THRONE IS STILL HIS

Buffalo had a bye, so Allen had no Week 7 box score to pad his résumé. That does not change the standard we have used from the beginning: no player on this board is asked to create more of his offense in more ways. Allen can win a game with designed quarterback runs, extend a dead play, attack downfield or carry the offense through a drive when protection fails. That flexibility is not a bonus feature. It is the center of Buffalo's identity.

The Bills are 5–1 and return from the bye with a chance to turn an excellent start into separation in the AFC. We are not moving Allen simply because another candidate had a loud Sunday. Someone has to match his production, his pressure responsibility and his ability to dictate how a defense plays. Until then, No. 1 stays where it is.

02

Kenneth Walker III

Kansas City Chiefs • 5–1
PREVIOUS #2 • — HOLD
THE TOUGHEST WEEK YET

Walker remains No. 2, but Seattle gave his MVP case a serious test. Fourteen carries produced just 36 yards, and the Chiefs never found the rhythm that has made their rushing game a defining weapon. His four catches for 21 yards could not rescue a three-point night. We have to call that performance what it was: a setback.

The reason he stays here is the complete body of work and the way Kansas City's offense has been built around his threat. A running back competing with elite quarterbacks cannot afford many quiet games, especially when the opponent knows the run is coming. Walker's response will tell us whether this was a one-week shutdown or a new problem the Chiefs must solve.

03

Brock Purdy

San Francisco 49ers • 6–1
PREVIOUS #3 • — HOLD
THE FIRST REAL ADVERSITY

Purdy's 27-of-40, 288-yard afternoon in Atlanta included a touchdown and two interceptions. The 49ers committed four turnovers and lost their first game. We cannot overlook those mistakes, but we also cannot pretend he had a full receiving corps or a healthy Christian McCaffrey for the entire afternoon. San Francisco has asked Purdy to keep the offense functioning through injuries, and six wins remain part of the evaluation.

He holds third because one difficult Sunday should not wipe out the previous six. Still, the MVP argument changes when a defense shows it can force you into mistakes. The bye offers time for McCaffrey and several receivers to recover. Purdy's next assignment is to show the offense can regain its precision without depending on perfect conditions.

04

Jaxon Smith-Njigba

Seattle Seahawks • 4–3
PREVIOUS #10 • ▲ +6
THE BIGGEST RISER — UP SIX

Nine catches, 150 yards and a touchdown against the previously unbeaten Chiefs. Those numbers are loud enough, but the context makes them louder. Kansas City knew Seattle wanted to feed JSN and still could not take him away. His route craft, spacing and ability to win at different levels allowed Sam Darnold to stay on schedule while Seattle's defense handled the other side of the game.

We move him from No. 10 to No. 4 because this is what true game-changing receiving value looks like. Wide receivers have to overcome the quarterback bias in any MVP discussion, and a 4–3 team has to keep winning for that case to grow. But a player who bends coverage before the snap and delivers against elite competition belongs near the top. We are giving JSN his flowers.

05

Jared Goff

Detroit Lions • 4–2
PREVIOUS #6 • ▲ +1
DETROIT HAS TWO MVP ENGINES

Goff delivered 303 passing yards and two touchdowns on 22-of-32 passing against Green Bay. Detroit needed every bit of that command in a 20–17 divisional game. Amon-Ra St. Brown caught nine balls for 153 yards, Jameson Williams scored twice and Goff repeatedly found the right matchup. This was not just an offense piling up easy yards; it was a quarterback controlling the details of a game that remained close until the final kick.

We move him into the top five because the Lions' offense has regained its balance and his decision-making makes it work. The argument against him will always be the strength of the system and the playmakers. Our counter is that timing, accuracy and pre-snap command are skills, not freebies. Keep winning and the debate gets more serious.

06

Trevor Lawrence

Jacksonville Jaguars • 4–2
PREVIOUS #5 • ▼ -1
THE BYE WEEK HOLD

Lawrence falls one spot without playing in Week 7. Jacksonville entered the break at 4–2, coming off a 34–31 loss to Houston that showed both the ceiling and the inconsistency. He has the arm talent to stretch a defense and the mobility to solve a broken play, but our MVP Watch is about turning those tools into weekly control, not admiring what a quarterback could become.

The Jaguars' scoring differential and four wins keep Lawrence in the conversation. We want to see the offense return from the bye sharper in the red zone and more dependable late in games. This is a crowded field. A quiet week can cost a spot when players like JSN and Goff make statement performances.

07

Patrick Mahomes

Kansas City Chiefs • 5–1
PREVIOUS #4 • ▼ -3
NO REPUTATION VOTES

Mahomes drops three places because Seattle handed Kansas City the kind of offensive failure an MVP candidate cannot ignore. He completed 19 of 33 passes for 165 yards, no touchdowns and an interception. The Chiefs scored three points. Walker was bottled up, the timing of the passing game broke down, and Kansas City did not reach the end zone.

We still believe Mahomes can win this award. The point is that this board rewards the 2026 season, not career accomplishments. Rashee Rice's absence mattered, and a road trip to Seattle is a difficult assignment, but neither explains away every stalled drive. Mahomes now has the clearest bounce-back challenge on the list: prove the Chiefs can score when the run is taken away.

08

Caleb Williams

Chicago Bears • 5–2
PREVIOUS UNRANKED • NEW ENTRY
NEW ENTRY — A NEW LEVEL OF COMMAND

Caleb is in, and he earned it on the field. Against New England he completed 22 of 33 passes for 308 yards, two touchdowns and no interceptions in a 30–24 victory. It was his second straight 300-yard game since returning from a hamstring injury, and he did it against a Patriots team that had been winning. Luther Burden's 130 receiving yards gave him an explosive partner, but Williams still had to make the reads and deliver the ball.

The talent was never the question. The difference now is a more repeatable command of the offense: protecting possessions, trusting the structure and still creating outside it when needed. Chicago is 5–2. We are not crowning him yet, but we are not waiting for some imaginary future version of Caleb to acknowledge the one showing up now.

09

Bryce Young

Carolina Panthers • 3–3
PREVIOUS #8 • ▼ -1
PROGRESS DOES NOT ERASE A BAD SUNDAY

We keep Bryce inside the ten, but the Tampa Bay game demands accountability. He went 14-of-27 for 163 yards, one touchdown and two interceptions, and the Buccaneers sacked him five times in a 43–13 loss. Carolina's protection collapsed, and the offense repeatedly put its defense in bad positions. That was not an MVP-caliber Sunday, even allowing for the surrounding problems.

Why keep him ninth? Because we have watched a broader progression in his decisiveness, passing volume and ability to elevate a developing offense. One ugly game should not erase that work. But the cushion is thin now. With tackle Taylor Moton injured, Young's next stretch will test whether his growth can survive difficult conditions. He needs a response, and he needs it quickly.

10

Jahmyr Gibbs

Detroit Lions • 4–2
PREVIOUS #9 • ▼ -1
THE OTHER ENGINE IN DETROIT

Gibbs did the work that makes a defense respect every fake and every motion. He carried 29 times for 116 yards and added five catches for 27 more in the win over Green Bay. He did not score a touchdown, but 143 scrimmage yards in a tight division game is not empty production. Detroit can ask him to grind out four yards, split wide or threaten the edge, and those possibilities change the math for every defender.

We move him down one spot only because the competition above him made bigger weekly statements. Sharing the MVP Watch with Goff is not a contradiction; it is an acknowledgment that Detroit has two engines. Gibbs' path higher is clear: more explosive scoring performances and more wins where he is unmistakably the decisive player.

JUST OUTSIDE THE TOP TEN: Drake Maye's 321 passing yards and three total touchdowns in a loss to Chicago keep him close; this is a ranking squeeze, not a dismissal of his play. Joe Burrow's four-touchdown second half against Baltimore and Dak Prescott's three-touchdown Monday night performance are genuine new challenges to our board. Lamar Jackson remains outside the ten after missing three games with an ankle injury; his Week 8 return was not confirmed as of this edition.
