#1

Josh Allen

Buffalo Bills
PREVIOUS: #1 · HOLD

HOLDING THE THRONE

The case starts with responsibility. Buffalo asks Allen to manufacture answers when the pocket breaks down, when a drive needs four yards and when the defense cannot afford another possession. He has been a dual-threat scoring engine rather than a quarterback collecting empty passing totals. Through six games entering Week 7, his verified baseline was 1,504 passing yards, nine passing touchdowns, 240 rushing yards and eight rushing scores. Those are not Week 7 final totals. The larger argument is that the Bills can change their offensive identity from series to series because Allen makes both approaches credible. Our standard for removing him from No. 1 is high: another contender must match his individual production and his ability to determine games on multiple levels.

#2

Kenneth Walker III

Kansas City Chiefs
PREVIOUS: #2 · HOLD

THE RUNNING BACK CASE

Walker stays second because one difficult afternoon does not erase the foundation of an MVP campaign. Seattle held him to 36 rushing yards on 14 carries in Week 7; he added four catches for 21 yards. That is a real setback, not something we should dress up. But the game also showed how different Kansas City looks when opponents take away the rushing threat that has transformed its offense. Running backs rarely get serious MVP consideration without exceptional production and team impact. Walker has to continue showing both, especially when opponents build the entire front around stopping him. He retains No. 2 on the strength of the body of work, with less margin for another quiet week.

#3

Brock Purdy

San Francisco 49ers
PREVIOUS: #3 · HOLD

THE RESPONSE TEST

Purdy keeps third after San Francisco’s first loss, but the conversation has changed. Against Atlanta, he completed 27 of 40 passes for 288 yards, one touchdown and two interceptions. The turnovers matter because Atlanta turned disruption into the kind of game that denied the 49ers their usual rhythm. Still, one defeat should not rewrite six weeks of precision, decision-making and command of a contender. The question now is how Purdy answers when defenses force him into long-yardage situations and take away the easy answers. An MVP quarterback has to be more than efficient in wins; he has to stabilize the offense when a game turns against him. That response will determine whether he challenges Allen and Walker again.

#4

Jaxon Smith-Njigba

Seattle Seahawks
PREVIOUS: #10 · ▲ UP 6

THE BIGGEST RISER — UP SIX

No player changed the conversation more emphatically. Smith-Njigba moves from tenth to fourth because his impact has become impossible to treat as a side story. Against Kansas City, he delivered nine receptions, 150 yards and a touchdown in a 20–3 statement victory. The production is only half the story. Seattle can move him around formations, trust his timing and route craft, and keep feeding him despite defenses knowing where the ball wants to go. A wide receiver has a steeper MVP climb than a quarterback, but a player who dictates coverage and still dominates deserves a place near the front of our board. This is not a token receiver selection. JSN is one of the defining players of Seattle’s rise.

#5

Jared Goff

Detroit Lions
PREVIOUS: #6 · ▲ UP 1

THE COMMANDER MOVES INTO THE TOP FIVE

Goff climbs into the top five after delivering another composed performance in Detroit’s 20–17 win over Green Bay. His Week 7 line: 22 completions on 32 attempts, 303 passing yards and two touchdowns. What stands out is how comfortably Detroit can operate through his decisions, even when the opponent refuses to give away explosive plays. Goff has a system, but he is also the person making that system function at full speed: diagnosing leverage, distributing to the right matchup and keeping the offense out of panic mode. His MVP case depends on sustaining that command against elite defenses and producing the signature wins that separate a very good season from an award-winning one.

#6

Trevor Lawrence

Jacksonville Jaguars
PREVIOUS: #5 · ▼ DOWN 1

THE JAGUARS’ CEILING

Lawrence slides one spot without losing his place among the contenders. Jacksonville’s trajectory is tied to his ability to turn athletic tools into consistent winning football. At his best, he can stretch a defense vertically, punish pressure with movement and keep a drive alive after the original play has disappeared. The next step is consistency: avoiding the handful of forced decisions that can undo otherwise impressive work. We are measuring the entire campaign, not simply rewarding whichever quarterback had the loudest box score on Sunday. Lawrence remains a top-six MVP candidate because the Jaguars have a genuine ceiling with him, but the players ahead of him have made more compelling recent statements.

#7

Patrick Mahomes

Kansas City Chiefs
PREVIOUS: #4 · ▼ DOWN 3

THE STANDARD DOES NOT EXEMPT HIM

Mahomes drops from fourth to seventh. Seattle’s 20–3 victory exposed an offense that never found a comfortable rhythm; Mahomes finished 19 of 33 for 165 yards, no touchdowns and one interception. That is a significant strike against his weekly case, even with his established reputation. Our MVP Watch is about this season, not a lifetime achievement award. Kansas City now has to prove it can function when Walker is bottled up and opposing defenses force Mahomes to solve everything through the air. The talent and track record remain unquestioned, but a top-five ranking has to be earned in the present. The path back is obvious: respond against quality opponents and reestablish offensive control.

#8

Caleb Williams

Chicago Bears
PREVIOUS: UNRANKED · NEW ENTRY

NEW ENTRY — CHICAGO’S SPARK

Williams enters our top ten at eighth, and the ranking is about growing command as much as highlight plays. Chicago needs its quarterback to turn individual creativity into dependable offense: set protections, recognize pressure, protect possessions and deliver when a defense knows a pass is coming. The flashes of arm talent have always been part of his appeal. What makes an MVP candidacy possible is pairing those flashes with winning structure and repeatable execution. We are not presenting him as a finished product or inventing a Week 7 statistical line. We are recognizing that his influence on Chicago’s direction now deserves a place in the conversation. To rise further, he must stack complete games against the strongest defenses on the schedule.

#9

Bryce Young

Carolina Panthers
PREVIOUS: #8 · ▼ DOWN 1

RESPECT THE GROWTH

Young moves down one place because of movement above him, not because we are abandoning the progress that put him on the board. The most important development in Carolina is his growing willingness to play decisively instead of waiting for a perfect picture. Quarterback play is often about solving a defense before the ball is snapped and then staying calm when the picture changes. Young’s value is magnified by the responsibility of lifting a developing roster. To push back toward the top five, he needs sustained efficiency, scoring production and wins that carry his fingerprints. He remains firmly in our ten because we are evaluating the quarterback’s effect on his team, not just reputation.

#10

Jahmyr Gibbs

Detroit Lions
PREVIOUS: #9 · ▼ DOWN 1

THE OTHER ENGINE IN DETROIT

Gibbs slides to tenth in a crowded field, but his offensive value remains enormous. In Detroit’s Week 7 win over Green Bay, he carried 29 times for 116 yards and caught five passes for 27 more. The lack of a touchdown should not obscure how much of the workload he absorbed. His combination of speed, receiving ability and space creation forces defenses to account for multiple threats on every snap. That helps Goff, too, which is why both Lions can appear on this list without contradiction. The challenge for Gibbs is the same one facing any running back in an MVP race: build a season so consistently explosive that voters cannot default to quarterback production alone.
