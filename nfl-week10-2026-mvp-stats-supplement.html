4DK EDITORIAL READ

Burrow's case: Twenty-three passing touchdowns make the Bengals quarterback a major production benchmark in this race. The touchdown total cannot be separated from Cincinnati's record or its AFC North fight.

Love's case: The passing-yardage leader has accumulated 2,618 yards. Green Bay's 6–3 record and uneven week-to-week results belong in the analysis alongside the volume.

JSN's case: A receiver with 71 catches, 1,036 yards and nine touchdowns after nine games is no ceremonial addition to an MVP list. The Seahawks' 5–4 record is the counterargument; the individual workload is the response.

Cook's case: Buffalo's 7–1 record rests on more than quarterback production. Cook's 814 yards and eight rushing scores make the running game a substantive part of the Bills' offensive identity.

Allen's complete case: Nine rushing touchdowns add an entirely different dimension to his passing line. Any MVP comparison that leaves out 285 rushing yards understates the workload.
