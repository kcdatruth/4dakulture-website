SAN FRANCISCO 49ERS • 4–0 • AT SEATTLE

The 49ers Have Looked Like Football's Best Team. Now They Have To Prove It Travels.

Four games in, San Francisco has done just about everything we needed to see from a legitimate championship team. The 49ers are 4–0, the defense has been able to win stretches of games on its own, the offensive line has been one of the stabilizing forces of the entire operation, and Brock Purdy has played himself directly into the early MVP conversation.

Purdy enters Week 5 with 1,007 passing yards, 11 touchdown passes and only one interception, while adding 122 yards and another touchdown on the ground. The numbers are elite. More importantly, the tape has looked elite. Purdy is controlling the offense, pushing the ball downfield when it is there and getting San Francisco into the right answers without turning every possession into a high-wire act.

But for us, this week is bigger than Purdy's stat line.

The question hanging over San Francisco for years has been health. The talent has rarely been the problem. The issue has been whether enough of that talent can stay on the field together long enough to make another complete run through January. That is why every week the Niners get through without another major loss matters. It is also why the way Kyle Shanahan manages workloads, the offensive line and the defensive rotation could matter as much in December as anything San Francisco does in October.

Now the schedule is giving them the perfect measuring stick. Seattle is 3–1, the defending Super Bowl champion and the team that ended San Francisco's previous season with a 41–6 playoff beating in Seattle. This is not another early-season home game where the Niners can simply lean on familiarity. This is Lumen Field, against a championship defense, inside the division.

That makes the offensive line one of the biggest parts of the game. If San Francisco can protect Purdy, keep the run game efficient and prevent Seattle from creating the type of chaos that can flip a road divisional game, the Niners can force the Seahawks to play on San Francisco's terms. On the other side, the defense has to prove its early dominance can hold up against a team that already knows what championship football looks like.

And then there is the bigger NFC West picture. The Niners are 4–0. Seattle is 3–1. The Rams are 2–2 but still look like a team nobody is going to want to see in January. We view all three as having legitimate Super Bowl ceilings. That means every divisional result carries extra weight.

A San Francisco win would not decide the West in October. But getting to 5–0 with a road win over the defending champs would create separation, strengthen Purdy's MVP résumé and give the 49ers an early head-to-head advantage in what may be the toughest division race in football.

4DK TAKE

The biggest question is not whether San Francisco is talented enough. We already know the answer. It is whether this roster can stay healthy long enough to sustain what it has started — and whether its defense and offensive line can impose themselves on the road against the champs.

BUFFALO BILLS • 3–1 • AT LOS ANGELES RAMS

Buffalo Does Not Need To Panic. It Does Need Its A-Game In Los Angeles.

We're not treating Buffalo's first loss like some warning siren that changes everything we thought about the Bills. Division games are different. Buffalo and New England know each other, New England beat Buffalo last season, and sometimes familiarity creates the type of game that does not fit the broader story of either team.

Still, the 29–26 loss gave Buffalo something real to clean up. The Bills allowed 405 total yards, had trouble getting off the field at key moments and lost D.J. Moore during the game after he aggravated the shoulder that has already bothered him this season. That is enough to make the Rams matchup more than just another Monday night game.

Los Angeles is coming off a 24–20 win in Philadelphia, and the most impressive part may have been what happened on defense. The Rams held the Eagles to 0-for-12 on third down. That is the type of situational performance that travels, and now that defense gets a completely different challenge in Josh Allen, James Cook and Buffalo's offense.

Allen changes every defense because the play is never truly dead when the structure breaks down. Cook changes it because Buffalo does not have to ask its quarterback to be the entire rushing attack. When those two are functioning together, defenses have to defend the full width of the field, the quarterback run game and explosive throws outside the original design.

That makes the Rams' third-down success in Philadelphia a fascinating setup rather than a guarantee. Jalen Hurts and Philadelphia presented one set of problems. Allen presents another. If Los Angeles can get Buffalo behind the chains and force Allen to live in obvious passing situations, Chris Shula's defense has a chance to make the night uncomfortable.

Buffalo's defense has the equally difficult assignment. Puka Nacua, Davante Adams and Kyren Williams can stress a defense in completely different ways. Puka can win through volume, strength and timing. Adams still understands leverage and route detail at an elite level. Kyren gives Los Angeles enough run-game credibility that Buffalo cannot simply spread out and chase receivers all night.

And D.J. Moore's availability matters. Buffalo already got a glimpse of what its offense looks like when its top receiving option disappears in the middle of a game. If Moore cannot go — or is limited — the Rams will have even more freedom to decide how aggressively they want to attack Allen.

This is why we see Monday night as a contender check for both teams. Buffalo gets a chance to show one divisional loss was exactly that: one divisional loss. Los Angeles gets a chance to follow its road win in Philadelphia by beating another team with championship expectations.

4DK TAKE

The Patriots loss does not change our opinion of Buffalo by itself. What matters now is the response. L.A. has the weapons and the defense to punish a team that shows up anything less than sharp.

LAS VEGAS RAIDERS • 3–1 • AT NEW ENGLAND

No More Mendoza Watch. The Raiders Have A Playoff Standard To Live Up To Now.

The Raiders entered this season with a much different type of conversation around them. We expected the quarterback situation to eventually turn toward Fernando Mendoza and the future. Four weeks later, that conversation has almost completely disappeared.

Las Vegas is 3–1. Kirk Cousins has stabilized the offense. Klint Kubiak has the Raiders playing with structure, balance and confidence. And even in the first loss of the season, there was more to like than dislike.

Kansas City escaped Las Vegas with a 30–27 win, but the Raiders finished with 437 total yards and held the ball for more than 35 minutes. The offense did not look like a unit simply trying to survive against one of the AFC's standards. It looked like an offense capable of dictating stretches of the game.

Ashton Jeanty was one of the biggest reasons. His 126 scrimmage yards against Kansas City may have been the most complete glimpse yet of what he can become inside this offense. He was useful on the ground, dangerous as a receiver and gave Cousins another way to punish a defense without forcing the ball outside.

Brock Bowers has also immediately changed the shape of the passing game since returning after missing the first two games. He gives Cousins a middle-of-the-field mismatch and a target who can create after the catch. Tre Tucker, Mike Washington Jr., Jalen Nailor and Cody White have all given Kubiak enough production around him that Vegas is not dependent on one player winning every possession.

That depth is a big reason the Raiders have scored at least 26 points in all four games. This is not an offense living off one hot afternoon.

But expectations change when a team starts 3–1.

Going to New England is no longer the kind of game where we are satisfied if the Raiders compete. The Patriots are 2–2 and just beat Buffalo. They are good enough to punish mistakes, they are at home and they have confidence after taking down one of the AFC favorites. That is exactly why this has to be viewed as a must-win if Vegas wants to be taken seriously as a playoff team.

The Chiefs game showed the Raiders can stand across from an elite opponent and belong. The Patriots game has to show they can handle business after the emotional high of proving that point.

And if they do? Then Cousins is no longer a temporary bridge we are waiting to move past. He is simply the quarterback of a good football team trying to get into the postseason.

4DK TAKE

Vegas has moved past the “interesting start” phase. A playoff contender has to win games like this on the road. The Chiefs loss raised our opinion of the Raiders; New England can validate it.

JAGUARS • BEARS • BROWNS • GIANTS • EARLY SURPRISES

Which Fast Starts Are Built To Last? Jacksonville Has Our Trust First.

By the time an NFL season reaches the quarter mark, we have enough football to stop treating every unexpected record like a fluke — but not enough to assume every September story is going to survive November and December.

Of the teams that have surprised people early, Jacksonville is the one we trust most.

The Jaguars are 3–1, Trevor Lawrence has played good football and the roster looks balanced enough that Jacksonville does not need one side of the ball to carry the entire team every Sunday. They just went into Cincinnati and won 22–17, which matters because it showed they can win a physical game away from home against a team with proven offensive firepower.

There is also a level of familiarity here that matters. Jacksonville is not discovering what playoff-level football feels like for the first time. The Jaguars have enough players who have been through meaningful games to make a 3–1 start feel more sustainable than surprising.

Chicago is the other team we are watching closely. The Bears are also 3–1 despite Caleb Williams missing the last two games with a hamstring injury. That is important because it tells us the defense, running game and quarterback depth can keep the season afloat while the franchise quarterback heals.

If Caleb comes back at 100 percent and the defense keeps playing the way it has, Chicago can absolutely make the NFC North a real race. The goal should not be to rush him back just because Green Bay is on the schedule. The goal should be to make sure the version of Caleb that returns is capable of carrying the offense for the rest of the year.

Cleveland and New York are probably the bigger surprises.

The Browns are 3–1 and have already shown a defense capable of controlling games. The Giants are 3–1 and sitting on top of the NFC East after another late defensive play sealed their Week 4 win over Arizona. Those wins count. We are not taking them away.

But those two teams are still in the “show us more” stage. As the schedule gets harder, opponents build more film and the margin between a good start and a good season gets thinner. We want to see how Cleveland and New York respond when they have to beat higher-level opponents instead of simply protecting the momentum they already built.

That is the line heading into Week 5: Jacksonville looks sustainable. Chicago has real division upside if Caleb gets healthy. Cleveland and the Giants have earned respect — and now they have to keep earning it.

4DK TAKE

Jacksonville is our safest buy among the early surprises because the Jaguars look balanced and Lawrence is playing winning football. Chicago may have the most upside if Caleb comes back healthy.

LAMAR • CHASE • MOORE • BARKLEY • WEEK 5 INJURY TEST

The Injuries Are Piling Up. The Good News Is There Is Still Time To Survive Them.

Injuries are always part of an NFL season. Every contender eventually gets tested by them. The difference in early October is that there is still time.

A team can lose a star for a week or two now, change the plan, survive a game and still have months to get healthy before the postseason. That does not make the injuries less important. It just means the season is not automatically doomed because a key player misses time in Week 5.

Baltimore is the clearest example. Lamar Jackson suffered an ankle injury in the Ravens' Week 4 win and did not practice Wednesday. The early reporting suggests the injury is not considered long-term or surgical, but he may miss time.

That is exactly where the line is for Baltimore. The Ravens can survive a short absence. They cannot afford an extended one. Lamar is not just their quarterback. His running ability changes the math of every defensive front, his improvisation creates explosives that are not available to most quarterbacks, and the entire offense is built around what defenses have to respect when he is on the field.

Cincinnati is facing its own version of that problem. Ja'Marr Chase left the Jacksonville game with a concussion, while Tee Higgins also went down late. Joe Burrow nearly dragged the Bengals back anyway, but an offense built around the Burrow-Chase connection is fundamentally different when Chase is not available.

Buffalo just experienced that in real time. D.J. Moore aggravated his shoulder in the loss to New England after already dealing with the issue earlier this season. The Bills still moved the football, but losing the No. 1 receiving option in the middle of a divisional game changes how the defense can allocate coverage and how aggressively the offense can attack outside.

Philadelphia is watching Saquon Barkley's hamstring situation as well. Around the league, the list is getting longer.

This is why we keep coming back to roster depth when we talk about contenders. Everybody looks dangerous when the top 22 are healthy. The teams that survive a season are the ones that can lose a starter, adjust the workload, trust the next player and avoid turning a two-week injury into a two-month collapse.

So no, we are not ready to say injuries have wrecked anybody's season yet. We are saying the injury test has officially begun. The next month will tell us which teams were actually built to withstand it.

4DK TAKE

The timing is the one positive. These injuries are arriving early enough for teams to recover. But Baltimore cannot lose Lamar for long, Cincinnati needs Chase, and Buffalo has already seen how different the offense looks when its No. 1 receiver goes down.

San Francisco has spent four weeks looking like the standard. Now it has to take that level into Seattle and beat the defending champion on the road. Vegas has moved from rebuilding conversation to playoff expectations faster than anybody anticipated. Jacksonville, Chicago, Cleveland and New York are no longer just nice starts on a standings page. And injuries are beginning to test whether the league's best teams are as deep as they looked in September.

Week 5 is where some of these stories start becoming identities.
