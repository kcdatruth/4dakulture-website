4DK / PLAYER OF THE NIGHT

STEPHEN CURRY.
THE IMPOSSIBLE SWING.

31 POINTS · 8 THREES · 6 ASSISTS · 25-POINT COMEBACK · WARRIORS 112, LAKERS 95

Some nights the best stat line is not the biggest one. Giannis Antetokounmpo posted 31 points, 17 rebounds and ten assists. Jamal Murray scored 39. Scottie Barnes delivered 41 in a loss. Kyrie Irving poured in 35 and won a late-game duel. All belong in the conversation. We give the night to Curry because the scale of Golden State's reversal was different: down 25 to the Lakers, tied by halftime and ahead by 17 when the final buzzer sounded.

Curry made eight threes, moved past Dominique Wilkins into 18th place on the NBA's career scoring list and changed the emotional center of the game after Luka Dončić's first-quarter explosion. This award is not simply for one crossover or a barrage of jumpers. It is for the leadership and gravity that helped Golden State stop playing like a team waiting for its next mistake. The Warriors have won three straight after an 0–3 start. Friday was their loudest answer yet.

4DK podium: Giannis Antetokounmpo's 31/17/10; Kyrie Irving's 35 in Dallas' win over unbeaten Houston; Jamal Murray's 39 and seven threes against Phoenix.

GAME 01 / NBA CUP

WASHINGTON TURNS CHARLOTTE'S OFFENSE INTO A WARNING SIGN.

WASHINGTON WIZARDS 95 — CHARLOTTE HORNETS 79
Washington 3–3 · Charlotte 3–3
Anthony Davis (WSH): 25 PTS, 13 REB · Trae Young: 21 PTS, 9 AST, 7 REB · AJ Dybantsa: 17 PTS · Brandon Miller (CHA): 19 PTS · Naz Reid: 12 PTS, 11 REB

Washington did not need an offensive masterpiece to make a statement. It needed the discipline to turn Charlotte's shot selection into a liability. The Hornets looked comfortable early, opening four-for-ten from deep and building a double-digit first-quarter lead. Then the perimeter game collapsed. Charlotte finished 12-for-56 from three and shot only 29.7 percent overall. When an offense keeps choosing the same difficult release without forcing defensive rotations, possessions become hopeful launches rather than attacks. Washington recognized the pattern and made the Hornets keep playing against a set defense.

Anthony Davis supplied interior certainty with 25 points and 13 rebounds. Trae Young contributed 21 points, nine assists and seven rebounds, providing the organization that kept Washington from becoming equally dependent on jump shots. AJ Dybantsa added 17 points in his home debut after missing the beginning of the season with an ankle injury. The rookie attacked with confidence, giving the Wizards a different angle when the game slowed down. This is the version of Washington that can win without scoring 120: defend, rebound, let Young organize and trust Davis to control the paint.

The decisive stretch was the third quarter, when Charlotte managed only 13 points. Brandon Miller's 19 and Naz Reid's 12-and-11 double-double were individual bright spots, not evidence of a functional collective attack. We credit the Wizards' defense, but Charlotte has to find a counter when the three stops falling. Washington's 3–3 start now has a useful defensive reference point; the Hornets leave with questions about spacing, shot selection and how quickly they can change an unproductive plan.

TURNING POINT — Washington limited Charlotte to 13 points in the third quarter after the Hornets’ early three-point shooting disappeared.
4DK OUTLOOK — The Wizards open Cup play with a +16 result and a defensive blueprint. Charlotte must diversify its half-court attack.

GAME 02 / NBA CUP

LEBRON DELIVERS THE FINISH. PHILLY TAKES ANOTHER ONE FROM NEW YORK.

PHILADELPHIA 76ERS 98 — NEW YORK KNICKS 93
Philadelphia 5–1 · New York 2–4
Tyrese Maxey (PHI): 27 PTS · LeBron James: 20 PTS, 9 REB, 8 AST · Anfernee Simons: 21 PTS · VJ Edgecombe: 13 PTS, 8 REB · Jalen Brunson (NYK): 24 PTS · Karl-Anthony Towns: 22 PTS, 12 REB

Philadelphia's second victory over the defending champions already carries weight because the Sixers had to win this one in the kind of half-court environment that decides playoff games. They led 53–44 at halftime and 77–68 after three, but New York kept coming. Karl-Anthony Towns attacked late, Jalen Brunson found enough space to hit the three that put the Knicks ahead 90–89 with 1:32 left, and the arena suddenly felt like a postseason test. Philadelphia's response came from the player most familiar with that kind of pressure.

LeBron James finished with 20 points, nine rebounds and eight assists, but his defining work came in the final 70 seconds. He answered Brunson's go-ahead three with a difficult fadeaway, VJ Edgecombe hit a shot-clock-beating insurance three, and James converted a late putback to protect the result. Tyrese Maxey's 27 points sustained the offense, while Anfernee Simons added 21 off the bench with Jaylen Brown unavailable because of knee soreness. This is why Philadelphia's roster construction is interesting: it has creators who can score, pass and make the correct decision without needing the same player to do everything.

Brunson scored 24 and Towns added 22 and 12, yet the Knicks are 2–4 and have already lost twice to Philadelphia. That does not erase their championship pedigree. It does make their closing execution a legitimate early-season issue. Brunson also appeared to turn an ankle late, something worth monitoring without assuming a diagnosis. We saw a game decided less by the talent on the floor than by which team made the final three possessions count. Philadelphia made them count.

TURNING POINT — Brunson's three put New York ahead 90–89 with 1:32 left; James answered, Edgecombe hit a late three and James added the sealing putback.
4DK OUTLOOK — Philadelphia owns two early wins over the defending champions. New York must stabilize its closing offense and monitor Brunson’s condition.

GAME 03 / NBA CUP

PRITCHARD SETS THE TEMPO. BOSTON MAKES CHICAGO CHASE AGAIN.

BOSTON CELTICS 125 — CHICAGO BULLS 111
Boston 4–1 · Chicago 1–5
Payton Pritchard (BOS): 30 PTS · Jayson Tatum: 26 PTS · Paul George: 18 PTS, 8 AST · Caleb Wilson (CHI): 23 PTS, 6 3PM · Norman Powell: 16 PTS

Boston has seen Chicago twice in five days, and the Bulls still have not solved the central problem: how to keep the Celtics from turning small mistakes into a run of high-quality offense. Payton Pritchard scored 20 of his 30 before halftime. Jayson Tatum added 26, and Paul George supplied 18 points and eight assists. The Celtics could initiate through multiple players, so Chicago could not load up on one creator without leaving another open. The ball moved, the paint opened and the score got away early.

Boston led by as many as 32 before halftime and went to the locker room ahead 78–49. That stretch defined the game, not Chicago's fourth-quarter push after Boston had already pulled its starters. The Celtics scored 56 points in the paint and converted 16 Bulls turnovers into 21 points. Those are controllable details that have now hurt Chicago repeatedly against the same opponent. When the ball is loose and the defense is late, Boston's offense becomes a machine that is difficult to slow with a timeout alone.

Caleb Wilson deserves his own spotlight. The rookie scored 23 and made six threes, showing the offensive versatility and confidence that make his development worth tracking even through losses. But one prospect's growth cannot cover a defense allowing repeated straight-line entries. Boston's four-game winning streak looks increasingly credible because the production is coming from multiple sources. Chicago has to make opponents work harder for their first good shot and stop giving away the possessions that turn a competitive game into a blowout.

TURNING POINT — Boston led by as many as 32 in the first half and carried a 78–49 advantage into the break.
4DK OUTLOOK — Boston’s four-game winning streak gains substance; Wilson is a bright spot, but Chicago’s turnover problems remain urgent.

GAME 04 / NBA CUP

ORLANDO FLIPS THE REMATCH. BARNES' 41 ISN'T ENOUGH.

ORLANDO MAGIC 121 — TORONTO RAPTORS 114
Orlando 2–3 · Toronto 3–2
Desmond Bane (ORL): 28 PTS, 6 3PM · Franz Wagner: 26 PTS, 11 AST · Wendell Carter Jr.: 20 PTS · Anthony Black: 17 PTS, 10 AST · Scottie Barnes (TOR): 41 PTS · Immanuel Quickley: 17 PTS

Forty-eight hours changed the matchup. Toronto beat Orlando by 30 on Wednesday, but the Magic returned with a different offensive picture. After hitting only ten of 35 threes in the first meeting, Orlando made 18 of 34 on Friday. Desmond Bane scored 28 and made six threes; Franz Wagner produced 26 points and 11 assists. The difference was not simply better luck. Orlando spread its playmaking responsibilities and created cleaner looks, making it harder for Toronto to load up on one action or one driver.

Scottie Barnes nearly stole the night. His career-high 41 points came on 19-for-34 shooting, and he kept attacking with Kawhi Leonard sidelined because of knee swelling. Toronto briefly regained the lead at 102–100 on an Immanuel Quickley three with 8:38 remaining. It was the moment when Wednesday's rout could have become a psychological advantage in the rematch. Instead, Anthony Black hit a three to restore Orlando's lead and spark a 13–4 run. Black finished with 17 points and ten assists; Wendell Carter Jr. added 20.

Barnes eventually fouled out, leaving Toronto without its most reliable offensive engine at the finish. We see this as a useful adjustment win for Orlando. The Magic did not merely repeat the same game plan and hope the shots fell; they generated more balanced creation. Toronto can take pride in Barnes' star-level response, but the Raptors must defend multiple initiators and find secondary offense when Leonard is out. One great individual performance cannot substitute for a complete team response.

TURNING POINT — Quickley put Toronto ahead 102–100 in the fourth; Black's three sparked Orlando's decisive 13–4 answer.
4DK OUTLOOK — Orlando earns a Cup road win and splits the two-game set. Toronto needs secondary creation and more consistent perimeter coverage.

GAME 05 / NBA CUP

DETROIT TAKES THE HIT, THEN TAKES OVER.

DETROIT PISTONS 110 — BROOKLYN NETS 94
Detroit 5–1 · Brooklyn 0–5
Cade Cunningham (DET): 20 PTS · Ron Holland II: 18 PTS · Duncan Robinson: 17 PTS · Moritz Wagner (BKN): 16 PTS · Julius Randle: 15 PTS

Brooklyn's first quarter offered a brief argument that its winless start might end. The Nets led 28–21 after twelve minutes and pushed their advantage to ten early in the second. Detroit did not answer with panic. The Pistons tightened the defensive floor, ran harder after stops and steadily turned the game toward their strengths. They closed the first half with an 18–7 burst to lead 56–49, then made the third quarter a demonstration of why their early record deserves respect.

Detroit won the third period 36–17. Ron Holland II provided an explosive bench stretch, including twelve consecutive Pistons points, while Cade Cunningham controlled the broader rhythm and finished with 20. Duncan Robinson's 17 kept the floor spaced. The more revealing numbers were collective: Detroit outscored Brooklyn 48–36 in the paint and 27–11 on the fast break. Stops became immediate chances instead of dead-ball resets. The Nets shot only 40 percent overall and could not sustain their early advantage once the Pistons' pressure increased.

Brooklyn got 16 from Moritz Wagner and 15 from Julius Randle, but the offense again lacked a dependable way to halt a run. Detroit's 5–1 start is not simply a product of favorable matchups. The Pistons are building a defensive identity that gives them room to survive uneven shooting nights. We still want to see that formula against more disciplined playoff offenses, but the outline is already clear: win the physical possessions, keep Cade connected to the game and turn defensive activity into points before the other team can set its coverage.

TURNING POINT — An 18–7 Detroit run closed the first half; a 36–17 third quarter put Brooklyn away.
4DK OUTLOOK — Detroit moves to 5–1 with a repeatable defensive formula. Brooklyn remains winless and must find a way to resist extended runs.

GAME 06 / NBA CUP

KYRIE AND FLAGG END HOUSTON'S PERFECT START.

DALLAS MAVERICKS 116 — HOUSTON ROCKETS 111
Dallas 4–2 · Houston 5–1
Kyrie Irving (DAL): 35 PTS, 9 AST · Cooper Flagg: 33 PTS, 9 REB · Daniel Gafford: 18 PTS · Kevin Durant (HOU): 28 PTS · Jabari Smith Jr.: 22 PTS

Houston arrived unbeaten and looking like a team that could score through multiple combinations. Dallas had lost the first meeting between these clubs, and the Mavericks spent much of Friday fighting uphill again. The Rockets turned a seven-point first-quarter deficit into a 51–44 halftime lead and at one stage led by eleven after the break. Kevin Durant scored 28 and Jabari Smith Jr. added 22. Houston's depth kept the offense moving even without Alperen Sengun. But the late possessions exposed the difference between having options and converting the ones that matter.

Kyrie Irving scored 35 points and Cooper Flagg added 33. They combined for 27 in the fourth quarter, giving Dallas two very different late-game weapons. Irving's jumper put the Mavericks ahead 102–100 for good. Durant missed two free throws with Houston down two, and Amen Thompson later missed another pair after a Dallas turnover offered a gift. Flagg answered those mistakes with clutch scoring, including a three-point play. Daniel Gafford's late free throws helped protect the lead. Houston went 24-for-33 at the line; nine misses are enormous in a five-point loss.

We are not lowering Houston's ceiling because of one result. The Rockets were 5–0 for a reason, and Durant remains a dependable creator. But championship-level teams cannot surrender that many points at the stripe and expect talent to solve every closing possession. Dallas earned a statement win with substance: Irving's shot-making and Flagg's size, timing and aggression gave the Mavericks two ways to win the same late-game battle. With Memphis idle at 5–0, Houston's defeat leaves the Grizzlies as the league's last unbeaten club.

TURNING POINT — Irving's jumper gave Dallas a 102–100 lead; missed Houston free throws were followed by Flagg's clutch baskets.
4DK OUTLOOK — Dallas moves to 4–2 with a Cup statement. Houston falls to 5–1 and must sharpen free-throw and late-game execution.

GAME 07 / NBA CUP

GIANNIS TRIPLES. KLAY RAINS THREES. MIAMI FINDS ITS OFFENSIVE SHAPE.

MIAMI HEAT 145 — INDIANA PACERS 125
Miami 2–4 · Indiana 2–4
Giannis Antetokounmpo (MIA): 31 PTS, 17 REB, 10 AST · Bam Adebayo: 27 PTS · Klay Thompson: 25 PTS, 7 3PM · Quenton Jackson (IND): 24 PTS · Andrew Nembhard: 22 PTS

Indiana scored 42 in the first quarter and still lost by twenty. That explains why Miami's offensive response deserves more attention than the final margin. The Heat adjusted to the Pacers' fast start, then took control by outscoring Indiana 67–46 across the middle two periods. Giannis Antetokounmpo finished with 31 points, 17 rebounds and ten assists in 31 minutes, his second triple-double of the season. He could finish, rebound and initiate the next possession without Miami waiting for a traditional point guard to organize every trip.

Bam Adebayo scored 27 on 11-for-14 shooting, making the frontcourt partnership look like a shared problem rather than two stars taking turns. Klay Thompson hit seven threes and scored 25, providing the spacing this roster construction needs. When defenders collapse toward Giannis, Klay's movement punishes the help. When perimeter defenders stay attached, Bam and Giannis can attack isolated matchups. Davion Mitchell added 15 points and eleven assists. Miami's 145 points came from several players finding complementary roles, not from a single star monopolizing every possession.

Indiana was short-handed, with Tyrese Haliburton sidelined by a hamstring strain and Obi Toppin unavailable because of a back issue. Quenton Jackson scored 24 and Andrew Nembhard 22, but the Pacers could not match Miami once the Heat began stacking efficient possessions. We are not calling Miami fixed after two straight wins; the defensive standard still matters. But the offensive blueprint is visible. Giannis does not need to dominate every shot attempt for Miami to dominate a game. His gravity can make everyone else more dangerous.

TURNING POINT — After Indiana's 42-point first quarter, Miami won the middle two periods 67–46.
4DK OUTLOOK — Miami’s second straight win opens Cup play at +20. Indiana needs healthier rotation depth and better middle-quarter defense.

GAME 08 / NBA CUP

LAMELO LEADS. McDANIELS DEFENDS. MINNESOTA FINDS A WAY.

MINNESOTA TIMBERWOLVES 127 — LA CLIPPERS 101
Minnesota 4–2 · LA Clippers 1–4
LaMelo Ball (MIN): 24 PTS, 7 AST · Jaden McDaniels: 23 PTS, 6 REB, 7 AST · Bones Hyland: 19 PTS, 8 AST · Darius Garland (LAC): 24 PTS

Minnesota's first victory without Anthony Edwards matters because his knee surgery forces the Wolves to rethink who bends the defense, who creates late-clock shots and how much offense can come from the supporting cast. LaMelo Ball answered with 24 points and seven assists. He gave Minnesota an engine for pace and improvisation, but the win worked because he was not the only player making decisions. This was a glimpse of how the Wolves can stay competitive without asking one guard to replace everything Edwards provides.

Jaden McDaniels scored 23 on 11-for-15 shooting, with six rebounds and seven assists. His two-way contribution is crucial because Minnesota cannot afford to sacrifice its defensive identity while searching for new scoring. Bones Hyland's 19 points and eight assists off the bench provided another source of acceleration. The Wolves finished with 32 assists and outscored the Clippers 62–42 in the paint. Losing an elite perimeter scorer does not have to mean settling for more difficult jumpers; Minnesota used movement and interior pressure to create its best looks.

The Clippers received 24 from Darius Garland, but their fourth consecutive loss showed how hard it is to compete without consistent resistance. Minnesota closed with a 20–2 fourth-quarter surge that turned a solid lead into a rout. LA's injuries have limited its options, but the defensive breakdowns remain concerning. We are not pretending this replaces Edwards' star-level scoring. It demonstrates something more practical: Ball can initiate, McDaniels can expand his offensive role and Minnesota can remain dangerous if its defense and secondary creation travel together.

TURNING POINT — A 20–2 fourth-quarter Minnesota run ended the game as a contest.
4DK OUTLOOK — Minnesota improves to 4–2 with its first win during Edwards’ absence. The Clippers drop to 1–4 and need a defensive response.

GAME 09 / NBA CUP

DOWN 25. WIN BY 17. CURRY AND THE WARRIORS STEAL THE SHOW.

GOLDEN STATE WARRIORS 112 — LOS ANGELES LAKERS 95
Golden State 3–3 · Los Angeles Lakers 4–2
Stephen Curry (GSW): 31 PTS, 8 3PM, 6 AST, 4 REB · Brandin Podziemski: 21 PTS, 6 AST, 5 REB · De'Anthony Melton: 16 PTS · Luka Dončić (LAL): 37 PTS, 7 REB, 5 AST

For twelve minutes, this looked like a Luka Dončić takeover. The Lakers won the first quarter 38–15, and Luka scored 21 with five threes while Los Angeles forced Golden State into mistake after mistake. The lead eventually reached 25. That is normally when the road team begins managing the clock. This was not a normal game. Golden State answered with the exact reverse score in the second quarter — 38–15 — to reach halftime tied. A 23-point first-quarter hole vanished in twelve minutes. The comeback was not a single lucky run; it was a complete change in who dictated the possessions.

Stephen Curry finished with 31 points, eight threes, six assists and four rebounds. Brandin Podziemski added 21, six assists and five boards; De'Anthony Melton delivered 16 off the bench. The Warriors tightened their defense, moved the ball before the Lakers could load up and turned Curry's gravity into cleaner looks for everyone else. His crossover and three over Dončić became the defining highlight, but the more important image was the scoreboard: Golden State kept widening the lead while the Lakers' offense became isolated. Curry also passed Dominique Wilkins into 18th place on the NBA's career scoring list.

Dončić still scored 37 with seven rebounds and five assists, so this is not an indictment of his individual production. It is an indictment of how little Los Angeles generated once Golden State changed the terms of the game. The Lakers scored only 57 after the first quarter. The Warriors, who opened 0–3, have now won three straight. We give this result extra weight because Golden State did not merely survive the comeback; it won by 17. That is a meaningful Cup point differential and the kind of team-wide resilience that changes a locker room's belief.

TURNING POINT — Golden State answered a 38–15 first quarter with a 38–15 second, erased a 25-point deficit and pulled away.
4DK OUTLOOK — The Warriors move to 3–3 and open Cup play at +17. The 4–2 Lakers must examine why their offense stalled after Luka’s opening explosion.

GAME 10 / NBA CUP

MURRAY AND BRAUN LIGHT UP PHOENIX. THE SUNS CAN'T CLOSE THE GAP.

DENVER NUGGETS 127 — PHOENIX SUNS 120
Denver 3–2 · Phoenix 1–3
Jamal Murray (DEN): 39 PTS, 7 3PM · Christian Braun: 31 PTS, 7 3PM · Nikola Jokić: 18 PTS, 10 AST · Miles Bridges (PHX): 24 PTS, 10 REB · Devin Booker: 20 PTS

Denver did not need Nikola Jokić to carry every possession. It won because the players around him punished Phoenix for every inch of defensive space. Jamal Murray scored 39 and hit seven threes. Christian Braun added 31 with seven of his own, including 22 first-half points. The Nuggets finished 20-for-38 from deep, a 52.6 percent clip that turned ordinary closeouts into impossible decisions. Stay attached to the shooters and Jokić becomes the release valve. Help inward and Murray or Braun has room to fire.

Phoenix fought. Miles Bridges scored 24 with ten rebounds, Devin Booker had 20 on his birthday and Collin Gillespie contributed 16 points and ten assists. Dillon Brooks provided a first-half jolt before being ejected in the third quarter after a flagrant-2 foul involving Murray; he finished with 22. The Suns kept trying to manufacture another run, but Denver led 103–93 after three and never allowed Phoenix to turn its late push into a one-possession game. That is the key distinction: the Suns created pressure but never took away Denver's preferred answers.

Murray scored 28 after halftime, giving Denver the second-half control it needs when opponents try to disrupt Jokić's playmaking. Jokić finished with 18 points and ten assists without chasing a huge scoring total. The Nuggets have won two straight and opened Cup play with a road victory. Phoenix has lost three of its first four. We see competitive energy, but not enough defensive connectivity. When two opposing perimeter players combine for 70 points, the Suns have to ask whether it was simply extraordinary shooting or a scheme that kept offering the same windows.

TURNING POINT — Braun hit six first-half threes; Murray scored 28 after halftime to keep Phoenix at arm’s length.
4DK OUTLOOK — Denver improves to 3–2 with a +7 Cup win. Phoenix falls to 1–3 and needs more reliable perimeter coverage.
