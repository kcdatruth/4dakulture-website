WHAT WE LEARNED SUNDAY.

The last unbeaten team falls. Burrow delivers six touchdowns. Buffalo and Denver make statements. Twelve Sunday games. Twelve stories.

Lions 19, Vikings 15

DETROIT ENDS MINNESOTA’S PERFECT START

The last unbeaten team in football finally fell. Minnesota’s defense kept the game within reach, but Detroit made the necessary plays to secure a physical NFC North victory. The Lions did not need an offensive fireworks show to prove they belong in the conference conversation.

The Vikings are still 6–1, and that body of work matters. But their offensive inconsistency deserves attention now that an opponent has shown it can survive Minnesota’s defensive pressure and win a tight fourth-quarter game.

The defining sequence came late. Jared Goff found Jameson Williams for the decisive fourth-quarter touchdown, turning Minnesota’s narrow lead into another test of its offense. Detroit’s response was not merely about one throw; it was about staying composed after the Vikings briefly seized momentum.

This result also shifts the conference picture. San Francisco, idle this week, moves into the NFC’s top-seed position while Minnesota must answer a sharper question than whether its defense is elite: can the offense generate enough against teams capable of matching that defense possession for possession?

Bengals 43, Titans 36 (OT)

JOE BURROW OWNS SUNDAY

Joe Burrow completed 38 of 48 passes for 462 yards and five touchdowns, then scored the overtime winner on a nine-yard run. Tee Higgins contributed 155 receiving yards and two touchdowns, while Ja’Marr Chase added 141 yards and a score. This was a reminder of the Bengals’ extraordinary offensive ceiling.

The warning is on the other side of the ball. Tennessee forced Cincinnati into a shootout, with Cam Ward passing for 304 yards and Tony Pollard rushing for 117. A contender cannot assume Burrow will produce six touchdowns every time the defense struggles.

The Bengals needed nearly every bit of Burrow’s production to survive a 1–7 Titans team. Tee Higgins’ 155 receiving yards and two touchdowns and Ja’Marr Chase’s 141 yards illustrated how difficult Cincinnati becomes to defend when both receivers command attention.

But a contender cannot treat a six-touchdown quarterback afternoon as a weekly survival plan. Tennessee’s Tony Pollard rushed for 117 yards, and Cincinnati repeatedly allowed the Titans to stay alive. The offense is capable of beating anyone; the defensive volatility remains the reason the Bengals’ ceiling and floor are so far apart.

Bills 36, Ravens 10

BUFFALO MAKES A COMPLETE-TEAM STATEMENT

Buffalo overwhelmed Baltimore and held the Ravens to just 144 net yards. The Bills’ defense supplied the kind of performance that complements Josh Allen rather than relying on him to solve every problem.

Baltimore’s quarterback injuries are essential context. Lamar Jackson’s thumb injury dramatically altered the afternoon and raises difficult questions about the weeks ahead. Buffalo deserves its credit, but the Ravens’ health situation must be assessed separately from the final margin.

Josh Allen’s 289 passing yards were enough because the defense set the terms. Baltimore managed only ten points, and the Bills no longer needed to win through a back-and-forth offensive showcase. That is the most encouraging part of this result for Buffalo.

Context matters: Lamar Jackson’s thumb injury ended his return almost immediately, and Tyler Huntley was also injured. The Ravens’ personnel crisis changes what can be learned about their offense, but it does not diminish Buffalo’s focus. The Bills have put themselves at the front of the AFC playoff race.

Broncos 28, Chiefs 24

DENVER SHAKES THE AFC WEST

Denver rallied from an 11-point deficit and survived Kansas City’s final opportunity near the goal line. That is a signature divisional victory against an opponent accustomed to winning games in exactly those circumstances.

The Chiefs have now lost two straight. Patrick Mahomes remains capable of changing any game, but the Broncos have earned a genuine upward-ranking conversation. Kansas City’s résumé still matters; so does its recent inability to finish.

The ending explained the rivalry’s stakes better than the final score alone. Kansas City reached the doorstep of the winning touchdown, but a false start on fourth down pushed the Chiefs backward. Denver then pressured Mahomes and finished the game with a sack. The Broncos did not merely survive; they executed with the division on the line.

Kansas City’s 11 penalties were a major self-inflicted problem. Denver, now 6–2, holds the AFC West lead over the 5–2 Chiefs. That standings swing is real, and it is why the Broncos’ Power 32 case should be judged on more than the reputation of the team they beat.

Jaguars 42, Colts 7

JACKSONVILLE LEAVES NO DOUBT

The Jaguars demolished Indianapolis, producing one of the week’s clearest statements. A team with serious postseason ambitions has to handle struggling opponents, and Jacksonville did so without allowing the game to become complicated.

The result adds substance to the Jaguars’ AFC standing. Indianapolis must address not only the scoreboard but how quickly the contest slipped out of reach. This was a mismatch in execution as much as talent.

Trevor Lawrence threw three touchdown passes, and rookie Bhayshul Tuten contributed 104 rushing yards and a touchdown. Jacksonville had both the quarterback production and the ground-game punch to keep Indianapolis from ever settling into a competitive rhythm.

There is a difference between surviving a weak opponent and imposing your identity on one. The Jaguars did the latter. With the AFC South lead strengthening, the next question is whether this balanced attack travels against the conference’s strongest defenses.

Steelers 27, Browns 6

PITTSBURGH’S DEFENSE SETS THE TERMS

Pittsburgh limited Cleveland to six points in an important AFC North meeting. The Steelers showed that their physical identity can carry them through games where every possession and field-position battle matters.

Cleveland entered with a winning record, making this result more consequential than a routine victory over a struggling team. The Browns have to find offensive answers against opponents capable of dictating down-and-distance.

Jaylen Warren rushed for 143 yards and a touchdown, giving Pittsburgh an offensive anchor while the defense controlled the Browns. The Steelers did not have to manufacture risky downfield plays because the ground game and defensive pressure consistently kept them ahead of the chains.

At 6–2, Pittsburgh has taken sole possession of the AFC North lead. Cleveland entered with a winning record, which makes the six-point output especially troubling. This was not simply a bad afternoon for the Browns; it was a reminder that offensive limitations become magnified against disciplined division rivals.

Commanders 27, Eagles 10

PHILADELPHIA’S SLIDE CAN’T BE EXPLAINED AWAY

Washington controlled its divisional matchup and kept Philadelphia’s offense from generating sustained momentum. The Commanders earned the win by executing rather than relying on the Eagles to beat themselves.

At 2–6, Philadelphia is well beyond the point where preseason expectations should carry weight. The Eagles need to demonstrate improvement on the field. Until then, their Power 32 position must reflect the results.

Philadelphia has now lost six consecutive games. The Eagles are not simply struggling to win the close ones; they were held to ten points in a divisional game against a Washington team that needed a statement of its own.

Washington’s victory changes the emotional temperature of both seasons. The Commanders can build around a result that showed control, while Philadelphia must confront the gap between roster expectations and on-field execution. At 2–6, there is no credible shortcut around that conversation.

Rams 24, Chargers 9

THE RAMS CONTROL THE LOS ANGELES MATCHUP

The Rams kept the Chargers out of the end zone and won without needing a high-scoring offensive showcase. Defensive control and timely execution were enough to decide the city matchup.

The Chargers fall to 0–7. A winless record this deep into the season cannot be reduced to bad luck. The Rams, meanwhile, move back to .500 with a performance built on structure.

Puka Nacua caught 11 passes for 80 yards and a touchdown, providing a dependable chain-moving presence as the Rams kept control of the game. Los Angeles did not require a flood of explosive plays to put the Chargers away.

The Chargers’ 0–7 start is the more urgent story. Close losses can be framed as missed opportunities; being held to nine points demands questions about the entire offensive process. The Rams, meanwhile, reached .500 and remain close enough to matter in a crowded NFC.

Buccaneers 16, Falcons 0

TAMPA BAY WINS THE PHYSICAL BATTLE

Tampa Bay shut out Atlanta in a game shaped by its ground attack and defensive control. Bucky Irving rushed for 170 yards as the Buccaneers repeatedly imposed their preferred style.

Atlanta quarterback Michael Penix Jr. threw four interceptions. The Falcons need answers for both the turnovers and the lack of offensive adjustment. Tampa Bay’s record is still disappointing, but this win offers a viable blueprint.

The conditions made this one of Sunday’s strangest victories: Tampa Bay won without completing a pass. The Buccaneers rushed for 324 yards, with Bucky Irving leading the way, and leaned into what the game allowed rather than forcing a conventional passing script.

Atlanta had no such adjustment. Four Michael Penix Jr. interceptions compounded an afternoon in which the Falcons could not generate a point. The Buccaneers remain below .500, but their adaptability deserves recognition; Atlanta’s inability to protect the football deserves equal scrutiny.

Cardinals 33, Cowboys 30 (OT)

DALLAS LETS A WIN SLIP AWAY

Arizona erased a late Dallas advantage and finished the comeback in overtime. The Cardinals displayed the resilience required to survive a chaotic fourth quarter and still execute when the game extended.

Dallas has enough offensive ability to compete with almost anyone, but closing remains a recurring issue. At 3–5, another close loss carries real playoff consequences. Arizona earned the final word by making the final plays.

Arizona erased a 14-point deficit, and Gardner Minshew helped expose a Dallas secondary that could not close the door. Michael Wilson emerged as a central receiving threat as the Cardinals scored repeatedly in the second half.

The Cowboys’ problem is not a lack of offensive potential. It is the failure to pair that potential with late-game defensive stops. At 3–5, Dallas cannot afford to keep treating blown leads as isolated accidents. Arizona earned the comeback; Dallas made it possible.

Raiders 38, Jets 33

LAS VEGAS WINS THE SHOOTOUT

The Raiders reached 4–4 by producing enough offense to outlast the Jets. A high-scoring win is still a win, and Las Vegas has kept itself relevant entering the second half of the season.

But allowing 33 points against New York is a reminder that this formula is difficult to sustain. The Raiders need defensive consistency if they intend to turn a .500 start into a serious playoff push.

Las Vegas found an effective offensive rhythm against the Jets, but the Raiders’ running back situation is worth watching. Ashton Jeanty has been playing through lower-body injuries, and his reduced workload raises questions about how sustainable the offense will be against stronger defenses.

At 4–4, the Raiders have kept themselves in the AFC conversation. That is progress, not proof. Their next step is to produce cleaner defensive performances so the offense does not need to win a weekly race to 35 points.

Patriots 24, Dolphins 19

NEW ENGLAND FINDS A WAY

New England escaped Miami with an important divisional victory and improved to 5–3. Not every successful team wins with style; some develop the ability to execute just enough in uncomfortable road games.

For Miami, another close defeat adds pressure to a difficult season. The Patriots can bank the win, but the AFC playoff race will demand more complete performances against stronger opponents.

This is the kind of win that rarely dominates national highlight shows but can matter in January. New England handled a division opponent on the road and improved to 5–3, reinforcing its ability to survive games that turn into field-position and execution battles.

The Patriots are now in the AFC wild-card picture. Miami’s 1–6 record, by contrast, leaves little room for optimism without a rapid turnaround. The difference between the teams is increasingly not star power but the ability to finish the possessions that decide close games.

GAME OF THE WEEK — LIONS 19, VIKINGS 15

The NFC North showdown changed the conversation. Minnesota remains a top contender, but Detroit proved the Vikings are not invulnerable. A head-to-head win matters without automatically erasing the larger season résumé.

PLAYER OF THE DAY — JOE BURROW

462 passing yards. Five passing touchdowns. The overtime-winning rushing touchdown. The most spectacular individual performance of Sunday belongs to Cincinnati’s quarterback.
