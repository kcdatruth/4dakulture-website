WHAT WE LEARNED SUNDAY

The contenders are separating, the challengers are answering, and every Sunday result tells us something different about who is built for January.

11 Sunday games • All final • Monday Night Football not included

Sunday's bigger picture

San Francisco's rout of Minnesota was the loudest NFC statement. Carolina won a chaotic finish that Baltimore could not close. Dallas scored 51 and still exposed defensive concerns. Kansas City and Buffalo kept building their AFC cases, while Philadelphia and the Giants delivered wins that challenged expectations. We judge each result on its own terms: the score, the opponent, the way the game was won, and what remains unproven.

Game 01 • Final

49ers 35, Vikings 10

San Francisco made the loudest statement of Sunday

The scoreboard says domination, but the bigger story is how completely San Francisco controlled the terms of a meeting between NFC contenders. Brock Purdy did not need a desperate fourth-quarter comeback to make his mark. His reported three-touchdown performance put the Vikings in a position where their offense had to chase the game rather than patiently establish its own rhythm.

For the 49ers, that matters because the offense is built to threaten defenses in multiple directions. Christian McCaffrey changes how linebackers and safeties react to the backfield, while George Kittle and Mike Evans give Purdy different answers in the passing game. Minnesota could not match that combination on the scoreboard. The Vikings' 10-point output is an equally serious concern: a contender must be able to survive when its opening plan is disrupted.

4DK verdict: San Francisco strengthens its case for the top tier of the Power 32 and Purdy strengthens his MVP argument. Minnesota needs an honest assessment of offensive adaptability, not just a dismissal of one ugly result.

Game 02 • Final

Panthers 35, Ravens 33

Carolina won the moments Baltimore keeps losing

Seven lead changes made this more than a surprise result. Carolina had to keep answering, and Bryce Young's 254 passing yards and two touchdowns gave the Panthers a way to respond when the game threatened to slip away. Chuba Hubbard supplied 120 rushing yards and two touchdowns, creating a productive second dimension that prevented the offense from becoming entirely quarterback-dependent.

The finish tells the story of two teams heading in different emotional directions. Ryan Fitzgerald's 37-yard field goal with 1:44 left put Carolina ahead; Tyrel Dodson's late interception sealed it. Will Lee III's forced fumble near the goal line was a particularly costly Baltimore swing. The Ravens had enough offensive output to win, yet suffered a sixth consecutive loss. Their problem is not merely the ability to move the ball: it is converting opportunities into winning football.

4DK verdict: Carolina earns genuine Stock Up consideration. Baltimore's repeated failure in decisive moments demands a tougher review of ball security, late-game execution and defensive responses.

Game 03 • Final

Cowboys 51, Titans 30

Dak's explosion cannot erase the defensive questions

Dak Prescott's 325 passing yards and four touchdowns powered one of Sunday's most explosive performances. Dallas did what a superior offense should do against a struggling opponent: build scoring pressure and force Tennessee to operate from behind. Fifty-one points demonstrate an enormous offensive ceiling, but they do not prove that every part of this team is ready for January.

Tennessee still scored 30. That matters because the Cowboys will not be able to rely on a half-century every week, especially against opponents capable of matching their playmakers. The Titans deserve a separate assessment, too: producing 30 points suggests they found offensive answers, but their defense never gave those answers enough value. Dallas needs to identify why its opponent kept finding scoring opportunities even while the game was slipping away.

4DK verdict: Prescott and the offense belong among the week's winners; the Cowboys' defense remains a legitimate obstacle to a sustained playoff push.

Game 04 • Final

Chiefs 34, Cardinals 7

Kansas City's balance is the real warning

Patrick Mahomes threw for 324 yards and three touchdowns, but the most encouraging part of Kansas City's performance was that the offense did not need to lean on one solution. Travis Kelce delivered six catches, 74 yards and two touchdowns, while Emmett Johnson's 105 rushing yards and touchdown gave the Chiefs a credible ground-game complement.

The defense also imposed its will. George Karlaftis recorded 2.5 sacks and forced a fumble, illustrating how much easier the game becomes for Mahomes when opponents are under pressure to protect the football and keep pace. Arizona was overmatched and its offensive instability must temper the conclusions. Dominating a vulnerable opponent is expected of a contender; proving the same against elite competition is the next test.

4DK verdict: Kansas City looks increasingly complete. The coming Buffalo matchup is the measuring stick, not a victory lap after beating Arizona.

Game 05 • Final

Bills 26, Dolphins 9

Buffalo controlled the rivalry without chasing style points

Josh Allen's reported 304 passing yards and James Cook's 89 rushing yards with a touchdown helped Buffalo win in two dimensions. Khalil Shakir's 101 receiving yards provided a productive passing target, and the defense held Miami to nine points. That is a valuable combination for a team with AFC championship ambitions.

The Bills did not need a 40-point performance to create a comfortable margin. They needed to protect the game state, avoid giving Miami easy scoring chances and finish possessions well enough to make the Dolphins chase. Miami's offensive limitations also matter: Buffalo will face opponents with more firepower and more ability to punish conservative stretches. The next step is proving that the same defensive control translates against a high-end attack.

4DK verdict: Buffalo remains firmly in the AFC's top tier. The defense deserves as much credit as the offense for this result.

Game 06 • Final

Lions 31, Buccaneers 21

Detroit keeps showing why balance travels

Jared Goff's 279 passing yards and two touchdowns, paired with Jahmyr Gibbs' 98 rushing yards and two scores, highlight the pressure Detroit can apply through different personnel and concepts. A defense that overcommits to limiting explosive passes risks allowing Gibbs to keep drives efficient; a defense that crowds the run creates opportunities for Goff.

Tampa Bay did score 21, so the Lions' defense was not impenetrable. But Detroit's ability to maintain a double-digit margin against an opponent still capable of generating offense matters. For the Buccaneers, the result reinforces the need for more complete performances: scoring three touchdowns is not enough if the other side controls the more important stretches. Detroit's coaching challenge is to preserve this balance when facing defenses that can disrupt early downs.

4DK verdict: Detroit stays among the NFC's serious contenders. Gibbs' impact is central to its ceiling, not a supporting footnote.

Game 07 • Final

Bears 21, Saints 13

Chicago is building a winning identity beyond highlights

An eight-point victory over New Orleans will not dominate the highlight shows, but Chicago reached eight wins by doing enough to control the scoreboard. Holding the Saints to 13 points gave the Bears room to win without asking Caleb Williams and the offense to turn every possession into a spectacular play.

The challenge is distinguishing a sustainable identity from a narrow escape against a weaker opponent. Chicago's defense deserves recognition for the result, while the offense still has to show it can create separation against tougher competition. New Orleans, meanwhile, needs more reliable scoring production; staying within one possession is not the same as consistently threatening to win. For the Bears, these are the games playoff teams must bank even when they are not aesthetically impressive.

4DK verdict: Chicago deserves respect for stacking wins. Its next test is showing the offense can carry more weight when the defense cannot hold an opponent to 13.

Game 08 • Final

Eagles 19, Steelers 16

Philadelphia earned a win it badly needed

The Eagles entered this game with little room to waste opportunities, and a three-point victory over Pittsburgh was meaningful precisely because it required them to finish a tight contest. Nineteen points are not proof of an offensive breakthrough, but winning against a team with a stronger record offers a reason to reassess Philadelphia's competitiveness.

Pittsburgh must confront the other side of that equation. Sixteen points left almost no margin for error, and the Steelers could not create the decisive scoring separation expected of a team trying to establish itself in the AFC. The close score should not tempt us to invent a dramatic tactical narrative without a drive-by-drive review. What is clear is that Philadelphia secured the result and Pittsburgh missed an opportunity to protect its position.

4DK verdict: The Eagles earn Stock Up for the result, with caution about the offense. Pittsburgh needs a better response when the game becomes a possession-by-possession fight.

Game 09 • Final

Jets 27, Chargers 24

The Jets found a finish; the Chargers face bigger uncertainty

New York's three-point road victory deserves recognition because the Jets converted a competitive game into an actual win. For a team with limited positive momentum, finishing matters. The result does not by itself mean the Jets have solved their broader consistency problems, but it offers a performance to build on.

Los Angeles faces two separate questions. First, why did the Chargers allow a struggling opponent to win a close game? Second, what will Justin Herbert's reported entry into concussion protocol mean for the next matchup? His status must be checked against an updated team injury report rather than guessed from the immediate postgame news. Any Power 32 adjustment should account for the result and the uncertainty without assuming an absence that has not been confirmed.

4DK verdict: New York gets credit for delivering under pressure. The Chargers need both a football correction and clarity on their quarterback's health.

Game 10 • Final

Giants 23, Jaguars 18

New York's defense changed the conversation

J.J. McCarthy completed 18 of 38 passes for 173 yards and a touchdown, with Malik Nabers catching seven passes for 55 yards and a score. Those are not overwhelming passing numbers. The Giants won because they combined enough offensive moments with a defense that repeatedly disrupted Jacksonville's plans.

Abdul Carter's two sacks gave the Giants a disruptive presence, and the late-game result ended Jacksonville's three-game winning streak. New York also nearly undermined itself: Cam Skattebo's fumble and a stalled offense after establishing a fourth-quarter cushion kept the door open. Jacksonville had chances to turn the game but could not consistently protect Trevor Lawrence or convert its opportunities. This was a win built on defensive impact and survival, not flawless execution.

4DK verdict: New York earns deserved credit for an upset and defensive urgency. Jacksonville's protection and response to pressure demand scrutiny.

Game 11 • Final

Broncos 38, Raiders 24

Denver handled its business; Vegas still needs answers

Denver scored 38 against a Las Vegas team that could not consistently keep the game within reach. The 14-point margin matters for a Broncos team trying to stabilize its standing among AFC contenders. Putting up that kind of total indicates Denver found offensive solutions, though the exact mechanisms require a verified possession and box-score review.

For the Raiders, 24 points should have created a path to competitiveness, but surrendering 38 made the offense's production insufficient. The problem is complementary football: a team cannot expect its offense to win every exchange when its defense keeps yielding scores. Denver should enjoy the victory without confusing success against a struggling division rival with proof that every issue is solved.

4DK verdict: Denver earns an upward evaluation for decisive divisional execution. Las Vegas must address how its defensive performance keeps undercutting whatever the offense produces.

4DK Player of the Day conversation

Brock Purdy is the leading editorial candidate after directing San Francisco's 35–10 statement against Minnesota. Bryce Young deserves consideration for guiding Carolina through seven lead changes, and Dak Prescott belongs in the conversation after four touchdown passes. This is an editorial shortlist rather than an invented official award.

Stock Up / Stock Down

Stock Up: 49ers, Panthers, Chiefs, Bills, Lions, Eagles and Giants — for very different reasons. Stock Down: Ravens' late-game execution, Cowboys' defensive reliability despite the win, Jaguars' protection and Chargers' near-term uncertainty.
