4DK THURSDAY NIGHT RECAP • WEEK 4

CLEVELAND IS 3–1.
NOW WHAT?

Deshaun Watson played one of his best games of this Browns run, Cleveland's defense battered Aaron Rodgers, and a young core that once looked like a rebuild timeline is already helping win games. The Browns beat Pittsburgh 27–24. The bigger question is whether this start has real playoff legs.

By Kcdatruth • 4 Da KultureOctober 2, 2026Thursday Night Football

CLEVELAND BROWNS3–1
THE STORY

Cleveland was not clean. It committed 14 penalties for 88 yards, gave Pittsburgh two second-half takeaways and blew an 11-point lead. But good teams are not defined only by the games they dominate. Sometimes the evidence is in the game they should have lost — and still found a way to finish.

START WITH THE RECORD: THE BROWNS ARE 3–1.

That sounds obvious, but it matters because Cleveland has not reached 3–1 since 2021. The Browns opened this season by getting blown out in Jacksonville, 34–10, and the easy reaction was that another season was about to disappear before it started. Since then: three straight wins.

They won 23–19 at Tampa Bay. They survived Carolina 21–18. Now they have beaten Pittsburgh in a divisional game on national television. As of Friday morning, Cleveland sits first in the AFC North, with Baltimore and Cincinnati still holding 2–1 records before the rest of Week 4 is played.

The important part is that the three wins have not looked identical. The Browns have won with defense, late-game offense, timely special teams and now a quarterback who is beginning to look comfortable inside Todd Monken's system. That is why 3–1 deserves more respect than simply calling it an early-season hot streak.

DESHAUN WATSON PLAYED WELL — AND THAT CHANGES THE CEILING.

Watson finished 24-of-33 for 268 yards, one touchdown and one interception, good for a 94.0 passer rating. He also lost a fumble. So no, this was not a perfect quarterback game.

But it was a good one. More importantly, it looked functional in ways Cleveland desperately needs. Watson was decisive in the middle of the field, took the deep shot when Pittsburgh gave him the look, used his legs when the pocket broke down and kept multiple young receivers involved.

The second quarter was the best example. Cleveland scored three touchdowns and produced 191 yards in the period. Jaleel McLaughlin broke a 28-yard touchdown run. Watson's 60-yard completion to rookie Denzel Boston changed the geometry of the game and set up Quinshon Judkins' touchdown. The touchdown throw to Harold Fannin Jr. came on third-and-goal. For an offense that spent September fighting for rhythm, that quarter looked like a real blueprint.

The final possession mattered just as much. Pittsburgh had erased Cleveland's lead and tied the game 24–24 with 1:42 remaining. Watson completed passes to KC Concepcion Jr., Jerry Jeudy and Fannin, moving the Browns 36 net yards into field-goal range. Andre Szmyt did the rest from 56.

Through four games, Watson is now 80-of-115 for 855 yards, six touchdowns and two interceptions. Cleveland is 3–0 since Week 1, and Watson has led a go-ahead fourth-quarter drive in each of those three wins.

That last sentence is why we should separate "Watson is back to his old peak" from "Watson is playing winning football." The first claim would be way too much. The second is fair. He is not carrying a top-five offense. He is giving Cleveland enough competent quarterback play that the rest of the roster can matter.

4DK QB TAKECleveland does not need the old Houston version of Watson every Sunday. It needs this version: efficient enough to keep the offense on schedule, mobile enough to punish pressure and calm enough to make the last drive when the game gets tight.

CLEVELAND'S DEFENSE IS THE REASON THIS TEAM HAS A FLOOR.

Watson playing better raises the ceiling. The defense is what makes the Browns difficult to kill.

Cleveland sacked Aaron Rodgers five times, intercepted him twice and broke up eight passes. Denzel Ward's interception in the second quarter changed the entire game. Pittsburgh had scored on a 17-play opening drive and looked comfortable. Ward's pick gave Cleveland life, and the Browns followed with three straight touchdown drives.

The pressure did not come from one player. Isaiah McGuire got home on a third down that pushed Pittsburgh backward before Chris Boswell missed from 48. Mike Hall Jr. produced a huge third-down sack after Watson's fumble, knocking the Steelers out of scoring range. Maliek Collins got Rodgers down. Cleveland kept forcing the Steelers to restart drives from behind the sticks.

That depth matters even more because Mason Graham left in the first quarter with a knee injury and was ruled out. Hall later left with an ankle injury. Cleveland still found five sacks without having Graham available for most of the night.

Grant Delpit delivered the final blow, intercepting Rodgers with one second left after Szmyt's go-ahead kick. The Browns did not dominate every snap — Rodgers still threw for nearly 300 yards — but they repeatedly won the downs that changed points.

The most encouraging thing about this defense is the combination of veteran stability and young legs. Ward and Delpit provide experience in the back end. Carson Schwesinger is already the communicator in the middle. Graham had become the interior pressure piece before the injury. Rookie Emmanuel McNeil-Warren added two passes defensed Thursday. The group has enough answers to survive when one part of the unit is missing.

AARON RODGERS IS 42. CAN HE STILL PLAY SOLID FOOTBALL?

Yes. Last night actually showed both sides of the answer.

Rodgers finished 22-of-40 for 299 yards, three touchdowns and two interceptions. He took five sacks, was hit repeatedly and spent long stretches dealing with Cleveland's rush. But when Pittsburgh needed a comeback, he still delivered enough high-level quarterbacking to make the Browns sweat.

In the fourth quarter, Rodgers hit Darnell Washington for a 21-yard touchdown, later found Pat Freiermuth for another score and then ran in the two-point conversion himself to tie the game at 24. The deep touch throw to DK Metcalf during the comeback was the kind of ball that reminds you the arm talent and anticipation have not disappeared.

Through four games, Rodgers has 999 passing yards, seven touchdowns and four interceptions. He has also been sacked 13 times. Over the last two games alone he has thrown for 591 yards and six touchdowns. That is not a quarterback who is incapable of playing solid football.

The question at 42 is not whether he can still make NFL throws. He can. The question is how often Pittsburgh can put him in conditions where those throws matter. Rodgers is not the quarterback you want absorbing five sacks and living in obvious passing downs. He needs protection, a real run game and enough early-down efficiency that the offense does not become a weekly test of whether he can rescue the pocket.

Jaylen Warren gave Pittsburgh 93 yards on 17 carries Thursday. That should be part of the answer. If Warren is averaging better than five yards per carry, the Steelers cannot let every difficult situation become a Rodgers problem. The best version of 42-year-old Rodgers is still dangerous when he can control protections, work play action and throw on schedule. The worst setup is asking him to take 40 attempts behind a pocket that is collapsing.

So our answer is pretty simple: Rodgers can still be a solid starting quarterback. Pittsburgh just cannot treat him like a 28-year-old escape artist.

THE YOUNG CORE IS WHY CLEVELAND FEELS DIFFERENT.

This is the Browns story that deserves more attention. Cleveland is not 3–1 because a bunch of aging veterans are squeezing one last run out of the roster. A large portion of the actual production is coming from players in Year 1 or Year 2.

RB • YEAR 2

QUINSHON JUDKINS

96 yards from scrimmage Thursday. He ran 17 times for 53 yards and his first rushing touchdown of 2026, then added a career-high six catches for 43. Through four games he has 177 rushing yards and 96 receiving yards. The rushing efficiency still needs to rise, but the receiving growth is making him more than a downhill runner.

WR • ROOKIE

DENZEL BOSTON

4 catches, 89 yards vs. Pittsburgh. His 60-yarder was Cleveland's longest offensive play of the season. Through four games he has 13 catches for 284 yards and two touchdowns — the most receiving yards by a Browns rookie through four games in franchise history.

WR • ROOKIE

KC CONCEPCION JR.

5 catches, 64 yards Thursday. He gives Watson a different type of target from Boston: quick separation, underneath movement and yards after the catch. He also made a clutch catch on the final drive. Through four games: 17 receptions, 144 yards.

TE • YEAR 2

HAROLD FANNIN JR.

3 catches, 27 yards and a touchdown Thursday. He already had 72 catches for 731 yards and six touchdowns as a rookie. Now he has 17 catches, 153 yards and three touchdowns in 2026. He is not a developmental piece anymore. He is already one of Watson's most dependable chain-movers and red-zone options.

LB • YEAR 2

CARSON SCHWESINGER

The reigning AP Defensive Rookie of the Year is the signal-caller in the middle of the defense. After 146 tackles, 11 tackles for loss and two interceptions as a rookie, he already has 33 tackles through four games. His value is bigger than the box score because he organizes the front and coverage behind it.

DT • YEAR 2

MASON GRAHAM

Graham entered Thursday with four sacks through three games after making the PFWA All-Rookie Team in 2025. He injured his knee in the first quarter and did not return. The severity has not yet been announced, and his status is the biggest defensive concern coming out of the win.

JUDKINS ISN'T EXPLODING YET — BUT HIS GAME IS GETTING BIGGER.

The raw rushing number is not going to scare anybody: 177 yards on 59 carries through four games, right at 3.0 per attempt. That part has to improve, and some of it falls on an offensive line that has been working through injuries and lineup changes.

But Thursday showed why Judkins still matters to the playoff argument. He caught six passes. He was available as a checkdown when pressure won. He finished the touchdown drive after Boston's 60-yard catch. He gave Watson a reliable answer instead of forcing every successful play to travel downfield.

After last year's ankle and fibula injury ended his rookie season, simply becoming a three-down contributor again is meaningful. If the Browns can get the run blocking cleaner, the offense already has evidence Judkins can stay on the field for passing situations instead of becoming predictable by personnel.

DENZEL BOSTON MIGHT BE THE MOST IMPORTANT DEVELOPMENT.

Boston's start deserves its own spotlight because Cleveland has been searching for explosive receiving production.

At 6-foot-4, he gives the offense size, but the surprise has been how quickly the vertical game has translated. His 284 yards have come on only 13 catches — almost 22 yards per reception. That is not empty volume. He is changing where safeties have to stand.

When Boston threatens over the top, the field opens for Concepcion underneath and Fannin in the seams. It also helps Judkins because a defense that has to respect vertical play-action cannot crowd the box with the same freedom.

His 60-yarder against Pittsburgh was the clearest example. One play immediately flipped the field and put Cleveland in position to score. Those are the plays the Browns did not have enough of in the opener.

The Browns do not need Boston to become a finished No. 1 receiver in October. They need him to keep forcing defenses to account for the explosive play. He is already doing that.

CONCEPCION AND FANNIN GIVE WATSON THE EASY ANSWERS.

Boston stretches the defense. Concepcion and Fannin are the players who can punish the space that creates.

Concepcion's 17 catches through four games are not producing huge yardage yet, but that is not really the point. He can work inside, move across formations and give Watson a quick target against pressure. His 25-yard catch Thursday was his longest of the season, and the final drive showed the trust developing between quarterback and rookie receiver.

Fannin is even further along. Seventy-two catches as a rookie already established him as more than a gadget tight end. Three touchdowns through the first four games of Year 2 show where his role is growing. On third-and-goal against Pittsburgh, Watson knew where the matchup was and Fannin finished it.

That combination — vertical size from Boston, movement from Concepcion, middle-of-the-field reliability from Fannin and backfield receiving from Judkins — is what makes the young group interesting. None of them has to become a 1,500-yard superstar for the offense to improve. They just have to keep giving Watson different answers.

SCHWESINGER AND GRAHAM ARE THE DEFENSIVE FOUNDATION.

Carson Schwesinger's name belongs in this conversation even when his Thursday box score is not flashy. He was the 2025 AP Defensive Rookie of the Year, led all rookies with 146 tackles and 11 tackles for loss, and wore the green dot as the Browns' defensive signal-caller.

That is not normal responsibility for a young linebacker. It means Cleveland trusts him to get the defense aligned, communicate checks and play every situation. Through four games in 2026, he has 33 tackles and remains the connective piece between a disruptive front and an experienced secondary.

Graham's growth has been more explosive. After a solid All-Rookie first season, he came into Thursday with four sacks in three games. That is the leap Cleveland needed from the former fifth overall pick — turning interior strength into actual pass-rush production.

Which is why the knee injury matters so much. Cleveland proved Thursday that it has defensive depth, but there is a difference between surviving one night without Graham and replacing what he can do for several weeks. Until the team gives a clearer diagnosis, that is the injury we are watching most closely.

IS CLEVELAND A PLAYOFF TEAM?

Right now, they look playoff-caliber. That is different from calling them a playoff lock after four games.

The case is straightforward. Cleveland has a defense capable of winning games when the offense is uneven. Watson is playing his best sustained football of this stretch with the Browns. The young skill talent is improving in real time. Szmyt has made 22 straight field goals dating to Week 10 of last season. And the team has already shown it can win close games late.

The Browns have also allowed only 61 points during this three-game winning streak — just over 20 per game — after the defense got hit for 34 in Jacksonville. That matters because it suggests the opener may not be the only version of this team.

The concerns are just as real. Fourteen penalties against Pittsburgh is unacceptable. Watson still had two turnovers. The run game is not efficient enough yet to consistently control games. The offensive line has dealt with injuries. And Graham's knee is now a major variable because interior pressure is part of what makes this defense dangerous.

There is also a long AFC North season left. Baltimore and Cincinnati entered this week at 2–1, while Pittsburgh is now 2–2. Cleveland has put itself in position. It has not separated from the division.

The next checkpoint is useful too. After the mini-bye, Cleveland goes to the Jets on October 11 and then hosts Baltimore on October 18. If the Browns can come out of that stretch still sitting near the top of the North, the conversation changes from "nice start" to "real AFC factor."

Our answer today: yes, this roster is capable of making the playoffs. The defense gives them a legitimate floor. The question is whether Watson and the young offense can become consistent enough that Cleveland is not asking the defense to rescue every fourth quarter.

WHAT LAST NIGHT SAID ABOUT PITTSBURGH.

The Steelers are 2–2, and the most frustrating part of Thursday is that they had enough good football to win.

Warren ran effectively. DK Metcalf finished with five catches for 115 yards. Rodgers produced three touchdown passes. T.J. Watt forced a fumble. Pittsburgh erased a double-digit deficit in the fourth quarter.

But the offense disappeared for too long in the middle of the game, and the protection could not keep Rodgers clean. The defense created second-half takeaways but allowed Cleveland to score 21 points in the second quarter and move into field-goal range on the final possession.

For Rodgers, the takeaway should not be "he cannot play anymore." The better question is whether Pittsburgh can build an offense that does not require a 42-year-old quarterback to absorb punishment and manufacture comebacks every week.

He still has the mind, the ball placement and enough arm talent to punish a defense. What he does not need is five sacks, 40 attempts and a game script where the Steelers wait until the fourth quarter to rediscover balance.

THE GAME-WINNING KICK MATTERS TOO.

Quarterbacks and pass rushers get the attention, but the Browns do not leave Thursday at 3–1 without Andre Szmyt.

He hit from 44 yards earlier in the fourth quarter, then lined up from a career-long 56 after Cleveland's final drive stalled near the edge of comfortable range. Ten seconds left. Division game. National television. Tie score.

Good.

That was his 22nd straight made field goal dating back to Week 10 of last season. A team trying to win close games needs that kind of trust. Cleveland has it right now.

4DK FINAL WORD

THE BROWNS HAVE EARNED OUR ATTENTION.

3–1 is not a playoff berth. It is not proof Cleveland is ready to run the AFC. But this does not feel hollow either.

Watson is giving the offense competent — and lately clutch — quarterback play. The defense can wreck a game. Boston, Judkins, Concepcion and Fannin are producing now instead of only promising something for later. Schwesinger already looks like a defensive cornerstone. Graham was becoming one before the knee injury interrupted his night.

And on the other sideline, Rodgers showed that 42 does not automatically mean finished. He can still play. Pittsburgh just has to stop putting him in situations where age, pressure and protection all become the story at once.

Cleveland's next step is consistency. Clean up the penalties, protect the football, get healthier up front and keep the young core growing. Do that, and this is absolutely a team that can still be playing meaningful football in January.
