There was always going to be pressure on The Drop. Any show connected to Snowfall was going to get compared to the original immediately. Franklin Saint's story became the center of that universe, so trying to recreate the same formula would have been the easiest move — and probably the worst one.

Thankfully, Episode 1 doesn't seem interested in doing that.

The Drop carries the DNA of Snowfall, but the premiere lets us know almost immediately that we've entered a different period, with different characters, different ambitions and a different rhythm.

THE BIG TAKEAWAY
Not better. Not worse. Just different.

WELCOME TO THE '90s

One of the strongest things about Episode 1 is how quickly the show establishes its era. We're not in the same Los Angeles that defined the beginning of Snowfall. The Drop pushes the world forward into the '90s, and the energy changes with it.

The clothes, the movement of the city and especially the music immediately create a new backdrop. It still feels connected to South Central and everything that came before it, but it doesn't feel trapped in the past.

CHECK
THE MUSIC IS ALREADY A CHARACTER

EPISODE 1 SOUNDS 🔥.

The soundtrack is one of the biggest reasons the premiere works. The music isn't just there to remind us what era we're in — it gives scenes extra personality and helps shape the atmosphere of the whole show.

The standout was the use of Kendrick Lamar's “m.A.A.d city” during the bus-stop chase. That placement was genius. It wasn't just a fire song over a tense scene; it connected generations of South Central L.A. culture and made the sequence hit even harder.

If the series keeps using music this well, the soundtrack could become one of the defining parts of The Drop.

WANDA GETS ROOM TO BECOME SOMETHING MORE

Wanda might be the most interesting connection between the two shows so far. Instead of bringing her back just so longtime fans can recognize a familiar face, the premiere appears committed to actually moving her character forward.

She feels more grounded, more focused and like somebody genuinely trying to build a life after everything she survived. There's almost a redemption quality to it.

That's what makes this direction work. The Drop can acknowledge Wanda's past without trapping her inside of it. We already know what she's been through. Now the more interesting question is who she gets to become after surviving it.

CHARACTER WATCH • WANDA THIS FEELS LIKE A SECOND CHANCE.

The premiere doesn't erase her history. It lets that history give her new story more weight.

LAMAR FEELS IMPORTANT ALREADY

Then there's Lamar. From Episode 1, he immediately gives off the feeling that he's going to be an integral part of the show.

There's a little bit of that Earn from Atlanta energy to him — not because they're the exact same character, but because of the vibe. He feels observant, ambitious and like somebody who's going to have to figure out how to move through increasingly complicated situations as the world around him gets bigger.

That's the type of new character The Drop needs. Wanda gives us a direct emotional connection to the universe we already know. Lamar can help take us somewhere new.

ARTILLERY MAKES AN ENTRANCE

Buddy getting the platform to play Artillery is another part of the premiere I liked. We already know what he can do musically, but this gives him a chance to showcase another part of his skill set.

And the show doesn't waste the opportunity. By the end of the episode, Artillery isn't just somebody standing around the edges of the story — he becomes part of the moment that completely sold me on coming back for Episode 2.

LEON FEELS LIKE A LONG-GAME PIECE

Episode 1 doesn't unload everything with Leon, and it doesn't need to. There's clearly more to come. His history with Wanda and everything he experienced in Snowfall means his presence carries weight even when he isn't dominating the episode.

I definitely expect Leon to be seen throughout the season. The smart move is letting him remain a connection to the original without forcing the new series to revolve around him.

20 MINUTES IN...

THAT ENDING SOLD ME

By about 20 minutes into Episode 1, I was already thinking: yeah, I'm locked in.

Then the final stretch took it another level.

Wanda, Lamar and his brother heading over into Blood territory already raises the tension. Then Artillery comes out flowing and openly dissing the Crips.

That's when the show really announced what kind of world we're entering — one where music, neighborhood politics, gang culture and ambition are all going to collide.

And then the episode just ends. No long cooldown. No easing out of the moment. It just leaves you there.

MY REACTION
“Yeah… this show might really be a go-to.”

DIFFERENT FROM SNOWFALL — IN A GOOD WAY

That's really my biggest takeaway from the premiere. The Drop already feels a little different from Snowfall, and I mean that as a compliment.

The original show had a heaviness hanging over it. Even when Franklin was winning, danger always felt like it was waiting somewhere nearby. The Drop feels looser and younger. The music is more prominent. The '90s backdrop gives it another texture. The newer characters bring another kind of ambition to the story.

But the DNA is still there: Wanda, Leon, South Central and the consequences of everything that came before. That gives the show history without forcing it to become Franklin Saint Part Two.

4DK VERDICT • EPISODE 1

I'M ALL THE WAY LOCKED IN.

Episode 1 didn't just bring the Snowfall universe back. It gave me a reason to care about where it's going next.

The music is already elite. Wanda's redemption arc has real potential. Lamar looks like somebody worth investing in. Artillery made an immediate impression. Leon gives the show a connection to its past without overwhelming the new story.

One episode obviously isn't enough to know whether The Drop can eventually reach the heights of Snowfall. But I don't think it needs to.

After Episode 1, I'm not watching because I want another Snowfall. I'm watching because I want to see what The Drop becomes.
