NBA Nightcap • Sunday, November 8, 2026 • All 7 games final

REAVES GOES FOR 43. FLAGG DEALS 12 DIMES. THE EAST SENDS A MESSAGE.

Seven games. Seven complete breakdowns. A night of star turns, survival wins and the early-season standings beginning to take shape.

PLAYER OF THE NIGHT: AUSTIN REAVES — 43 POINTS

Game 01 / 07
PHILADELPHIA 76ERS 127 — CHICAGO BULLS 121

PHILADELPHIA IS WINNING THE POSSESSION WAR

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Tyrese Maxey 27 PTS • Jaylen Brown 29 PTS • LeBron James 22 PTS • Joel Embiid 18 PTS / 10 REB

Josh Giddey 19 PTS / 15 AST • Matas Buzelis 19 PTS

4DK Deep Dive

Philadelphia seized the lead late in the second quarter and never gave it back, but the six-point final margin should not disguise how much work it took to finish Chicago. The Sixers dominated the second-chance battle 33–12, a massive possession advantage in a game decided by two trips down the floor. Maxey kept the offense moving, Brown delivered the scoring punch, LeBron supplied another productive all-around presence, and Embiid made the interior work count.

The Turning Point

Chicago trimmed the gap to three with a little over five minutes left. Kentavious Caldwell-Pope answered with a three, preventing the Bulls from turning a strong rally into a genuine change of control.

What It Means

At 9–2, Philadelphia has a legitimate case among the East’s early pace-setters. The scoring depth is dangerous, but the offensive-rebounding edge is the kind of repeatable advantage that matters in playoff basketball. Chicago’s seventh consecutive loss is a warning: competitive stretches mean little without defensive possessions finished.

Game 02 / 07
LOS ANGELES LAKERS 121 — DETROIT PISTONS 116

AUSTIN REAVES TAKES THE KEYS — AND DELIVERS

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Austin Reaves 43 PTS • Walker Kessler 12 PTS / 16 REB

Daniss Jenkins 31 PTS • Kevin Huerter 24 PTS

4DK Deep Dive

No Luka Dončić, no excuse. Los Angeles raced to a 40–14 lead early in the second quarter with astonishing first-quarter shotmaking, but Detroit made this a real examination of the Lakers’ composure. Reaves did more than accumulate 43 points: he carried the offense through Detroit’s extended pressure and still had the legs to hit a difficult late three. Kessler’s rebounding and timely three-point play provided the structural support.

The Turning Point

Detroit’s 13–2 late-third-quarter run reduced the margin to 84–81. Cade Cunningham missed the second free throw that could have tied the game at 90 with 9:14 remaining. Reaves’ off-balance three with 46 seconds left was the defining late-game shot.

What It Means

The Lakers move to 9–2 and have another example of their ability to win in different ways. Detroit drops to 6–3 after opening 6–1; the Pistons deserve credit for the comeback, but the disastrous opening quarter forced them to spend the entire night chasing. Reaves earns 4DK Player of the Night.

Game 03 / 07
MINNESOTA TIMBERWOLVES 115 — UTAH JAZZ 100

MINNESOTA KEEPS WINNING WITHOUT ANT

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Ayo Dosunmu 26 PTS • LaMelo Ball 24 PTS / 5 AST • Rudy Gobert 10 PTS / 15 REB

Darryn Peterson 26 PTS / 6 REB • Keyonte George 19 PTS / 8 AST

4DK Deep Dive

The Timberwolves are making the Anthony Edwards absence an identity test rather than an excuse. Dosunmu gave Minnesota efficient scoring pressure, Ball created rhythm and transition chances, and Gobert supplied the back-line rebounding that kept Utah from finding sustained second opportunities. Minnesota’s offensive versatility is becoming the story: it is not simply replacing Edwards’ shots, it is finding different routes to quality offense.

The Turning Point

Ball’s midcourt steal and breakaway dunk made it 70–63 in the third. Bones Hyland then injected seven quick points and Minnesota stretched the lead to 90–72. Utah opened the fourth 0-for-9 from the floor and went almost four minutes without scoring.

What It Means

Minnesota is 8–3, has won four straight and is 5–2 without Edwards. That is serious early-season resilience. Utah is 1–9 after eight straight losses, with Keyonte George leaving with an ankle sprain and Lauri Markkanen unavailable; Peterson’s 26 are an encouraging individual bright spot in a difficult team picture.

Game 04 / 07
DALLAS MAVERICKS 130 — BROOKLYN NETS 93

COOPER FLAGG PUTS ON A PLAYMAKING CLINIC

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Cooper Flagg 25 PTS / 12 AST • Kyrie Irving 21 PTS • Daniel Gafford 18 PTS / 3 BLK

Michael Porter Jr. 19 PTS

4DK Deep Dive

This was not just a blowout; it was Dallas showing how much cleaner its offense can look when Cooper Flagg is both a scoring threat and a distributor. The rookie’s career-high 12 assists came alongside 25 points, and his ability to feed Gafford repeatedly made the Mavericks more difficult to guard than a simple isolation-heavy attack. Kyrie’s 21 provided a second source of creation, while Dallas hit 14 of 35 from deep.

The Turning Point

Dallas built the early advantage and never let Brooklyn create a meaningful comeback window. Flagg shot 8-for-13 overall and 3-for-5 from three, combining efficiency with creation in under 30 minutes.

What It Means

At 6–4, the Mavericks have a useful blueprint: Flagg can orchestrate, not merely finish. Brooklyn falls to 2–8, and Michael Porter Jr.’s 19 were not enough to offset a 37-point loss. The Nets must find a defensive foundation before their offense can consistently matter.

Game 05 / 07
BOSTON CELTICS 117 — MILWAUKEE BUCKS 97

BOSTON RESPONDS LIKE AN EASTERN CONTENDER

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Paul George 27 PTS • Jayson Tatum 21 PTS

Kyle Kuzma 14 early first-quarter PTS

4DK Deep Dive

Milwaukee punched first, but Boston never let the early 11-point deficit become the story. The Celtics settled in defensively, found their spacing, and flipped the game with a 17–7 second-quarter burst. Paul George delivered 16 points in that period alone, while Jayson Tatum had already scored 16 in the first. That balance between primary stars and the interior work of Neemias Queta and Mitchell Robinson is exactly why Boston’s ceiling deserves respect.

The Turning Point

Boston led 58–45 at halftime, pushed the advantage to 20 early in the third, and Payton Pritchard buried three consecutive fourth-quarter threes to make sure there was no late drama.

What It Means

Boston moves to 8–2 and continues to look like one of the East’s strongest teams. Milwaukee drops to 1–9. The Bucks can point to Kuzma’s explosive opening, but a good five-minute burst means little when the offense cannot sustain pressure across four quarters.

Game 06 / 07
MIAMI HEAT 114 — SACRAMENTO KINGS 108

GIANNIS STABILIZES MIAMI IN SACRAMENTO

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Giannis Antetokounmpo 29 PTS / 14 REB / 3 AST / 2 BLK • Klay Thompson 23 PTS / 7 3PM • Bam Adebayo 18 PTS / 8 REB

Darius Acuff Jr. 32 PTS / 10 AST

4DK Deep Dive

Miami needed this one. Giannis attacked the interior for 29 points on 14-of-23 shooting and owned the glass, while Klay Thompson’s seven threes stretched Sacramento’s defense in a way the Kings could not consistently solve. Bam’s work gave the Heat a second interior presence. The game was tied at 55 before Miami’s 13–4 run produced a nine-point halftime lead, but the Heat still had to respond to a furious Sacramento rally.

The Turning Point

The Kings tied it at 95 with 6:12 remaining on De’Andre Hunter free throws. Miami won the remainder 19–13, a much-needed late-game response after allowing a 16-point advantage to disappear.

What It Means

Miami improves to 4–6 and has a chance to build on the result, but surrendering a large lead remains a concern. Sacramento is 1–9 and still winless at home. Acuff’s 32 and 10 show enormous individual promise; the larger roster needs to give him more defensive support, especially with Domantas Sabonis sidelined by knee soreness.

Game 07 / 07
LOS ANGELES CLIPPERS 139 — NEW ORLEANS PELICANS 123

THE CLIPPERS FINALLY BREAK THROUGH

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Darius Garland 24 PTS / 10 AST • Brook Lopez 20 PTS • Gradey Dick 15 PTS • Keaton Wagler 15 PTS

Trey Murphy III 26 PTS • Zion Williamson 25 PTS • Jeremiah Fears 25 PTS

4DK Deep Dive

Los Angeles finally turned its offensive talent into a complete victory, ending a seven-game skid with a season-high 139 points. Garland controlled tempo and generated quality looks, Lopez supplied interior and perimeter scoring, and Dick and rookie Wagler gave the Clippers timely complementary offense. The team shot 54.8 percent from the field. New Orleans scored enough to win plenty of nights, but could not get stops when it mattered.

The Turning Point

New Orleans briefly led 78–77 after an 18–10 start to the second half. The Clippers answered, then opened the fourth with a decisive 15–6 run to lead 117–101, avoiding the late collapses that had haunted them in recent games.

What It Means

The Clippers improve to 2–7. One win does not erase seven consecutive defeats, but it gives them a reference point for their offensive ceiling and fourth-quarter execution. New Orleans falls to 4–6; the 25-point efforts from Zion and Fears, plus Murphy’s 26, underscore how costly the defensive breakdowns were.

4DK Player of the Night

AUSTIN REAVES — 43 POINTS

With Luka Dončić sidelined, Reaves carried the Lakers past Detroit, answering a furious comeback with an improbable late three and clutch free throws. The distinction is not simply the point total; it is that the biggest baskets came when the game was slipping away.

Nightcap Verdict

Philadelphia and Boston keep setting the East’s standard, Minnesota is building real depth through adversity, and Dallas is discovering Flagg’s playmaking ceiling. But Sunday belongs to the Lakers’ No. 15.
