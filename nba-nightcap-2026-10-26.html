4DK NBA NIGHTCAP · OCTOBER 26, 2026

NINE GAMES.
ALL THE STORIES.

Ant goes off. Cleveland survives. Houston rises. Boston makes a statement. Golden State finds a new spark. We break down every game and what comes next.

Cleveland Cavaliers 127, Minnesota Timberwolves 125 (OT)

CLEVELAND OUTLASTS MINNESOTA — BUT DON'T OVERREACT

Anthony Edwards delivered the kind of offensive performance that reminds the league why Minnesota belongs in the contender conversation. He attacked downhill, found his three-point rhythm and repeatedly created offense when the Wolves needed a response. Cleveland still found a way to win, and that deserves credit. The Cavaliers kept applying pressure, worked through a deficit and forced Minnesota to execute deep into an exhausting night.

Minnesota had built a substantial lead, but the second half told a different story. The Cavaliers' sustained pressure mattered, and so did the Wolves playing on the second night of a back-to-back. Legs get heavy, decisions become harder, and an opponent with Cleveland's talent can capitalize on even small openings. Edwards carried a tremendous burden, and the final possessions offered opportunities Minnesota couldn't quite convert.

The 4DK verdict: This was a tough loss, not a referendum on Minnesota's ceiling. There is no need to invent a crisis around Chris Finch's rotations or the supporting cast after one October game. The NBA season is an 82-game grind. Cleveland earned the victory; Minnesota remains an elite, top-five-caliber team with legitimate championship aspirations.

Golden State Warriors 134, Denver Nuggets 114

GOLDEN STATE'S FUTURE IS STARTING TO SHOW

Golden State's win over Denver gave us something more interesting than a single impressive result: another reason to watch how this roster evolves around Stephen Curry. The Warriors can still produce the ball movement, shooting and disruptive defensive stretches that make them a difficult matchup for anybody. But our attention keeps returning to Yaxel Lendeborg and what he could mean beyond the Curry era.

Lendeborg's appeal is the completeness of his game. He can contribute without hijacking possessions, connect actions on offense, rebound, defend and make smart decisions. There's a version of his development in which he becomes an immediate successor to some of Draymond Green's connective responsibilities while providing a more varied scoring threat. Our early offensive projection is 15–17 points per game if his role and opportunities continue to grow. That is a projection, not a statistical claim.

The complementary pieces matter, too. We like what Williams and De'Anthony Melton bring to the roster, and Jimmy Butler's eventual return could reshape the team's rotation and late-game options. There are enough ingredients here for the Warriors to trouble strong opponents on a given night.

The 4DK verdict: Don't mistake a dangerous opponent for an automatic contender. We see Golden State as a dangerous play-in-level team until it proves it can sustain this quality over time. The long-term intrigue around Lendeborg may be just as compelling as the immediate results.

Dallas Mavericks 113, Miami Heat 109

DALLAS EXPOSES MIAMI'S EARLY FIT PROBLEMS

Miami's early struggles are not just about losing close games. They're about a roster that has yet to demonstrate how its best players fit together. Giannis Antetokounmpo can fill a box score and still leave the Heat searching for the offensive organization they need. Against Dallas, the larger issue was impossible to ignore: Miami has star power, but not enough cohesion.

Bam Adebayo looks uncomfortable alongside Giannis. Both can affect a game in multiple ways, yet the spacing, timing and division of offensive responsibilities have not consistently brought out Bam's strengths. This is not an argument that either player lacks talent. It is a question of whether the structure around them gives both room to thrive.

The absence of a true floor general magnifies those problems. Miami needs someone who can establish tempo, get Bam touches in productive areas, reduce the burden on Giannis and create cleaner opportunities for the supporting cast. Dallas took advantage of a Heat team still trying to find its offensive identity.

The 4DK verdict: Miami's 0–4 start warrants a significant Power 30 reassessment. We discussed a possible fall outside the top 20, but no exact ranking is locked. Giannis remains a superstar; that alone does not make the Heat a cohesive contender. They'll have to show the fit works before we restore their preseason standing.

Boston Celtics 134, Chicago Bulls 99

DON'T SLEEP ON BOSTON: TATUM STILL HAS A SQUAD

Anybody expecting Boston to disappear from the Eastern Conference picture after Jaylen Brown's departure should reconsider. The Celtics still have Jayson Tatum, a superstar capable of anchoring a high-level offense, and a supporting cast that understands how to play around him. Their emphatic win over Chicago reinforced the point: this is a different Boston roster, not necessarily a diminished basketball program.

Tatum remains the foundation. His size, scoring versatility and ability to create opportunities for teammates give the Celtics a reliable centerpiece. The supporting cast doesn't need to replace Brown with a single identical player; it needs to combine its strengths to make Boston difficult to defend. Payton Pritchard's willingness to assume a bigger offensive role is especially important. His shooting stretches defenses and gives Tatum another trusted option.

Boston's most convincing stretches come when the ball moves, the defense creates momentum and multiple players contribute. That formula can travel across an 82-game season even when the roster looks different from previous years.

The 4DK verdict: We're projecting Boston as a top-five team in the East with legitimate 50-win potential, assuming reasonable health. Not an automatic title favorite, but absolutely a team that deserves respect in the conference race.

Houston Rockets 120, Atlanta Hawks 110

HOUSTON IS PUTTING THE LEAGUE ON NOTICE

Houston's appeal goes far beyond one marquee name. Kevin Durant gives the Rockets an elite shot creator and championship-tested scorer. Alperen Şengün gives them an unusually versatile offensive hub in the frontcourt. Amen Thompson's development adds another layer of athleticism, defensive disruption and transition pressure. And Fred VanVleet's return provides the experienced organizing presence that can help all those pieces function together.

Şengün is central to the identity. He can score inside, facilitate from the high post and punish defenses that devote too much attention to Durant. When an offense can flow through a skilled big man without sacrificing the threat of a superstar perimeter scorer, opponents have difficult choices to make on nearly every possession.

Amen is the swing piece we keep watching. His defensive versatility and ability to influence the game without monopolizing the ball already matter. As his offensive skill set grows, Houston's ceiling rises with it. VanVleet's presence is equally important for different reasons: he can control tempo, initiate sets and reduce the amount of improvisation required from the stars. That's the kind of stability that becomes especially valuable in the postseason.

The Rockets also have the defensive personnel to challenge multiple styles. Their athleticism and lineup flexibility give them options that teams built around only two offensive stars often lack.

The 4DK verdict: Houston is our No. 2 team in the developing Power 30. That ranking reflects Durant's scoring, Şengün's playmaking, Amen's growth, VanVleet's leadership and the team's defensive potential. The Rockets aren't merely building for tomorrow. Their expectations are about winning now.

Oklahoma City Thunder 125, Phoenix Suns 114

OKC'S DEPTH RESPONDS — THE INJURY QUESTIONS DON'T DISAPPEAR

Oklahoma City has championship-level talent, but the injuries to Jalen Williams and Cason Wallace change the equation. A strong performance against Phoenix shows the Thunder still have players capable of answering the call. It does not settle the question of whether they can sustain their standard over months without important contributors.

Ajay Mitchell's reported scoring breakout illustrates why organizational depth matters. The Thunder can ask younger and complementary players to take on larger roles because the system gives them a framework. Still, replacing an established contributor for a night is not the same as replacing his impact throughout a long season. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and Chet Holmgren face added responsibility as the rotation adjusts.

Phoenix's challenge was to make Oklahoma City uncomfortable enough to disrupt that rhythm. When the Thunder establish early offensive control and their supporting cast contributes, opponents have to defend more than the headline stars. The Suns couldn't consistently turn their own offensive production into the stops required to reverse the game.

The 4DK verdict: Give OKC credit for every win, but don't hand it the top ranking on reputation. We have already discussed moving the Thunder down because of the injuries and early-season concerns. Their exact Power 30 placement remains open while we evaluate the rest of the league. Let the games answer the question.

Charlotte Hornets 110, Washington Wizards 103

BRANDON MILLER IS SENDING A MESSAGE

Charlotte's future depends on developing a reliable identity, and Brandon Miller offers one of the clearest reasons to be optimistic. His reported explosive scoring performance against Washington highlighted the variety of ways he can create offense: perimeter shooting, pull-ups, mismatches and attacks off the dribble. For a young wing, that versatility is the difference between occasional hot nights and the possibility of becoming a dependable first-option scorer.

The challenge now is consistency. Miller doesn't need to deliver a spectacular point total every night, but Charlotte does need him to keep making efficient decisions when defenses adjust. His development becomes even more meaningful when teammates handle the physical work around him: rebounding, defending, screening and creating extra possessions. A young star needs an ecosystem, not simply more shots.

Washington has a different set of questions. The Wizards possess a mix of established talent and developing players, but individual production doesn't automatically translate into coordinated offense or dependable defense. Finding clear roles, especially around the frontcourt, is part of their challenge. Their late push showed resilience; Charlotte's ability to protect its advantage showed progress of its own.

The 4DK verdict: Miller's upside is the headline. Charlotte should continue building an environment in which his scoring translates into team success. Washington needs to turn flashes of talent into consistent, connected basketball. Both franchises have work to do, but Miller gave the Hornets another reason to believe.

Milwaukee Bucks 110, Indiana Pacers 102

MILWAUKEE GETS A MUCH-NEEDED RESPONSE

Milwaukee needed a win after a difficult start, and the Bucks found one by responding to adversity. Falling behind early could have turned into another frustrating night. Instead, Milwaukee tightened its defense, competed harder on the glass and produced a stronger finish against Indiana. The response matters because teams trying to establish a new identity have to prove they can change the direction of a game.

Tyler Herro's reported all-around production underscores the importance of having a perimeter scorer who can do more than look for his own shot. When Herro mixes scoring with playmaking and rebounding, the offense has a better chance to function as a unit. The interior contributions of Kel'el Ware are also important. A young big who rebounds, finishes and provides size can create the extra possessions that swing close games.

Indiana has its own lesson to absorb. Tyrese Haliburton's ability to organize the offense remains a major strength, but a team can't rely on a productive opening half to carry it through the finish. The Pacers' late offensive slowdown gave Milwaukee the opening it needed. Sustained execution, especially when the opponent increases its defensive pressure, separates a solid performance from a road victory.

The 4DK verdict: One win doesn't erase Milwaukee's early struggles, but it offers a blueprint: organized offense, stronger rebounding and connected defense. Indiana has to learn how to finish games it controls. This was a useful early-season test for both teams.

Memphis Grizzlies 110, Utah Jazz 105

MEMPHIS STACKS ANOTHER WIN; UTAH'S YOUTH KEEPS FIGHTING

Memphis came away with the victory, but Utah made the Grizzlies work for it. That's the kind of road result that can help a team develop confidence. Not every win will be clean, and a team with serious ambitions has to be able to withstand an opponent's late push without losing its structure.

Cedric Coward's reported productive night is a reminder that complementary players can shape a game without dominating the ball. Rebounding, efficient scoring and timely decisions are valuable because they allow the offense to remain balanced. Memphis needs contributors who can make winning plays around its primary creators, especially when the game becomes physical or possessions slow down.

Utah's loss shouldn't obscure the developmental picture. Keyonte George continues to have opportunities to grow as a scorer and organizer, while Ace Bailey represents another young piece with intriguing two-way potential. The Jazz are trying to turn those individual flashes into a coherent team identity. That process rarely moves in a straight line, and close losses can still reveal useful progress.

The 4DK verdict: Memphis deserves credit for finding a way to win and keeping its momentum. Utah's young core remains worth watching, particularly how George and Bailey develop their decision-making and consistency. The Grizzlies are trying to stack victories; the Jazz are still discovering what their next foundation can become.

THE NIGHTCAP FINAL WORD

This night offered the full range of early-season NBA storylines: Anthony Edwards carrying a massive offensive load; Cleveland showing resilience; Golden State uncovering intriguing possibilities for its future; Miami searching for an identity; Boston demonstrating that its competitive window hasn't simply closed; Houston looking like a serious contender; and Oklahoma City testing the limits of its depth.

Elsewhere, Brandon Miller offered a glimpse of star potential, Milwaukee found a response it badly needed, and Memphis kept stacking results while Utah's young players continued learning.

October doesn't decide the championship. But it reveals which teams understand their identity, which teams need to adjust and which players are ready to take on more.

That's the Nightcap: not just the final score, but the performance, the adjustments and the bigger picture.
