4DK NBA • 2026–27 Division Outlook

THE NORTHWEST HAS NO EASY LANE.

OKC expects the Finals. Denver still has Jokic. Minnesota believes LaMelo unlocks Ant. Portland has too many point guards and one huge Ja decision. Utah is building for what comes next.

By Kcdatruth • 4 Da Kulture • September 2026

This division has three teams with real Western Conference ambitions and two teams trying to figure out exactly what their future should look like. The standard at the top is championship-level. At the bottom, development and asset management matter more than forcing wins.

OKLAHOMA CITY THUNDER

THE REGULAR SEASON ISN'T THE TEST ANYMORE.

60+ wins • Top seed • NBA Finals

OKC is still elite. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander is going to give them around 30 a night, and if Jalen Williams stays healthy he can get back to looking like the clear No. 2 option he was in 2024–25. That alone keeps the Thunder in the top tier of the West.

Chet Holmgren is where the pressure changes. He was still Third Team All-NBA and an All-Defense level player, but the way Wemby overwhelmed him in Game 7 of the Western Conference Finals cannot just be brushed off. Chet has to get stronger, play with more physicality, and prove in the postseason that OKC can trust him against the biggest frontcourt matchups in the league.

This year is not about proving the Thunder are good. Everybody already knows that. It is about health, Jalen Williams staying on the floor, Chet answering the playoff questions and Shai leading them back to the Finals.

The standard is no longer “contender.” The standard is 60-plus wins, the top seed and a trip back to the NBA Finals.

Playoff barNBA Finals
Swing pieceChet's physicality
DENVER NUGGETS

JOKIC STILL MAKES THEM DANGEROUS.

55 wins • Top-3 West • WCF

Nikola Jokic keeps Denver competitive by himself, and his production is needed more than ever as the roster around him and Jamal Murray has thinned out. As long as Jokic stays healthy and Murray avoids missing extended time, Denver still belongs in the contender conversation.

The problem is the supporting cast. Over the last few years Denver has lost KCP, Michael Porter Jr. and Peyton Watson. Murray has already proven he can be the No. 2 on a championship team, so the answer is not replacing him. The need is another dependable wing scorer — somebody who can recreate the role MPJ gave Denver at his best, punish mismatches and take pressure off the Jokic-Murray two-man game.

Murray is coming off a career year at 25.4 points and 7.1 assists per game, and at 29 he is right in the middle of his prime. He has averaged 23.7 points across 85 career playoff games. Denver needs that 2023-level playoff Murray again — maybe even flashes of the Bubble version — because the supporting cast does not give Jokic as much margin for error anymore.

Jokic is the constant. The question is whether Murray can turn back into that superstar second option when Denver needs another deep run.

MINNESOTA TIMBERWOLVES

LAMELO MAY BE THE MISSING PIECE.

52–55 wins • Ant MVP leap • WCF minimum

Minnesota lost Julius Randle and Naz Reid, but the fit between LaMelo Ball and Anthony Edwards could make the offense cleaner than it was before. LaMelo is the point guard Ant has never really had — a creator who can initiate the offense, get his own bucket and take the ball out of Edwards' hands when Ant does not need to create every possession from scratch.

LaMelo is the clear No. 2. If he can stay healthy and give Minnesota around 20 points and 9 assists, that is a win. If he does it for 70-plus games, it is an even bigger one. Around them, Jonathan Kuminga can provide scoring whether he starts or becomes the microwave option off the bench, Ayo Dosunmu adds another creator, and Jaden McDaniels should have a chance to push his scoring toward 15 a night with LaMelo setting the table.

The roster is still young: Edwards, Ball and McDaniels are 25, Kuminga is 24 and Ayo is 26. Gobert remains the defensive anchor, while Terrence Shannon Jr. and Cody Williams add more developmental depth. The biggest concern is health. LaMelo has missed big chunks in the past, Ant missed 21 games last season and also dealt with playoff injuries, and Minnesota still has to settle its best starting group with DiVincenzo hurt.

This is a season of expectations. Can LaMelo be the missing piece on a real contender? Can Ant go from superstar to MVP-level player?

52–55 wins, a healthy LaMelo, an MVP leap from Ant and at least another Western Conference Finals trip. That's the bar.

PORTLAND TRAIL BLAZERS

TOO MANY POINT GUARDS. ONE BIG DECISION.

45 wins • 7 seed / Play-In • Define the backcourt

Portland has too many point guards. Ja Morant, Scoot Henderson, Damian Lillard and Jrue Holiday cannot all be part of the long-term plan, and at least two of them should be gone by the deadline. Scoot has the youth argument. Dame may retire as a Blazer. Jrue is the obvious veteran trade chip. Ja is the real swing decision.

At 27, Ja is still young enough and athletic enough to become elite again in the right situation. Portland should use the first half of the season to see what he looks like before making a permanent call. If Ja looks like a star again, maybe he and Deni Avdija become the foundation together — and maybe Ja becomes the alpha. If it does not work, Portland can still turn him into a major future package.

Avdija should not be asked to do too much. He is 25, entering his prime and coming off an All-NBA-caliber 24/7/7 season. The ideal next step is either building on that production or finding a dynamic with Ja that lets both players carry less of the burden. Donovan Clingan also has to become the defensive anchor Portland needs.

A successful Portland season is not just wins. It is Ja and Deni proving they can coexist, Clingan owning the paint and the front office finally cleaning up the guard room.

UTAH JAZZ

DEVELOP THE KIDS. STACK THE ASSETS.

Development • Draft position • Around 35 wins

Utah should not rush this rebuild. The season is about development and draft position: how good can Darryn Peterson become, is Keyonte George a star in the making, is Ace Bailey legit, and what can the Jazz get if they eventually decide to move Lauri Markkanen?

Keyonte enters 2026–27 as the No. 1 option and the architect of the offense. At 22, if he can average around 23 points and 8 assists, help Utah win roughly 35 games and make life easier for Peterson and Bailey, that is real progress. Peterson can gradually grow into more responsibility instead of being forced into the franchise role immediately, while Bailey and possibly Isaiah Collier remain important pieces of the future.

Lauri should stay until the deadline unless Utah gets blown away earlier. Then the front office should see what kind of future value is available. Jaren Jackson Jr., at 27, fits the timeline better, but even he should not be untouchable if a team is willing to send enough draft capital back.

If Utah can develop Keyonte, Peterson and Ace while flipping Lauri and/or JJJ into more future picks, that's a win by itself.
