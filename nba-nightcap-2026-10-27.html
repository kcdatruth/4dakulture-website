01 / BOSTON / BROOKLYN NETS @ BOSTON CELTICS

TATUM SAVES BOSTON AT THE BUZZER

BOSTON 125 · BROOKLYN 124
FINAL / OVERTIME
BOX-SCORE LEADERS
BOS — Jayson Tatum: 40 PTS · 5 REB · 8 AST
BOS — Paul George: 20 PTS · 7 REB · 7 AST
BKN — Michael Porter Jr.: 27 PTS · 12 REB
BKN — Julius Randle: 24 PTS · 9 REB
BKN — Mikel Brown Jr.: 17 PTS · 5 REB · 5 AST

Boston won the game. Brooklyn made sure it felt like a loss for long stretches. The Celtics needed all 40 of Jayson Tatum’s points, a 17-foot jumper at the overtime horn and several second chances just to escape a Nets team that entered the night without a win. It was an incredible ending, but the deeper story was how often Boston made the night harder than it needed to be.

The Celtics shot just 2-for-16 from three in the first half and trailed 61–56 at the break. Their third-quarter response — outscoring Brooklyn 30–21 — briefly looked like the correction a more experienced team should make. Yet Brooklyn kept attacking. Michael Porter Jr. gave the Nets reliable perimeter scoring and rebounding, Julius Randle supplied physical creation, and rookie Mikel Brown Jr. refused to disappear when the game tightened.

The final seconds of regulation were chaos. Derrick White finished a three-point play to put Boston up 114–111. With the Celtics in position to close it out, Mitchell Robinson missed two late free throws. Brown buried a corner three with 0.2 seconds left to force overtime. That is the kind of shot a rookie remembers for years — and it was almost the beginning of a stunning road upset.

In overtime, Brown’s three pushed Brooklyn ahead 124–119 with about two minutes remaining. Boston kept finding ways back, helped by Randle fouling out. Brown then missed a late layup with the Nets protecting a one-point lead. Tatum got the final possession, found his spot near the left elbow and delivered the buzzer-beater. That sequence shows both sides of a young player’s education: fearless shot-making and a decision that can change a result.

For Boston, the win makes it three straight after the opening-night collapse in Detroit. Tatum looked like the player determined to own the biggest moments, and George’s 20 points, seven rebounds and seven assists were important support. But the Celtics cannot make a habit of needing rescue acts against struggling opponents. For Brooklyn, 0–4 looks ugly in the standings, yet this was a competitive performance that offered real developmental value.

THE TURNING POINT — BROWN FORCES OT; TATUM WINS IT. Brooklyn had the tying three with 0.2 seconds left in regulation and a five-point lead in overtime, but could not close. Tatum’s 17-footer at the horn was the final answer.

THE 4DK READ — Tatum is our Player of the Night. Forty points and the final shot deserve that recognition. Boston should celebrate the closer while studying why the door stayed open. Brooklyn should keep empowering Brown without pretending the late-game lessons do not matter.

UP NEXT: Boston hosts Chicago on Friday. Brooklyn hosts Detroit on Friday.

02 / NEW YORK / DETROIT PISTONS @ NEW YORK KNICKS

THE CHAMPS FINALLY ANSWER BACK

NEW YORK 111 · DETROIT 97
FINAL
BOX-SCORE LEADERS
NYK — Karl-Anthony Towns: 27 PTS · 20 REB
NYK — Jalen Brunson: 23 PTS · 7 AST
NYK — Mikal Bridges: 12 PTS · 4 MADE THREES
DET — Paul Reed: 22 PTS · 9 REB
DET — Cade Cunningham: 19 PTS · 9 REB · 8 AST

There was pressure on New York before the ball went up. The defending champions were 0–3, their offensive rhythm had been inconsistent, and a fourth consecutive loss would have made the start historically bad for a title defender. Detroit arrived 3–0, fresh off the kind of opening week that made the Pistons look like an early Eastern Conference threat. The Knicks needed a response, not another explanation.

Detroit started like the more comfortable team, scoring 38 in the first quarter and taking a 13-point lead. Paul Reed, filling in for the injured Jalen Duren, was active around the basket and supplied 22 points and nine rebounds. Cade Cunningham still controlled stretches of the game as a genuine floor general. But New York did not fold. The Knicks cut the deficit to one by halftime, then completely changed the game coming out of the locker room.

The third quarter was the answer. New York outscored Detroit 37–13. Brunson, who had been struggling with his shot, made his first three attempts of the half as the Knicks opened on an 11–0 run. Mikal Bridges finally found rhythm from deep, and Karl-Anthony Towns took over the interior battle. Towns finished with 27 and 20 rebounds — the kind of dominant rebounding night that matters as much as his scoring for this particular roster.

The most telling number was not the final margin. It was Detroit’s 13-point third quarter. The Pistons had been playing with pace, confidence and physicality, but their half-court offense stalled as New York raised the pressure. Without Duren, Detroit had less of the interior presence that normally stabilizes possessions, and the Knicks punished the stretches when the Pistons failed to get clean looks.

This does not erase Detroit’s 3–0 start or the progress we have seen from Cunningham’s leadership. It does, however, show what a contender must handle on the road: the other team’s best adjustment, a crowd getting loud and a physical game suddenly turning against you. For New York, the win matters because it came with a recognizable identity — aggressive rebounding, a committed second-half response and multiple contributors rather than one player trying to save everything.

THE TURNING POINT — THE THIRD QUARTER: NEW YORK 37, DETROIT 13. An 11–0 opening burst flipped a one-point halftime edge into a 62–50 lead; the Knicks never surrendered control.

THE 4DK READ — The Knicks finally played like champions with something to prove. Towns’ 27-and-20 performance was the anchor. Detroit is still dangerous, but the offense has to stay functional when Cade sees extra attention and Duren is unavailable.

UP NEXT: Detroit hosts Charlotte Wednesday. New York visits Chicago Wednesday.

03 / SAN ANTONIO / SACRAMENTO KINGS @ SAN ANTONIO SPURS

WEMBY AND THE SPURS PUT ON A FIRST-HALF CLINIC

SAN ANTONIO 130 · SACRAMENTO 103
FINAL
BOX-SCORE LEADERS
SAS — Victor Wembanyama: 29 PTS · 10 REB · 5 BLK
SAS — Julian Champagnie: 26 PTS · 8 MADE THREES
SAS — Dylan Harper: 13 PTS · 6 REB · 6 AST
SAC — De’Andre Hunter: 24 PTS
SAC — Domantas Sabonis: 20 PTS · 14 REB · 8 AST
SAC — Zach LaVine: 22 PTS

San Antonio needed to make its home floor look like an advantage again. After losses to Oklahoma City and Houston, the Spurs had shown flashes but had not sustained the execution expected from a team with Victor Wembanyama at the center of its future. Against Sacramento, they did not merely improve — they overwhelmed the Kings before halftime.

Eighty points in two quarters. Twenty-three first-half assists. Thirteen first-half threes. The ball moved faster than Sacramento could rotate, and the Spurs turned early offensive confidence into a flood. Wembanyama scored 15 in the first quarter, finishing lobs and attacking the rim with the kind of force that changes how opponents position their help defenders. Julian Champagnie punished the other end of those rotations, hitting eight first-half threes and finishing with 26 points.

Wembanyama’s final line — 29 points, 10 rebounds and five blocks in 27 minutes — captures both ends of the impact. The blocks matter because his presence changes decisions before shots are even taken. But we also liked the variety around him. Devin Vassell added 14 points and eight assists, Dylan Harper supplied 13 points, six rebounds and six assists, and all five Spurs starters reached double figures. San Antonio looked like a team creating advantages together, not one waiting for its superstar to solve every possession.

Sacramento’s offensive production was not completely absent. De’Andre Hunter scored 24, Zach LaVine added 22 and Domantas Sabonis produced 20 points, 14 rebounds and eight assists. The problem was at the other end. The Kings repeatedly gave up clean perimeter looks and struggled to handle Wembanyama near the basket. Even when Sacramento scored, it could not consistently force San Antonio into difficult possessions.

The Spurs move to 2–2 with their first home win. That is not a declaration that they have solved every matchup issue — Houston’s frontcourt had already exposed areas they must improve — but this was a clear example of the upside when the spacing, passing and defensive activity connect. Sacramento falls to 0–4. The Kings have to find a defensive baseline quickly, because offensive talent cannot keep rescuing possessions that begin with breakdowns.

THE TURNING POINT — SAN ANTONIO SCORED 80 BEFORE HALFTIME. The Spurs led 80–54 at the break after a 39-point first quarter and 41-point second; the result was effectively decided before the fourth.

THE 4DK READ — Wemby set the tone, but this was a complete offensive response. Champagnie’s shooting stretched the floor and the supporting cast moved the ball with purpose. Sacramento needs better perimeter resistance and transition organization immediately.

UP NEXT: San Antonio visits Utah Wednesday. Sacramento visits New Orleans Thursday.

04 / LOS ANGELES / PORTLAND TRAIL BLAZERS @ LOS ANGELES LAKERS

PORTLAND STEALS THE SHOW — AND THE UNBEATEN RECORD

PORTLAND 136 · LOS ANGELES 129
FINAL
BOX-SCORE LEADERS
POR — Deni Avdija: 23 PTS · 9 AST
POR — Ja Morant: 23 PTS
POR — Jrue Holiday: 22 PTS
POR — Damian Lillard: 21 PTS · CLUTCH THREE
LAL — Luka Dončić: 41 PTS · 9 AST
LAL — Austin Reaves: 26 PTS · 7 AST

This was the late-night matchup we wanted: two unbeaten teams, a loaded Portland backcourt and Luka Dončić coming off a 52-point masterpiece. The Lakers scored 129 and still lost. That tells you everything about how much Portland’s offensive balance and shot-making stressed Los Angeles over four quarters.

Luka came out in complete control, scoring 16 in the first quarter and finishing with 41 points and nine assists. Austin Reaves gave him 26 and seven, while Sandro Mamukelashvili provided 17 off the bench. The Lakers led 73–68 at halftime and stretched the advantage to 79–68 early in the third. They were getting plenty of quality offense; the problem was that the defensive stops never came consistently enough to separate.

Portland did not need one player to match Luka basket for basket. Deni Avdija finished with 23 points and nine assists, Ja Morant added 23, Jrue Holiday scored 22 off the bench and Damian Lillard contributed 21. Robert Williams III gave the Blazers 14 points and 10 rebounds, while Donovan Clingan also collected 10 boards. Portland shot 59 percent from the field and 18-for-36 from three. That is an enormous amount of offensive pressure to withstand, especially when the ball is moving and multiple guards can make the next play.

The swing came in the final stretch of the third and the opening of the fourth. Los Angeles had been up 97–88, but Portland scored 14 of the last 20 points of the third to close within 103–102. Lillard and Toumani Camara then hit threes to give the Blazers control early in the fourth. The Lakers answered and tied the game at 116, which made the closing possessions a genuine test of composure.

Portland passed that test. Avdija hit a three to make it 128–124. Morant answered the Lakers’ push with a floater and another basket. After Luka cut the margin to three with just over a minute left, Lillard buried the defining three with 43 seconds remaining. Dončić and Reaves both missed on the ensuing possession, and the Blazers closed out the seven-point victory.

Portland is now 3–0 and has a legitimate early statement win. The combination of Morant’s rim pressure, Lillard’s shooting, Avdija’s connective play and Holiday’s veteran defense gives the Blazers several ways to attack. For Los Angeles, the 3–1 start is still strong, but this is the clearest reminder yet that elite offense alone will not guarantee wins in the West. The Lakers need better point-of-attack resistance, cleaner rotations and more possessions finished with a rebound.

THE TURNING POINT — LILLARD FROM DEEP, 43 SECONDS LEFT. With Portland ahead by three, Dame hit the dagger to push the lead to 135–129. Los Angeles never recovered.

THE 4DK READ — Portland deserves full credit: this was not Luka failing to carry the Lakers. It was the Blazers having more answers. The Lakers’ defensive consistency is the story to monitor after their first loss.

UP NEXT: Los Angeles faces the Clippers Wednesday. Portland hosts Memphis Thursday.

4DK PLAYER OF THE NIGHT

JAYSON TATUM.
FORTY AND THE FINAL WORD.

There were worthy candidates everywhere: Wembanyama’s two-way dominance, Towns’ 27-and-20 answer, and the four-headed Portland offense that took down the Lakers. But Tatum gave us the night’s defining moment. Forty points, eight assists and a 17-foot buzzer-beater in overtime. That is what a closer is supposed to do.

The other lesson? The league is not waiting for anyone to settle in. New York has a pulse. San Antonio has a blueprint when the ball moves. Portland has more than one way to beat you. Boston has its superstar — and plenty to clean up. We’ll keep watching the details that matter beyond the final score.
