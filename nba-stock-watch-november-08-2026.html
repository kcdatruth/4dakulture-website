Ajay Mitchell

18.9 PPG · 3.7 RPG · 3.3 APG · 10 games 2026–27 early-season snapshot
Oklahoma City Thunder

THE NEXT MAN UP IS BECOMING THE MAIN EVENT

Mitchell has turned Oklahoma City’s injury-related uncertainty into a stage for his own emergence. His 18.9 points per game through ten appearances represent a meaningful leap from 13.8 last season, and the 35-point eruption against Memphis was the kind of performance that forces opponents to change their scouting report. His growth is about more than one explosive night: he is attacking with conviction, finding opportunities in the middle of the floor and showing the composure to shoulder possessions that once belonged to established options.

The caution is that a larger scoring role does not automatically make a player a primary star. His perimeter shot has been inconsistent, and Oklahoma City will eventually have to redistribute touches as its rotation gets healthier. But that is precisely the test worth watching. Mitchell has earned the right to be evaluated as a real offensive weapon, not merely a replacement for an injured teammate. For 4DK, this is the clearest early-season upward trajectory on the board.

The tactical change is worth studying. When Oklahoma City is missing proven creators, Mitchell is not just absorbing shots that somebody else would have taken. He has to recognize when a defense is loading toward the ball, decide whether to turn the corner or pull up, and get the offense organized before the possession stalls. Those are lead-guard responsibilities, and the speed at which he is learning them matters more than one scoring outburst.

There is also a healthy skepticism built into this selection. A young player can look like a revelation when a depleted rotation gives him freedom, then struggle once opponents build a scouting report around his tendencies. We want to see Mitchell maintain efficiency, keep his turnovers manageable and create for teammates even when his own jumper is cold. The more those skills survive defensive adjustments, the harder it becomes to treat him as a temporary solution.

For 4DK, the most interesting question is what happens when the Thunder get healthier. The easy assumption is that Mitchell returns to a smaller role. The stronger possibility is that he has earned a permanent place in closing-lineup discussions because his growth gives Oklahoma City another way to attack. That is the difference between a hot start and a real breakout.

4DK WATCHPOINT — OKC'S UNEXPECTED LIFELINE: With Jalen Williams, Chet Holmgren and Cason Wallace sidelined, Mitchell has an opportunity to establish himself as a long-term starter alongside Shai Gilgeous-Alexander. How long can he sustain this elevated production as opponents adjust? And if Mitchell continues delivering as a scorer, creator and defender deep into the season, just how high is Oklahoma City's ceiling? Could his emergence be the difference between a championship contender surviving its injury crisis and a team losing its place among the West's elite?
02 / THE RISERS

Stephen Curry

Golden State Warriors

THE STANDARD DOES NOT AGE

Curry is forcing another conversation about the difference between age and decline. At 38, he continues to command defensive attention well beyond the three-point line, warping possessions before he even touches the ball. Golden State’s winning stretch has put his leadership and shot creation back under the spotlight. When an offense can still be organized around a veteran’s movement, spacing and late-clock ingenuity, that veteran remains a foundational player.

The 4DK Top 50 discussion is unavoidable. Curry entered our current board at No. 11, and his case for returning to the top ten deserves serious scrutiny. We will not move him on reputation alone; efficiency, availability and the Warriors’ performance must hold up over time. But the early message is clear: the league has not solved Stephen Curry, and his value is not measured only by points per game.

The game plan against Curry has been familiar for more than a decade: crowd him beyond the arc, chase him through screens and make somebody else beat you. The fact that he continues to generate efficient offense against that attention is a testament to his conditioning and basketball intelligence. His gravity opens cuts and passing lanes for teammates before the shot clock ever becomes a crisis.

His scoring surge also creates a roster-building question for Golden State. The Warriors can still get elite offense from Curry, but how much help can they give him when opponents switch everything and force secondary creators to make decisions? The emergence of younger contributors is encouraging, yet the championship standard demands more than another spectacular night from the franchise icon.

We are not handing Curry a Top 50 promotion because of nostalgia. His case has to rest on present-day impact: shot creation, efficiency, late-game control and the wins his play helps generate. Right now, those categories are giving the 4DK board a legitimate reason to reconsider where he belongs.

4DK WATCHPOINT — WHAT'S NEXT: At 38, Curry is still producing elite scoring and gravity. Can Golden State turn his current shooting brilliance into sustainable wins without asking him to carry every difficult late-clock possession? His 4DK Top 50 case moves higher if his efficiency and team impact remain this strong.
03 / THE RISERS

Luka Dončić

Los Angeles Lakers

THE OFFENSE STILL RUNS THROUGH LUKA

The Lakers’ offensive identity remains inseparable from Dončić’s command of pace, angles and defensive matchups. He can manipulate a possession without rushing it, turning small advantages into layups, free throws or clean perimeter looks. His recent scoring aggression reinforces why he belongs in the highest tier of 4DK’s player discussions. The question is not whether he can pile up points; it is whether the Lakers can consistently convert that creation into efficient team offense.

Luka’s stock rises when his dominance translates beyond the box score. That means involving teammates early, managing turnovers and sustaining defensive effort in the biggest moments. His individual ceiling is already established. This early-season stretch is about the level of control he can exert over a team with championship expectations.

The raw scoring headline is impossible to ignore: the early-season league leaders have Dončić at the very top of the points-per-game column. But 4DK is interested in how he is getting there. His strength allows him to dictate contact, his change of pace prevents defenders from timing his drives, and his passing forces the weak side to choose between helping at the rim and surrendering an open shooter.

That creates a different responsibility for the Lakers' coaching staff. A team can be dependent on an extraordinary creator without being entirely predictable. Off-ball movement, early actions and productive minutes when Luka sits are essential if Los Angeles wants to translate individual brilliance into postseason resilience. His best possessions make everybody dangerous; his least effective ones can become a test of how long one player can hold the ball.

The stock is rising because the standard for Luka is already MVP-level. A good night is no longer enough to move the needle. What matters is whether he can turn his command of the game into a sustained run of high-efficiency team offense while limiting the defensive compromises opponents try to expose.

4DK WATCHPOINT — WHAT'S NEXT: Luka's MVP-level scoring is already undeniable. The question is whether he can keep Los Angeles efficient around him while reducing costly turnovers and maintaining defensive engagement. The Lakers need his control of the entire game—not simply another 40-point masterpiece.

Jayson Tatum

Boston Celtics

BOSTON'S CENTER OF GRAVITY

Tatum’s early-season performances have reasserted his role as the player around whom Boston’s biggest offensive decisions revolve. His value comes from the full menu: scoring over smaller defenders, attacking closeouts, making the next pass and absorbing the pressure that follows every Celtics possession. On a team with changing roles and a demanding standard, his consistency gives the rest of the rotation a reference point.

The 4DK evaluation is not simply a celebration of high scoring nights. We are watching whether Tatum’s decision-making and two-way impact remain strong as Boston’s supporting cast adapts. His stock is rising because he is answering questions about leadership and responsibility, not because anyone suddenly discovered that he can score.

Tatum's growth as a playmaker is central to this conversation. Defenses have spent years trying to load up against his scoring, and the answer is not always another difficult jumper. When he reads the second defender early and delivers the ball to a cutter or shooter, Boston gets the benefit of a superstar who is willing to make the possession easier rather than merely more dramatic.

The Celtics also need him to set a defensive standard. He has the size to switch, rebound and cover ground across multiple matchups, which becomes especially valuable when lineups change around him. A truly elite wing affects both sides of the court, and Boston's ceiling depends on getting that version of Tatum regularly.

We are evaluating him against a high bar because he has earned one. The question is not whether Tatum belongs among the league's best; it is whether this stretch strengthens his argument near the very top of the 4DK hierarchy. His scoring, facilitating and leadership must be judged together.

4DK WATCHPOINT — WHAT'S NEXT: Tatum's next step is turning individual command into complete Celtics possessions: read the second defender early, elevate the supporting cast and set the defensive tone. A sustained two-way stretch, not a single scoring explosion, would strengthen his case near the very top of 4DK's player hierarchy.
05 / THE RISERS

Walker Kessler

Los Angeles Lakers

A RIM PROTECTOR WITH REAL LEVERAGE

Kessler’s value is built around possessions that never become highlight clips. He changes drives, challenges shots without needing to chase blocks and gives his guards the confidence to pressure the ball. For a Lakers team built around elite offensive creation, reliable interior protection and efficient finishing are not luxury skills. They are the difference between an exciting roster and a balanced one.

The next step is proving he can stay effective when opponents spread the floor and force him into difficult coverage decisions. His rebounding and finishing must remain dependable, and the Lakers need to maximize his vertical spacing. If those pieces hold, Kessler’s stock rises as one of the more consequential role players in the Western Conference.

The most persuasive part of Kessler's case is that his offensive contributions do not require the Lakers to run plays for him. A hard screen, a timely dive and a clean offensive rebound can all create high-value chances around a ball-dominant perimeter star. That is how a traditional center remains relevant in a modern offense built around spacing and manipulation.

Defensively, the distinction between blocking shots and protecting the rim is important. Kessler does not need to chase every attempt into the stands; he needs to be in the right position early, keep guards from turning the corner too easily and finish the possession with a rebound. Those habits give Los Angeles a more stable defensive identity.

The question is playoff adaptability. Teams with shooting at all five positions will try to drag him away from the paint and make him defend in space. His stock continues upward if he can survive those possessions without sacrificing the interior presence that makes him valuable in the first place.

4DK WATCHPOINT — WHAT'S NEXT: Kessler gives the Lakers the rim protection and vertical finishing their perimeter stars need. Can he stay on the floor when opponents pull him away from the basket and target him in space? His ability to survive those playoff-style matchups will determine how far his stock can climb.

Defenders will close out more aggressively if he makes open jumpers, and then the next read becomes the test: can he attack the closeout, finish through contact or find a teammate before the defense recovers? Orlando's struggles do not erase an individual bright spot. In fact, they make it more important to separate a young player's progress from the team's record. Da Silva deserves this placement because he is beginning to offer solutions to problems the Magic have not consistently solved.

The 4DK standard is now higher. We will not celebrate San Antonio merely for being competitive or exciting. The next step is closing out good opponents, surviving difficult road stretches and showing that the supporting cast can win possessions even when Wembanyama is on the bench.

RJ Barrett

Toronto Raptors

THE CONSISTENCY CASE

Barrett is putting together the kind of steady scoring stretch that can be overlooked when attention gravitates toward flashier stars. His ability to get downhill, absorb contact and contribute on the glass gives Toronto a dependable source of offense. When he is decisive rather than settling, the Raptors have a wing who can pressure defenses without needing every possession designed for him.

The missing piece is outside shooting consistency. Teams will continue testing his jumper, and Barrett must make them pay often enough to keep driving lanes open. His upward stock does not mean automatic admission to the 4DK Top 50, but sustained production in the low twenties with solid rebounding and playmaking would make that debate increasingly difficult to ignore.

Barrett's offensive profile works best when he makes his first move with conviction. He is strong enough to finish through smaller defenders and patient enough to recognize when a help defender has committed. Toronto needs that downhill pressure because it forces opponents to collapse and opens the perimeter for the rest of the rotation.

His progress also deserves to be judged against the expectations that followed him earlier in his career. The conversation used to revolve around whether he would become a primary star. Now the more useful question is whether he can be a reliable, efficient high-level wing who fits alongside other creators. There is no shame in that evolution; it may be the clearest path to maximizing his talent.

The ceiling remains tied to the jumper and defensive consistency. If defenders can ignore him away from the ball, driving lanes shrink. If he makes them respect the catch-and-shoot threat while staying engaged defensively, his overall value rises considerably. That is the next test for a player whose stock is moving in the right direction.

4DK WATCHPOINT — WHAT'S NEXT: Barrett's downhill scoring is giving Toronto dependable offense. The next jump depends on whether his outside shot stays credible and his defensive engagement holds up, turning a productive scorer into a more complete winning wing.

Myles Turner

Milwaukee Bucks

THE IMPACT IS NOT MATCHING THE EXPECTATION

Turner was supposed to give Milwaukee reliable spacing, rim protection and a veteran presence capable of stabilizing its frontcourt. The early production has instead raised questions about offensive rhythm and the balance between his responsibilities and opportunities. A center can contribute without dominating the scoring column, but when the shots are not falling, the floor-spacing promise loses some of its force.

4DK is not writing off his defensive instincts or treating a small sample as a final verdict. We are asking whether Milwaukee is getting the complete version of the player it expected. Better offensive efficiency, stronger rebounding possessions and a clearer role are the quickest paths back up.

Turner's value has always been built on an unusual combination: legitimate shot-blocking instincts and enough perimeter shooting to pull an opposing center away from the paint. That combination is especially attractive in a frontcourt that needs both defensive protection and offensive space. When one half of that formula becomes unreliable, the roster's balance changes.

The difficult part of evaluating Turner is separating scheme from execution. A big man can be asked to cover too much ground defensively, and a stretch center can go several possessions without touching the ball. But established veterans are expected to make an impact within imperfect circumstances. Rebounding, screen quality and decisiveness on open attempts still fall within his control.

The path out of Stock Down is not complicated: make the open shots, finish possessions on the glass and give Milwaukee the kind of defensive stability his reputation promises. If those fundamentals return, the early criticism will look temporary. Until then, the fit remains a question rather than a solution.

4DK WATCHPOINT — WHAT'S NEXT: Milwaukee needs the full Turner package, not just occasional blocks or open jumpers. Watch his three-point consistency, defensive positioning and ability to finish possessions on the glass. If the spacing and rim protection don't work together, the frontcourt fit remains under pressure.

Darius Garland

Los Angeles Clippers

CREATION WITHOUT CONTROL

Garland’s scoring and passing talent remain evident, but his early turnover burden has made his overall impact harder to defend. A lead guard is judged not only by the opportunities he creates but also by how consistently he gets his team into the right action. When the ball changes hands too often, even strong counting stats can conceal lost possessions and disrupted rhythm.

The Clippers need a more controlled version of Garland, not necessarily a less aggressive one. His stock falls because the expectation is polished decision-making from an established offensive organizer. A stretch of cleaner possessions and more efficient late-game execution could change the narrative quickly.

Garland is at his best when his dribble creates an advantage that the next pass or shot can exploit. He has the touch to punish a defender who ducks under a screen and the vision to find a rolling big. But those strengths lose value when possessions end in careless turnovers or when he over-dribbles into a defense that has already recovered.

The Clippers' context matters. Changing teams, learning a new hierarchy and building timing with unfamiliar teammates can produce early mistakes even for an accomplished guard. We should account for that transition rather than treat every miscue as evidence of decline. Still, the job of an established lead guard is to accelerate chemistry, not merely wait for it.

For 4DK, the next evaluation point is the assist-to-turnover balance and the quality of his late-game decisions. Garland does not need to become a different player. He needs to make the aggressive, creative version of himself more dependable from possession to possession.

4DK WATCHPOINT — WHAT'S NEXT: Garland's scoring talent isn't in question. His Stock Down case turns on whether he can reduce turnovers, control the tempo and create cleaner late-game possessions as he settles into the Clippers' offense. Better decision-making must accompany the production.

Payton Pritchard

Boston Celtics

THE BREAKOUT THAT HAS NOT ARRIVED

Boston entered the season with a familiar question about Payton Pritchard: was he ready to move from valuable rotation guard to a consistent high-volume offensive option? The expectation was not that he would suddenly become the Celtics’ best player. It was that the confidence, range and self-created shooting he had displayed in a smaller role could translate into a more substantial nightly scoring burden. For 4DK, a push into the high teens—and perhaps the low twenties on his best stretches—was a reasonable bar to evaluate.

The early return has not matched that projection. His scoring has hovered around 12.9 points per game, while reduced minutes and less consistent shot-making have complicated the idea of a true breakout. There is an important distinction between regression and opportunity: a guard cannot manufacture a twenty-point average when the rotation gives him fewer possessions. The question is whether Pritchard is commanding more responsibility through his play or simply waiting for the offense to find him.

Pritchard can still influence a game without posting a headline scoring number. He stretches defenses with his range, keeps the ball moving, competes on the perimeter and can turn a broken possession into a quick shot. Those contributions matter to a Celtics team with championship expectations and an evolving supporting cast. Yet they are also the baseline of what Boston already knew it had. Stock Up would require something new: greater scoring consistency, a more reliable off-the-dribble attack and stronger impact when opponents run him off the line.

That is why this selection is about the missed leap rather than a claim that Pritchard has become a bad player. A high-efficiency run could still make him one of Boston’s most important offensive complements. Until then, the distance between his established skill set and the larger role we expected remains one of the more interesting early-season disappointments.

4DK WATCHPOINT — WHAT'S NEXT: Pritchard was expected to take a bigger scoring leap, but his minutes and efficiency have gone the other way. Bobby Portis

Miami Heat
7.3 PPG · 6.9 RPG · 1.9 APG · 37.9% FG 2026–27 · 9 games through Nov. 5

THE SPARK HAS GONE QUIET — AND MIAMI NEEDS IT BACK

When Miami brought Bobby Portis into a frontcourt featuring Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo, the appeal was obvious: a veteran big man who could space the floor, attack second units and bring an edge to the glass. Portis had spent years establishing himself as one of the league’s more dependable reserve scorers. Last season in Milwaukee he averaged 13.7 points, 6.4 rebounds and hit 45.6 percent of his threes. His value was never about being a primary option; it was about giving a contender a second wave of offense that opposing benches had to respect.

Through nine games with the Heat, that version has been difficult to find. Portis is averaging 7.3 points, 6.9 rebounds and 1.9 assists in 20.8 minutes while shooting 37.9 percent from the field and 33.3 percent from three. The rebounding has held up reasonably well, but the scoring has dropped by more than six points per night. His minutes are also down from 24.2 last season, so it would be misleading to treat every lost point as pure decline. The more revealing problem is efficiency: fewer opportunities have coincided with a much less reliable jumper.

The game-by-game trend is especially hard to ignore. Portis scored 13 against Charlotte on October 24 and recorded 10 points with 12 rebounds against Dallas two nights later. Those performances looked like the familiar version of Portis. But the next stretch brought 11 against Atlanta, eight against Indiana, two against the Lakers, four against the Clippers and then a scoreless 14-minute appearance against Golden State on November 5. He averaged only five points over his last five appearances in this snapshot. The Clippers game illustrates the nuance: Portis grabbed ten rebounds and handed out five assists, so he was not entirely ineffective even when the scoring disappeared.

The 4DK concern is about fit as much as form. Miami does not need Portis to dominate possessions alongside two high-profile frontcourt teammates. It does need him to make defenses pay when the ball swings his way. If defenders can help off him without consequences, one of the reasons to carry a stretch big disappears. His minutes could stabilize if he reestablishes himself as a credible spot-up threat, rebounds with force and brings the same emotional edge that made him a fan favorite in Milwaukee.

Stock Down is not a verdict that Portis can no longer play. Nine games are too few for that. It is an acknowledgment that the Heat are receiving less offensive value than his recent track record suggested, and that the role adjustment alone does not explain everything. A few strong shooting nights could move this conversation quickly, but right now the gap between expectation and delivery is real.

4DK WATCHPOINT — WHAT'S NEXT: Portis is still contributing on the glass, but Miami needs the dependable scoring and floor spacing that defined his best Milwaukee seasons. Watch whether his three-point accuracy recovers and whether he can produce double-digit offense consistently in a reduced role.

THE NEW REALITY OF JOEL EMBIID

Embiid’s early-season scoring and efficiency have fallen well below the level that once made him one of basketball’s most overwhelming offensive forces. The contrast with his near-27-point production last season is stark. Philadelphia can still benefit from his rebounding, passing and defensive presence, but the old assumption that he will dominate every matchup offensively no longer feels automatic.

The injury context is essential, not optional. Managing his health is a rational priority, and judging a physically limited player against his absolute MVP peak would be unfair. Yet the Sixers still need a reliable offensive anchor when the postseason pressure rises. 4DK’s verdict balances both truths: we should adjust the expectation, but we should not ignore the production gap.

Embiid's offensive dominance used to create a predictable problem for every opponent: defend him one-on-one and risk foul trouble, or send help and concede open shots elsewhere. When his mobility and touch are compromised, that calculus changes. Defenders can crowd his catches more confidently, and Philadelphia must work harder to manufacture the clean looks that once arrived naturally through his presence.

The health conversation cannot be treated as a footnote. A player managing recurring physical limitations may need a different shot diet, shorter bursts and fewer high-impact possessions. Philadelphia would be reckless to chase regular-season box scores at the expense of having him available later. But the team also cannot build its entire postseason identity around a version of Embiid that may not be there every night.

The 4DK evaluation sits between those realities. We are not asking him to recreate his absolute MVP peak on command. We are asking whether he can still be the reliable two-way anchor that changes a playoff series. Better efficiency, stronger defensive mobility and consistent availability would matter more than one isolated 30-point night.

4DK WATCHPOINT — WHAT'S NEXT: The Sixers shouldn't sacrifice Embiid's health chasing his old MVP box scores. But they still need him to become an efficient, reliable two-way anchor.
