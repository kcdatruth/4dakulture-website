NBA • 4DK NIGHTCAP • OCTOBER 21, 2026

ELEVEN GAMES. NEW ERAS. NO EASY ANSWERS.

ANT DROPS 45. LAMELO ARRIVES IN MINNESOTA. GIANNIS DEBUTS IN MIAMI. DAME, HALI AND KYRIE RETURN. AD PUTS WASHINGTON ON NOTICE.

Opening week is about more than box scores. We examine how teams fit together, what changed, and what the first night tells us about the season ahead.

Minnesota 131, Miami 106

ANT’S 45-PIECE, LAMELO’S DEBUT AND GIANNIS IN MIAMI

Edwards poured in 45 points and grabbed 10 rebounds, hitting nine threes. LaMelo Ball contributed 21 points and six assists in his Minnesota debut. Giannis scored 35 in his first Miami game, but Minnesota’s 21 made threes and 52–34 rebounding advantage exposed the Heat’s perimeter coverage and defensive rebounding. The Ball–Edwards pairing offers two complementary creators: LaMelo can initiate the half-court offense while Edwards attacks tilted defenses. Miami must create better spacing around Giannis and Bam Adebayo.

HOW MINNESOTA TOOK CONTROL

Minnesota did more than ride a hot shooting night. Its spacing forced Miami to choose between protecting the paint and chasing shooters, while its 52–34 rebounding advantage eliminated second-chance opportunities for the Heat. The 23–7 surge across the second and third quarters was the separation point.

WHAT WE ARE WATCHING

LaMelo can relieve Edwards of full-time creation duties without taking away his aggression. For Miami, Giannis scoring 35 on 13-for-19 is encouraging, but the Giannis–Bam pairing needs more reliable perimeter support and better point-of-attack defense.

THE EDWARDS–BALL DYNAMIC

The difference between having two scorers and two compatible creators is timing. Ball can advance the possession before the defense loads up, then let Edwards attack the second rotation. That means Minnesota can create an advantage without Edwards having to manufacture one against five stationary defenders. The nine made threes are the headline, but Edwards’ ten rebounds also speak to the physicality he brought to the game.

THE MIAMI QUESTION

Giannis finishing efficiently does not automatically translate into efficient team offense. The Heat have to decide where Bam operates when Giannis drives, who occupies the weak-side corners and which perimeter defenders can survive without constant help. Minnesota’s rebounding dominance compounded every missed assignment.

4DK VERDICT: Edwards set the tone, Ball broadened Minnesota’s offensive possibilities, and Miami learned that adding Giannis is the beginning of a system-building challenge—not the end of it.

Lakers 127, Warriors 105

LUKA GETS A VERTICAL THREAT

Luka Dončić finished with 28 points, eight rebounds and seven assists. Walker Kessler scored 26 on perfect 12-for-12 shooting in his Lakers debut, while Austin Reaves added 23. Kessler changes the geometry of Luka’s pick-and-roll offense: opponents must decide whether to stop the ball or protect the rim. Golden State received 23 from Stephen Curry but struggled to match Los Angeles inside. The Lakers’ next challenge is sustaining this balance against bigger Western contenders.

WHY KESSLER MATTERS

Kessler’s 26 points on 12-for-12 shooting demonstrated the value of a dependable lob target. Luka can punish defenses that trap him or collapse on his drives, and Kessler’s size gives Los Angeles an interior presence at the other end. Reaves adds a third source of offensive creation.

THE TEST AHEAD

The question is whether that interior efficiency and defensive protection hold up against the West’s biggest frontcourts. Golden State still has Curry’s shot-making, but its inability to match the Lakers’ size was an early warning.

THE PICK-AND-ROLL CHESS MATCH

Kessler’s perfect shooting is not just a remarkable box-score entry. It is a warning to defenses that load up on Luka. When the center steps high, Luka can deliver the ball behind the defense; when that defender stays back, he can probe into space. Kessler also gives Los Angeles a natural offensive rebounding target.

WHY REAVES STILL MATTERS

Reaves scoring 23 means opponents cannot devote every possession to denying Luka. His ability to handle secondary actions, attack a closeout and create his own look keeps the offense from becoming predictable. Against Golden State, Los Angeles’ size and shot selection established the terms of the game.

4DK VERDICT: The Lakers did not simply add another big man. They added a finisher whose strengths directly address how opponents defend Luka, while Reaves keeps the offense from being one-dimensional.

Houston 128, Dallas 110

DURANT AND JABARI DELIVER, KYRIE RETURNS

Kevin Durant and Jabari Smith Jr. scored 30 apiece, and Alperen Şengün added 23 as Houston took control with a 38–12 second quarter. Fred VanVleet’s return restores organization and ball security. Kyrie Irving scored 15 in his return for Dallas, while second-year forward Cooper Flagg posted 32. The Mavericks have two major offensive weapons, but Houston’s superior connectedness and defensive pressure made the difference.

THE GAME-BREAKING QUARTER

Houston’s 38–12 second quarter transformed a competitive matchup into a rout. Durant and Jabari Smith Jr. scoring 30 each illustrates the pressure opponents face when Houston’s perimeter options are clicking, while Şengün can operate as a scorer and connective passer.

DALLAS HAS A LONGER VIEW

Kyrie’s 15 points in his return were an important milestone. Flagg’s 32 showed how much offensive responsibility he can handle, but the Mavericks must build consistency around both players and avoid the defensive breakdowns that turned this opener.

HOUSTON’S SECOND-QUARTER STATEMENT

A 38–12 quarter is more than a hot stretch. It reflects one team consistently finding its preferred shots while preventing the opponent from resetting. Durant and Jabari Smith Jr. scoring 30 apiece forced Dallas to defend multiple scoring zones, and Şengün’s playmaking made it harder to shade help toward one option.

THE MAVERICKS’ NEXT STEP

Kyrie’s return brings another late-clock creator, but a player returning from a major injury must be integrated carefully. Flagg’s 32 points show how much responsibility he can handle; Dallas now has to define when Kyrie runs the offense, when Flagg initiates and how the defense holds up when both are hunting opportunities.

4DK VERDICT: Houston looked connected and dangerous across its lineup. Dallas has individual answers, but the Rockets exposed how much team-level organization still matters.

Washington 128, Milwaukee 116

AD AND TRAE BEGIN A NEW ERA

Anthony Davis delivered 34 points and 12 rebounds in his Washington debut, while Trae Young supplied 20 points and 13 assists. Their inside-out partnership gives the Wizards a clear offensive identity. Milwaukee got 24 from Tyler Herro and 22 from Jaime Jaquez Jr., a fourth-year player in his first season with the Bucks, but Washington’s decisive second-half surge changed the game. The Wizards still await injured rookie AJ Dybantsa.

WASHINGTON FINISHED STRONG

The Wizards turned the game with a 21–4 fourth-quarter run and a 77–60 second-half edge. Davis was a force inside, while Trae’s 13 assists gave Washington a steady playmaking source. The pairing creates immediate pick-and-roll problems for defenses.

MILWAUKEE’S RESET

Herro and fourth-year wing Jaime Jaquez Jr. offered useful scoring in the first season after Giannis. Jaquez is not a rookie; this is his first year with the Bucks. Milwaukee’s biggest issue was closing: good stretches meant little once Washington controlled the final quarter.

DAVIS AND TRAE: TWO DIFFERENT PRESSURES

Trae’s passing stretches a defense horizontally through ball screens and weak-side reads. Davis puts pressure on it vertically as a roller and near the basket. The more consistently Washington links those actions, the harder it becomes for opponents to choose where to send help. Davis’ 12 rebounds also help end possessions on defense.

THE FOURTH-QUARTER LESSON

Washington’s late run showed what happens when stops lead to organized offense. Milwaukee had productive scoring from Herro and fourth-year wing Jaime Jaquez Jr., but its defensive interior and late-game decision-making failed at the worst time. Washington should be encouraged without assuming every night will break that cleanly.

4DK VERDICT: AD looked like the centerpiece Washington envisioned, and Trae supplied the connective playmaking. Milwaukee’s new era will be judged by whether its defensive structure catches up to its scoring talent.

Indiana 123, New Orleans 120

HALIBURTON RETURNS AND SIAKAM CLOSES

Tyrese Haliburton returned with 23 points and Pascal Siakam scored 30, including 18 in the fourth quarter. Indiana overcame a late deficit with the ball movement and timely shot creation that define its best offense. New Orleans received 22 from Zion Williamson and 23 from Dejounte Murray, while Yves Missi provided 13 points and 14 rebounds. The Pelicans must convert competitive stretches into disciplined closing possessions.

THE FOURTH-QUARTER RESPONSE

Indiana entered the final period down six. Haliburton then scored eight of the Pacers’ next ten points during a pivotal stretch, and Siakam poured in 18 in the fourth. The Pacers regained the ability to manage late possessions through a trusted point guard.

WHAT NEW ORLEANS CAN TAKE AWAY

Zion and Dejounte Murray supplied scoring, while Yves Missi’s rebounding and rim presence stood out. The Pelicans competed for most of the night; their next challenge is sustaining defensive discipline when the opposing guards begin controlling tempo.

THE RETURN OF INDIANA’S TEMPO

Haliburton changes the speed of decisions as much as the speed of the game. He moves the ball before defenders are comfortable and gives Siakam chances to catch it in advantageous positions. His scoring burst while Indiana trailed was a reminder that he can take responsibility rather than merely distribute.

SIAKAM FINISHES THE JOB

Siakam’s 18 fourth-quarter points mattered because New Orleans had built a lead and was still producing inside. Zion, Murray and Missi gave the Pelicans multiple points of attack, but Indiana found a reliable closer. Missi’s rebounding and interior activity should not disappear from the conversation because New Orleans lost.

4DK VERDICT: Haliburton’s return restores Indiana’s identity, but Siakam’s ability to own winning time may be just as important when opponents take away the Pacers’ preferred pace.

Memphis 123, Utah 103

TY JEROME’S 45 STEALS THE ROOKIE SPOTLIGHT

Ty Jerome erupted for 45 points on 17-for-25 shooting, overshadowing the first NBA meeting between Cam Boozer and Darryn Peterson. Boozer recorded seven points, 11 rebounds and five assists despite shooting 2-for-15; Peterson finished with nine points. Boozer’s passing and rebounding matter even on a poor shooting night, while Utah must carefully build Peterson’s role alongside Keyonte George and Lauri Markkanen.

JEROME OWNED THE SPOTLIGHT

Jerome’s 45 points on 17-for-25 shooting made him the unexpected headline in a game billed around two rookies. He played with control rather than trying to imitate the explosive style of the departed Ja Morant.

THE ROOKIE FILM ROOM

Cam Boozer’s 2-for-15 shooting showed the adjustment required to score against NBA size, yet 11 rebounds and five assists demonstrate he contributed without scoring. Darryn Peterson’s nine points were a quieter debut; Utah must develop his fit beside Keyonte George and Lauri Markkanen. Neither first game defines the player.

JEROME DID NOT WAIT FOR THE ROOKIES

Jerome’s 45 points on 17-for-25 shooting made Memphis’ transition into a new backcourt era look far less uncertain for one night. His controlled shot-making is a different kind of pressure than Ja Morant’s explosiveness, but it can still bend a defense and open opportunities for teammates.

BOOZER VS. PETERSON: MORE THAN SHOOTING

Boozer’s 2-for-15 shooting stands out, yet 11 rebounds and five assists show he kept contributing. Peterson’s nine points are a modest beginning, but Utah must give him room to build rhythm alongside George and Markkanen. The important early questions are how each rookie responds to length, processes defensive rotations and contributes when his jumper is missing.

4DK VERDICT: Jerome won the night decisively. Boozer and Peterson are longer-term evaluations, and their next several games will tell us far more than their opening shooting percentages.

Toronto 135, Chicago 92

KAWHI RETURNS; CALEB WILSON FACES HIS FIRST TEST

Kawhi Leonard scored 23 points, grabbed six rebounds and recorded four steals in 24 minutes, shooting 5-for-17 and 11-for-11 from the line. Scottie Barnes and Immanuel Quickley also scored 23; RJ Barrett added 20. Toronto forced 27 turnovers and scored 35 points off them. Kawhi’s half-court scoring complements Barnes’ versatility, while their combined defensive size creates enormous pressure. Chicago rookie Caleb Wilson scored 16 with four rebounds and three steals, but shot 7-for-21, missed seven of eight threes and committed nine turnovers. His confidence and defensive tools are real; his decisions against NBA pressure need refinement.

TORONTO’S TWO-WAY CEILING

The Raptors forced 27 turnovers and converted them into 35 points. Kawhi’s 23 points came on 5-for-17 shooting, but his four steals and perfect 11-for-11 at the line showed his impact beyond shot-making. Barnes, Quickley and Barrett provided enough offense that Toronto never had to lean exclusively on Leonard.

CALEB WILSON’S FIRST LESSON

Wilson’s 16 points, three steals and a block showed fearlessness and defensive tools. His nine turnovers, 7-for-21 shooting and 1-for-8 mark from three revealed how much NBA pressure can speed up a rookie. Chicago needs to let Wilson develop alongside Giddey and Buzelis while improving spacing and ball security.

KAWHI’S ROLE IN A DEEPER TORONTO ATTACK

Leonard shot only 5-for-17, yet Toronto won by 43. That is precisely why the fit deserves attention: Barnes can initiate, Quickley can create from the perimeter and Barrett can drive through contact. Kawhi no longer has to be the sole source of half-court offense. His four steals also offered a glimpse of what the Raptors’ length can do defensively.

CALEB WILSON’S FIRST REAL LESSON

Wilson’s nine turnovers and inefficient shooting came against a team forcing mistakes across the floor. The response should be development, not panic. His 16 points and three steals showed activity and confidence; now Chicago needs to help him recognize when to attack, when to move the ball and how to handle aggressive defenders. Giddey and Buzelis have to help build that structure.

4DK VERDICT: Toronto looked deep, disruptive and versatile. Chicago’s prospects remain intriguing, but the Raptors exposed the gap between individual promise and coordinated NBA execution.

Atlanta 132, Orlando 125

NAW ANSWERS PAOLO

Paolo Banchero scored 35 points and delivered 10 assists, but Nickeil Alexander-Walker answered with 31 for Atlanta. Jalen Johnson and Dyson Daniels added 20 each. Atlanta’s balanced offense and late-game execution proved decisive. Orlando has a genuine star in Banchero, but needs sharper defensive rotations and more consistent support in close games.

ATLANTA’S CLOSING EDGE

Paolo’s 35 points and ten assists were outstanding, but Atlanta had more answers late. Nickeil Alexander-Walker’s two decisive threes shifted the final minutes, and the Hawks received 20-point contributions from Jalen Johnson and Dyson Daniels.

THE BIGGER PICTURE

Orlando’s offense has a clear hub in Banchero, but a team with playoff aspirations must limit defensive lapses in tight finishes. Atlanta’s mix of creation, size and late-game shooting deserves another look.

WHY ATLANTA HAD AN ANSWER

Banchero’s 35 points and ten assists showed that Orlando has a player who can carry an offense. But Alexander-Walker’s 31 and his late threes showed how a second or third scoring option can change the ending. Atlanta did not need one player to manufacture every possession.

THE MARGIN IN A CLOSE GAME

Late possessions demand more than talent: they require clean spacing, the right matchup and the willingness to trust a teammate. Atlanta found the decisive shots while Orlando struggled to get the stops it needed. Jalen Johnson and Dyson Daniels adding 20 apiece underscores why Atlanta’s balance matters.

4DK VERDICT: Paolo looked like a franchise centerpiece, but Atlanta’s shared creation and late execution earned the win. Both teams gave us useful early evidence about their ceiling.

Charlotte 110, Brooklyn 103

LIFE AFTER LAMELO STARTS WITH A WIN

Naz Reid produced 20 points, 11 rebounds and seven assists in his Hornets debut. Grayson Allen scored 21, Dennis Schröder added 19, and Brandon Miller contributed 18. Charlotte showed it can distribute creation across the roster without LaMelo. Julius Randle was outstanding in his Brooklyn debut with 33 points, eight rebounds and seven assists, while Michael Porter Jr. added 26. The Nets need more balanced production beyond their two primary scorers.

CHARLOTTE’S NEW FORMULA

Without LaMelo, the Hornets leaned on balance: Reid’s 20 and 11, Allen’s five threes, Schröder’s veteran control and contributions from Miller and Diabaté. Charlotte handled the late possessions more cleanly after Brooklyn cut into its lead.

RANDLE’S BROOKLYN INTRODUCTION

Randle produced 33 points, eight boards and seven assists, while Porter Jr. scored 26. That is a useful foundation, but the Nets need more from the rest of the rotation. Rookie Mikel Brown Jr. can learn without carrying the scoring burden, provided Brooklyn creates clearer roles.

A NEW KIND OF CHARLOTTE OFFENSE

Naz Reid’s 20 points and 11 rebounds gave Charlotte an interior scoring option with perimeter skill. Schröder can organize the group, Allen forces defenders to respect the arc, and Miller can grow into more demanding offensive responsibilities. The result was not a perfect replacement for LaMelo’s creativity; it was a different structure altogether.

BROOKLYN NEEDS THE NEXT LAYER

Randle’s 33 points, eight rebounds and seven assists were an excellent debut, and Porter’s 26 helped keep Brooklyn competitive. But two players accounting for 59 points leaves little margin when the offense stalls. The Nets need more consistent secondary playmaking and sharper late-game possessions. Mikel Brown Jr. should be evaluated within that larger development process.

4DK VERDICT: Charlotte’s balanced contributions won the opener. Brooklyn has two proven scorers, but its ability to close games will depend on the rest of the rotation becoming reliable.

Portland 124, Phoenix 83

DAME TIME RETURNS WITH JA

Damian Lillard scored 25 points and hit six threes in 24 minutes in his Portland return, while Ja Morant added 14 points and eight assists. The Blazers opened with a 21–0 run and never looked back. Morant’s rim pressure and Lillard’s long-range gravity could create an exceptionally difficult defensive puzzle, though their shared ball-handling roles and backcourt defense still require work. Devin Booker scored 23 for Phoenix.

A 21–0 OPENING PUNCH

Portland made its intentions obvious immediately. Dame’s six threes stretched Phoenix’s defense, and Morant’s downhill attacks created a very different kind of threat. The contrasting styles could become a genuine offensive advantage.

THE BACKCOURT QUESTION

Both guards are accustomed to having the ball. The Blazers must find a rhythm that keeps both engaged and addresses their defensive responsibilities. Phoenix, meanwhile, has to find ways to generate offense when Booker faces concentrated attention.

WHY THE BACKCOURT FIT COULD WORK

Morant’s speed pulls help defenders toward the rim; Lillard’s range forces them to remain attached far from the basket. That tension can create open lanes even when neither guard is taking the shot. Dame’s six threes in limited minutes illustrated the shooting threat, while Ja’s eight assists suggested a willingness to facilitate.

THE DEFENSIVE TRADE-OFF

Portland cannot rely on a 21–0 opening run every night. Opponents will test a small backcourt with physical screens and bigger creators, so the wings and frontcourt must protect it. Phoenix’s poor offensive showing made the debut look effortless, but tougher matchups will reveal how sustainable the partnership is.

4DK VERDICT: Dame’s return gave Portland a memorable night. The real story is whether he and Ja can turn contrasting offensive strengths into a durable two-way identity.

Clippers 121, Kings 96

GARLAND’S 30, WAGLER’S DEBUT

Darius Garland scored 30 points on 11-for-19 shooting, and rookie Keaton Wagler contributed 15 points, five rebounds and five assists. Brook Lopez supplied 17 points and 11 rebounds. The Clippers’ 32–16 third quarter turned a manageable lead into a rout. Sacramento received 28 from Zach LaVine and 14 points, 18 rebounds and eight assists from Domantas Sabonis. Rookie Darius Acuff Jr. struggled to 12 points on 5-for-24 shooting. The Clippers’ new identity already has structure, while Sacramento needs better guard efficiency and defensive resistance.

GARLAND SET THE TONE

Garland’s 30 points on 11-for-19 shooting gave the Clippers a reliable offensive leader. Los Angeles never trailed and broke the game open with a 32–16 third quarter. Fourteen steals and a 53–34 bench scoring advantage showed that this was a team-wide performance.

WAGLER AND ACUFF: TWO ROOKIE PATHS

Keaton Wagler’s 15 points, five rebounds and five assists came within the flow of a comfortable win. Sacramento’s Darius Acuff Jr. had a tougher introduction: 12 points on 5-for-24 shooting and 0-for-7 from three. The contrast is a reminder that role, shot quality and team structure shape early rookie results.

GARLAND SETS THE PACE

Garland’s 30 on 11-for-19 shooting gave the Clippers a clear offensive organizer. The 32–16 third quarter reflected a team turning defensive activity into separation, and 14 steals underscored how disruptive Los Angeles could be. Lopez’s 17 points and 11 rebounds gave the younger group a dependable veteran presence.

WAGLER’S OPENING STATEMENT

Wagler’s 15 points, five rebounds and five assists mattered because he did not need the offense built around him to contribute. That is a useful trait alongside a high-usage guard. Sacramento’s Acuff, by contrast, struggled to find efficient shots, finishing 5-for-24. Both rookies will have opportunities to adjust, but their opening circumstances were very different.

4DK VERDICT: Garland and Wagler supplied an encouraging first look at the Clippers’ next chapter. Sacramento must improve shot quality and defensive resistance before its veteran production translates into wins.
