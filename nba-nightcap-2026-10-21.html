Minnesota 131, Miami 106

ANT’S 45-PIECE, LAMELO’S DEBUT AND GIANNIS IN MIAMI

Edwards poured in 45 points and grabbed 10 rebounds, hitting nine threes. LaMelo Ball contributed 21 points and six assists in his Minnesota debut. Giannis scored 35 in his first Miami game, but Minnesota’s 21 made threes and 52–34 rebounding advantage exposed the Heat’s perimeter coverage and defensive rebounding. The Ball–Edwards pairing offers two complementary creators: LaMelo can initiate the half-court offense while Edwards attacks tilted defenses. Miami must create better spacing around Giannis and Bam Adebayo.

4DK VERDICT: Opening night is evidence, not a final judgment. We will track the adjustments and revisit these evaluations as the season develops.

Lakers 127, Warriors 105

LUKA GETS A VERTICAL THREAT

Luka Dončić finished with 28 points, eight rebounds and seven assists. Walker Kessler scored 26 on perfect 12-for-12 shooting in his Lakers debut, while Austin Reaves added 23. Kessler changes the geometry of Luka’s pick-and-roll offense: opponents must decide whether to stop the ball or protect the rim. Golden State received 23 from Stephen Curry but struggled to match Los Angeles inside. The Lakers’ next challenge is sustaining this balance against bigger Western contenders.

Houston 128, Dallas 110

DURANT AND JABARI DELIVER, KYRIE RETURNS

Kyrie Irving scored 15 in his return for Dallas, while second-year forward Cooper Flagg posted 32. The Mavericks have two major offensive weapons, but Houston’s superior connectedness and defensive pressure made the difference.

Memphis 123, Utah 103

TY JEROME’S 45 STEALS THE ROOKIE SPOTLIGHT

Ty Jerome erupted for 45 points on 17-for-25 shooting, overshadowing the first NBA meeting between Cam Boozer and Darryn Peterson. Boozer recorded seven points, 11 rebounds and five assists despite shooting 2-for-15; Peterson finished with nine points. Boozer’s passing and rebounding matter even on a poor shooting night, while Utah must carefully build Peterson’s role alongside Keyonte George and Lauri Markkanen.

Toronto 135, Chicago 92

KAWHI RETURNS; CALEB WILSON FACES HIS FIRST TEST

Kawhi Leonard scored 23 points, grabbed six rebounds and recorded four steals in 24 minutes, shooting 5-for-17 and 11-for-11 from the line. Scottie Barnes and Immanuel Quickley also scored 23; RJ Barrett added 20. Toronto forced 27 turnovers and scored 35 points off them. Kawhi’s half-court scoring complements Barnes’ versatility, while their combined defensive size creates enormous pressure. Chicago rookie Caleb Wilson scored 16 with four rebounds and three steals, but shot 7-for-21, missed seven of eight threes and committed nine turnovers. His confidence and defensive tools are real; his decisions against NBA pressure need refinement.

Atlanta’s balanced offense and late-game execution proved decisive. Orlando has a genuine star in Banchero, but needs sharper defensive rotations and more consistent support in close games.

Julius Randle was outstanding in his Brooklyn debut with 33 points, eight rebounds and seven assists, while Michael Porter Jr. added 26. Damian Lillard scored 25 points and hit six threes in 24 minutes in his Portland return, while Ja Morant added 14 points and eight assists. The Blazers opened with a 21–0 run and never looked back. Morant’s rim pressure and Lillard’s long-range gravity could create an exceptionally difficult defensive puzzle, though their shared ball-handling roles and backcourt defense still require work. Devin Booker scored 23 for Phoenix.

Clippers 121, Kings 96

GARLAND’S 30, WAGLER’S DEBUT

Darius Garland scored 30 points on 11-for-19 shooting, and rookie Keaton Wagler contributed 15 points, five rebounds and five assists. Brook Lopez supplied 17 points and 11 rebounds.
