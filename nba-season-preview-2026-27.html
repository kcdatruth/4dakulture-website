THE BIG IDEA PARITY

Eight seasons. Eight different champions from 2019 through 2026.

The NBA hasn’t had a dynasty in a while. Toronto. Los Angeles. Milwaukee. Golden State. Denver. Boston. Oklahoma City. New York.

Different champion. Different story. Different path.

That is the backdrop for 2026–27. There isn’t one team where I can look at the roster and say everybody else is playing for second. The Knicks are champions, but the East got stronger. San Antonio just reached the Finals with a superstar who is 22 years old. OKC already knows what a championship feels like. Minnesota made a major move. The Lakers finally belong completely to Luka. And Philadelphia basically said fuck patience and went all-in.

Everybody has a flaw. Everybody has something to prove.
EAST
THE CHAMPS ARE STILL STANDING

NEW YORK STARTS ON TOP.
THE REST OF THE EAST IS COMING.

01

New York: The Crown Is Theirs Until Somebody Takes It

The Knicks bring back the core of the team that beat San Antonio in five games for the 2026 title. Continuity matters. So does the fact that repeating in this version of the NBA has become ridiculously hard.

They deserve to open as the team to beat in the East. They just don’t feel untouchable.

PHILADELPHIA

Can This Starting Five Stay Healthy?

Tyrese Maxey. VJ Edgecombe. Jaylen Brown. LeBron James. Joel Embiid. On paper, Philly has the talent to challenge New York immediately.

THE QUESTION:

Can the Sixers get to April with the people they need actually available?

DETROIT

Cade Is Better. Is Detroit Ready?

The Pistons are past the cute-young-team stage. Cade Cunningham keeps ascending, but last postseason showed how quickly playoff basketball can expose weaknesses.

WATCH:

Jalen Duren remains one of the league’s most prominent restricted free agents with extension talks still unresolved.

MIAMI

Giannis + Bam: Monster or Traffic Jam?

Defensively, the idea is terrifying. Offensively, I need to see it. Can Miami create enough space late in games with Giannis and Bam sharing the floor?

CEILING:

If the fit works, Miami can win the conference.

BOSTON

The Burden Moves Even More Toward Tatum

Jaylen Brown is gone and Paul George is in. This isn’t literally Tatum by himself, but this season should tell us even more about how much weight he can carry as the clear centerpiece.

THE QUESTION:

Can Tatum keep Boston in the top tier while the roster changes around him?

ORLANDO

When Does Potential Become Pressure?

Paolo Banchero and Franz Wagner are young and talented. At some point, that has to turn into a deeper playoff run.

THE NEXT STEP:

Paolo has to push from star toward superstar.

TORONTO

Kawhi + Scottie Is Intriguing — If It Becomes Official

The reported Kawhi Leonard reunion remains one of the East’s biggest swing points. The league investigation has concluded, but the trade has still been listed as pending final completion.

THE QUESTION:

If Kawhi lands in Toronto, is there enough around him and Scottie Barnes to get over the top?

CLEVELAND

Does Cleveland Need One More Scorer?

Donovan Mitchell can be the lead scorer on a contender. I’m still wondering whether the Cavs need to move one of their bigs to find another creator next to him.

THE MOVE:

Jarrett Allen? Even Evan Mobley in the right blockbuster? At some point, Cleveland has to decide how aggressive it wants to be.

INDIANA

Can A Healthy Haliburton Put Them Back?

Tyrese Haliburton changes the entire shape of Indiana’s offense. If he is healthy and back to himself, the Pacers are too talented to dismiss.

THE QUESTION:

Was their previous success sustainable — or did they miss their window?

4DK EAST READ

New York has the crown. Philadelphia might have the highest ceiling. Miami has the biggest new variable. Detroit and Orlando are trying to graduate from young-and-good to dangerous.

WEST
THE TWO IMMEDIATE FAVORITES

OKC AND SAN ANTONIO
START IN FRONT.

OKLAHOMA CITY

Can They Solve The Playoffs Again?

SGA still drives an elite offense. Jalen Williams returns healthy. The defensive scheme is going to travel.

And can somebody find Chet when the games matter most? Game 7, anyone?
SAN ANTONIO

The Future Already Arrived

Wemby is 22 and already took San Antonio to the Finals. Stephon Castle looks built to become an elite two-way guard. Dylan Harper is 20 and already showed he can handle playoff pressure.

The scary part: this team is nowhere close to finished.

San Antonio’s Biggest Question Might Be How Much Is Too Much for Wemby

There were times in the Finals where Wembanyama looked gassed. That is understandable — he was playing the biggest games of his life — but it also showed how much San Antonio asks him to do.

You don’t want your 7-foot-4 center to have to be your point guard, point forward, primary scorer and entire defensive system every night.

De’Aaron Fox took criticism in the Finals, and some of it was deserved. But does San Antonio get there without him? And as Dylan Harper keeps ascending, do the Spurs eventually move Fox to fully unleash the younger backcourt?

Is this a 2012 OKC situation all over again?
LOS ANGELES LAKERS

The Luka Era Is Official

LeBron is gone. Austin Reaves is the official No. 2. This is Luka Dončić’s team now — the Snowtime Lakers.

Can Luka take them to another level? Or are we about to watch Luka and AR play isolation basketball for 48 minutes? More importantly: can this team defend enough in May?

DENVER

How Much Longer Can Jokic Fix Everything?

Joker is still Joker. Jamal Murray can still score. The supporting cast keeps getting thinner.

Denver needs a real third option so every rough possession doesn’t become: Joker, fix this shit.

MINNESOTA

Is This Finally Ant’s Year?

LaMelo Ball gives Anthony Edwards a different kind of creator next to him. If LaMelo stays healthy and plays winning playoff basketball, Minnesota’s ceiling changes.

And if Jaden McDaniels takes another offensive step? That’s when this starts looking like a Finals team.

GOLDEN STATE

Steph Is Still Steph. Is That Enough in 2027?

Stephen Curry still makes you dangerous. I’m just not sure his existence alone still makes Golden State a championship plan against younger, deeper teams.

PHOENIX

Trade DBook… Maybe.

I’m just saying: Devin Booker could be a hell of a second option on a real contender instead of carrying a mediocre Suns team forever.

HOUSTON

Contender or Still Just Potential?

A healthy Fred VanVleet returns to a group with Kevin Durant, Amen Thompson and Alperen Şengün.

Is KD still elite at this age? Can Amen make the scoring jump — and actually shoot? And here’s the one Rockets fans might hate: is Şengün overrated?

DALLAS

Which Timeline Wins?

Cooper Flagg is the future. Kyrie Irving is returning healthy. Do the Mavericks keep Kyrie and let him help Flagg grow, or move him while he can still bring back value?

PORTLAND

The Most Interesting Mess in the West

Ja Morant joins a backcourt that already includes Damian Lillard, Jrue Holiday and Scoot Henderson. That is a lot of guards.

Can Ja resurrect his career? Was Deni Avdija’s breakout real? And if Portland doesn’t become a playoff fixture, which guard gets moved first?

NEW ORLEANS

Same Question. Different Year.

Are y’all finally trading Zion or what?

4DK WEST READ

OKC and San Antonio begin in front. Minnesota, the Lakers, Denver and Houston all have paths. Portland is the chaos team. Nobody has a clean road.

4DK MVP PICK

Luka Dončić

If the Lakers win around 55 games and finish top three in the West, Luka has the team-success argument to go with the numbers we already know are coming.

Wemby is the obvious threat. He feels destined for multiple MVPs and DPOYs, but at 22 there may be less urgency around voters giving him this one immediately.

Wemby has time. Luka’s moment might be right now.

The old guard hasn’t left yet. The next generation isn’t waiting. Nobody has a clean path to June.

That’s the NBA right now. And I wouldn’t want it any other way.
