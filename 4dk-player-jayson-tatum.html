The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

Tatum opens the Top 10 because there is still almost nothing he cannot do at an All-Star level. The strange part is that last season gave us only 16 games, so his ranking is based on what we know the complete player is rather than a normal statistical sample. In those games he averaged 21.8 points, 10 rebounds and 5.3 assists, but the 41.1 percent shooting showed the rust and disrupted rhythm.

Boston is also a different team now. Jaylen Brown is gone, which puts more creation and leadership responsibility back onto Tatum whenever he is fully available. The offense can no longer assume there is another elite wing who can carry a quarter while Tatum rests. That raises both his ceiling and his burden.

At his best, Tatum gives you size, pull-up shooting, rebounding, switchable defense and enough playmaking to run offense through him. He has already proved he can be the best player on a championship team. The only reason he is not higher is that entering this season, the players above him either had stronger 2025–26 campaigns or bring a more singular game-changing trait. If Tatum returns to full health and his normal efficiency, he can move back toward the Top 5.
