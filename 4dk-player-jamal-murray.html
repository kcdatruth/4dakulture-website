The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

Murray finally gave Denver the full regular-season version of the player we have seen in the playoffs for years. He averaged a career-level 25.4 points and 7.1 assists, shot 43.5 percent from three and earned All-NBA recognition. That matters because the old criticism was that his postseason explosions were not always reflected across six months.

The partnership with Jokić remains one of basketball’s hardest actions to solve. Both players can pass, both can score at three levels and both understand exactly how defenses want to cover their two-man game. Murray’s pull-up shooting forces bigs higher, and once that happens Jokić gets the short roll with the entire floor available.

The reason Murray stops at 20 is two-way value and primary responsibility. He is brilliant next to the best offensive center ever, but he does not have to carry the same creation burden as some guards above him. Still, last season closed the gap between “playoff Murray” and “regular-season Murray.”
