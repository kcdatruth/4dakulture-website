THEME OF MONTH 1GOOD TEAMS, WRONG ORDER

We identified many of the right contenders. The surprises have been where they sit — and who crashed the party.

THE FIRST CHECKPOINT

What We Got Right. What We Got Wrong.

The goal is not to grade ourselves on four games like the season is over. It is to compare the exact preseason logic to what the league has actually shown us.

BEST CALLS

49ers • Chiefs • Bills • Seahawks • Bears

The contender tier mostly held. San Francisco and Kansas City are undefeated, Buffalo and Seattle are 3–1, and Chicago has survived without getting the full Caleb Williams season we projected.

WE UNDERRATED THEM

Vikings • Raiders • Browns • Jaguars

Minnesota, Vegas and Cleveland did not just beat our projections — they changed their entire season story. Jacksonville was liked, but not enough.

WE OVERRATED THEM

Texans • Chargers • Buccaneers • Dolphins

Houston is the hardest miss. The Chargers have turned urgency into emergency, while Tampa Bay and Miami have yet to win.

The biggest lesson so far:

Our preseason board did a better job identifying who had real talent than predicting the exact order. Thirteen of fourteen projected playoff teams are still .500 or better, but only a few projected division leaders are actually leading their divisions right now. The contender pool was mostly right. The hierarchy was not.

AFC

Kansas City Chiefs

▲ 2

AFTER WEEK 4#2 • 4–0
CHECKPOINTHIT
“A revenge season with a healthy Mahomes is enough to keep Kansas City near the top.”

Kansas City is one of the cleanest hits on the preseason board. We projected 12 wins because Patrick Mahomes still sets the floor, and four weeks later the Chiefs are 4–0 and No. 2 in our Week 4 power rankings. The surprise is how much Kenneth Walker III has changed the offense. We liked the move because it could take pressure off Mahomes; we did not expect Walker to become the league’s rushing leader through a month and take over the biggest AFC West game of the season.

That matters because Kansas City no longer has to ask Mahomes to solve every high-leverage down through the air. The 12–5 call looks healthy, and the championship case is stronger now than it was in September because the offense has found more balance than we projected.

CHECKPOINTHIT

Buffalo losing to New England does not change the core of the preseason call. The Patriots game exposed areas that need cleaning up, but division games are different and Buffalo has been through this before.

We still view Buffalo as a Super Bowl-level team, but the Week 5 trip to Los Angeles is the first real test of whether the New England loss was just a divisional stumble or something we need to track.

Denver is where our logic still makes sense even though the result has not matched it. “Cousins is the bridge and Mendoza is the future.”

Nobody has flipped our preseason story faster than Las Vegas. We entered the year viewing Kirk Cousins as a bridge, Fernando Mendoza as the eventual direction and the Raiders as a 6–11 team still building toward the future. After four weeks, Vegas is 3–1, sixth on our board and playing like a legitimate playoff team.

Cousins has stabilized the offense, Brock Bowers has been excellent since returning, the supporting receivers have produced, and Ashton Jeanty’s game against Kansas City showed what the backfield can become. Most importantly, the Raiders pushed the 4–0 Chiefs to the final seconds and looked like they belonged. The Mendoza watch is effectively gone for now. This is a miss we have to own.

The Jets are 1–3 and 27th on our board, which is actually a little better than the preseason slot while still fitting the same overall season shape. The problem is offensive control. The Jets can still steal games through defense and individual talent, but until the offense can sustain drives, the ceiling remains exactly where we put it.

WHAT CHANGES NOW?

We Keep The Original Picks. We Update The Evaluation.

That is the whole point of this series. The preseason pages stay exactly where they are so the receipts never move. The checkpoint tells the story of how the league moved around them.

After four weeks, we are buying San Francisco and Kansas City even harder. Buffalo, Seattle and Chicago still fit the contender profile. Jacksonville has moved from “first team out” into the playoff conversation. Minnesota, Las Vegas and Cleveland have forced complete rewrites. Houston, the Chargers, Tampa Bay and Miami have put their preseason projections in real danger.

And we are not overreacting to every 2–2 team. The Rams, Denver, Detroit, Philadelphia, Cincinnati and New England still have enough roster quality to change the picture quickly. The point is not to pretend September is January. The point is to keep score honestly.

NEXT CHECKPOINT: AFTER WEEK 8. By then the sample doubles, the division races have more shape and the difference between a slow start and a wrong prediction becomes much harder to explain away.
