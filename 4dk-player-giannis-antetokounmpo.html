The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

Giannis is still one of the most overwhelming physical forces in basketball. Even in a season limited to 36 games, he averaged 27.6 points, 9.8 rebounds and 5.4 assists while shooting an absurd 62.4 percent from the field. When he gets downhill, there is still no clean answer. Single coverage gets overwhelmed, small lineups get buried at the rim and transition defense becomes an emergency.

Miami changes the stakes. The Heat did not trade for Giannis to be competitive; they traded for him to reopen a championship window. Playing with Bam Adebayo gives Miami a defensive frontcourt capable of switching, protecting the rim and destroying the glass. The offensive fit is more complicated because both stars do their best work inside the arc. Erik Spoelstra has to create movement, screening and enough shooting around them that opponents cannot simply build a wall.

The other issue is health. Thirty-six games last season is a major data point, especially for a player whose greatness is built on force. If he is healthy, Giannis can still be the best player in a playoff series against anybody. No. 6 is not a decline statement. It is a reflection of how much availability and half-court versatility matter when the very top of the league is this loaded.
