4 DA KULTURE • NFL WEEK 7 • MONDAY NIGHT FOOTBALL • OCTOBER 26, 2026

DALLAS FOUND ITS RESPONSE. PHILLY STILL HASN'T FOUND ITS ANSWERS.

COWBOYS 38 — EAGLES 21
DAL 3–4PHI 2–5DAK: 212 YDS / 3 TDJAVONTE: 155 RUSH YDSDALLAS: 416 TOTAL YDS / 0 TURNOVERS

THE NIGHT DALLAS CHANGED THE CONVERSATION

Dallas entered Philadelphia at 2–4 with its season in danger of getting away. It left Lincoln Financial Field 3–4 after a 38–21 divisional win that was about far more than the scoreboard. The Cowboys finished with 416 total yards, 204 on the ground, and no turnovers. The Eagles briefly led 21–20 early in the third quarter. Dallas responded with 18 unanswered points and turned a tight game into a statement. The result doesn't erase six weeks of inconsistency, but it changes what Dallas can reasonably believe about its offense.

DAK DIDN'T NEED TO BE SUPERMAN

Dak Prescott threw for 212 yards and three touchdowns, connecting twice with CeeDee Lamb and once with George Pickens. His best contribution wasn't a gaudy passing total; it was keeping the offense organized, avoiding turnovers, and taking advantage of Philadelphia's defensive attention shifting toward the run. A quarterback with Lamb and Pickens shouldn't have to manufacture every yard himself. For one night, Dallas finally made opponents defend the entire offense.

JAVONTE WILLIAMS GAVE THE COWBOYS AN IDENTITY

Williams ran for a career-high 155 yards and scored a late touchdown, while Dallas piled up 204 rushing yards. That's the performance that changes the geometry of the offense. When a defense has to honor the interior run, Prescott has cleaner windows and Lamb and Pickens can work against less predictable coverage. The next question is whether Dallas can repeat this physical formula against a better run defense. One great night is a breakthrough, not yet a trend.

LAMB AND PICKENS MADE PHILADELPHIA PAY

Lamb's two touchdown receptions and Pickens' scoring catch were the payoff for an offense that didn't become one-dimensional. Philadelphia had to handle Dallas' receivers while the Cowboys controlled stretches of the game on the ground. Lamb remains the top receiving threat, but Pickens gives the offense a second scoring option that changes coverage decisions. Their production mattered most because it came within a balanced plan.

THE TURNING POINT: 21–20 BECOMES 38–21

Philadelphia took a 21–20 lead early in the third quarter on Tanner McKee's touchdown pass to rookie Makai Lemon. It was the moment the Eagles could have seized control of a game they desperately needed. Instead, Prescott found Lamb for another touchdown, Dallas added the two-point conversion, Brandon Aubrey supplied a field goal, and Williams finished a short-field drive with a rushing score. The Eagles were shut out in the fourth quarter. Dallas closed; Philadelphia couldn't answer.

THE EAGLES' OFFENSIVE PROBLEMS ARE BIGGER THAN ONE QUARTERBACK

With Jalen Hurts unavailable, Tanner McKee was again responsible for keeping Philadelphia competitive. He threw for 211 yards with one touchdown and one interception. Saquon Barkley returned and scored from eight yards out, while Dameon Pierce also scored on the ground. Those moments were real, but they weren't enough. Philadelphia moved the ball for 321 yards and still couldn't sustain the offense after taking the lead. Injuries explain some of the limitations; they do not fully explain a fifth straight defeat.

WHAT DALLAS MUST PROVE NEXT

At 3–4, Dallas is back in the conversation but not back among the NFC's elite. The Cowboys have shown their offensive ceiling; now they need to show a floor. Can they protect the football every week? Can Williams provide enough rushing consistency to keep pressure off Prescott? Can the defense hold up when the offense isn't scoring 38? Those are the questions that determine whether this victory was a turning point or an isolated performance.

PHILADELPHIA IS RUNNING OUT OF MARGIN

The Eagles fall to 2–5 with five consecutive losses. The injury situation is serious, but the broader concern is the inability to finish competitive games. Philadelphia led in the third quarter and still lost by 17. That should force hard questions about defensive adjustments, offensive structure, and the ability to withstand adversity. The season isn't mathematically over, but the runway is getting short.

4DK FINAL VERDICT

The Cowboys didn't just win a rivalry game; they delivered the most balanced offensive blueprint they've shown this season. Prescott managed it, Williams powered it, and Lamb and Pickens finished drives. The Eagles had an opening and watched it disappear. Dallas found a response. Philadelphia still needs answers. The Week 7 Power 32, MVP Watch and Rookie Watch will be evaluated with this result included—not with Monday night's game left pending.
