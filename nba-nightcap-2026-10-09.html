THE FULL SLATE

TWO GAMES. PLENTY TO TAKE.

Houston showed depth. Dallas showed young talent. Memphis held its nerve. Chicago's rookie gave the crowd a reason to stay locked in.

HOUSTON @ DALLAS • MACAO

Houston Looked Like A Team With Too Many Ways To Hurt You.

135–117

The Rockets led wire to wire and never really let Dallas turn the game into anything more than an evaluation night. Houston scored 65 in the first half, finished with 135 and got production from everywhere: Tari Eason had 20 and nine, Fred VanVleet scored 17 with seven assists in his return, Reed Sheppard had 16, Kevin Durant 15, and Şengün controlled the middle with a 16-point triple-double.

The encouraging part was not Durant having to carry them. He didn't. Houston shot the ball well, moved it and created layers. VanVleet's return gives the offense another organizer. Şengün's passing keeps the center of the floor alive. Eason and Jabari Smith Jr. kept the wings active. This looked like a roster with answers.

Dallas still got a useful night out of Cooper Flagg, who scored 19 points in 19 minutes and looked more decisive as a scorer. But without Kyrie Irving, the Mavericks never matched Houston's organization or shotmaking. It was a preseason game, but the difference in offensive cohesion was obvious.

MEMPHIS @ CHICAGO

Memphis Won. Caleb Wilson Made The Loudest Statement.

104–100

Memphis controlled the first half, built a 60–50 lead and got enough balance to survive once Chicago finally found its offense. Ty Jerome scored 16 in only 14 minutes. GG Jackson had 14. Zach Edey gave the Grizzlies 10 points, five rebounds, two steals and a block in 16 minutes. Cam Boozer added 10 points, four rebounds and three assists without a turnover.

Then Caleb Wilson woke the game up.

He had one point in the first half. He finished with 21. Wilson hit five three-pointers, scored 20 after halftime and gave Chicago its best chance to steal the game late. Norman Powell added 17 and Josh Giddey had 13, but the night still felt like another early checkpoint for Wilson more than anything else.

Memphis gets credit for closing. Chicago gets another reason to believe the development track is real. Wilson is already showing the shotmaking volume and confidence that can make him more than just a long-term upside play.
