The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

Cade has become the oversized primary creator Detroit envisioned when it drafted him first overall. He nearly averaged a double-double with 23.9 points and 9.9 assists, and the biggest difference now is how controlled the game looks. He can see over traps, manipulate the low man and use his size to keep smaller guards on his hip until a passing lane opens.

The shooting is solid enough but still leaves room for another jump. He hit 34.2 percent from three last season, and if that number moves into the high 30s defenses lose one of their last comfortable coverages. Going under screens against a 6-foot-6 playmaker who can already punish the paint would stop being an option.

Detroit’s expectations matter now. This is not a development ranking anymore. Cade is a Top-10 player because the Pistons need him to control playoff possessions, not just accumulate regular-season numbers. His next step is proving that the huge playmaking volume can remain efficient when teams scheme specifically to take away his first read.
