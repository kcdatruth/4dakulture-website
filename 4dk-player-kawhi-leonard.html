The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

Kawhi finally gave us enough regular-season basketball to evaluate the player instead of only the injury report. He played 65 games for the Clippers, averaged 27.9 points on 50.5 percent shooting and 38.7 percent from three, and looked like the same ruthless half-court scorer when healthy. He still gets to his spots with almost no wasted movement, and strong wing defenders rarely move him off the line he wants.

Now he is back in Toronto, where the context is almost perfect for an older star. Scottie Barnes can handle defensive versatility and connective playmaking, RJ Barrett can carry scoring stretches and the roster does not need Kawhi to dominate the ball for 38 minutes every night. What Toronto does need is the closer. When a playoff game slows down, Kawhi remains one of the best players in the league at getting a clean midrange look or punishing a switch.

The defensive version is no longer the 2016 monster who erased entire stars every possession, but he can still take elite assignments in important stretches. The ranking comes with the same warning as always: availability. If Toronto gets 60-plus regular-season games and a healthy postseason, Kawhi can still play like a Top-5 player in a series.
