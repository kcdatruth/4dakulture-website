01 • THE MAIN EVENT • LAS VEGAS

THE LAKERS HAD THE GAME. THE WARRIORS HAD THE ANSWER.

GOLDEN STATE 101 — LOS ANGELES 89
AUSTIN REAVES • 28 PTS / 7 ASTWALKER KESSLER • 11 PTS / 9 REBYAXEL LENDEBORG • 17 PTSBRANDIN PODZIEMSKI • 17 PTS

A 20-point lead and a 13-point halftime advantage became a 12-point loss. The Lakers scored only 26 points after intermission. That is not a reason to panic in October. It is a reason to look closely at how this roster functions when the first unit comes off the floor.

Reaves was the best scorer on the floor for long stretches. He put up 23 of his 28 before halftime, found his perimeter rhythm and looked comfortable carrying creation duties while Luka Dončić rested. The positive takeaway for Los Angeles is real: Reaves can manufacture offense and give the Lakers another player who bends a defense instead of waiting for Luka to do everything.

Kessler’s debut offered a different kind of encouragement. Eleven points, nine boards and visible rim presence in 16 minutes made the case for why a true interior anchor matters to this group. He does not need a play called for him to affect possessions. Screens, rebounding, paint positioning and vertical protection can all change the way the Lakers defend and finish possessions. The question is how cleanly those benefits translate alongside the team’s primary creators.

But 30 turnovers are impossible to wave away. Golden State converted those mistakes into 26 points. When the Lakers’ established rotation left the floor, their offense lost its structure, and the Warriors held them to 13 points in each of the final two quarters. Some of that is preseason substitution chaos. Some of it is a legitimate depth warning: who settles this team down when the stars sit?

Golden State deserves credit for the response, too. Podziemski and rookie Lendeborg scored 17 apiece, and the Warriors’ energy rose as the game moved toward bench-heavy minutes. Lendeborg’s willingness to contribute without needing the offense built around him is particularly interesting. His value may come from fitting into the gaps: rebounding, quick decisions, defensive effort and opportunistic scoring.

There is a second side to the Warriors’ story. Steve Kerr’s group is still adjusting offensively, and this comeback does not automatically mean the new system is clicking. The defense and younger rotation players carried the night. A winning result can still reveal an unfinished offense.

THE 4DK READ: Los Angeles should be encouraged by Reaves and Kessler but concerned by the offense’s dependence on a narrow group of creators. Golden State should be encouraged by its young contributors but not confuse a comeback against reserves with proof its regular-season offense is solved. That’s the entire purpose of these games: not the final score alone, but the questions behind it.

02–05 • AROUND THE LEAGUE

FOUR MORE GAMES. FOUR MORE QUESTIONS.

TORONTO • NEW YORK

RAPTORS 148, KNICKS 98

A 50-point preseason result grabs attention, but context still matters. New York did not field its normal starting group, so this is not a verdict on the Knicks’ contender status. Toronto, however, can take something meaningful from the way it attacked: 86 first-half points and relentless pace. The challenge is turning that aggressive approach into disciplined offense when opponents bring their full rotations.

4DK QUESTION: Can Toronto sustain its tempo and execution against playoff-caliber lineups rather than preseason depth units?

OKLAHOMA CITY • INDIANA

THUNDER 114, PACERS 112 (OT)

There is little value in treating a two-point overtime preseason win as a statement about either team’s ceiling. What matters is that both sides got another high-pressure possession sample. Oklahoma City’s expectations are championship-level; Indiana’s challenge is to keep competing with structure through changing rotations. Close-game habits—ball security, shot selection and late-clock defense—are worth tracking even when the minutes are managed.

4DK QUESTION: Which habits carry over when the top seven or eight players absorb the real minutes?

ORLANDO • CLEVELAND

MAGIC 132, CAVALIERS 116

Orlando scored 72 in the first half and 108 through three quarters. That is an encouraging offensive display for a team trying to make its physical identity work without sacrificing spacing or scoring efficiency. Cleveland’s defensive problems in one tune-up should not be exaggerated, but the Magic have reason to like how forcefully they played.

4DK QUESTION: Can Orlando pair its established defensive toughness with this kind of offensive confidence once opponents tighten their schemes?

PORTLAND • SACRAMENTO

TRAIL BLAZERS 110, KINGS 98

Portland jumped ahead early, led 66–39 at halftime and held off Sacramento’s late push. The larger story is not the 12-point margin; it is whether Portland’s young core is learning to establish control before games turn chaotic. Sacramento, meanwhile, still needs to sort out how its pieces fit and how quickly it can find dependable lineups.

4DK QUESTION: Can Portland’s promising preseason habits survive the nightly physicality and scouting of an 82-game schedule?

THE BIG PICTURE

DON’T CROWN ANYBODY. DON’T IGNORE THE FILM.

We refuse to treat October preseason scores like playoff evidence. Stars sit, rotations change at halftime, coaches experiment, and final margins often say more about the last eight players on the floor than the first five. But that does not make these games meaningless.

We are watching how a team creates good shots without its leading scorer, whether its bigs change the paint, whether young players make quick reads, and whether defensive effort survives substitutions. Those details travel into the regular season. The scoreboard is a snapshot. The habits are the story.

Our takeaway after Tuesday: Reaves and Kessler gave Lakers fans something to like; the turnovers gave them something to watch. Golden State’s youth showed life; its offense remains a work in progress. Toronto, Oklahoma City, Orlando and Portland each supplied another piece of the preseason puzzle. Opening night is when the evaluation gets serious.
