Rookie Watch

01 / ROOKIE WATCH
Washington Commanders · Nov 10 #2 · ▲ 1

Sonny Styles

Season snapshot
80 tackles · 1 sacks · 5 TFL · 4 passes defended · 1 INT

Thirteen tackles and one behind the line in a three-point division win pushed Styles to the top. New York still gained 399 yards, so we wouldn't use his tackle count as proof Washington controlled the game. What earns him first is the breadth of his season. He has interrupted passes, finished plays in the backfield and handled the volume of keeping an offense from turning a modest gain into an explosive one.

02 / ROOKIE WATCH
New York Giants · Nov 10 #3 · ▲ 1

Arvell Reese

Season snapshot
81 tackles · 4.5 sacks · 8 TFL · 4 passes defended · 1 INT

Styles and Reese each made thirteen tackles Thursday. What separates Reese's own case is the pressure production he brings in addition to that workload. Four and a half sacks and eight tackles for loss give New York a reason to send him forward even when his range is valuable behind the rush. We'd judge his next step by how often those negative plays leave the Giants in a third down they can close.

03 / ROOKIE WATCH
New York Jets · Nov 10 #1 · ▼ 2

David Bailey

Season snapshot
45 tackles · 7.5 sacks · 10 TFL · 3 passes defended · 0 INT

Last week's No. 1 broke up two passes against Buffalo even though he registered no sack and one tackle. That is a useful counterpoint to the idea that his whole value is whether the quarterback goes down. His 7.5 sacks and twelve quarterback hits remain the best argument for giving him first place again. We drop him two spots because Styles and Reese added more this week to already substantial season cases.

04 / ROOKIE WATCH
Miami Dolphins · Nov 10 #5 · ▲ 1

Jacob Rodriguez

Season snapshot
80 tackles · 1 sacks · 4 TFL · 1 passes defended · 1 INT

One credited tackle against Indianapolis is a thin Sunday on which to base a promotion. We have Rodriguez fourth on the weight of eighty season tackles and the range of situations in which Miami needs him. The Colts getting 160 rushing yards from Taylor and McGowan also shows the limit of judging a linebacker by volume. We'd look for more stops behind the line before moving him into the top three.

05 / ROOKIE WATCH
Cleveland Browns · Nov 10 #4 · ▼ 1

Denzel Boston

Season snapshot
33 rec · 523 rec yds · 3 rec TD

A single 16-yard catch on two targets wasn't the engine of Cleveland's comeback. Fannin and Jeudy did more of the damage Sunday. Boston's season still gives him a specific place in the offense. His 523 yards on 33 catches yield almost sixteen per reception, enough to change field position without needing double-digit targets. We want to see him keep that threat while earning a steadier share of Watson's throws.

06 / ROOKIE WATCH
Dallas Cowboys · Nov 10 #9 · ▲ 3

Caleb Downs

Season snapshot
66 tackles · 2 sacks · 1 TFL · 3 passes defended · 1 INT

Eleven tackles against San Francisco meant plenty of work for the rookie safety. It also meant the ball reached his level far more often than Dallas wanted. We give him credit for the season's two sacks and interception alongside the tackle volume. That combination gives the staff a case for moving him closer to the action. The Cowboys first have to limit the explosives that make his depth indispensable.

07 / ROOKIE WATCH
Kansas City Chiefs · Nov 10 #7 · — Hold

Mansoor Delane

Season snapshot
30 tackles · 0 sacks · 3 TFL · 7 passes defended · 1 INT

Atlanta reached the red zone five times and scored one touchdown. We can't assign the other four stops to a single defender, but Delane's two passes defended give us concrete evidence of his contribution on a difficult afternoon for Atlanta's receivers. Seven passes defended through this point and an interception make a stronger case than tackle volume for a corner. We keep him seventh.

08 / ROOKIE WATCH
Cleveland Browns · Nov 10 #10 · ▲ 2

KC Concepcion

Season snapshot
40 rec · 458 rec yds · 1 rec TD · 43 rush yds · 1 rush TD

Cleveland targeted him eight times and got three catches for 21 yards. The more interesting contribution was the rushing touchdown. Giving a receiver a carry near scoring range makes the defense account for him even when Watson keeps the ball. Concepcion and Boston now have the same number of season targets and quite different uses. We moved Concepcion up for expanding the ways Cleveland can finish a drive.

09 / ROOKIE WATCH
Tennessee Titans · Nov 10 #6 · ▼ 3

Carnell Tate

Season snapshot
34 rec · 413 rec yds · 1 rec TD

Tate's receiving line stayed at 34 catches for 413 yards through the Jacksonville game. Tennessee needed more completions beyond the sticks, which is exactly where a receiver averaging over twelve yards a catch can help. We keep him on the board for the work already banked. For him to regain ground, the Titans need to turn his targets into a recurring answer for Ward on scoring drives.

10 / ROOKIE WATCH
Las Vegas Raiders · New to Top 10

Fernando Mendoza

Season snapshot
191 pass yds · 0 pass TD · 0 INT · 25 rush yds · 1 rush TD

His first start came against Seattle, and he left it with a rushing touchdown and no interceptions. Seventeen completions on 29 attempts for 143 yards tell the other half. Las Vegas needed more from each successful throw just to keep drives affordable. We give him the last spot for protecting a close game while creating a score with his legs. Passing production under pressure will decide how long he holds it.
