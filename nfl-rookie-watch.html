#1 ▲ Up 2
New York Jets · Nov 17 #3

David Bailey

Season through Week 11
50 tackles · 9.5 sacks · 12 TFL · 4 passes defended · 0 INT
Week 11 · 5 tackles · 2 sacks · 2 TFL · 1 pass defended · 0 INT

Bailey takes No. 1 after sitting third last week. We give extra weight to disruption that costs an offense a down and forces it out of its preferred personnel.

Two sacks in a three-point road win over the Chargers bring Bailey to 9½. Los Angeles converted eight of 12 third downs, so those disruptions mattered on a day the Jets struggled to end drives. Bailey gives a 3–8 team a way to win a passing down even when the offense has put the defense back on the field.

#2 ▼ Down 1
Washington Commanders · Nov 17 #1

Sonny Styles

Season through Week 11
85 tackles · 1 sack · 5 TFL · 4 passes defended · 1 INT
Week 11 · 5 tackles · 0 sacks · 0 TFL · 0 passes defended · 0 INT

Styles drops one place without losing his standing as an impact rookie. His evaluation should focus on how reliably he processes run/pass conflict, closes space and finishes tackles, rather than treating a single box-score statistic as the complete measure of linebacker play.

Washington held Cincinnati to one rushing first down even as Burrow piled up 300 passing yards. Styles’ five tackles belong in that context. With 85 on the season, his value is how often the next defender gets to play second-and-long. Bailey moves ahead on backfield disruption, while Styles still has the stronger volume of finishes.

#3 ▼ Down 1
New York Giants · Nov 17 #2

Arvell Reese

Season through Week 11
88 tackles · 4.5 sacks · 9 TFL · 4 passes defended · 1 INT
Week 11 · 7 tackles · 0 sacks · 1 TFL · 0 passes defended · 0 INT

Reese remains in the top three because of the defensive upside in his athletic profile and disruptive role. The key scouting question is whether his pressure and pursuit translate into disciplined, repeatable assignments when offenses change tempo and formation.

Reese had seven tackles and one for loss against Jacksonville. New York needed that work because the Jaguars ran 68 plays and reached the red zone six times. The combination of 88 tackles and 4½ sacks gives Reese a credible case on both early downs and passing downs for an NFC East leader.

#4 ↔ Hold
Miami Dolphins · Nov 17 #4

Jacob Rodriguez

Season through Week 11
93 tackles · 1 sack · 4 TFL · 2 passes defended · 2 INT
Week 11 · 13 tackles · 0 sacks · 0 TFL · 1 pass defended · 1 INT

Rodriguez holds fourth. His case depends on recognition, pursuit angles and the ability to remain assignment-sound when opponents stress the middle of the field. We give him credit for both the steady tackling workload and the possessions he ends himself.

Thirteen tackles and an interception against Buffalo give Rodriguez the strongest argument for moving into the top three. The limit is visible in the same game. Miami conceded 304 passing yards to Allen, so tackle volume alone cannot establish that a defense controlled the middle. Two season interceptions give us a second measure of his influence.

#5 ▲ Up 2
Kansas City Chiefs · Nov 17 #7

Mansoor Delane

Season through Week 11
31 tackles · 0 sacks · 3 TFL · 7 passes defended · 1 INT
Week 11 · 1 tackle · 0 sacks · 0 TFL · 0 passes defended · 0 INT

Delane rises two places. Cornerback evaluation demands more than interceptions: coverage responsibility, target difficulty, tackling support and the ability to recover after losing leverage all matter. His next assignment against Buffalo puts that balance under pressure.

One tackle against Arizona is a poor measure of a cornerback’s workload. His seven passes defended over the season offer firmer evidence that he has affected throws at the catch point. Against Buffalo, tackling the receiver on a short completion may be as important as breaking up a deep attempt if it keeps Allen facing third down.

#6 ↔ Hold
Dallas Cowboys · Nov 17 #6

Caleb Downs

Season through Week 11
73 tackles · 2 sacks · 2 TFL · 3 passes defended · 1 INT
Week 11 · 7 tackles · 0 sacks · 1 TFL · 0 passes defended · 0 INT

Downs holds sixth. The defining question for a versatile defensive back is how much freedom his range and recognition give the coordinator. We value the flexibility to play the run and still recover depth when the offense changes the picture.

Downs had seven tackles and a tackle for loss against Tennessee. Dallas also gave up 30 points. We credit his ability to finish plays near the line while keeping open the question of how effectively the whole secondary limits explosives. The Philadelphia game gives Dallas a very different problem in run support.

#7 ▼ Down 2
Cleveland Browns · Nov 17 #5

Denzel Boston

Season through Week 11
33 catches · 63 targets · 523 rec yds · 3 rec TD
Week 10 · 1 catch · 2 targets · 16 rec yds · 0 rec TD

Boston slips two positions but remains in the top ten. For a rookie receiver, we want to separate target volume from the ability to win independently: releases, route pacing, contested-catch reliability and run-after-catch decisions all shape the verdict.

Cleveland’s bye leaves his last game at one catch for 16 yards. Over the season, Boston and teammate KC Concepcion drew 63 targets apiece, yet Boston produced 65 more yards and two more receiving scores. He stays higher because his catches have changed field position more often, even with fewer completions.

#8 ▲ Up 1
Tennessee Titans · Nov 17 #9

Carnell Tate

Season through Week 11
36 catches · 56 targets · 471 rec yds · 2 rec TD
Week 11 · 2 catches · 5 targets · 58 rec yds · 1 rec TD

Tate rises one spot. The receiving evaluation should examine how consistently he creates separation, wins at the catch point and fits into his team's passing structure. A single splash play is not enough; repeatable down-to-down value is the 4DK standard.

Two catches on five targets in Dallas yielded 58 yards and a touchdown. That combination captures both sides of his ballot. Tennessee can get a scoring drive out of very few Tate touches, but an offense playing from behind also needs receptions that keep ordinary drives alive. Jacksonville is the next test.

#9 New entry
Washington Commanders · Outside Nov 17 top ten

Antonio Williams

Season through Week 11
37 catches · 52 targets · 448 rec yds · 2 rec TD
Week 11 · 9 catches · 11 targets · 122 rec yds · 0 rec TD

Williams enters the ballot after a nine-catch, 122-yard performance for Washington against Cincinnati. His 22- and 42-yard receptions on the final drive positioned the winning field goal, making the production directly consequential. That is strong evidence for his breakthrough, but a lasting top-ten case requires examining his route variety, separation against different coverages and how he contributes when the offense is not chasing a game.

Those final two catches accounted for 64 of his 122 yards. More revealing for his long-term case are the other seven catches for 58. Washington could use him to sustain possessions before it needed him to steal one at the end. His 37 season receptions give that final drive a foundation.

#10 ▼ Down 2
Cleveland Browns · Nov 17 #8

KC Concepcion

Season through Week 11
40 catches · 63 targets · 458 rec yds · 1 rec TD
Week 10 · 3 catches · 8 targets · 21 rec yds · 0 rec TD

Concepcion rounds out the top ten after sitting eighth. His evaluation should weigh how his acceleration and movement skills affect defensive spacing against the demands of reliable route execution. The unanswered question is whether he can give his quarterback an easy completion when the first design breaks down.

Concepcion caught three of eight targets for 21 yards in Cleveland’s last game before the bye. He still has more catches than Boston on the same number of season targets. His route to climbing again runs through first downs after the catch, especially against Las Vegas when Cleveland can protect its defense by extending drives.
