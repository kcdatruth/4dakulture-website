👑 Rookie No. 1

Sonny Styles, Washington. All-down defensive responsibility still wins the argument.

📈 Rising

Denzel Boston ▲2 and Arvell Reese ▲1; the production is no longer a projection.

🆕 New Entry

Jonah Coleman. His winning touchdown against Kansas City puts him in the ten.

Rookie rankings are 4DK editorial judgments. Season figures are verified against player pages; a player with a bye or missed game does not receive an invented Week 8 line.

01
LB • Washington Commanders • — HOLD

Sonny Styles

52 tackles · 1 sack · 1 INT · 4 passes defended

Styles keeps the top spot because the Commanders are asking him to handle real defensive responsibility, and he keeps answering. Washington beat Philadelphia 27–10, and his presence in the middle helps explain why the defense can close throwing lanes without abandoning the run. He is not just a tackle collector; he reads blocking surfaces, covers space and changes what offenses are willing to attempt. Bailey has the louder pass-rush résumé, but Styles still gives us the broader every-down impact.

02
EDGE • New York Jets • — HOLD

David Bailey

5.5 sacks · 35 tackles through Week 8

Bailey remains close enough to take No. 1 with one dominant afternoon. Five and a half sacks are not a projection; they are production that changes protection calls. The Jets have struggled to finish games, but that should not bury the rookie defender most capable of disrupting a third-down plan by himself. We want to see him convert more of that pressure into game-turning stops. He stays second because Styles is influencing more phases, not because Bailey has cooled off.

03
LB • New York Giants • ▲ +1

Arvell Reese

55 tackles · 1.5 sacks · 1 INT

Reese moves into the top three after the Giants’ bye because the sustained body of work has become too substantial to overlook. He has 55 tackles, a sack and a half and an interception in seven games, with a role that requires him to recognize runs and survive in coverage. This is the difference between a gifted athlete and a dependable NFL defender. The next leap is making more of those tackles behind the line, but the foundation is already worthy of a top-three spot.

04
WR • Cleveland Browns • ▲ +2

Denzel Boston

26 catches · 409 receiving yds · 3 TD

Boston climbs even after Cleveland’s ugly loss because his season production is stronger than the previous board fully captured. Twenty-six receptions for 409 yards and three touchdowns give the Browns a vertical and contested-catch threat that the offense desperately needs. He is not responsible for the failure to convert 324 passing yards into touchdowns against Pittsburgh. The challenge is turning individual flashes into weekly command of coverage. His ability to win downfield has earned this promotion.

05
LB • Miami Dolphins • — HOLD

Jacob Rodriguez

68 tackles · 1 INT · 1 pass defended

Rodriguez holds the top five because his workload has become impossible to dismiss. Sixty-eight tackles in seven games reflect a defender who diagnoses quickly and stays involved across formations. Miami’s record is poor, but that does not reduce the difficulty of what he is being asked to do. We want to see more third-down and turnover impact as the season continues. For now, the instincts and reliability are a legitimate foundation for a rookie-of-the-year case.

06
WR • Tennessee Titans • ▼ -3

Carnell Tate

34 catches · 413 receiving yds · 1 TD (7 games)

Tate drops three spots because the defensive rookies and Boston have supplied stronger recent arguments, not because his season has been disappointing. Thirty-four catches and 413 yards on a one-win offense remain impressive. Cam Ward’s 304-yard afternoon against Cincinnati showed the passing game can grow, and Tate remains an important part of that future. The next stage is finishing more drives and turning target share into scores. We are keeping the standard high because the ceiling is high.

07
CB • Kansas City Chiefs • — HOLD

Mansoor Delane

26 tackles · 1 INT · 5 passes defended (5 games)

Delane stays seventh because coverage responsibility matters even when it does not produce a highlight. The Chiefs have trusted him with meaningful snaps, and an interception plus five passes defended in five appearances gives us a tangible baseline. Denver exposed problems in Kansas City’s ability to finish defensively, so the young corner has to help stabilize the back end. We are watching technique, leverage and third-down discipline as closely as the box score.

08
WR • Pittsburgh Steelers • ▲ +2

Germie Bernard

32 catches · 284 receiving yds · 1 TD

Bernard climbs two places because he is becoming a reliable piece of a winning offense. Thirty-two receptions in eight games represent consistent involvement, and his ability to create underneath options matters when Pittsburgh builds its identity around the run and defense. He is not yet the most explosive rookie receiver, but the weekly role is real. The next step is stretching the field and producing more scoring plays without losing the trust he has earned.

09
RB • Denver Broncos • NEW ENTRY

Jonah Coleman

134 rush yds · 2 rush TD · 97 receiving yds (4 games)

Coleman is our new entry because his best work arrived in the biggest moment. Against Kansas City he supplied 41 rushing yards, 63 receiving yards and the late touchdown that put Denver in front. His season totals are still modest after an injury interruption, so we are not inventing a full-season résumé. We are rewarding a rookie who returned, handled meaningful touches and helped decide a division game. Sustaining that role will determine whether he stays.

10
RB • Arizona Cardinals • ▼ -1

Jeremiyah Love

268 rush yds · 1 rush TD · 36 receiving yds · 1 receiving TD

Love remains in the ten, but the new ankle injury clouds the immediate outlook. He has 268 rushing yards in six games and has flashed enough acceleration to matter when Arizona gets him space. Tyler Allgeier carried the comeback against Dallas after Love exited, which illustrates both the roster depth and the rookie’s need to get healthy. We will not guess at a return date. His talent keeps him here; availability and consistent efficiency will decide whether he can climb.

OUTSIDE THE TEN

Jalon Daniels drops out: 501 passing yards, three touchdowns and five interceptions in five appearances, with no passing attempts in Week 8. Dani Dennis-Sutton and Emmett Johnson also deserve attention after their Week 8 performances. We are measuring current NFL impact, not draft position.
