01

Minnesota

6–0 • W 16–10 vs Indianapolis
PREVIOUS #4 • ▲ +3

We put Minnesota on the throne because six wins and no losses still mean something, especially when the defense can carry a difficult afternoon. The Vikings held Indianapolis to ten points, and Harrison Smith closed the door with a late interception. Blake Cashman supplied the pressure, while Kyler Murray and the offense finally found the touchdown they needed in the fourth quarter. The concern is that Minnesota has not always looked explosive with the ball; the strength is that it has not needed to. That defensive foundation is a serious championship ingredient.

4DK FILM ROOM: Cashman: 12 tackles, two sacks. Murray connected with T.J. Hockenson for the go-ahead TD. Aaron Jones aggravated his knee injury and required further evaluation.
02

Buffalo

5–1 • BYE WEEK
PREVIOUS #3 • ▲ +1

The Bills move to No. 2 without taking a snap. This is not a reward for sitting home; it is the result of the 49ers and Chiefs both losing while Buffalo's six-game body of work remains strong. Josh Allen gives this offense a second playbook when the first one breaks down, and that is the difference between a good quarterback and a weekly problem for every defensive coordinator. The question coming out of the bye is whether the supporting cast can create enough easy offense that Allen does not have to manufacture everything himself.

4DK FILM ROOM: No Week 7 game. Buffalo returns from its bye with Allen still atop our MVP Watch.
03

San Francisco

6–1 • L 20–29 at Atlanta
PREVIOUS #1 • ▼ -2

The perfect start is gone, not the contender status. Atlanta forced four turnovers and turned them into 13 points, and that was too much for a banged-up San Francisco offense to overcome. Brock Purdy threw for 288 yards but was intercepted twice; the absence of healthy receiving options showed up when the 49ers needed to answer. We still believe the roster and coaching are top-three quality, but this loss exposed the cost of playing thin at key spots. The bye comes at exactly the right time.

4DK FILM ROOM: Purdy: 27-of-40, 288 yards, one TD, two INT. Christian McCaffrey has a mild hamstring strain; the team is optimistic about his post-bye return, not guaranteeing it.
04

Kansas City

5–1 • L 3–20 at Seattle
PREVIOUS #2 • ▼ -2

Three points. For a Patrick Mahomes offense, that is the headline, and we are not dressing it up. Seattle dictated the physical terms, erased Kenneth Walker III's rushing lanes and made Mahomes play from uncomfortable positions. The Chiefs still own a 5–1 record and the roster to answer this, so they remain in the elite tier. But a defense just handed the league a blueprint: take away the run, disrupt timing and force Kansas City to sustain difficult drives. The next response matters more than the first loss itself.

4DK FILM ROOM: Mahomes: 165 passing yards, one INT. Walker: 36 rushing yards on 14 carries. Rashee Rice missed the game with hamstring/knee issues.
05

Denver

5–2 • W 30–16 at Arizona
PREVIOUS #10 • ▲ +5

Denver jumps five spots because three straight wins and an increasingly reliable defensive identity deserve a real reward. Bo Nix finally provided the kind of explosive play that changes the geometry of a game, and the Broncos handled Arizona by two touchdowns. We still need to see more consistent down-to-down passing efficiency before putting them in the top four. But they are no longer just a defense trying to survive the offense; they are building a more complete formula.

4DK FILM ROOM: Nix broke a 62-yard touchdown run; RJ Harvey scored on a 52-yard screen. Jaylen Waddle left with a chest injury; his Week 8 availability needed monitoring as of Oct. 27.
06

Seattle

4–3 • W 20–3 vs Kansas City
PREVIOUS #17 • ▲ +11

Our Team of the Week, and it is not close. Seattle held the previously unbeaten Chiefs to 213 total yards and no touchdown while Jaxon Smith-Njigba repeatedly won the matchup everyone knew mattered. This is the defensive identity Mike Macdonald has been building, and the offense had enough balance to protect it. The reason we stop at No. 6 is the full 4–3 résumé, not the quality of Sunday night's win. Stack another performance like this and the top five becomes a serious conversation.

4DK FILM ROOM: JSN: nine catches, 150 yards, one TD. Emanuel Wilson: 100 rushing yards. Charles Cross suffered a high-ankle sprain; early news on Rashid Shaheed's knee was encouraging.
07

Jacksonville

4–2 • BYE WEEK
PREVIOUS #5 • ▼ -2

The Jaguars drop two places through no fault of a Week 7 performance. They are still 4–2 with a positive scoring differential, and Trevor Lawrence remains the hinge between a dangerous AFC team and a frustratingly inconsistent one. Their 34–31 Week 6 loss to Houston is the reminder: explosive capability does not matter if you cannot close. We want Jacksonville to return from the bye with cleaner decisions, better situational defense and a more complete four-quarter identity.

4DK FILM ROOM: No Week 7 game. Jacksonville's next test is proving its pre-bye inconsistency has been corrected.
08

Chicago

5–2 • W 30–24 vs New England
PREVIOUS #7 • ▼ -1

Chicago won, Caleb Williams threw for more than 300 yards again, and the Bears are 5–2. So why one spot down? Because Seattle and Denver delivered more forceful statements while the top of the board compressed. This is not a knock on Chicago. Williams is turning his improvisational gifts into a more controlled offense, and Luther Burden III is giving him a genuine explosive outlet. The next step is finishing games without inviting a late comeback.

4DK FILM ROOM: Williams: 308 yards, two TD, no INT. Burden: four catches, 130 yards. Cairo Santos hit a 58-yard field goal late.
09

Cincinnati

4–2 • W 35–13 at Baltimore
PREVIOUS #14 • ▲ +5

A 13–0 halftime deficit turned into a 35–13 road win. That is what a real statement looks like. Joe Burrow threw four touchdowns after the break, and Cincinnati punished a Ravens team missing Lamar Jackson. We do not confuse beating an injured rival with beating a fully healthy one, but the Bengals' response was too strong to ignore. Their offensive ceiling is unmistakable. Now they have to build a defense and a weekly identity that travel beyond one dominant half.

4DK FILM ROOM: Burrow: 22-of-30, 292 yards, four TD. Tee Higgins: six catches, 148 yards. Cincinnati scored 35 unanswered points.
10

Pittsburgh

5–2 • W 16–10 at New Orleans
PREVIOUS #12 • ▲ +2

Pittsburgh keeps winning games that are not designed to impress a highlight reel. The Steelers held New Orleans to 18 rushing yards, got 101 yards from Jaylen Warren and left Paris with another road victory. That is a sustainable defensive identity, and at 5–2 they have earned top-ten status. The offense still needs to create more than isolated big plays, particularly against teams that can score in bunches. For now, we respect the toughness and the result.

4DK FILM ROOM: Warren: 18 carries, 101 yards. Aaron Rodgers: 212 passing yards, one TD. Saints limited to 18 team rushing yards.
11

Cleveland

5–2 • W 27–24 at Tennessee
PREVIOUS #11 • — HOLD

Cleveland stays eleventh, but the Browns are pushing hard on the top ten. Deshaun Watson has now directed five fourth-quarter go-ahead scoring drives this season, and his late completion to Harold Fannin Jr. set up another walk-off kick. That is not an accident anymore. At the same time, giving a one-win Tennessee team a real chance to steal this game keeps us cautious. The Browns have learned to win close; the next step is proving they can control one.

4DK FILM ROOM: Watson: 308 passing yards. Quinshon Judkins: 96 rushing yards, two TD. Rookie KC Concepcion: eight catches, 129 yards. Andre Szmyt won it from 32 yards.
12

Detroit

4–2 • W 20–17 vs Green Bay
PREVIOUS #13 • ▲ +1

Detroit's divisional win matters because Green Bay made the Lions earn every yard. Jared Goff threw for 303 yards and two scores, Jahmyr Gibbs carried a heavy rushing load, and Amon-Ra St. Brown kept winning underneath and downfield. The Lions still have moments when the ball-security picture gets messy, but this was a statement in composure. We are not rushing them into the top ten on one narrow result; we are acknowledging a team with the weapons and balance to get there soon.

4DK FILM ROOM: Goff: 303 yards, two TD. Gibbs: 116 rushing yards. St. Brown: nine catches, 153 yards. Jake Bates kicked the winning field goal at the gun.
13

Green Bay

4–3 • L 17–20 at Detroit
PREVIOUS #9 • ▼ -4

Green Bay was close enough to win, but being close is not the same as finishing. Jordan Love threw two touchdowns, yet the Packers could not turn the late possessions into a victory. More troubling than the three-point margin is the damage up front: center Sean Rhyan suffered a high-ankle sprain and backup Jacob Monk also went down. That line instability changes what Green Bay can ask of its offense on a short week. The talent is there; the margin for sloppy execution is shrinking.

4DK FILM ROOM: Love: 274 passing yards, two TD, one INT. Rhyan reportedly faces at least four weeks out; Monk was placed on injured reserve with a quad injury.
14

NY Giants

4–2–1 • W 27–21 (OT) at Houston
PREVIOUS #16 • ▲ +2

The Giants are doing something we cannot dismiss: winning with resilience and a running game that can finish. J.J. McCarthy protected the football, Cam Skattebo ripped off 124 rushing yards, and Devin Singletary ended overtime with a 30-yard touchdown run. The Giants gained 420 yards while holding Houston to 237. That is a more complete performance than their record alone suggests. We still need to see this offense sustain it against a stronger opponent, but the NFC East door is wide open.

4DK FILM ROOM: McCarthy: 252 passing yards, no turnovers. Skattebo: 124 rushing yards. Singletary: 30-yard walk-off TD in overtime.
15

LA Rams

3–4 • W 35–21 at Las Vegas
PREVIOUS #23 • ▲ +8

The Rams finally showed the balance we have been waiting for. Matthew Stafford moved the ball, Puka Nacua found the end zone, and the Kyren Williams–Blake Corum rushing combination gave Los Angeles a real second dimension. The Raiders made a late push, but the Rams had already built too much of a cushion. We still have to account for a 3–4 record and an uneven opening six weeks. If this offense starts stacking complete games, fifteenth will look conservative.

4DK FILM ROOM: Puka: eight catches, 68 yards, one TD. Davis Allen caught two TD. Williams and Corum combined for 192 rushing yards and two scores.
16

Baltimore

4–3 • L 13–35 vs Cincinnati
PREVIOUS #8 • ▼ -8

Baltimore is sliding because the last two weeks have exposed a dangerous inability to finish. The Ravens led Cincinnati 13–0 at halftime and were outscored 35–0 after the break. Lamar Jackson's absence matters, but the defensive collapse cannot be placed on the backup quarterback. The team needs its leader back and needs a stronger response from the entire roster. The Bills game ahead is a chance to stop the slide, not a guarantee that Lamar will be available.

4DK FILM ROOM: Jackson missed his third straight game with a high-ankle sprain. Coach Jesse Minter was hopeful for Week 8, pending practice evaluation.
17

New England

4–3 • L 24–30 at Chicago
PREVIOUS #6 • ▼ -11

Dropping eleven spots looks severe when Drake Maye actually played well, so here is our reasoning: New England's previous No. 6 ranking was built on a hot run, and Chicago exposed the remaining gap in late-game execution. Maye threw for 321 yards and produced three total touchdowns, but the end-zone interception was a swing play. This is still a legitimate, improving team. The ranking corrects an aggressive prior placement more than it condemns one road loss.

4DK FILM ROOM: Maye: 24-of-31, 321 yards, two passing TD, one rushing TD, one INT. New England fell to 4–3.
18

Atlanta

3–4 • W 29–20 vs San Francisco
PREVIOUS #20 • ▲ +2

Atlanta earns a real bump for taking down the last unbeaten team in the NFC West. Michael Penix Jr. threw three touchdowns without a pick, and the defense forced four turnovers. We are not pretending a 3–4 team has suddenly become an elite contender, but this is the kind of win that can change a season's confidence. The Falcons have to prove their pass rush and turnover creation can show up without a parade of short fields.

4DK FILM ROOM: Penix: 25-of-34, 213 yards, three TD. Atlanta converted four 49ers turnovers into 13 points.
19

Tampa Bay

2–5 • W 43–13 at Carolina
PREVIOUS #29 • ▲ +10

The biggest warning against reading records without context: Tampa Bay was 1–5 and then walked into Carolina and won by thirty. The Buccaneers ran for 258 yards, sacked Bryce Young five times and took the ball away repeatedly. We cannot ignore five losses, but we also cannot leave a team that dominant at No. 29. The challenge is whether the physical edge travels into another game rather than disappearing as quickly as it arrived.

4DK FILM ROOM: Bucky Irving: 106 rushing yards, two TD. Yaya Diaby: three sacks. Tampa Bay produced 258 rushing yards and 20 points off turnovers.
20

Carolina

3–3 • L 13–43 vs Tampa Bay
PREVIOUS #15 • ▼ -5

The Panthers have shown real progress this season, and that is why this result is so jarring. Bryce Young was hit constantly, the offense turned the ball over, and Tampa Bay controlled the trenches from the opening quarter. This is not a reason to abandon the larger development story; it is a reminder that Carolina has not yet built a reliable floor. The injury to right tackle Taylor Moton only increases the urgency around protecting Young.

4DK FILM ROOM: Young: 163 passing yards, one TD, two INT; sacked five times. Moton suffered a fractured fibula.
21

Dallas

3–4 • W 38–21 at Philadelphia (MNF)
PREVIOUS #24 • ▲ +3

Monday night changes Dallas' outlook. Dak Prescott threw three touchdown passes, Javonte Williams bullied his way to a career-high 155 rushing yards, and the Cowboys finished with 416 yards and no turnovers. They took Philadelphia's third-quarter lead and answered with eighteen straight points. At 3–4, Dallas is not a contender on paper yet, but the division is vulnerable and the offensive formula finally looked balanced. We want to see it against a defense playing with more confidence.

4DK FILM ROOM: Prescott: 212 yards, three TD. Williams: 155 rushing yards, one TD. CeeDee Lamb caught two TD; George Pickens caught the other.
22

Houston

2–5 • L 21–27 (OT) vs NY Giants
PREVIOUS #18 • ▼ -4

Houston's record has moved beyond the point where talent alone can excuse the results. The Texans gave up 420 yards to the Giants, managed only 237 themselves and lost in overtime at home. There are defensive flashes and enough offensive personnel to compete, but the situational mistakes keep repeating. This is a team that needs a sharper identity, not another close-loss explanation.

4DK FILM ROOM: The Giants' Cam Skattebo gained 124 rushing yards; Devin Singletary ended the game on a 30-yard overtime touchdown.
23

Indianapolis

2–5 • L 10–16 at Minnesota
PREVIOUS #25 • ▲ +2

Indianapolis competed against the league's only unbeaten team and still left without a win. Jonathan Taylor's early rushing touchdown gave the Colts a chance, but the offense could not solve Minnesota's pressure and Daniel Jones was intercepted on the final drive. The defense kept the game close enough for a late steal. The problem is that the passing game is not giving the team enough sustainable answers. A third straight loss pushes urgency higher.

4DK FILM ROOM: Taylor: 95 rushing yards and a TD. Jones: 108 passing yards, one INT. Minnesota held Indianapolis to ten points.
24

Las Vegas

3–4 • L 21–35 vs LA Rams
PREVIOUS #19 • ▼ -5

Four straight losses have turned a 3–0 start into a 3–4 problem. Kirk Cousins still produced passing yards and Ashton Jeanty remained a receiving outlet, but the Rams controlled the line of scrimmage and took away the run. This is the question for Las Vegas: can the offense remain dangerous when it cannot dictate tempo? The defense has also surrendered too many manageable drives. The Raiders need a reset before the slide becomes their identity.

4DK FILM ROOM: Cousins: 295 passing yards, two TD. Tre Tucker: 103 receiving yards. Jeanty and Mike Washington Jr. combined for just 42 rushing yards.
25

Washington

1–4–1 • BYE WEEK
PREVIOUS #26 • ▲ +1

Washington moves up one place without playing, but the record remains a warning. The Commanders entered their bye after a 23–17 loss to San Francisco and have struggled to turn competitive stretches into victories. A week off gives them time to address protection, defensive discipline and late-game decision-making. Our ranking is not about dismissing the roster; it is about demanding evidence that the direction is changing.

4DK FILM ROOM: No Week 7 game. Washington's last result was a 23–17 loss to San Francisco on Oct. 19.
26

New Orleans

2–5 • L 10–16 vs Pittsburgh
PREVIOUS #27 • ▲ +1

The Saints kept Pittsburgh within reach but could not run the football. Eighteen team rushing yards made the offense too predictable, and Tyler Shough had to shoulder nearly everything through the air. That is a difficult way to beat a defense that wants to pressure the passer. New Orleans has competitive pieces, but the offensive balance and protection have to improve. The loss also came with more defensive and backfield injury concerns.

4DK FILM ROOM: Shough: 278 passing yards, one TD. Saints: 18 rushing yards. Safety Justin Reid entered concussion protocol; CJ Donaldson hurt a shoulder.
27

Arizona

2–5 • L 16–30 vs Denver
PREVIOUS #22 • ▼ -5

Arizona has surrendered 208 points in seven games, and that number explains more than any one quarterback debate. Trey McBride gave the offense a productive target, but the defense could not stop Denver's explosive plays or consistently win on early downs. The Cardinals are not without skill talent. They are without the complementary football needed to make that talent matter. Until the defense improves, the ceiling stays low.

4DK FILM ROOM: McBride: ten catches, 111 yards, one TD. Denver scored on a 62-yard Nix run and a 52-yard RJ Harvey screen.
28

NY Jets

2–5 • L 25–28 vs Miami
PREVIOUS #21 • ▼ -7

The Jets nearly erased a double-digit fourth-quarter deficit, but the early mistakes made the comeback necessary. Geno Smith found Mason Taylor for late scores, yet New York could not get the ball back after Miami converted a crucial fourth-and-one. Losing at home to a previously winless division rival is not a small blemish. The Jets need more reliable starts and a defense that forces opponents into difficult decisions before the final five minutes.

4DK FILM ROOM: Mason Taylor caught two TD passes. Miami QB Malik Willis converted the late fourth-and-one that sealed the game.
29

Philadelphia

2–5 • L 21–38 vs Dallas (MNF)
PREVIOUS #28 • ▼ -1

Five straight losses and a season-altering quarterback absence have changed the entire conversation in Philadelphia. Tanner McKee competed and Saquon Barkley returned, but Dallas ran for 204 yards and scored eighteen unanswered after the Eagles briefly led in the third. The football questions are urgent, but Jalen Hurts' health is bigger than any ranking; the team has announced a very good prognosis while ruling out contact activity during treatment. On the field, the Eagles must rebuild the run defense and protect their young quarterback.

4DK FILM ROOM: McKee: 211 yards, one TD, one INT. Barkley scored a rushing TD in his return. Hurts is expected to miss the rest of 2026 while receiving treatment for blood clots.
30

Miami

1–5 • W 28–25 at NY Jets
PREVIOUS #31 • ▲ +1

Miami finally has its first win, and we are giving that progress its due. Malik Willis delivered two passing touchdowns and another on the ground, while Ollie Gordon II supplied a 100-yard rushing day. It was a strong answer after the bye, but the Dolphins still nearly let an eleven-point fourth-quarter lead disappear. At 1–5, one good Sunday does not erase the opening month. It does give the team a workable offensive template.

4DK FILM ROOM: Willis: 208 passing yards, two passing TD, one rushing TD. Gordon: 103 rushing yards, one TD. De'Von Achane remains out for the season with an ACL injury.
31

Tennessee

1–6 • L 24–27 vs Cleveland
PREVIOUS #30 • ▼ -1

Tennessee played hard enough to beat Cleveland, which makes the ending more painful. Tony Pollard scored three times and ran for 129 yards, but the Titans surrendered another last-minute scoring drive. The rookie quarterback and the offense have flashes, yet the defense and situational execution have not met them halfway. There is development worth recognizing here; there is also a one-win record that cannot be argued away.

4DK FILM ROOM: Pollard: 129 rushing yards, three TD. Cleveland's walk-off 32-yard field goal ended Tennessee's upset bid.
32

LA Chargers

0–6 • BYE WEEK
PREVIOUS #32 • — HOLD

The Chargers remain thirty-second because the scoreboard has offered no alternative. Six losses, 90 points scored and 151 allowed tell the story of an offense that cannot consistently protect Justin Herbert or create enough manageable situations. The bye was a chance to examine the structure rather than simply the injuries. We need to see a credible plan, better early-down execution and, above all, a win before this ranking can move.

4DK FILM ROOM: No Week 7 game. Los Angeles remains the league's only winless team at 0–6.
