Power Rankings

01

San Antonio Spurs

9-2 provisional · Previously #4 · ▲ +3 · West / Southwest

San Antonio owns the top line in our editorial race because Victor Wembanyama gives the Spurs a defensive advantage that is difficult to reproduce. His November 9 explosion against Phoenix—40 points and nine blocks—captures the full equation: elite offense at one end, a rewritten shot chart at the other. The Fox–Harper–Castle guard rotation matters because opponents cannot simply crowd the paint and assume the Spurs will run out of creation. The next question is how the supporting cast responds when Wembanyama is forced into early foul trouble or a slower half-court game.

Key names: Victor Wembanyama · Dylan Harper · De’Aaron Fox · Stephon Castle

02

Philadelphia 76ers

9-2 provisional · Previously #3 · ▲ +1 · East / Atlantic

Philadelphia's 9–2 provisional mark puts the Sixers firmly in the No. 1 conversation, but we are keeping them second because the full defensive identity is still coming into focus. Tyrese Maxey sets the pace while LeBron James and Jaylen Brown give the offense multiple ways to attack switches. Embiid's health and workload are the swing variables: the ceiling is enormous when he can anchor the interior without slowing the ball. We want to see the Sixers win possessions with connected defense, not simply outscore opponents through star power.

Key names: Tyrese Maxey · LeBron James · Jaylen Brown · Joel Embiid

03

Oklahoma City Thunder

8-2 provisional · Previously #10 · ▲ +7 · West / Northwest

The Thunder make the week's loudest climb among the contenders, from tenth to third. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander's shot creation remains the stable center of the attack, and Chet Holmgren gives Oklahoma City a second axis of defensive pressure. The challenge is sustaining their intensity across a rotation that has dealt with injuries. A contender can survive missing talent for a night; maintaining its offensive structure through those absences is the test that separates a deep team from a merely talented one.

Key names: Shai Gilgeous-Alexander · Ajay Mitchell · Isaiah Hartenstein · Chet Holmgren

04

Los Angeles Lakers

10-2 provisional · Previously #7 · ▲ +3 · West / Pacific

The Lakers' 10–2 provisional record is the best mark on this board, and Luka Dončić is producing like the league's most dangerous offensive player. His 32-point, 10-assist performance on November 9 demonstrated how he can punish both single coverage and the help defense that follows. Austin Reaves gives Los Angeles another live-ball decision-maker, while Walker Kessler's rim protection changes the defensive floor. We are keeping them fourth for now because the schedule's next wave will test whether the defense holds when Luka sits.

Key names: Luka Dončić · Austin Reaves · Walker Kessler

05

Boston Celtics

8-2 provisional · Previously #6 · ▲ +1 · East / Atlantic

Boston is still a top-five team in our eyes because Jayson Tatum gives it a reliable late-clock answer and the collective structure has survived major personnel changes. Paul George adds a different rhythm to the offense, while Payton Pritchard's spacing and Neemias Queta's interior work help the Celtics win possessions that never make highlight reels. The issue to monitor is how consistently Boston creates easy paint touches; leaning on difficult jumpers is a fragile way to protect an elite record.

Key names: Jayson Tatum · Paul George · Payton Pritchard · Neemias Queta

06

Golden State Warriors

8-3 provisional · Previously #9 · ▲ +3 · West / Pacific

Golden State's rise reflects more than Stephen Curry's scoring. His movement forces defenses into impossible choices, and rookie Yaxel Lendeborg has provided the kind of energetic secondary play that can turn those choices into efficient possessions. Brandin Podziemski and Draymond Green help the Warriors keep the ball moving and organize their defense. The concern is sustainability: opponents will test whether Golden State can finish defensive possessions and manufacture offense when Curry is denied clean looks.

Key names: Stephen Curry · Yaxel Lendeborg · Brandin Podziemski · Draymond Green

07

Detroit Pistons

7-3 provisional · Previously #2 · ▼ -5 · East / Central

Detroit falls from second to seventh, but that should not be mistaken for a collapse in our evaluation. Cade Cunningham still drives the offense, Ausar Thompson supplies perimeter disruption, and Paul Reed's role has grown in the frontcourt. The concern is the physical burden of playing without a fully settled interior rotation. A team that built its identity on pressure and rebounding has to keep those advantages against opponents with size. Detroit can climb again quickly if it restores that consistency.

Key names: Cade Cunningham · Paul Reed · Ausar Thompson · Jalen Duren

08

Houston Rockets

8-3 provisional · Previously #1 · ▼ -7 · West / Southwest

Houston's slide from first to eighth is about the strength of the field, not a loss of respect for its talent. Alperen Şengün remains one of the most creative big-man hubs in basketball, and Kevin Durant supplies the half-court shot-making that prevents opponents from overloading the paint. Amen Thompson's athleticism raises the defensive ceiling. What we need from the Rockets is more dependable control of momentum: turnovers and transition defense can undo several minutes of excellent execution.

Key names: Alperen Şengün · Kevin Durant · Amen Thompson · Fred VanVleet

09

Minnesota Timberwolves

8-3 provisional · Previously #11 · ▲ +2 · West / Northwest

Minnesota's record demands top-ten respect, especially given the pressure created by Anthony Edwards's absence. LaMelo Ball brings an improvisational element to the offense, while Rudy Gobert gives the team a defensive anchor around which to build its coverage. The Wolves must find dependable half-court possessions when opponents take away the first action. This stretch is a real test of organizational depth: wins collected without a primary star carry weight, but the defensive details must remain sharp.

Key names: LaMelo Ball · Ayo Dosunmu · Rudy Gobert · Anthony Edwards

10

New York Knicks

5-4 provisional · Previously #17 · ▲ +7 · East / Atlantic

New York makes a meaningful move upward because its core still has the tools to win ugly games. Jalen Brunson can control tempo late, Karl-Anthony Towns bends matchups with his shooting, and OG Anunoby gives the Knicks defensive versatility on the wing. The question is how well the pieces function together when the game speeds up. We need more than isolated scoring bursts; the next step is creating consistent advantages early in possessions and protecting the defensive glass.

Key names: Jalen Brunson · Karl-Anthony Towns · Josh Hart · OG Anunoby

11

Denver Nuggets

5-4 provisional · Previously #16 · ▲ +5 · West / Northwest

Denver is moving up, but the Nuggets still have work to do to match the expectations created by Nikola Jokić's all-around brilliance. Jamal Murray's shot creation and the spacing around the big man are crucial when defenses refuse to send a second defender. The challenge is reducing the possessions where Jokić has to solve everything from the top of the floor. Denver's ceiling remains high; a cleaner defensive effort and steadier support scoring are what will push it toward the top ten.

Key names: Nikola Jokić · Jamal Murray · Cam Johnson · Trevon Brazile

Zach Edey's size creates another problem for opponents near the basket. The Grizzlies need to show they can dictate the terms of games against disciplined defenses rather than relying on their young talent to win every broken possession.

Key names: Ty Jerome · Cameron Boozer · Zach Edey · Cedric Coward

The next challenge is consistency away from home and against teams that can match their physicality without sacrificing perimeter coverage.

We still need to see whether the defensive execution and offensive decision-making remain stable when Flagg is not scoring at that level.

5-6 provisional · Previously #13 · ▼ -3 · East / Central

The Raptors need better control of the margins: defensive rebounding, turnovers, and shot selection will decide whether their balanced roster translates into a winning streak. The Suns need a more stable possession-to-possession defensive approach and better shot quality when the first option is taken away. That is the route out of the bottom third.
