GAME 01 · NOVEMBER 21 · FINAL

Philadelphia 76ers 132 — Milwaukee Bucks 129

Herro's 50 meets Philadelphia's survival instinct

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Tyler Herro (MIL): 50 PTS, 11 REB, 7 AST, 7 threes; Brayden Burries: 26 PTS. Joel Embiid (PHI): 26 PTS, 17 REB, 9 AST; LeBron James: 21 PTS; Tyrese Maxey: 17 PTS.

THE TURNING POINT

The Sixers led by ten with just over two minutes remaining, then survived Milwaukee's furious comeback. Embiid's final free throws made it a three-point game; Herro's apparent tying three left his fingertips after the buzzer.

WHAT WE LEARNED · THE 4DK VERDICT

A team with Philadelphia's record should not need the clock to save it against the league's bottom club, and that is exactly why this win deserves scrutiny. The Sixers' offensive hierarchy worked: Embiid punished mismatches inside and on the glass, James delivered 13 fourth-quarter points, and the supporting creators gave Milwaukee multiple threats to track. Philadelphia's 33 assists against just four turnovers show that the ball movement was not the problem. Its inability to finish the game defensively was. Herro repeatedly attacked gaps and made difficult jumpers look routine, while rookie Burries supplied 26 points of genuine support. Milwaukee's 13th straight loss is painful, but the offense showed more fight than its record suggests. For 4DK, Embiid's near triple-double is a winning foundation; Herro's 50 is an extraordinary losing performance. The Sixers improve to 14-3, while Milwaukee drops to 1-16.

GAME 02 · NOVEMBER 21 · FINAL

New York Knicks 97 — Portland Trail Blazers 96

Bridges rescues New York from a 26-point collapse

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Jalen Brunson (NYK): 30 PTS; Landry Shamet: 20 PTS; Mikal Bridges: 12 PTS and the winning three. Donovan Clingan (POR): 22 PTS, 13 REB; Deni Avdija: 20 PTS; Damian Lillard: 15 PTS.

THE TURNING POINT

After New York squandered a 26-point lead, Avdija's free throws put Portland up 96-94 with 3.4 seconds left. Josh Hart found Bridges, whose buzzer-beating three won it.

WHAT WE LEARNED · THE 4DK VERDICT

The final play will live in the highlight reel, but we cannot let it disguise what happened in the second half. New York scored 66 before halftime and only 31 after it, with Portland's size and activity turning a comfortable game into a possession-by-possession fight. Clingan's 22 and 13 made the paint a problem, Avdija's all-around pressure helped reverse momentum, and Lillard's late three was part of the comeback. Brunson had 19 points in the opening quarter and 30 overall, yet the Knicks' offense tightened as the lead evaporated. Bridges saved the result with a poised catch-and-shoot response under maximum pressure. That is valuable late-game shotmaking, but championship-level teams cannot repeatedly depend on emergency execution after surrendering huge advantages. New York's fourth straight win moves it to 10-6; Portland falls to 10-6, but its resilience on the road is worth respecting.

GAME 03 · NOVEMBER 21 · FINAL

Dallas Mavericks 110 — Los Angeles Clippers 100

Kyrie closes, Flagg creates, and Dallas finds separation

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Kyrie Irving (DAL): 25 PTS; Cooper Flagg: 22 PTS, 8 REB, 6 AST; Walter Clayton Jr.: 16 PTS. Brook Lopez (LAC): 21 PTS; Rui Hachimura: 19 PTS.

THE TURNING POINT

Kris Dunn's corner three trimmed Dallas's lead to 91-88 with 5:12 left. Dallas answered with 11 consecutive points, capitalizing on two Clippers turnovers.

WHAT WE LEARNED · THE 4DK VERDICT

This was not a runaway; it was a test of which team could execute once the game got tight. Irving started 1-for-7 but scored 12 in the fourth, giving Dallas a veteran closer when its offense needed clean possessions. Flagg's 22 points, eight rebounds and six assists showed why his development matters beyond a scoring average: he can connect actions, attack a matchup and keep the ball moving. His 7-for-13 free-throw night remains a real area to clean up, but the broader offensive responsibility is expanding. Clayton and Christie supplied timely perimeter scoring, helping the Mavericks survive without P.J. Washington Jr. The Clippers got a strong second half from Lopez, yet late giveaways undid their chance to steal a road game. Dallas rises to 9-7; Los Angeles slips to 4-11 and remains winless on the road. The difference was composure in the final five minutes.

GAME 04 · NOVEMBER 21 · FINAL

Minnesota Timberwolves 104 — Memphis Grizzlies 95

Minnesota's defense interrupts the league's hottest offense

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Jaden McDaniels (MIN): 25 PTS, 5 REB, 3 AST; Ayo Dosunmu: 19 PTS; Jonathan Kuminga: 16 PTS. Cedric Coward (MEM): 23 PTS, 7 REB.

THE TURNING POINT

After Memphis scored 34 in the first quarter, Minnesota held the Grizzlies to 12 in the second and 16 in the third, a 55-28 middle-quarter swing.

WHAT WE LEARNED · THE 4DK VERDICT

The matchup offered a revealing contrast: Memphis arrived with the league's top offensive rating, while Minnesota owned the top-rated defense. For 24 minutes spanning the middle quarters, the Timberwolves made that defensive identity the story. McDaniels supplied 25 points without sacrificing the length and activity that make him central to the team's scheme. Dosunmu and Kuminga gave Minnesota secondary scoring, and the Wolves hit a season-best 58 percent from the floor. Yet we cannot call this a clean offensive performance: more than twenty turnovers are still too many, especially while Anthony Edwards is unavailable. Memphis scored only 95, its season low, and lost a five-game winning streak; Coward's 23 was not enough to rescue an offense that struggled to generate efficient looks. Both teams leave at 12-5. Minnesota demonstrated contender-grade defensive capacity, but ball security remains the difference between surviving and controlling elite opponents.

GAME 05 · NOVEMBER 21 · FINAL

San Antonio Spurs 114 — New Orleans Pelicans 91

Wembanyama turns dominance into a 50-point statement

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Victor Wembanyama (SAS): 50 PTS, 19 REB, 5 BLK, 4 STL; Stephon Castle: 19 PTS, 6 AST. Jeremiah Fears (NOP): 29 PTS; Jordan Poole: 25 PTS; Derik Queen: 15 PTS, 15 REB.

THE TURNING POINT

Wembanyama scored 32 before halftime, sending San Antonio into the break ahead 61-45; the Spurs extended the margin to 89-66 after three quarters.

WHAT WE LEARNED · THE 4DK VERDICT

There are huge scoring nights, and then there are games in which one player seems to dictate the geometry of the entire floor. Wembanyama's 50 points were only part of the story. Nineteen rebounds kept possessions alive or ended them, while five blocks and four steals turned San Antonio's rim protection into a transition weapon. New Orleans had to account for him on every interior touch, yet he also imposed himself defensively without needing a specific matchup to target. Castle's 19 points and six assists offered the complementary guard play that makes the Spurs harder to reduce to a one-man show. Fears and Poole combined for 54 points, but the Pelicans could not create enough defensive resistance, especially without Zion Williamson. San Antonio improves to 14-3 and joins the Lakers atop the West; New Orleans drops to 5-13. This is a two-way superstar performance, not simply a scoring headline.

GAME 06 · NOVEMBER 21 · FINAL

Sacramento Kings 97 — Boston Celtics 96

Murray's last-second putback gives Sacramento its breakthrough

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Darius Acuff Jr. (SAC): 21 PTS, 6 REB, 8 AST; De'Andre Hunter: 20 PTS; Keegan Murray: 15 PTS, 12 REB; Domantas Sabonis: 15 PTS, 17 REB. Paul George and Mike Conley (BOS): 19 PTS each.

THE TURNING POINT

Boston led 96-95 after Payton Pritchard's jumper. Acuff missed his drive, but Murray tipped in the rebound with five seconds left; Pritchard missed the final attempt.

WHAT WE LEARNED · THE 4DK VERDICT

For a Sacramento team that had not won at home in nine attempts, this was about more than a single rebound. The Kings trailed by 13 at halftime, then reversed the game with a 35-13 third quarter that gave their young guards and wings confidence to attack. Acuff's 21 points and eight assists reflect an increasingly important role as a decision-maker; he did not make the final shot, but his penetration forced the defense to react, opening the weak-side second chance for Murray. Murray's 12 rebounds and final defensive contest were as important as the tip-in. Boston, resting Jayson Tatum and Derrick White, struggled from distance and surrendered the third-quarter momentum despite recovering the lead late. The Celtics still had a chance at the buzzer, but could not finish. Sacramento ends an eight-game losing streak and reaches 2-14; Boston falls to 11-5. The 4DK lesson: effort on the glass can decide the tightest game, and this one may be a meaningful confidence-builder for Acuff.

4DK PLAYER OF THE NIGHT

Victor Wembanyama · San Antonio Spurs

50 points · 19 rebounds · 5 blocks · 4 steals. We give Wembanyama the award over Tyler Herro's remarkable 50-point, 11-rebound, seven-assist night because this was a comprehensive two-way takeover in a 23-point Spurs victory. He dominated the paint, erased chances defensively and carried San Antonio into a share of the Western Conference lead. Honorable mentions: Tyler Herro, Joel Embiid, Jalen Brunson, Mikal Bridges and Darius Acuff Jr.
