SAN FRANCISCO 49ERS • 3–0 • SUPER BOWL WINDOW

The 49ers Look Like A Super Bowl Team. Health Is Still The Question.

Three weeks into the season, San Francisco has looked like one of the NFL's most complete teams. The 36–30 win over Arizona pushed the Niners to 3–0, and Brock Purdy delivered another statement performance with four touchdown passes. The numbers matter, but the way he is playing matters just as much: efficient, accurate, poised and comfortable controlling an offense that does not depend on one traditional No. 1 receiver.

That is the part of Purdy's start that deserves attention. San Francisco has options rather than one target who has to carry the passing game. George Kittle remains a matchup problem. Mike Evans has already given Purdy another veteran weapon and caught a touchdown against Arizona. Deebo Samuel has returned home and immediately restored another familiar dimension to the offense. The result is a group that can attack a defense in different ways without forcing Purdy to play hero ball every week.

Christian McCaffrey is another reason the 3–0 start is encouraging. The Niners have not needed to bury him under touches just to survive September. At 30, with the mileage he has accumulated, San Francisco should be thinking about January every time it decides how much work to give him in September and October. A fresher McCaffrey late in the season is more valuable than chasing unnecessary early-season volume.

Defensively, the Niners have also shown they can withstand adversity. Nick Bosa was ruled out for Week 3 with knee and calf issues, yet San Francisco still found a way to move to 3–0. That does not mean the pass rush is permanently solved without him; it means the defense has enough structure and talent to survive stretches without one of its stars.

That is also why the front office should remain aggressive. Another defensive playmaker — ideally somebody who can create pressure — would protect the defense against attrition. Another receiver would give Purdy even more stability and insurance if injuries hit the room. Neither move has to be a blockbuster. The goal is to keep the roster deep enough that one December injury does not change the entire offense or defense.

San Francisco has been here before. Talent has rarely been the issue. Availability has. If this roster can get through the next few months and arrive late in the season close to full strength, another Super Bowl appearance is a realistic ceiling for this group.

4DK TAKE

Purdy has looked like a top-five quarterback through the early season. It is not just the production. It is the efficiency, poise and the way he is maximizing a collection of weapons without needing a single receiver to dominate the offense.

LAS VEGAS RAIDERS • 3–0 • NEW ERA?

The Raiders Finally Look Like They're Trying To Win Now.

This season started with a completely different type of conversation around Las Vegas. Fernando Mendoza was waiting behind the veteran quarterback, and it was easy to imagine another season eventually turning toward the future. Three games later, the Raiders are undefeated and the conversation has changed.

Klint Kubiak's early impact goes beyond play design. The Raiders look organized, balanced and — most importantly — like a team operating with the expectation that it can win now. Kirk Cousins still looks capable of starting productive football for a good NFL team. He threw three touchdowns in the 35–27 comeback win over New Orleans, while Brock Bowers returned with 10 catches for 116 yards and a score. That veteran stability at quarterback has allowed the rest of the offense to develop without forcing the Raiders into desperation mode.

The running game gives Vegas another layer. Ashton Jeanty has not had the monster breakout game yet, but the talent is obvious and the opportunity is there. Rookie Mike Washington Jr. has also given the Raiders a legitimate second option behind him. If the offensive line holds up and Cousins continues to keep defenses honest, the backfield has room to become even more important as the season develops.

Defensively, Maxx Crosby remaining in Las Vegas matters. His value is not limited to sacks or pressures. Keeping the face of the defense while trying to establish a new coaching era sends a message that the organization is not simply stripping everything down again.

Week 4 against Kansas City is the perfect measuring stick. The Chiefs are also 3–0 and Patrick Mahomes still looks like Patrick Mahomes. Kenneth Walker III has added the type of running threat Kansas City needed, while Rashee Rice, Xavier Worthy, Tyquan Thornton and Travis Kelce give Mahomes answers throughout the passing game.

Vegas cannot afford a good half or three good quarters. It needs a complete four-quarter performance. If the Raiders can contain Kansas City's offense and go toe-to-toe with one of the AFC's established powers, the conversation changes again. Vegas stops being a cute September story and starts becoming a legitimate dark-horse factor in the AFC playoff picture.

4DK TAKE

This may already be the most balanced Raiders team of the Las Vegas era. But 3–0 earns attention — Kansas City is the game that can earn them real credibility.

BUFFALO BILLS • 3–0 • AFC BREAKTHROUGH?

Buffalo Has Been Here Before. This Version Feels Different.

Calling Buffalo a contender is nothing new. Josh Allen has already led Bills teams with championship expectations, and the franchise has spent years living near the top of the AFC without finishing the trip to the Super Bowl. The question is not whether Buffalo is good enough to win regular-season games. It is whether this version has enough balance around Allen to finally break through when January gets tight.

The early returns under Joe Brady are encouraging. Buffalo entered Week 3 with one of the league's most efficient and productive offenses, then showed a completely different trait against the Chargers: survival. The Bills turned the ball over five times, fell behind by double digits and still found a way to win 24–16. That is not a formula anybody wants to repeat, but championship teams eventually have to win games when the clean version of the offense does not show up.

Allen remains the centerpiece, but James Cook changes the burden on him. When Buffalo can create offense on the ground without relying on Allen to become the running game himself, it makes the quarterback more dangerous and potentially keeps him fresher. Through the first three weeks, Allen and Cook have looked like one of the strongest quarterback-running back combinations in football.

The defensive improvement is the piece worth watching. Buffalo surrendered 31 points in each of its first two games, then held Los Angeles to 16 in Week 3. The unit is still adjusting to a new scheme, so one strong Sunday does not settle the question. But if the defense continues trending upward while the offense remains one of the league's most difficult units to contain, Buffalo's margin for error gets much larger.

That matters because the AFC path is never going to be easy. Kansas City looks strong again. Buffalo does not need to prove in September that it can beat everybody in January. It needs to build a team that can win in more than one way when that moment comes.

Through three weeks, that is what makes this Bills start feel more meaningful than another hot opening. They have star power, offensive balance and early evidence of defensive growth. The question is whether this is finally the season that ceiling becomes the result.

4DK TAKE

This might be Buffalo's year because Allen no longer has to be the entire identity. The offense is elite, Cook gives it balance, and the defense is starting to give the offense the support it needs.

CHARGERS + TEXANS • 0–3 • AFC PRESSURE

Forget The Big Picture For A Minute. Win A Damn Game.

Los Angeles and Houston entered the season with very different rosters and different questions, but three weeks later they share the same problem: 0–3. Once a team reaches that point, every long-term discussion becomes louder because the season can get away from you before October really begins.

For the Chargers, the offense has been the biggest disappointment. Los Angeles is averaging 14.7 points per game, and Justin Herbert has opened the season with 627 passing yards, three touchdowns and four interceptions. Against Buffalo, the Chargers were handed five takeaways and still scored only 16 points. That is the type of game an 0–2 team has to steal.

The immediate danger is what happens if 0–3 becomes 0–4 and then 0–5. Coaching decisions get scrutinized harder. Offensive problems become organizational questions. The pressure around Herbert and the direction of the team gets louder. At some point, there is no schematic explanation that matters more than putting a win in the standings.

Houston's problem is different because the defense has given the Texans a foundation. They have 11 sacks through three games, among the league leaders, and the defense has been good enough to keep games within reach. The offense has not consistently rewarded it. C.J. Stroud has 794 passing yards and three touchdowns through three games, and Houston is averaging 18 points per game.

Context matters with Stroud. Houston's offensive environment has not been ideal, and the offensive line remains a concern. That is why three bad weeks should not become a reckless quarterback decision. But patience is not the same as refusing to evaluate.

If Houston spends an entire season wasting a high-level defense because the offense cannot create explosive plays, sustain drives or consistently score, the front office has to ask difficult questions about personnel, protection, scheme and eventually quarterback. Those are conversations for later. Right now the solution for both teams is much less complicated: win a damn game.

4DK TAKE

Houston should ride this season out with Stroud. But if the offense cannot get itself together, the Texans eventually have to evaluate the entire offensive situation — including the long-term plan at quarterback.

NEW YORK GIANTS • J.J. McCARTHY • QB AUDITION

McCarthy Is Not Replacing Dart. He's Auditioning For His Own Future.

The Giants' move for J.J. McCarthy should not be treated like New York is abandoning Jaxson Dart. Dart's knee injury is expected to keep him out for the remainder of the regular season, and the Giants still have every reason to keep their long-term plan centered on him. The trade is about the present — and about giving another 23-year-old quarterback a chance to rewrite his own trajectory.

New York is sending Minnesota a 2027 fifth-round pick for McCarthy, the former No. 10 overall selection in the 2024 draft. That is a manageable price for a team that still wants to compete now and suddenly needs another legitimate quarterback option.

McCarthy's Minnesota story never developed cleanly. He entered the league with expectations, lost his rookie season to knee surgery and never established the long-term foothold the Vikings hoped for. At 23, though, he is far too young to treat his first stop as a final verdict on his career.

New York gives him something Minnesota no longer could: a fresh evaluation. He does not have to prove he is better than Dart for the next decade. He has to show that he is capable of running an NFL offense, making winning decisions and looking like somebody a team could trust as a starter.

The Harbaugh connection makes the landing spot even more interesting. Giants coach John Harbaugh is the brother of Jim Harbaugh, who coached McCarthy at Michigan during the Wolverines' national-championship run. That does not guarantee anything, but it gives New York a logical framework for understanding the environment in which McCarthy previously succeeded.

The upside scenario is fascinating. If McCarthy plays competent football, the Giants offense looks respectable and New York stays in the playoff race — or actually reaches the postseason — his value changes dramatically. Quarterback-needy teams entering the 2027 offseason would have recent NFL tape on a still-young former first-round pick.

Then the Giants can make the decision based on Dart's recovery rather than emotion. If Dart is progressing well and McCarthy's market becomes expensive, New York should not create a quarterback controversy simply because the backup played well. If the market is modest, keeping a talented young backup becomes attractive insurance. The order matters: evaluate Dart's recovery, evaluate McCarthy's play, evaluate the market.

4DK TAKE

This is an audition for McCarthy's NFL future, not an audition to steal Jaxson Dart's future in New York.

THE WEEK 3 BOARD

Different Teams. Different Kinds Of Pressure.

San Francisco is trying to keep a championship roster healthy. Las Vegas is trying to prove its undefeated start belongs in the contender conversation. Buffalo is trying to turn years of being close into the season it finally breaks through. Los Angeles and Houston are trying to stop September from wrecking their seasons. And J.J. McCarthy is trying to turn an emergency opportunity in New York into a second life as an NFL starter.

None of those stories are finished. That's the point. Week 3 gave us the questions. The next month starts giving us the answers.
