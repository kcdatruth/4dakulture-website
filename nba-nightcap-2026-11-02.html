Fifteen games. Thirty teams. A Western Conference lead changed hands, the league’s final undefeated team took its first loss, and the MVP race got another 42-point reminder from Los Angeles. We saw a rookie lift Sacramento off the bottom, another rookie make noise for Chicago, and a Portland team win a game in Denver that deserves to change how the league discusses it. This is the kind of night that separates a standings glance from a real understanding of where the season is heading.

The most important part is not simply who scored the most. It is who imposed a style, who found a solution when a star was missing, who survived a fourth-quarter test and who failed to protect a lead. Below, we go through every final with the same attention to the story inside the score.

PLAYER OF THE NIGHT · LUKA DONČIĆ · 42 POINTS · 13 ASSISTS · 7 REBOUNDS

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander scored 43 in a clutch road victory and Ty Jerome scored 40 in an overtime loss. Both deserve serious recognition. But Dončić followed his 45-point Sunday with another 42, pairing elite scoring with 13 assists in a decisive Lakers win. The consistency and the control give him the narrow 4DK edge tonight.

GAME 01 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

OKLAHOMA CITY OUTLASTS CLEVELAND

THUNDER 117 · CAVALIERS 112
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Shai Gilgeous-Alexander: 43 points, 8 assists; Isaiah Hartenstein: 14 rebounds. Cleveland: Donovan Mitchell 17 points, 11 assists; Meleek Thomas 17 points.

Oklahoma City arrived in Cleveland with a rotation already stripped of familiar defensive and scoring options, yet Shai Gilgeous-Alexander gave the Thunder the one luxury injuries cannot remove: a late-game offense that knows exactly whose hands should hold the ball. His 43 points were more than a scoring binge. Against a Cleveland defense that could crowd the lane, Shai still found the tempo changes and foul-line precision that keep a possession alive when a set breaks down. The Thunder won the paint battle and were much steadier at the stripe, two details that mattered as the score tightened into a one-possession game.

Cleveland's response was not empty. Donovan Mitchell organized the offense with 11 assists, and rookie Meleek Thomas contributed 17 points in his first NBA start. But a young contributor playing well does not automatically solve a late-clock matchup against an elite creator. The Cavs had the chance to make the night theirs; Shai made the defining shot instead.

THE TURNING POINT

Sam Merrill tied the game at 112 with a three, then Gilgeous-Alexander answered with the go-ahead step-back three; Ajay Mitchell closed it at the line.

WHAT IT MEANS

The Thunder move to 5-2 despite major absences. Cleveland drops to 4-3, and the rematch will test whether its late-game coverages can force the ball out of Shai’s hands.

GAME 02 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

CADE MAKES A STATEMENT, DETROIT MAKES A MESS OF CHARLOTTE

PISTONS 124 · HORNETS 91
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Cade Cunningham: 33 points, 7 assists, 5 rebounds, 5 steals; Duncan Robinson: 24 points, six threes. Charlotte: Brandon Miller 17 points.

Detroit did not simply win this game. It turned a competitive first half into a warning to the Eastern Conference. Cade Cunningham scored 33 points in only three quarters, finding almost every route a lead guard can use to control a defense: early offense, midrange touch, drive-and-kick reads and timely defensive pressure. When a franchise player can put up that line without seeing the fourth quarter, the story is as much about the collective gap as his individual brilliance.

Duncan Robinson's six threes made Charlotte pay for every late closeout. The Pistons' spacing and transition attack gave Cunningham choices instead of forcing him to create a miracle on every trip. Charlotte, meanwhile, struggled to protect the ball or build any sustained offensive rhythm. The return of Coby White was a positive development, but it could not change the shape of the night.

THE TURNING POINT

Detroit carried an 11-point halftime advantage into a 98-75 third-quarter lead, then pushed the margin near 30 with consecutive perimeter strikes.

WHAT IT MEANS

Detroit improves to 6-1 with a real argument for the top tier of the East; Charlotte falls to 3-5. The Pistons’ next test against Philadelphia carries serious early ranking weight.

GAME 03 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

ATLANTA SURVIVES A GAME IT HAD NO BUSINESS LOSING

HAWKS 108 · PACERS 104
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Nickeil Alexander-Walker: 34 points, 4 rebounds, 4 assists. Indiana: Jay Huff 22 points, 8 rebounds; Quenton Jackson 20; Braden Smith 19 points, 10 assists.

Atlanta got the result, but this is not the kind of victory that automatically strengthens a contender’s case. Indiana was down to six available players, a roster situation that should have forced the Hawks to establish control early and protect it. Instead, Nickeil Alexander-Walker’s 34 points became the lifeline for a team that kept giving the Pacers reasons to believe. With Jalen Johnson unavailable, Atlanta needed a secondary creator to shoulder a primary load, and Alexander-Walker answered that part of the assignment.

Indiana deserves more than a sympathetic footnote. Jay Huff attacked the scoring opportunities available, Quenton Jackson made winning plays, and Braden Smith gave the Pacers legitimate organization with 19 points and 10 assists. That kind of resistance from a depleted roster reveals both the competitiveness of the players still standing and the problems in Atlanta’s ability to put an opponent away.

THE TURNING POINT

Jackson cut the deficit to 106-104 with 1:36 left; after a missed potential go-ahead three, Onyeka Okongwu hit the late jumper that settled the result.

WHAT IT MEANS

Atlanta reaches 4-4 but has execution questions to answer. Indiana falls to 2-6 amid a severe availability crisis; the effort is worth respecting even if the standings do not reward it.

GAME 04 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

THE KNICKS FINALLY TURN THE SWITCH

KNICKS 103 · NETS 90
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · New York: Landry Shamet 22 points; Jalen Brunson 19; Karl-Anthony Towns 11 points, 16 rebounds. Brooklyn: Julius Randle 21 points, 11 rebounds.

Brooklyn led after three quarters, and for much of the evening the Knicks looked like a team still searching for the fluidity expected of a defending champion. The offense was choppy, the perimeter attack carried too much of the burden and the Nets had enough physicality to make the building uncomfortable. But the fourth quarter demonstrated why New York's depth and defensive versatility still matter: the Knicks stopped treating each possession like a separate rescue mission and began stringing stops and shots together.

Landry Shamet's 22 points gave New York an unexpected scoring headliner, while Towns' 16 rebounds provided the foundation when the offense misfired. Julius Randle's 21 and 11 made this a personal and competitive return against his former club, but Brooklyn did not have a counter once the Knicks' defensive pressure changed the rhythm. This was a game won through one sustained burst, not four quarters of dominance.

THE TURNING POINT

New York opened the fourth quarter on a 25-5 surge after trailing 79-75 through three periods.

WHAT IT MEANS

The Knicks climb to 3-4 and extend their run of wins over Brooklyn. The Nets slip to 1-6 and are still looking for a dependable late-game identity.

GAME 05 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

WASHINGTON HAS ANSWERS ORLANDO CANNOT FIND

WIZARDS 103 · MAGIC 94
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Washington: Anthony Davis 27 points, 9 rebounds; Kyshawn George 22; AJ Dybantsa 21; Trae Young 15 points, 9 assists. Orlando: Franz Wagner 24 points; Desmond Bane 20.

Washington is beginning to look like more than a collection of recognizable names. Anthony Davis gave the Wizards a reliable scoring and interior presence, Trae Young managed the pressure points of the game, and rookie AJ Dybantsa continued to show that his offensive confidence is not waiting for a long adjustment period. With Alex Sarr sidelined, the Wizards still found a structure that allowed their veteran creators and young wings to complement one another.

Orlando’s problem was not a lack of early energy. The Magic made an aggressive start and briefly held a nine-point advantage in the second quarter. But once Washington tightened the spacing and began attacking the gaps, Orlando's perimeter shooting fell apart. The absences of Paolo Banchero and Jalen Suggs remain important context; they do not erase the concern that the Magic have struggled to generate easy offense when the first option disappears.

THE TURNING POINT

Trae Young sparked a 13-2 Washington response in the second quarter, and the Wizards held Orlando to nine points in the third.

WHAT IT MEANS

Washington moves to 4-3 and adds another positive night for Dybantsa. Orlando drops to 2-5; getting healthy is urgent, but better shot creation is just as important.

GAME 06 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

PHILADELPHIA HAS TOO MANY WAYS TO BEAT YOU

76ERS 126 · BULLS 107
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Philadelphia: Tyrese Maxey 26 points, 11 assists; Jaylen Brown 25 points, 11 rebounds; VJ Edgecombe 21; LeBron James 15 points, 9 assists, 7 rebounds. Chicago: Caleb Wilson 24 points, 7 rebounds, 4 assists.

The depth of Philadelphia’s attack is becoming the most difficult part of the scouting report. When the full starting five is available, the 76ers can survive a quiet opening stretch from one star because another can tilt the possession. Maxey saved his sharpest work for the fourth quarter, scoring 15 of his 26 points after Chicago had kept enough pressure on the game to demand a closing response. Jaylen Brown supplied the two-way physicality and rebounding, while LeBron James moved the ball with the timing of a quarterback who can see the second and third read before the defense rotates.

Chicago had one of the night's encouraging rookie performances. Caleb Wilson's 24 points were not empty touches; he continues to look like a frontcourt talent capable of scoring without being reduced to a single play type. Josh Giddey contributed 10 assists, but the Bulls could not match Philadelphia's efficiency and size over the full game. That gap matters more than any one run.

THE TURNING POINT

Philadelphia stretched a five-point halftime lead to 17 in the third; Maxey’s fourth-quarter attack closed the door.

WHAT IT MEANS

The Sixers reach 7-1 and strengthen their case near the top of the 4DK Power 30. Chicago falls to 1-6, although Wilson keeps giving the franchise a serious developmental bright spot.

GAME 07 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

ACUFF GIVES SACRAMENTO ITS FIRST BREATH

KINGS 115 · MAVERICKS 100
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Sacramento: Darius Acuff Jr. 25 points, 5 rebounds, 6 assists; Malik Monk 24; Zach LaVine 23. Dallas: Daniel Gafford 20; Walter Clayton Jr. 20; Cooper Flagg 17.

The Kings finally got off the zero, and the identity of the player who led them there is significant. Rookie Darius Acuff Jr. produced 25 points and six assists, giving Sacramento an attacking point guard who can make a defense respond to him instead of simply waiting for the veterans to create an opening. Malik Monk and Zach LaVine supplied enough perimeter offense to prevent Dallas from overloading Acuff, and Sacramento's three-point shooting became the separating factor.

Dallas played without Kyrie Irving and badly missed his ability to settle an offense when the first action fails. Cooper Flagg reached 17 points, but his 3-for-19 shooting line shows the limits of judging a young star by his total alone. The Mavericks were too dependent on difficult looks and did not match Sacramento's perimeter production. It was a reminder that talent does not guarantee rhythm when the rotation changes.

THE TURNING POINT

Sacramento hit 19 threes to Dallas’ seven and outscored the Mavericks 34-26 in the fourth.

WHAT IT MEANS

The Kings improve to 1-6 and Acuff adds a meaningful Rookie Watch statement. Dallas drops to 4-3; Flagg’s difficult shooting night should be evaluated in context, not ignored.

GAME 08 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

THE LAST UNBEATEN TEAM FINALLY BLEEDS

PELICANS 135 · GRIZZLIES 134 · OT
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · New Orleans: Zion Williamson 25 points, 11 rebounds; Karlo Matkovic 25; Derik Queen 20 points, 7 rebounds, 5 assists; Jeremiah Fears 12. Memphis: Ty Jerome 40 points, 5 rebounds, 5 assists; Cam Boozer 18 points, 9 rebounds, 5 assists.

For six games Memphis had looked capable of answering every opponent's challenge. New Orleans brought the kind of physical and emotional resistance that finally broke that streak. Zion Williamson was the most powerful player on the floor in overtime, scoring nine points in the extra period and repeatedly putting the defense in a position where a clean contest was nearly impossible. But this was not a one-man rescue. Karlo Matkovic matched Zion with 25 points, Derik Queen supplied productive bench offense and the Pelicans held their nerve when the game threatened to become a Ty Jerome story.

Jerome nearly stole it anyway. Forty points, including nine straight late in regulation, is a reminder that his opening-week production is not a statistical accident. He pulled Memphis out of a nine-point hole and sent the game to overtime, then gave the Grizzlies a late lead. The decisive possession, however, belonged to rookie Jeremiah Fears, whose two free throws put New Orleans ahead with seconds remaining. Jerome's final jumper missed. The first blemish on Memphis's record came in a game that should make its competitors respect the Grizzlies more, not less.

THE TURNING POINT

Jerome scored nine straight to force overtime; Fears hit two free throws with 7.3 seconds left and Jerome missed the final attempt.

WHAT IT MEANS

New Orleans rises to 3-4 after starting 0-4; Memphis falls to 6-1. Houston inherits the West’s top spot, while Jerome and Boozer remain central to the Grizzlies’ early-season case.

GAME 09 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

LAMELO ANSWERS THE PRESSURE IN MINNESOTA

TIMBERWOLVES 112 · BUCKS 100
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Minnesota: LaMelo Ball 34 points, 6 rebounds, 3 assists; Ayo Dosunmu 19 points, 12 rebounds, 8 assists. Milwaukee: Tyler Herro 21 points; Jaime Jaquez Jr. 19.

Minnesota needed a convincing response from LaMelo Ball with Anthony Edwards unavailable, and Ball delivered a season-high 34 points. The shot-making mattered, but so did the willingness to take responsibility for the offense after several uneven outings. Ball was aggressive early and returned to the same attacking mindset when Milwaukee briefly made the game uncomfortable. His sixth three-pointer helped put the fourth-quarter margin beyond the Bucks’ reach.

Ayo Dosunmu's 19 points, 12 rebounds and eight assists provided the connective tissue: defensive rebounding, transition initiation and another ball-handler capable of making the simple play. Milwaukee again struggled to turn individual scoring into a functional team offense. Tyler Herro and Jaime Jaquez had productive stretches, but the Bucks' perimeter shooting and late turnovers made it impossible to sustain a comeback. Brayden Burries' first start was another useful data point for the Rookie Watch, even with a difficult shooting night.

THE TURNING POINT

Minnesota opened the fourth on an 11-3 run, then Ball hit a three to stretch the advantage to 15.

WHAT IT MEANS

The Wolves move to 5-3 and show a path to surviving Edwards’ absence. Milwaukee falls to 1-6 and needs better offensive organization, not simply another hot scorer.

GAME 10 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

HOUSTON TAKES THE WESTERN CONFERENCE LEAD

ROCKETS 109 · CELTICS 93
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Houston: Alperen Sengun 26 points, 10 assists, 7 rebounds; Amen Thompson 14; Jabari Smith Jr. 14. Boston: Jayson Tatum 19 points; Neemias Queta 16.

This was the kind of win that changes a power-ranking conversation. Houston did not need an absurd shooting night or a single impossible fourth-quarter shot. The Rockets beat Boston through control: paint scoring, transition pressure and a defense that made the Celtics' first options work for every clean look. Alperen Sengun was the organizing center of it all, finishing with 26 points and 10 assists while making decisions from the middle of the floor that repeatedly pulled Boston's coverage apart.

The Celtics had won five straight and still have a roster capable of defending and competing against quality opponents. But Jayson Tatum's 19 points on 5-for-16 shooting reflected Houston's ability to take away easy offense. The Rockets never trailed, won the fast-break scoring 18-2 and owned the paint 50-34. That is not a narrow tactical victory. It is a clear statement about which team imposed its preferred style.

THE TURNING POINT

After a 29-29 first quarter, Houston built a halftime lead and controlled the interior and transition game the rest of the night.

WHAT IT MEANS

Houston moves to 7-1 and takes first place in the West after Memphis loses. Boston falls to 5-2; its long-term outlook remains strong, but this was a legitimate measuring-stick defeat.

GAME 11 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

SAN ANTONIO DOES NOT NEED FORTY FROM WEMBY

SPURS 119 · JAZZ 97
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · San Antonio: Dylan Harper 17 points, 7 rebounds, 8 assists; Stephon Castle 16; Victor Wembanyama 12 points, 11 rebounds, 3 blocks. Utah: Lauri Markkanen 22 points, 8 rebounds; Darryn Peterson 14.

A dangerous team is not merely one with a superstar capable of dominating. It is one that can win decisively when the superstar has an ordinary scoring night. San Antonio gave Utah that lesson. Victor Wembanyama contributed 12 points, 11 rebounds and three blocks, but the Spurs' balanced scoring and perimeter decision-making carried the attack. Dylan Harper's 17 points and eight assists illustrated how much more versatile San Antonio can become when the guards consistently pressure the paint.

The Jazz had moments when their threes briefly brought them back, but they could not match the Spurs' efficiency across four quarters. Lauri Markkanen led Utah with 22, and rookie Darryn Peterson had 14, yet San Antonio's defense repeatedly forced Utah to create against a set floor. This was also a useful counterpoint to the idea that every Wembanyama win must be an individual highlight reel. The team is building a broader foundation.

THE TURNING POINT

San Antonio turned a manageable game into a rout with a 35-20 third quarter and a 26-9 advantage in fast-break points.

WHAT IT MEANS

The Spurs improve to 5-2 with five straight wins, strengthening their top-five Power 30 argument. Utah falls to 1-6 and needs more consistent secondary scoring.

GAME 12 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

PORTLAND WALKS INTO DENVER AND TAKES THE FLOOR

TRAIL BLAZERS 118 · NUGGETS 110
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Portland: Deni Avdija 28 points, 5 assists; Donovan Clingan 17 points, 18 rebounds; Jrue Holiday 17 points, 10 assists, 8 rebounds; Damian Lillard 20. Denver: Jamal Murray 35; Nikola Jokic 26 points, 9 rebounds, 8 assists.

This is the kind of road win that forces the rest of the league to revise its assumptions. Portland trailed by double digits in Denver, absorbed a 35-point night from Jamal Murray and still found a way to own the most important minutes. Deni Avdija scored nine of his 28 in the fourth quarter, providing the direct downhill scoring the Blazers needed when the game became a half-court battle. His rise is not just about bigger numbers; it is about the confidence to make the decisive possession his own.

Donovan Clingan was just as central to the outcome. Seventeen points and 18 rebounds, including nine offensive boards, gave Portland second chances against a team built around Nikola Jokic. Jrue Holiday nearly recorded a triple-double off the bench and made the ball movement less predictable. Denver had the established stars, but Portland won the extra-possession battle and got contributions from enough different places to keep the Nuggets chasing.

THE TURNING POINT

Portland opened the fourth 9-2, survived a Murray three that cut the lead to three, and sealed the game on Toumani Camara’s late three.

WHAT IT MEANS

Portland improves to 5-1 and 4-0 on the road, making its top-ten Power 30 case impossible to dismiss. Denver falls to 3-3 and needs stronger defensive rebounding.

GAME 13 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

CURRY SAVES THE STREAK AFTER DRAYMOND IS EJECTED

WARRIORS 116 · SUNS 113
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Golden State: Stephen Curry 34 points, 6 rebounds, 7 assists; Brandin Podziemski 20; Charles Bassey 19 points, 8 rebounds. Phoenix: Devin Booker 26 points; Miles Bridges 21.

Golden State had every excuse to let this one go. Phoenix built a 17-point lead, Stephen Curry had to manufacture offense against a defense determined to crowd his space, and Draymond Green was ejected in the third quarter. Instead, the Warriors' response became the night's most revealing example of their veteran core and supporting cast working together. Curry finished with 34 points and seven assists, but the closing minutes demanded more than shot-making: a steal, calm free throws and trust in the role players who had kept the game alive.

Brandin Podziemski's 20 points and Charles Bassey's 19 mattered because Phoenix had committed to making the secondary Warriors beat them. Georges Niang supplied the go-ahead three, while Golden State's defensive effort improved when the margin tightened. Devin Booker scored 26, but his sixth foul and Phoenix's fourth-quarter turnovers robbed the Suns of the late-game precision they needed. The Warriors won without their preferred script, and that can be more instructive than an easy victory.

THE TURNING POINT

Niang hit the go-ahead three after a late tie; Curry sealed the comeback with steals and free throws as Phoenix struggled to finish.

WHAT IT MEANS

Golden State moves to 5-3 with five straight wins. Phoenix drops to 1-4, and the loss underscores how difficult it is to close games when the offense becomes too dependent on Booker.

GAME 14 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

TORONTO STAGES A FOURTH-QUARTER TAKEOVER

RAPTORS 125 · CLIPPERS 118
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Toronto: Immanuel Quickley 27 points; RJ Barrett 26; Scottie Barnes 22, including 15 in the fourth; Collin Murray-Boyles 18. Clippers: Kobe Sanders 26; Isaiah Jackson 19.

Toronto spent three quarters looking like a team still adjusting to life without Kawhi Leonard. The Raptors were down 14 entering the fourth, and the Clippers appeared close to ending their losing streak. Then Scottie Barnes took control of the game's emotional and tactical direction. Fifteen fourth-quarter points from Barnes, combined with Immanuel Quickley's 14 in the period, turned a disjointed night into a 47-point closing explosion. Toronto did not simply catch fire; it began getting downhill and forcing the Clippers to defend multiple actions without a reset.

Quickley's 27 points and RJ Barrett's 26 supplied the scoring depth that made Barnes' takeover possible. Los Angeles got a career-high 26 from Kobe Sanders and productive reserve minutes from Isaiah Jackson, but the Clippers could not protect a lead or organize clean late-game possessions. Darius Garland's turnovers were costly, and the team's injury list continues to complicate the rotation. Still, a 14-point fourth-quarter advantage should not disappear this way.

THE TURNING POINT

Barnes put Toronto ahead 111-109 with 2:08 remaining during a 47-27 fourth-quarter avalanche.

WHAT IT MEANS

Toronto reaches 4-3 and earns an important win without Leonard. The Clippers drop to 1-6 after six straight losses; late-game ball security is now a defining concern.

GAME 15 / 15 · THE 4DK BREAKDOWN

LUKA MAKES FORTY LOOK LIKE THE BASELINE

LAKERS 138 · HEAT 117
BOX-SCORE SPOTLIGHT · Los Angeles: Luka Doncic 42 points, 13 assists, 7 rebounds; Austin Reaves 27; Walker Kessler 15 points, 15 rebounds. Miami: Giannis Antetokounmpo 30 points, 12 rebounds, 7 assists; Klay Thompson 19.

Another night, another Luka Dončić masterpiece. The numbers — 42 points and 13 assists — are enormous, but the more important story is how naturally Los Angeles is playing through his control of the game. Against a Miami team with Giannis Antetokounmpo anchoring the offense, the Lakers did not settle for trading difficult jumpers. Luka repeatedly forced the first defender to choose between his scoring and the pass that would break the coverage. Austin Reaves' 27 points and Walker Kessler's 15-and-15 gave those reads real consequences.

Miami got 30 points, 12 rebounds and seven assists from Giannis, and that line normally gives a team a fighting chance. But the Heat's defensive resistance cracked after halftime. The Lakers opened the third with an 11-0 run and turned a seven-point halftime edge into a game that increasingly belonged to them. Luka's fourth 40-point performance in five games is not just an MVP talking point; it is the foundation of an offense that is beginning to find reliable answers around him.

THE TURNING POINT

Los Angeles opened the second half 11-0, pushed the lead to 18 entering the fourth and finished with a 21-point win.

WHAT IT MEANS

The Lakers rise to 6-2 and Luka strengthens his position at the top of the 4DK MVP Watch. Miami falls to 2-5, with defensive cohesion now the immediate issue.
