Spurs 99, Lakers 96 · Final

The rebound that swung Wemby–Luka

With under a minute left, Victor Wembanyama missed a free throw. That should have been a break for Los Angeles. Instead De’Aaron Fox came away with the rebound and Stephon Castle buried a three to make it 96–90. On a night billed around the two men atop our MVP ballot, the most expensive mistake the Lakers made was letting the smallest Spur in that sequence get to the ball first.

Luka Dončić had already done the hard part. He gave Los Angeles 31 points and eight assists on a night he made one of 13 threes. Austin Reaves hit five of nine from outside, and the Lakers held San Antonio to 14 points in the first quarter. Up 20 at one point and 54–38 at the break, L.A. had built enough cushion to survive an ordinary cold stretch. What it couldn’t survive was scoring 14 in the third while the Spurs scored 30. Keldon Johnson’s three in the closing seconds of the quarter brought the teams level at 68. A lead that had taken Los Angeles an entire half to build was gone before the fourth even started.

Wemby gives this rivalry a different shape from a straight scoring duel. He shot 10 of 25, yet seven of his 16 boards came on offense. Add four steals and two blocks and there were possessions where his first shot mattered less than whether the Lakers could finally get the ball away from him. Castle was the man who cashed in. He shot 10 of 15, led San Antonio with 26 and handed out nine assists. Before Fox’s rebound, Carter Bryant had already earned Castle another fourth-quarter three by chasing down an offensive board. For our money, that repetition is the scouting lesson. Helping on Wemby is only the first assignment. Somebody still has to find Bryant or Fox when the ball comes off.

Reaves’ late three and Jake LaRavia’s two free throws squeezed the margin back to one. Fox answered at the stripe. We come away higher on Castle’s place in this matchup and still convinced of Luka’s ability to manufacture a close game on a poor perimeter shooting night. The Cup, though, only has room for one of these teams. San Antonio takes the West wild card and goes to Dallas. The Lakers take a 15–4 record back to regular-season business.

Mavericks 113, Suns 109 · Final

Flagg takes three different routes to the lead

4DK Player of the Night

Cooper Flagg

34 points · 7 rebounds · 8 assists
5 steals · 4 blocks

Phoenix led by 21 and took a 95–78 advantage into the fourth. Devin Booker scored 31. The Suns made six more threes than Dallas. Ordinarily that buys a home team the right to play a fairly conservative final quarter. What changed the math was how often Phoenix had to defend again without getting a shot of its own. The Suns committed 18 turnovers, and Dallas won the paint 60–38. Flagg could affect both ends of that exchange. He finished with five steals and four blocks before we even get to what he did as a scorer.

The final minute and a half was practically a scouting reel. Flagg hit a 16-footer to bring Dallas within one, followed his own miss for the go-ahead tip, then made a turnaround with 22.6 seconds left for a three-point cushion. Phoenix had to account for his pull-up, finish the defensive rebound and contest a shot over a set defender. It failed a different test each time. Max Christie made four late free throws, important work on a night Booker still had a three left in him. Naji Marshall’s 14 points and five assists also belong in the comeback. Dallas didn’t need Flagg bringing the ball up and creating the first advantage on every trip.

We give Flagg Player of the Night because every column in his line tells us something about how Dallas recovered those 21 points. Eight assists gave teammates a way into the offense. The go-ahead offensive board gave him a second shot at a possession he had nearly lost. His 34 points included the baskets that changed the lead. Dallas had secured West Group A before tipoff. Finishing 4–0 means Wembanyama has to come to Flagg’s building for the quarterfinal.

Heat 108, Knicks 105 · Final

Miami wins the glass and survives the line

Down 58–35, the Knicks still found a way to put the game in Jalen Brunson’s hands with a chance to tie. Their 35-point third quarter made the rally possible. His two makes in three attempts after being fouled on a three with 9.8 seconds left explain the finish. Neither explains why the defending Cup champions had to climb so far. Miami made six first-quarter threes and then put together a 15–0 second-quarter run. New York spent the rest of the night paying for that stretch.

Giannis Antetokounmpo had 22 points and 23 rebounds. Bam Adebayo scored 25, and his basket broke a 95–95 tie after New York had briefly gone in front. We like what that pairing asks of an opponent late. Finish a defensive possession against Giannis, then be ready for Bam to take the next important shot. Karl-Anthony Towns’ 19 and 10 and Brunson’s 27 and eight assists made this a real contest. New York’s 17 turnovers still cost it opportunities it couldn’t afford against that frontcourt. Miami ends the Knicks’ six-game winning streak but goes out too. Philadelphia’s win in Indiana shut the Heat’s route through East Group B.

Hawks 87, Celtics 86 · Final

Atlanta wins the ball before it wins the group

Neemias Queta collected 22 rebounds. Boston held Atlanta to 39 first-half points. If you’d offered the Celtics both figures before tipoff, they’d have had good reason to expect a home quarterfinal. Instead they turned it over 26 times. A stop and a rebound earn an offense one possession. Giving the ball away before the shot hands that advantage straight back. It’s how a Hawks team that shot 11 of 45 from three found enough chances to come back from 65–50.

Jalen Johnson supplied 24 and 12 while the offense was scuffling. A 20–4 run carried Atlanta into the fourth with the lead. Nickeil Alexander-Walker then scored the Hawks’ last four, the first two on a drive with 54.3 seconds left. Jayson Tatum turned it over down two in the closing seconds. Alexander-Walker’s free throws meant Derrick White’s buzzer three could only narrow the loss. We’d remember those free throws if these clubs meet in a series. Atlanta could keep a second scorer involved after Boston had spent the night trying to contain Johnson. The head-to-head win gives Atlanta Group C and a home date with Philly. Boston’s plus-67 group differential sends it to Detroit as the wild card.

Grizzlies 114, Timberwolves 109 · Final

Memphis has another guard for every run

Minnesota made Memphis prove itself twice. Down 61–50 at halftime, the Wolves opened the third on a 26–11 burst. Joan Beringer blocked Javon Small at one end and Jonathan Kuminga dunked at the other to put Minnesota ahead. Without Anthony Edwards or LaMelo Ball, that is the kind of possession the Wolves need. A live ball and an open floor let Kuminga attack before the defense can load up on Ayo Dosunmu. Ayo still produced 27 points and eight assists, then stole the ball twice for late layups. Minnesota had enough creation to win this game.

What separates Memphis for us is how little the change of lead altered its options. Cedric Coward scored 22, Small came off the bench for 19, and Ty Jerome and Cam Spencer added 18 and 16. The Grizzlies scored 32 in the fourth after Minnesota had held them to 21 in the third. Even with Zach Edey limited to 12 minutes before fouling out, Memphis collected 15 offensive boards. Minnesota left nine points at the line in a five-point loss. That’s a tough combination when you’ve worked this hard just to regain the lead. West Group B goes to the Grizzlies, who now get a road test in Portland.

Trail Blazers 116, Warriors 94 · Final

Portland protects a margin twice

The first quarter finished 37–11. Golden State missed 20 of 24 shots, turned it over six times and then gave up the first ten points of the second. Portland could have spent the whole second half trading baskets. The Cup made that a dangerous luxury. The group winner had yet to be decided, and a 36-point cushion shrank to ten in the fourth. Yaxel Lendeborg, who led the Warriors with 18, kept giving them a reason to run another possession hard even without Stephen Curry.

What we like about Portland’s response is where the points came from. Deni Avdija missed all six of his threes and still finished with 26 points, seven rebounds and seven assists. The Blazers scored 58 in the paint to Golden State’s 30. Toumani Camara added 20 and Scoot Henderson supplied 18 and seven assists from the bench. With Damian Lillard out, there was real value in being able to hand the ball to Henderson and still get downhill. For the Warriors, winning the third quarter 34–24 showed a viable way back. Scoring 16 in the fourth showed the limits of asking a depleted offense to sustain it. Portland wins West Group C and hosts Memphis.

76ers 111, Pacers 87 · Final

Philadelphia makes the same 37 attempts worth 30 more points

Each team attempted 37 threes. Philadelphia made 15 and Indiana made five. That’s a 30-point difference in a game decided by 24, and it explains why the Pacers could grab 18 offensive rebounds without ever taking the lead. Working for second shots is good offense only if the next attempt improves on the first. Against Philadelphia, all those extra chances couldn’t compensate for the possessions Indiana finished empty from outside.

LeBron James hit three triples in the opening four minutes. We usually talk about his passing as the way he gets teammates easier looks. Here his own shooting made it costly to sit back while he surveyed the floor. He finished with 21 points, seven boards and six assists in 27 minutes. Anfernee Simons supplied 19 and Joel Embiid had 14 and 11. An 11-point halftime lead became 27 when the Sixers won the third 30–14. For Indiana, Jay Huff’s 18 and Ivica Zubac’s 16 rebounds gave the frontcourt something to show for the night. The perimeter offense without Tyrese Haliburton couldn’t keep pace. Philadelphia wins East Group B and takes that depth to Atlanta.

Pistons 113, Bucks 98 · Final

Detroit’s bench changes the terms

At 19–3, Milwaukee had earned a chance to dictate the night. J.B. Bickerstaff pulled his starters. The five-man change could easily have cost Detroit any remaining rhythm, especially with Cade Cunningham and Jalen Duren unavailable. Instead the reserves gave the Pistons a second route into the game. Isaiah Joe’s 22 supplied the shooting. Paul Reed and Elijah Harkless each added 14, with Harkless also handing out eight assists. By halftime the Bucks’ lead was two.

The third quarter is where the recovery became something more durable. Detroit scored 42 with eight different scorers, and Daniss Jenkins’ three at the horn pushed the lead to 14. Milwaukee got 23 from rookie Brayden Burries and 20 from Tyler Herro, but committed 19 turnovers and allowed 24 fast-break points. Defending Joe in the half court is one problem. Finding him in transition after a giveaway is a harder one. We’d keep Harkless’ passing on the scouting sheet alongside Joe’s shooting. It’s what allowed the bench to run an offense instead of simply waiting for a hot hand. Detroit’s reserves outscored its starters 60–53. The Pistons win East Group A on differential and host Boston. Milwaukee finishes group play 0–4.

Raptors 124, Nets 100 · Final

Quickley does the organizing, Barnes does the damage

Scottie Barnes scored 34. Immanuel Quickley produced 24 points, ten rebounds and 14 assists. The distribution is the interesting part for Toronto. With Quickley collecting a defensive board himself, the Raptors could begin a possession without waiting for an outlet. With Barnes able to finish or deliver the next pass, Toronto didn’t need Quickley to take the shot that completed it. Collin Murray-Boyles added 19 and seven off the bench, another target for an offense that had more than one productive pairing.

Both teams made 12 threes. The separation came on the glass and in the third quarter, when Toronto turned an eight-point halftime lead into 23. The Raptors grabbed 19 offensive boards to Brooklyn’s eight. Julius Randle and Mikel Brown Jr. scored 21 apiece, but trading a made basket for a stop that never ends in a rebound is no way to work down a deficit. Toronto did just about everything a team chasing a wild card could ask of itself, including winning by 24. Its 3–1 Cup record briefly put it in position to advance. Atlanta’s win made Boston the wild card on differential and ended Toronto’s night on somebody else’s floor.

Nuggets 120, Jazz 107 · Final

Utah gets to 102 first. Denver knows where the next shot is coming from.

Tied at 85 after three, Utah went up 102–97 on an Ace Bailey alley-oop. That’s worth remembering about a 3–17 team. Keyonte George scored 25, Darryn Peterson had 20 and Jusuf Nurkić supplied 11 assists in his season debut. Denver had to account for a passing center and two perimeter scorers. When Utah got the ball to a finisher before the defense could recover, it looked capable of doing more than hanging around.

The closing possessions tell us why Denver is a different proposition. Nikola Jokić had 15 assists against nine rebounds and 17 points. Jamal Murray scored 36. After Utah pulled within one on a Svi Mykhailiuk three, the Nuggets could give Murray the ball to rise for a short jumper or put it in Jokić’s hands and send Murray toward the rim. Both options paid off late. Christian Braun’s 20 points gave the other three defenders a reason to stay home. Utah committed 19 turnovers to Denver’s nine, a particularly expensive way to give that pair extra attempts. Denver won the fourth 35–22 and finished group play 3–1. San Antonio took the West wild card on point differential.

Pelicans 105, Clippers 104 · Final

The final three makes the finish look different

Brook Lopez hit from 29 feet in the final second. It counts in the standings as a one-point loss, but Los Angeles was down four on that shot. Its better chance came earlier. Darius Garland’s late three had pulled the Clippers close before Zion Williamson found Trey Murphy III for a short floater. That pass captures the New Orleans advantage. Zion could take the ball into traffic himself or make the defender who stepped toward him responsible for an open teammate.

Williamson finished 11 of 15 for 25 points, seven rebounds and five assists. Murphy matched his 25 and came away with six steals. The Pelicans won the paint 64–34 despite making six fewer threes. For Los Angeles, Garland’s 19 points and nine assists gave the offense some order, and 18 offensive boards gave it second chances. Settling too many of those possessions from distance left the Clippers needing one more stop down the stretch. New Orleans snaps a four-game skid. Los Angeles has lost six straight. Both finish 1–3 in the Cup, but the Zion-to-Murphy connection gives the Pelicans something specific to take back into the regular-season schedule.
