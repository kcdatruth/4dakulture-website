GAME 01 · FINAL

MEMPHIS 126 · GOLDEN STATE 98

The Grizzlies are turning depth into a weapon

Box Score Spotlight

Jaylen Wells 22 PTS, 7 REB; Cedric Coward 19 PTS; Cam Spencer 11 AST. Warriors: Charles Bassey 16 PTS, 13 REB; Brandin Podziemski and Will Richard 15 PTS each.

The turning point

Memphis never trailed. Golden State briefly reduced the deficit to six early in the third, but Memphis answered with a 37–26 quarter and ultimately led by as many as 34.

What We Learned — 4DK Film & Context Desk

This was not a night when Memphis needed one superstar to rescue the offense. Wells supplied efficient perimeter scoring, Coward added another dependable option, and the Grizzlies' collective pressure turned Golden State's limited creation into a long evening. The Warriors shot just 5-for-29 from three, a brutal number that exposes how little margin they have when Stephen Curry is unavailable. Draymond Green also sat out. The broader 4DK concern is structural: without their offensive organizer and their defensive communicator, the Warriors can look like two incomplete teams occupying the same floor. Bassey's double-double gave them interior activity, but it did not solve the spacing and advantage-creation problems. Memphis, meanwhile, has now won five straight and sits 12–4; the question is how much of this depth-led production remains sustainable against healthier elite opponents.

GAME 02 · FINAL

PORTLAND 124 · MILWAUKEE 114

Lillard's return becomes a referendum on two franchises

Box Score Spotlight

Damian Lillard 33 PTS; Scoot Henderson 27 PTS, 7 REB; Jrue Holiday 22 PTS, 7 AST. Bucks: Tyler Herro 19 PTS; Nate Ament 18 PTS, 9 REB; Kasparas Jakučionis 18 PTS.

The turning point

After Herro's drive cut the margin to 115–114 with 3:18 remaining, Portland scored the final nine points.

What We Learned — 4DK Film & Context Desk

The headline writes itself, but the deeper story is how Portland won. Lillard's 33 points supplied the emotional center in his return to Milwaukee; Henderson's 27 gave the Blazers another attacking gear; and Holiday's 22 and seven assists gave them a veteran stabilizer. Toumani Camara scored all 18 of his points after halftime, making the closing lineup harder to load up against. Portland was without Deni Avdija and Ja Morant, which makes the shared scoring burden more consequential. Milwaukee has now dropped 12 straight and is 1–15. This is more than an ugly record: losing a one-point game over the last three minutes by surrendering a 9–0 finish points toward execution, lineup stability and the absence of a reliable late-possession identity. Ament's 18 and nine rebounds offered one developmental bright spot, but young-player flashes cannot substitute for consistent team offense and defense.

GAME 03 · FINAL

NEW YORK 102 · MINNESOTA 99

The Knicks survive; the Wolves reveal both their ceiling and their flaw

Box Score Spotlight

Karl-Anthony Towns 22 PTS, 12 REB, 3 BLK; Jalen Brunson 21 PTS, 7 AST; OG Anunoby 15 PTS. Wolves: Jonathan Kuminga 23 PTS; Jaden McDaniels 17 PTS; LaMelo Ball 15 PTS.

The turning point

New York built a 19-point third-quarter lead, then went scoreless for the final 3:26. Ayo Dosunmu cut it to three, a subsequent potential tying three was waved off after a shot-clock whistle, and Ball missed a tying attempt with 9.9 seconds left.

What We Learned — 4DK Film & Context Desk

The Knicks got the result without delivering a convincing closing argument. Towns was productive against his former team, scoring 17 of his 22 before halftime and controlling the glass. Brunson's 21 and seven assists helped establish the lead, but New York's scoreless finish left the door wide open. Minnesota's late rally showed the resilience that has defined its start without Anthony Edwards, yet Ball's 6-for-21 shooting highlights the burden of asking him to create clean late-game looks against set defenders. Kuminga's 23 in his return from a shoulder absence gave the Wolves a welcome downhill option; McDaniels continued to provide efficient offense. For 4DK, the takeaway is two-sided: New York's defense can preserve wins, but its late offense needs more precision; Minnesota's committee approach can erase deficits, but the last three minutes still underline why an elite closer matters. This result belongs in the Nightcap, not as a retrospective addition to the separately locked pregame Minnesota Film Room.

GAME 04 · FINAL

SAN ANTONIO 124 · LA CLIPPERS 112

Wembanyama and Castle restore the Spurs' command

Box Score Spotlight

Victor Wembanyama 32 PTS, 11 REB; Stephon Castle 21 PTS, 12 AST; Julian Champagnie 20 PTS. Clippers: Darius Garland 23 PTS, 5 AST; Brook Lopez 23 PTS (5-for-7 3PT); Max Strus 18 PTS.

The turning point

San Antonio used an 18–5 second-quarter surge to establish control. Los Angeles rallied and briefly led in the third, but the Spurs took the fourth 32–24 and finished with separation.

What We Learned — 4DK Film & Context Desk

San Antonio's response after Detroit snapped its 12-game winning streak mattered because the Clippers repeatedly tested its composure. Wembanyama's 32 and 11 were the central matchup problem: he can punish conventional coverage with length, touch and offensive versatility, then alter the other end simply by occupying the paint. Castle's 12 assists are just as important for the Spurs' long-term identity. His creation reduces the need for Wembanyama to initiate every possession and gives San Antonio a second route to quality offense. Champagnie's 20 illustrated how spacing and timely finishing make those stars harder to defend. Los Angeles generated real shooting pressure — Lopez made five threes and Strus added five — but too many productive stretches were followed by Spurs answers. The Clippers are now 4–10, while San Antonio moves to 13–3. The Spurs' contender case is not merely about Wembanyama's numbers; it is about how reliably the supporting creation survives when opponents force adjustments.

4DK Player of the Night

Victor Wembanyama · San Antonio

32 points, 11 rebounds, 13-for-26 shooting. We give Wembanyama the nod over Lillard's 33-point emotional return because San Antonio needed a statement response after its winning streak ended, and its franchise centerpiece delivered against a Clippers team that kept coming back. His gravity opened the floor for Castle's 12 assists and Champagnie's 20 points. The 4DK distinction: the best player did not have to monopolize every possession for the team to play through him.

Honorable mention: Damian Lillard

Thirty-three in Milwaukee, with Portland closing on a 9–0 run, is the night's most compelling individual storyline.

The 4DK Verdict

Memphis has earned attention as more than a pleasant surprise: a fifth straight victory and a 12–4 record suggest real roster-wide functionality. Portland's road win reminds us that veteran shot creation and emerging guard play can coexist. New York's win should not hide a shaky finish, and Minnesota's comeback should not hide its half-court closing limitations without Edwards. San Antonio's Wembanyama–Castle partnership is the clearest championship-scale development of the night: elite interior force plus increasingly trustworthy playmaking.

Our next questions: Can the Warriors create enough efficient offense without Curry? Can Milwaukee halt a 12-game slide? Can Minnesota finish close games without Edwards? And can the Spurs keep this two-creator balance against the West's deepest defenses?
