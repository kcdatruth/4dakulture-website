KCDATRUTH • 4 DA KULTURE • WEEK 2 MONDAY NIGHT RECAP

The Rams did not need a pretty win Monday night. They needed a response. Week 1 was the kind of loss that makes every offseason expectation look shaky: San Francisco beat them 27–7, Myles Garrett went from blockbuster addition to injured reserve after knee surgery, Aaron Donald was still ramping back up from retirement, and a roster built to chase the NFC suddenly looked old, injured and out of sync.

Then Los Angeles came home and beat the Giants 28–6 in a game that felt like a direct answer to almost every criticism from the opener. The offense created explosives. The run game had balance. The defense played fast. The stars looked like stars. Even with Puka Nacua unavailable and two turnovers still sitting on the stat sheet, the Rams produced 481 total yards and averaged eight yards per play.

WEEK 1 MADE THE RAMS LOOK LIKE A TEAM FALLING APART. WEEK 2 LOOKED LIKE A TEAM REMEMBERING EXACTLY WHO IT WAS SUPPOSED TO BE.

STAFFORD LOOKED LIKE THE MVP AGAIN.

Matthew Stafford’s Week 1 line — 155 yards, no touchdowns and an interception — did not look anything like the quarterback who won MVP the season before. Monday did. Stafford completed 22 of 31 passes for 327 yards and four touchdowns. More important than the raw numbers was the way the offense opened back up. He attacked every level, trusted one-on-one matchups and never played like a quarterback trying to survive the game.

The four touchdown passes went to Davante Adams twice, Kyren Williams and rookie tight end Terrance Ferguson. Stafford also moved past Philip Rivers into sixth place on the NFL’s all-time passing touchdown list with 427.

NO PUKA? ADAMS TOOK THE WHOLE LOAD.

Puka Nacua was out with a hip/groin injury. That should have made the Rams easier to defend. Instead, Davante Adams gave New York one of those nights that looked like the old version of himself: eight receptions, 195 yards and two touchdowns.

Adams was not just catching underneath volume. He was creating chunk plays and winning the matchups Stafford kept trusting. The Rams did not need to manufacture a fake version of their offense without Nacua; they let another elite receiver become the focal point.

THE RUN GAME MADE IT COMPLETE.

Kyren Williams and Blake Corum combined for 164 rushing yards on 24 carries. Both averaged better than six yards per attempt. That is the version of the Rams offense that is hardest to live with: Stafford does not have to throw 45 times, play-action has real weight, and the defense cannot sit in passing looks waiting for Adams and Nacua.

Los Angeles still left points and possessions on the field. Stafford threw an interception and Williams lost a fumble. That is exactly why this game should feel encouraging without feeling complete. The Rams won by 22 while still giving New York two takeaways.

AARON DONALD’S COMEBACK WAS ABOUT MORE THAN A BOX SCORE.

Aaron Donald had not played an NFL game in 32 months. He retired after the 2023 season, spent two full years away and did not officially return to the Rams until August 30. Los Angeles then kept him home from the Week 1 trip to Australia so he could continue ramping up. Monday was the final step of the comeback.

Donald was introduced last in the Rams’ starting lineup at SoFi and took the field on New York’s first offensive snap. The moment mattered because the Rams had just lost Garrett to knee surgery and injured reserve. Donald’s return gave the defense another center of gravity when the offseason plan had already been altered.

He did not need a three-sack night to matter. One of his clearest impact plays was exactly the kind of snap Rams fans remember: Donald penetrated and dropped Najee Harris for a three-yard loss. New York finished with only 182 total yards and averaged 3.3 yards per play. The Giants never scored a touchdown.

Donald is 35 and two years removed from regular NFL work. Nobody should expect 2018 Aaron Donald every snap immediately. But Monday proved something more basic: the comeback is real. He can get through an NFL game, still disrupt the run and still change how the Rams can build their front while Garrett is out.

GARRETT GOING TO IR COULD HAVE BROKEN THE IDEA OF THIS DEFENSE. DONALD’S RETURN GIVES THE RAMS A DIFFERENT WAY TO HOLD IT TOGETHER.

THE DART INJURY CHANGED THE GAME — BUT IT DOESN’T EXPLAIN ALL OF IT.

Jaxson Dart’s night ended almost as soon as it started. On New York’s opening possession, he was hit by Byron Young and Josaiah Stewart as he released a third-down pass. The force drove his left knee inward. Dart stayed down, grabbed the knee, walked off under his own power, went through the medical tent and then headed to the locker room. He finished 3-of-5 for 20 yards.

The Giants ruled him out, and Jameis Winston took over. Winston completed 11 of 27 passes for 111 yards with an interception. The offense never recovered its rhythm.

JAXSON DART INJURY STATUS

The Giants have officially identified it as a left-knee injury. Postgame reporting says the early belief is a possible MCL sprain, but that is not yet a confirmed diagnosis. Dart is expected to undergo an MRI Tuesday. Until the imaging is complete, 4DK is treating the MCL designation as suspected rather than official.

If it is isolated to the MCL, that would generally be better news than an ACL injury, but the grade would determine the recovery window. The Giants have a short week before Tennessee, so even a favorable diagnosis could still put Jameis Winston in line to start.

It would also be too simple to explain 28–6 entirely through the quarterback injury. The Rams controlled the line of scrimmage, ran for 164 yards, hit explosives in the passing game and held the Giants to 182 total yards. Dart’s exit changed New York’s ceiling. Los Angeles still had to dominate what was left in front of it — and did.

4DK POWER MOVEMENT: THE RAMS ARE BACK IN THE TOP 10.

One game does not erase the 27–7 loss to San Francisco. That is why Los Angeles is not jumping straight back into the elite tier. But a 28–6 win in which the Rams outgained New York 481–182 is too strong to ignore. The Week 2 board moves the Rams from No. 14 to No. 9.

The Giants move the other direction, from No. 15 to No. 20. Their Week 1 win over Dallas still counts, but six points, 182 yards and the uncertainty around Dart changes the immediate outlook.

THE 4DK VERDICT.

Week 1 asked whether the Rams had built a contender on paper that might not survive real football. Week 2 did not answer every question, but it answered the biggest one: this team still has another gear.

Stafford can still take over a game. Adams can still look elite. The backfield can still create balance. Donald is actually back — not just on the roster, but on the field making winning plays. And the defense showed it can function while Garrett heals.

THE RAMS DIDN’T JUST GET TO 1–1. THEY LOOKED LIKE THEMSELVES AGAIN.
