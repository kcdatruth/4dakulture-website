THE 4DK BIG PICTURE

THE PRESEASON EXCUSES
ARE OVER.

By October, the league begins to tell the truth. San Francisco and Kansas City are setting the pace, but the difference between being impressive in September and surviving January is depth. Buffalo is trying to build on the momentum of its overtime win against Los Angeles; the Rams must respond to a missed opportunity; and Green Bay and Dallas have a national-stage test that could change how the NFC is discussed. We have seen enough football to ask sharper questions — not enough to hand out a trophy.

Thursday supplied the warning: Denver beat Seattle 17–14 in overtime, but neither offense answered every question. Seattle's 3–3 record and Sam Darnold's late mistakes became the story; Denver's defense kept bailing out an attack that needs to finish more drives. That result belongs in the recap archive now. The rest of Week 6 is still unwritten.

THE NINERS ARE 5–0.
HOW HEALTHY ARE THEY?

WASHINGTON AT SAN FRANCISCO

PERFECT RECORD. IMPERFECT ROSTER.

That 5–0 record is real, and so is the pressure that comes with defending it. San Francisco already knows Brock Purdy can command this offense. Now it needs to prove the supporting cast can function when injuries reduce the margin for error.

Mike Evans was enormous in the win over Seattle, making difficult catches and giving Purdy a true contested-catch target. But he's questionable for Monday after missing practice with rib and Achilles soreness. We cannot project another Evans takeover until his game status clears. If he's limited or out, Christian McCaffrey and the secondary receivers must carry more creation.

Nick Bosa is out and Dre Greenlaw will serve his one-game suspension. That is a serious loss of pass-rush and second-level range. Kyle Juszczyk returning helps the offensive structure, but the defensive question is whether San Francisco can win third downs without those difference-makers.

Washington is shorthanded too: Jacory Croskey-Merritt, Marcus Mariota, Javontae Jean-Baptiste and Charles Omenihu have been ruled out. The missing running back can change Washington's early-down approach, but we will not confuse those absences with a guaranteed San Francisco win. San Francisco's biggest Week 6 test is the one that will follow it into December: can this team keep winning without putting too much of the load on its stars?

SUNDAY AFTERNOON · 1:25 PM PACIFIC

THE CHARGERS ARE HURT.
THE CHIEFS CAN'T GET COMFORTABLE.

PASS RUSH IS THE STORY.

Kansas City arrives undefeated and faces a Chargers line with little room left for error. Los Angeles is without Joe Alt and Kayode Awosika, plus Tre' Harris, Donte Jackson and Derwin James. Those losses touch protection, separation and coverage — multiple parts of the roster that an opponent can attack at once.

Justin Herbert has already absorbed 17 sacks through five games. He can still get the ball out, throw accurately under pressure and punish single coverage, but the challenge is whether the Chargers have enough protection and designed outlets to make his talent decisive. Kansas City's defensive front has to turn disruption into actual drive-killing plays rather than merely creating a hectic pocket.

KC has absences of its own — Josh Simmons and Rashee Rice are out. Winning the matchup in the trenches cannot be a substitute for designing a sustainable offense. Can Patrick Mahomes keep drives balanced and avoid the kind of long-yardage snaps that invite a comeback?

SUNDAY AFTERNOON · 1:25 PM PACIFIC

ALLEN IS COMING TO VEGAS.
CAN THE RAIDERS MAKE HIM WAIT?

THE RAIDERS NEED CONTROL.

Buffalo's overtime victory over the Rams showed how quickly Josh Allen can change a game. He does not need every possession to look perfect. Against a defense trying to regain consistency, the danger is permitting the scramble, the second-reaction throw or the late-down escape that turns one missed tackle into seven points.

Las Vegas is 3–2 and not without weapons. Ashton Jeanty and Brock Bowers practiced fully to close the week, and the Raiders will need their run and short passing game to keep the ball away from Allen. A shootout built entirely around impossible third downs is not the kind of game the Raiders should want.

Buffalo listed DJ Moore, Joshua Palmer, Ed Oliver, Dee Alford and T.J. Sanders as questionable. Vegas ruled out Tonka Hemingway, Carter Runyon and Dont'e Thornton Jr.; Trey Zuhn III is questionable. Those are pregame designations, not predictions about final inactive lists.

SUNDAY NIGHT FOOTBALL · 5:20 PM PACIFIC

LAMBEAU IS A TEST.
NOT AN EXCUSE.

DALLAS @ GREEN BAY

WHO CONTROLS THE EDGES?

Dallas has the offensive talent to stress Green Bay horizontally and vertically. The question is whether Dak Prescott can find clean answers when the Packers disguise coverage and force the ball underneath. Dallas cannot make every close game a referendum on its last two possessions; the offense must be disciplined throughout.

THE FRIDAY INJURY BOARD

GREEN BAY'S DEFENSIVE FRONT IS THIN.

The Packers have ruled out Javon Hargrave and Jonathan Ford, along with Skyy Moore and Benjamin St-Juste. Aaron Banks, Jacob Monk and Brandon Cisse are among those questionable. Dallas ruled out Cobie Durant and Drew Shelton, while CeeDee Lamb had no final injury designation. A depleted defensive line changes how Green Bay can defend the run without sacrificing coverage help.
