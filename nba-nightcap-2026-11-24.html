NBA Nightcap Deluxe · Tuesday, November 24, 2026 · Three games, three finals

FLAGG COMMANDS DENVER. TATUM ERUPTS. DETROIT LOCKS IN.

A three-game slate with three very different statements: Dallas wins with control and depth, Boston survives a comeback after a blistering first half, and Detroit turns defense into a road victory. We break down every final, every pivotal swing, and what comes next.

Game 01 / 03

DETROIT DOES IT WITH DEFENSE

DETROIT PISTONS AT ORLANDO MAGIC
104–86 · FINAL
BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Isaiah Joe: 21 PTS, 5 3PM; Daniss Jenkins: 15 PTS, 10 AST; Ausar Thompson: 10 REB, 4 BLK. Orlando: Desmond Bane 21 PTS; Wendell Carter Jr. 13 REB; Franz Wagner 20 PTS, 6 AST.

We can call this a convincing Detroit win without pretending it was a masterpiece from the opening tip. Orlando led 22–16 after one, and the Pistons had to change the terms of the game. They did it by turning a sluggish first quarter into a defensive statement: the Magic scored only 14 in the second, and Detroit took a 48–36 lead into halftime. That twelve-point halftime margin mattered because Detroit had seized control without needing one star to monopolize every possession.

Isaiah Joe's 21 points and five made threes supplied the spacing and perimeter scoring that a disciplined defensive team needs to convert stops into separation. Daniss Jenkins' ten assists speak to the other side of that equation: Detroit was not simply taking turns creating shots. We see a team that could spread the responsibility around, with Ausar Thompson contributing ten rebounds and four blocks. Those defensive contributions are a real part of the offense, because a disrupted opponent cannot dictate pace or matchups as easily.

Orlando's problem was not that Desmond Bane failed to score; he matched Joe's 21, and Franz Wagner added 20. It was that those individual contributions never produced enough sustained team offense. The Magic managed just 86 points, including 25 in each of the final two quarters. For Detroit, this is a meaningful response after a recent loss to Atlanta. For Orlando, it is a reminder that a talented roster still needs a reliable way to manufacture clean offense when the first option is challenged.

TURNING POINT: Detroit outscored Orlando 32–14 in the second quarter, flipping a six-point deficit into a twelve-point halftime lead.
WHAT IT MEANS: Detroit's collective defensive identity carried a road win; Orlando must solve its half-court scoring droughts.
Game 02 / 03

TATUM SETS THE TONE, THEN BOSTON HOLDS ON

BOSTON CELTICS AT WASHINGTON WIZARDS
131–116 · FINAL
BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Jayson Tatum: 41 PTS, 6 REB, 8 AST; Paul George: 9 AST; Derrick White: 7 REB. Washington: Anthony Davis 33 PTS, 13 REB; Trae Young 12 AST.

Boston needed a response, and Jayson Tatum made sure the Celtics did not spend the night searching for one. His 41 points, six rebounds and eight assists were the centerpiece of a 131-point road performance, but the important distinction is how early he established command. Tatum scored 28 in the first half. Boston poured in 46 points in the second quarter, made ten threes in that period, and carried an 80–49 lead into the break. That is not a narrow talent advantage; that is a half of basketball in which the Celtics overwhelmed Washington's defensive organization.

Yet the final score also warns us against grading Boston on its first half alone. Washington answered with a 40–24 third quarter and cut a once-massive deficit to five. Anthony Davis was a serious interior presence with 33 points and 13 boards, while Trae Young created opportunities with 12 assists. We give Washington credit for continuing to attack rather than accepting the game was over. The comeback also exposed a Boston vulnerability: a big lead is not a substitute for disciplined possessions, transition coverage, and a stable offensive rhythm after the initial avalanche.

Tatum is our headline because his production drove the separation, and his passing numbers reflect an offense with more dimensions than isolation scoring. Paul George's nine assists reinforce that Boston shared creation. But we cannot ignore the tension between a brilliant 80-point first half and the third-quarter letdown. The Celtics snapped a four-game skid, which is valuable in its own right; the next challenge is to make the defensive standard last for four quarters, not two.

TURNING POINT: Boston's 46-point second quarter produced an 80–49 halftime lead; Washington's 40-point third made the finish less comfortable.
WHAT IT MEANS: Tatum restored Boston's offensive authority, but protecting leads remains a real point of emphasis.
Game 03 / 03

FLAGG DIRECTS THE UPSET IN DENVER

DALLAS MAVERICKS AT DENVER NUGGETS
120–101 · FINAL
BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Cooper Flagg: 24 PTS, 6 REB, 12 AST, 1 TO; P.J. Washington: 14 PTS, 6 REB, +32; Caleb Martin: 13 PTS, 5 STL, 2 BLK. Denver: Jamal Murray 20 PTS; Nikola Jokić 18 PTS, 11 REB, 5 AST.

This was the statement game of the night. Dallas went into Denver and beat the Nuggets 120–101, and the final margin was not built on a single late shooting surge. The Mavericks won the first quarter 24–17, stretched the advantage to 58–39 at halftime, survived Denver's 38-point third quarter, then closed with a 36–24 fourth. That sequence tells us more than the nineteen-point final: Dallas could establish control, absorb a push from an elite opponent, and answer without losing its structure.

Cooper Flagg's 24 points and 12 assists, against only one turnover, are the clearest evidence of that structure. We are not just celebrating a young player who scored. We are watching one who repeatedly made the right decision with the ball while maintaining his own scoring threat. When a team gets twelve assists from a player who also leads it in scoring, the defense is forced to choose between helping on him and conceding the next action. Denver could not consistently solve that choice.

The depth story matters just as much. P.J. Washington returned and supplied 14 points, six rebounds and a game-best plus-32 in fewer than 25 minutes. Dallas ended the first half on a 15–0 run, with Washington providing multiple finishes during the surge. Caleb Martin's five steals and two blocks show how the Mavericks made the game uncomfortable at the other end. Denver got 20 from Jamal Murray and 18 points, 11 rebounds and five assists from Nikola Jokić, but Dallas kept the Nuggets from sustaining the kind of connected offense that usually makes them so difficult to defend. This is the kind of road performance that changes how we discuss Dallas' ceiling—provided the execution travels into the next game.

TURNING POINT: Dallas closed the second quarter on a 15–0 run to take a 58–39 halftime lead; it then won the fourth 36–24 after Denver's third-quarter push.
WHAT IT MEANS: Flagg's playmaking and Dallas' returning depth give the Mavericks a convincing blueprint against top-tier opponents.

The 4DK Final Word

We leave Tuesday with three different measures of winning basketball. Detroit imposed its defense, Boston leaned on Tatum to halt a slide, and Dallas delivered the most complete road performance of the night. The Mavericks did not need Kyrie Irving to have an efficient shooting night to beat Denver decisively; that balance is the headline that lasts beyond one box score.
