Murray and Jokić Go Nuclear. Bam Runs Miami. Golden State Survives.

Seven finals. A 296-point overtime thriller. A 24–15–12 masterpiece. An 18–0 fourth-quarter reversal. And a one-point escape in the Bay. We break down what the numbers reveal—and what the results mean.

GAME 01 · NOVEMBER 22 · FINAL
Atlanta Hawks 107 — Detroit Pistons 97

Alexander-Walker leads the comeback; Detroit disappears in the fourth

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Nickeil Alexander-Walker: 27 PTS, 6 threes; Corey Kispert: 18 PTS; Lu Dort: 16 PTS. Detroit: Isaiah Joe 19 PTS; Ausar Thompson and John Collins 18 each; Cade Cunningham 5 PTS, 15 AST.

THE TURNING POINT

Detroit carried an 85–74 lead into the fourth, but Atlanta opened the period on an 18–0 run and won the quarter 33–12.

What We Learned · The 4DK Verdict

We see more than an unlikely comeback here. Atlanta changed the game by turning a Detroit advantage into a test of ball security and shot creation. With Cunningham hampered by foul trouble and knee soreness, the Pistons could still manufacture looks through his 15 assists, but they could not manufacture a late-game scoring answer. The Hawks made the decisive stretch feel suffocating rather than frantic: consecutive stops turned into confidence, and Alexander-Walker supplied the perimeter punch that gave the run real weight.

Detroit must distinguish its ability to generate quality offense for three quarters from its ability to protect a lead when the opponent increases pressure. Atlanta’s fourth straight victory is a legitimate statement of collective resilience; Dort and the reserves helped make this more than one scorer carrying the comeback.

GAME 02 · NOVEMBER 22 · FINAL
Miami Heat 123 — Oklahoma City Thunder 113

Bam directs the entire game without Giannis

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Bam Adebayo: 24 PTS, 15 REB, 12 AST; Pelle Larsson: 21 PTS; Nikola Jović: 15 PTS. Oklahoma City: Bennett Stirtz 21 PTS; Jared McCain 20 PTS; Ajay Mitchell 17 PTS.

THE TURNING POINT

Miami led wire-to-wire, built a 20-point advantage and withstood Oklahoma City’s 33-point fourth quarter. Both teams were without their MVP: Giannis Antetokounmpo (groin) and Shai Gilgeous-Alexander (neck/back).

What We Learned · The 4DK Verdict

We have spent plenty of time talking about Miami’s talent, but this game is about its organizing principles. Adebayo’s 24–15–12 triple-double was not simply a center collecting assists; he became the hub that kept possessions connected when the Heat were without their highest-profile interior attacker. Miami’s offense had direction, the supporting cast moved into useful spaces, and Larsson’s efficient scoring ensured the Thunder could not load every possession toward Bam. Seven players reaching double figures is evidence of distributed responsibility, not a coincidence.

Oklahoma City’s undermanned nine-player group deserves context: its late push showed pride and shot-making, not a full-strength benchmark for the defending contender. Miami’s seventh consecutive win, and its ability to lead wire-to-wire without Giannis, is a stronger argument for system sustainability than any one spectacular scoring night.

GAME 03 · NOVEMBER 22 · FINAL
Washington Wizards 116 — Orlando Magic 96

Washington turns a Southeast matchup into a twenty-point statement

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Kyshawn George: 24 PTS for Washington; Tristan da Silva: 18 PTS for Orlando.

THE TURNING POINT

Washington finished with a 20-point margin after taking control of the matchup.

What We Learned · The 4DK Verdict

We judge Washington’s performance through the size and clarity of the result rather than inventing a possession-by-possession story. Beating Orlando by twenty requires more than a hot individual scorer; it means sustaining enough offensive production and defensive resistance to prevent the Magic from getting back into striking distance. George’s 24 points gave Washington a credible leading option, and the team converted that advantage into a complete result.

For Orlando, 96 points is the warning. The Magic’s offense must create more consistent scoring opportunities when the first action is disrupted. For Washington, this is a useful building block: the challenge is translating a decisive night against a division opponent into repeatable execution rather than treating it as a one-off upset.

GAME 04 · NOVEMBER 22 · FINAL
Denver Nuggets 151 — Chicago Bulls 145

Murray and Jokić survive a historic Chicago response

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Jamal Murray: 45 PTS (6 threes), including 8 in OT; Nikola Jokić: 37 PTS, 18 REB, 11 AST. Chicago: Buddy Hield 33 PTS; Josh Giddey 23 PTS, 14 REB, 22 AST; Jalen Smith 26 PTS.

THE TURNING POINT

Chicago erased a seven-point deficit in the final 40 seconds; Hield’s steal and Giddey’s layup tied it at 131. Denver then opened overtime with eight straight points.

What We Learned · The 4DK Verdict

This was a reminder of both the ceiling and the vulnerability of Denver’s offensive identity. Murray and Jokić combined for 82 points, and their two-man partnership rescued the Nuggets after the Bulls’ extraordinary closing rally. Murray’s overtime scoring was the separator, but Jokić’s 37–18–11 gave Denver the constant structure required to survive a game where defense could not reliably close the door. A 151-point win is spectacular; conceding 145 and a seven-point lead in the final forty seconds is not the formula Denver wants to normalize.

Chicago’s 25 made threes and Giddey’s historic 22 assists deserve their own spotlight. The Bulls were missing key scorers yet still stretched Denver through ball movement and perimeter volume. Their fight is real; their defensive resistance remains the issue. We take Denver’s star-driven composure seriously, but this should be a film-room warning as much as a celebration.

GAME 05 · NOVEMBER 22 · FINAL
Dallas Mavericks 116 — Utah Jazz 114

Flagg answers the shootout; Utah nearly steals it

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Cooper Flagg: 28 PTS, 4 AST; Kyrie Irving: 16 PTS, 8 AST. Utah: Svi Mykhailiuk 39 PTS, 10 made threes; Ace Bailey 16 PTS.

THE TURNING POINT

Dallas used a 16–3 third-quarter run to establish control, then survived Utah’s late comeback.

What We Learned · The 4DK Verdict

Flagg’s 28-point night is another meaningful sign of a young cornerstone who can carry real offensive responsibility without the Mavericks asking Irving to score every crucial basket. Dallas found separation with its third-quarter surge, but the two-point finish illustrates how quickly an apparent advantage can vanish against an opponent with one shooter operating at an extraordinary level. Mykhailiuk’s ten triples were not a footnote; they changed the entire margin for error.

For Dallas, winning on the road while its emerging star leads the scoring is progress, though late-game control still needs tightening. For Utah, this is the difference between explosive individual production and winning execution: 39 from Mykhailiuk kept the Jazz alive, but the Mavericks did enough to keep the last possession from changing the result.

GAME 06 · NOVEMBER 22 · FINAL
New York Knicks 113 — Indiana Pacers 106

Towns supplies the interior answer as New York closes

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Karl-Anthony Towns: 30 PTS, 13 REB, 5 AST, 4-for-4 from three; Jalen Brunson: 22 PTS, 7 AST. Indiana: Pascal Siakam 25 PTS, 6 REB; Andrew Nembhard 16 PTS, 8 AST.

THE TURNING POINT

Indiana delivered a 16–3 second-quarter run, but New York answered and later closed with a decisive 14–6 surge.

What We Learned · The 4DK Verdict

We saw New York win with a more balanced offensive hierarchy. Brunson remains the stabilizer, but Towns’ efficient 30 points, rebounding and flawless perimeter shooting gave Indiana an impossible choice: concede space outside or let the Knicks attack the interior with their center as the focal point. That versatility matters because it reduces the burden on late-clock guard creation. The Knicks did not merely outlast a Pacers run; they reestablished the terms of the game in the closing stretch.

Indiana can point to Siakam’s scoring and Nembhard’s playmaking, but the inability to finish after creating second-quarter momentum is the bigger concern. New York’s win strengthens its case as a team learning how to close with multiple offensive answers, rather than leaning on a single star every time the game tightens.

GAME 07 · NOVEMBER 22 · FINAL
Golden State Warriors 88 — Boston Celtics 87

Warriors survive the final shot and end the skid

BOX SCORE SPOTLIGHT

Brandin Podziemski: 18 PTS, 7 REB, 4 threes; Charles Bassey: 13 PTS, 9 REB. Boston: Jayson Tatum 27 PTS, 10 REB, 6 AST; Derrick White 14 PTS, 5 AST.

THE TURNING POINT

A late Podziemski three and foul call was overturned after review. Boston recovered possession but Tatum missed a contested 16-foot attempt at the buzzer.

What We Learned · The 4DK Verdict

This was not a pretty offensive night, and that is exactly why Golden State should value it. After five straight defeats, the Warriors needed evidence that they could survive a physical, possession-by-possession game without relying on a high-scoring shootout. Podziemski’s 18 points mattered, but so did the collective ability to protect a one-point edge after the late officiating reversal removed what appeared to be a much safer cushion. The final stop was a survival test, not a masterpiece of execution.

Boston’s fourth straight defeat demands attention, especially because Tatum delivered 27–10–6 and still could not drag the offense across the finish line. The Celtics had a final opportunity and did not convert it. We will not overreact to one missed jumper, but a contender losing four in a row must confront the bigger question: how dependable are its half-court options when every point becomes difficult?

4DK PLAYER OF THE NIGHT

Nikola Jokić · Denver Nuggets

37 points · 18 rebounds · 11 assists. Our choice is Jokić for the all-around offensive control that held Denver together through a 151–145 overtime shootout. Murray’s 45 points and eight overtime points make him a co-headliner, but Jokić supplied the rebounding, playmaking and scoring foundation that made the Nuggets’ extraordinary output possible.

Honorable mentions: Jamal Murray (45), Josh Giddey (23–14–22), Bam Adebayo (24–15–12), Nickeil Alexander-Walker (27), Karl-Anthony Towns (30–13), Svi Mykhailiuk (39 and ten threes).
