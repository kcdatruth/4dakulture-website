The NFC Is a Gauntlet.

The Rams look built like 2021 all over again. Detroit is still elite. Chicago is betting on a Caleb leap. Philly remains dangerous. And three 12-win wild cards turn January into a war.

The NFC is loaded in a different way than the AFC. The conference has veteran teams trying to squeeze one more run out of established cores, young quarterbacks trying to jump from promising to special, and entire divisions where a 10-win season might not guarantee anything.

Los Angeles is the headliner because the Rams went all-in again. Detroit is still built to win now. Chicago has the highest-upside young quarterback bet in the conference. Philadelphia still has Jalen Hurts and Saquon Barkley. And the NFC West might be the most unforgiving division in football.

Four division winners. Three 12-win wild cards. No soft landing anywhere in January.
Tradition. Pressure. No Easy Sundays.

NFC East

PHI 11–6 • DAL 10–7 • NYG 9–8 • WAS 8–9
Philadelphia Eagles

Still A Legitimate Threat.

11–6

Philadelphia does not have to feel unbeatable to remain dangerous. As long as Jalen Hurts and Saquon Barkley are healthy, the floor is still a playoff team and the ceiling is still a deep January run.

The new variable is rookie receiver Makai Lemon. How quickly he develops chemistry with Hurts matters for a remodeled receiver room that still includes DeVonta Smith and Hollywood Brown. Dallas Goedert remains a trusted option underneath.

Defensively, the pressure shifts toward Jalen Carter. He and Jordan Davis still give Philadelphia size inside, while Zack Baun, Nolan Smith Jr., Quinyon Mitchell, Cooper DeJean and newcomer Riq Woolen give the defense talent at every level. Carter now has to live up to the expectations that come with being one of the faces of that unit.

4DK Verdict: Not perfect. Still built to matter in January.
Dallas Cowboys

The Offense Is Real. Is The Defense?

10–7

Dak Prescott, CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens might be the best three-headed passing attack in football. Jake Ferguson gives Dak another dependable option, while Javonte Williams provides a veteran presence in the backfield.

The defense is the swing point. Dallas enters the year with Quinnen Williams and Kenny Clark inside, Rashan Gary and Von Miller off the edge, DeMarvion Overshown at linebacker and DaRon Bland on the back end. Rookie safety Caleb Downs adds another high-upside piece.

This roster has enough names. The question is whether those names become a consistent defense instead of a unit that forces the offense to win every shootout.

4DK Verdict: Playoff offense. The defense decides the ceiling.
New York Giants

Dart + Harbaugh Changes The Feel.

9–8

The Giants finally have a young quarterback worth being excited about and a head coach with a track record. Jaxson Dart enters Year 2 with the kind of play style and charisma that can fit New York perfectly, while John Harbaugh brings a winning standard to a team trying to become relevant again after a rough 2025.

Dart has real help: Malik Nabers, Darnell Mooney, Darius Slayton, Odell Beckham Jr. and Isaiah Likely give him options, while Cam Skattebo, Najee Harris and Tyrone Tracy Jr. make the backfield deep.

The defense can make the jump faster than the offense. Brian Burns, Abdul Carter and Kayvon Thibodeaux give New York an explosive pass-rush trio, Tremaine Edmunds adds experience in the middle, and Paulson Adebo, Greg Newsome II, Jevón Holland and Tyler Nubin give the secondary a stronger veteran/young mix.

4DK Verdict: One of the NFC's most improved teams.
Washington Commanders

Jayden Daniels' Response Season.

8–9

After a rough second season, Jayden Daniels has to remind people why the 2024 Rookie of the Year looked like one of the league's brightest young quarterbacks after Year 1.

This is not only about Daniels. Terry McLaurin remains the established target, Stefon Diggs adds veteran route-running, Rachaad White gives the backfield receiving value, and Chig Okonkwo adds another middle-of-the-field option. The offensive line has to keep the entire structure stable.

Defensively, Daron Payne and Javon Kinlaw give Washington size inside, while Frankie Luvu, Odafe Oweh and K'Lavon Chaisson bring range and pressure. Mike Sainristil remains one of the key young pieces in the secondary.

4DK Verdict: Daniels, the offense and the defense all need to bounce back together.
Tough Football. Huge Expectations.

NFC North

DET 13–4 • CHI 12–5 • GB 9–8 • MIN 7–10
Detroit Lions

Still Elite. But For How Long?

13–4

Detroit is still a playoff team and still good enough to win the division, but the championship window feels a little more urgent now. Jahmyr Gibbs might be the most consistent running back in the league, and he becomes even more important after David Montgomery's departure.

Detroit did add Isiah Pacheco to help behind Gibbs, but Jared Goff still has to play at a high level. Amon-Ra St. Brown remains the reliable star, and Jameson Williams is the player to watch for a leap from dangerous deep threat to full-blown star.

Defensively, Aidan Hutchinson remains the tone-setter up front and Jack Campbell leads the linebackers. The question is whether the secondary and defensive depth can stay healthy enough for Detroit to avoid living in shootouts.

4DK Verdict: 13 wins and a real shot at the NFC crown — but the window is not endless.
Chicago Bears

Caleb's Superstar Leap?

12–5

Chicago's season is about one thing above everything else: how much better can Caleb Williams become? After a strong second year, this is the season where he can jump from exciting young quarterback to genuine superstar.

Rome Odunze and Colston Loveland give him two premium young targets, while Luther Burden III becomes even more important after DJ Moore's departure. The two-headed backfield of D'Andre Swift and Kyle Monangai gives Ben Johnson balance and keeps everything from falling on Caleb's arm.

The defense has enough to support a contender. Montez Sweat anchors the front with Grady Jarrett and Dayo Odeyingbo, T.J. Edwards and Devin Bush patrol the middle, and Jaylon Johnson remains the top corner. First-round safety Dillon Thieneman adds more upside on the back end.

4DK Verdict: If Caleb becomes a star, Chicago can win the whole division.
Green Bay Packers

Too Many Ifs.

9–8

Green Bay has enough talent to make the playoffs, but there are too many unanswered questions to trust them over Detroit or Chicago. It starts with Jordan Love's health and whether the Packers can finally identify a true No. 1 receiver.

Christian Watson, Jayden Reed and Matthew Golden all have a chance to become that guy. Tucker Kraft and Jonnu Smith give Love quality tight-end options. But the availability questions extend beyond quarterback: Josh Jacobs opens on the Commissioner's Exempt List, and Micah Parsons opens on PUP.

When Parsons is right, he changes the entire defense. Xavier McKinney, Edgerrin Cooper and Zaire Franklin give Green Bay proven talent behind him. The problem is that this prediction requires too many things to go right at once.

4DK Verdict: Wild Card talent, division-title question marks.
Minnesota Vikings

Kyler Gets Another Chance.

7–10

At 29, Kyler Murray enters a make-or-break type season. After struggling through the final years of his Arizona run, Minnesota gives him a chance to re-establish himself as a legitimate quarterback talent.

The biggest reason for optimism is obvious: Justin Jefferson. If Murray and Jefferson build chemistry early, Jefferson could have a huge season. Jordan Addison, Jauan Jennings and T.J. Hockenson keep the passing game from being a one-man operation, while Aaron Jones and Jordan Mason provide veteran backfield options.

Brian Flores still gives the defense an identity. Dallas Turner and Andrew Van Ginkel can create pressure, Blake Cashman is the stabilizer inside, and Byron Murphy Jr. and Joshua Metellus bring experience to the secondary.

4DK Verdict: Interesting, dangerous, but still outside the playoff field.
Young Talent. Big Opportunity.

NFC South

CAR 10–7 • TB 9–8 • ATL 8–9 • NO 6–11
Carolina Panthers

The Sleeper Division Winner.

10–7

Carolina might quietly be the best team in this division. Bryce Young showed enough progress in 2025 that this season becomes a bigger question: can he go from improved starter to legitimate franchise quarterback?

Tetairoa McMillan gives him a real No. 1 receiver after winning Offensive Rookie of the Year with 1,014 yards and seven touchdowns. Jalen Coker and Xavier Legette give the receiving room more youth, while Chuba Hubbard and Jonathon Brooks keep the offense balanced.

The defense has enough established talent to help. Derrick Brown remains the anchor, Jaelan Phillips gives the pass rush more juice, Devin Lloyd adds athleticism at linebacker, and Jaycee Horn gives the secondary a true top corner.

4DK Verdict: Bryce's leap + McMillan's leap = Carolina wins the South.
Tampa Bay Buccaneers

Bucky Keeps Them Dangerous.

9–8

Bucky Irving is a very good running back and gives Tampa an offensive foundation. Baker Mayfield still gives the team stability at quarterback, while Jalen McMillan, Chris Godwin and Emeka Egbuka now carry more responsibility after Mike Evans' departure.

The defense is more interesting than it gets credit for. Vita Vea and Calijah Kancey still give Tampa power inside, Yaya Diaby brings edge pressure and first-rounder Rueben Bain Jr. adds another potential difference-maker. Antoine Winfield Jr. remains the star on the back end.

Tampa does not need an elite defense. It needs a consistent one that can keep Baker and Bucky from constantly having to chase games.

4DK Verdict: Competitive again, but Carolina has more upside.
Atlanta Falcons

Bijan Is The Constant.

8–9

The jury is still out on Michael Penix Jr., and Tua Tagovailoa gives Atlanta an experienced veteran option if needed. But no matter who is taking the snaps, Bijan Robinson is the player everything should run through.

Drake London remains a legitimate No. 1 receiver, Kyle Pitts still creates matchup problems and Brian Robinson Jr. gives the Falcons another physical runner. New head coach Kevin Stefanski has enough offensive talent to make this work if the quarterback play settles down.

Defensively, Jessie Bates III and A.J. Terrell are the established names in the secondary. Xavier Watts adds more young talent at safety, while Za'Darius Smith and Harold Perkins Jr. give the front seven a mix of veteran pressure and upside.

4DK Verdict: Bijan gives them a floor. The quarterbacks determine everything above it.
New Orleans Saints

Shough Is The Reason To Watch.

6–11

The record may not be pretty, but Tyler Shough gives New Orleans something worth developing. He went 5–4 after taking over last season and now gets his first full offseason as the unquestioned offensive leader.

Chris Olave remains the established target, and first-round receiver Jordyn Tyson was supposed to give Shough another explosive young weapon. Tyson's hamstring problems have complicated that plan and lower the offense's early ceiling. Alvin Kamara and Travis Etienne Jr. give the backfield plenty of experience.

On defense, Kool-Aid McKinstry is one of the younger pieces to build around, while Pete Werner and Kaden Elliss bring experience in the middle. Chase Young and Carl Granderson give the Saints edge pressure.

4DK Verdict: The record says rebuild. Shough's development says keep watching.
Star Power. Depth. Championship Expectations.

NFC West

LAR 14–3 • SF 12–5 • SEA 12–5 • ARI 4–13
Los Angeles Rams

2021 All Over Again.

14–3

This Rams team feels like 2021 all over again. Reigning MVP Matthew Stafford is coming off 4,707 passing yards, 46 touchdowns and only eight interceptions. Kyren Williams gives them balance, Puka Nacua is already a top-tier receiver, and Davante Adams gives Stafford another reliable veteran target.

Then Los Angeles attacked the defense. Trent McDuffie and Jaylen Watson bring championship experience to a secondary that needed more stability. The Rams then traded Jared Verse plus major draft capital for reigning DPOY Myles Garrett, the single-season sack record holder after 23 sacks in 2025.

And then the ridiculous part: Aaron Donald came out of retirement. Donald joins Garrett, Kobie Turner, Braden Fiske and the rest of a front that can be overwhelming on paper. Health is the only obvious concern with so many veteran stars.

4DK Verdict: The most complete team in the NFC — and my No. 1 seed.
San Francisco 49ers

Health Is Everything.

12–5

San Francisco still has contender-level talent, but injuries have defined too many of their seasons. Brock Purdy, 26, enters the year with a new-old weapon in Deebo Samuel and an old face in a new uniform with Mike Evans.

Christian McCaffrey remains the focal point of everything, George Kittle is still one of the best matchup tight ends in football and rookie De'Zhaun Stribling gives the receiver room another young piece. The biggest offensive concern is still the line, where 38-year-old Trent Williams remains the anchor.

Defensively, the healthy returns matter just as much. Nick Bosa, Fred Warner and returning Dre Greenlaw restore the core personality of the unit, while Osa Odighizuwa gives the interior another proven piece.

4DK Verdict: If healthy, they can beat anybody. No. 5 seed.
Seattle Seahawks

The Champs Lost Pieces, Not Their Identity.

12–5

Seattle has real losses to absorb, but there is still enough here to make another playoff run. Sam Darnold remains the stabilizer at quarterback, and first-round rookie running back Jadarian Price gets an immediate opportunity after Kenneth Walker III left for Kansas City.

Then there is Jaxon Smith-Njigba. After 119 catches, a league-leading 1,793 yards and 10 touchdowns — plus Offensive Player of the Year — JSN has a real argument as the best receiver in football. Rashid Shaheed and Cooper Kupp keep defenses from treating him like the only threat.

Defensively, Mike Macdonald still has Leonard Williams, DeMarcus Lawrence, Byron Murphy II, Ernest Jones IV, Devon Witherspoon, Julian Love and Nick Emmanwori. Losing Coby Bryant, Boye Mafe and Riq Woolen hurts the depth, but the top of the unit remains loaded.

4DK Verdict: Still a playoff team. Still dangerous. No. 7 seed only because this conference is stacked.
Arizona Cardinals

Forget The Record. Watch The Development.

4–13

Arizona is not about contention in 2026. This season is about figuring out which players belong in the next version of the team. Start with Jeremiyah Love, the No. 3 overall pick and the highest-drafted running back since Saquon Barkley.

Jacoby Brissett gives the quarterback room a veteran option, while third-round rookie Carson Beck is the long-term developmental question. Marvin Harrison Jr., Michael Wilson and Trey McBride give whoever plays quarterback legitimate targets.

Defensively, Josh Sweat and Budda Baker are the established veterans, while Walter Nolen III, Will Johnson and Dadrion Taylor-Demerson are among the younger pieces who need to keep developing under first-year head coach Mike LaFleur.

4DK Verdict: 4–13 can still be progress if Love looks special and the young core grows.

THE BIG TAKEThe NFC West is ridiculous.

Los Angeles gets the No. 1 seed, San Francisco gets your No. 5 seed, and Seattle still goes 12–5 and lands seventh. Add Detroit and a 12-win Chicago team, and there is no easy path through this bracket.

Final Word

The NFC does not have one story. It has several. The Rams are trying to buy another ring the aggressive way. Detroit is trying to keep its window open. Caleb Williams is trying to become a superstar. Bryce Young is trying to turn Carolina into a real division winner. The 49ers are fighting their own injury history. Seattle is trying to defend its place among the conference elite.

That is what makes this conference so interesting: the difference between a division winner and a Wild Card team might have almost nothing to do with talent.

The NFC is not short on contenders. It is short on room.
