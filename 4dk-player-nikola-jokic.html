The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

Jokić has nothing left to prove individually. Last season he averaged 27.7 points, led the league at 12.9 rebounds and 10.7 assists, shot 56.9 percent from the field and still produced the kind of offensive numbers that would be career peaks for almost any superstar in history. His passing is not just a skill; it is the organizing principle of Denver’s entire offense.

What makes Jokić different from almost every other great scorer is that there is no obvious way to take him out of a game. Double him and he finds the open shooter before the rotation arrives. Play him straight and he backs down smaller centers or scores with touch over bigger ones. Switch a wing onto him and Denver immediately clears the side. Drop against Murray and the two-man game creates a chain reaction that leads to a layup, a floater or a wide-open corner three.

The defense is never going to be the reason he ranks this high, but even there he survives through positioning, rebounding and anticipation better than the old stereotypes suggest. Denver’s bigger question is team context. The championship core is older, the West is deeper and every season of Jokić’s prime becomes more valuable.

Why is he only fourth? Because this is not a legacy ranking. Wemby, SGA and Luka enter 2026–27 with either greater two-way upside, fresher physical peaks or overwhelming current offensive volume. Still, if someone called Jokić the best player alive, there is no serious basketball argument that makes that opinion ridiculous. He remains one of the most complete offensive engines the sport has ever seen.
