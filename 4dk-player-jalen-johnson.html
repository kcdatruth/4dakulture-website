The 4DK scouting report

THE GAME BEHIND THE NUMBERS.

Jalen Johnson made the leap from promising young forward to legitimate star. Twenty-two and a half points, 10.3 rebounds and 7.9 assists is elite all-around production, especially from a 6-foot-8 forward who can grab a defensive rebound and become the point guard before the defense is organized. He earned All-NBA recognition because the numbers finally matched the physical tools.

What stands out is the playmaking growth. Atlanta can use him as a transition engine, short-roll creator, post facilitator or primary handler. The 35.2 percent three-point shooting was also good enough to keep defenses honest and open the floor for his drives.

The next test is whether those numbers drive winning at a higher level. With Trae gone, this is increasingly Johnson’s team and his responsibility. He has to become the player who settles the offense late, gets a good shot when the first action dies and anchors effort defensively. If he does that, Top 15 may be too low by midseason.
