NFL DESKPOWER 32MVP WATCHROOKIE WATCHSTANDINGSWEEK 7 ARCHIVE
👑 Still No. 1

Josh Allen. The two-way impact remains the most complete individual argument in football.

📈 Biggest Jump

Jared Goff climbs three places to No. 2. Joe Burrow enters at No. 5 after five passing touchdowns.

📉 The Slide

Walker falls five spots as Kansas City loses its second straight. Bryce Young exits the ten.

Season totals reflect games completed through Monday, November 2. Player movement is measured against the final 4DK Week 7 MVP Watch.

01
Buffalo Bills • — HOLD

Josh Allen

1,793 pass yds · 11 pass TD · 5 INT · 270 rush yds · 9 rush TD

The throne remains his because no contender depends on one player in quite the same way. Allen threw for 289 yards and two touchdowns against Baltimore and added a rushing score. Buffalo is 6–1, and his ability to force defenses to account for both the vertical pass and the quarterback run is the engine of that record. The Bills have imperfections, but Allen keeps turning them into manageable problems. We need a challenger to match his complete influence, not merely his passing total.

02
Detroit Lions • ▲ +3

Jared Goff

1,964 pass yds · 15 TD · 1 INT · 110.0 rating

Goff climbs because his season has been remarkably efficient and Detroit has just beaten unbeaten Minnesota. Fifteen touchdowns against one interception through seven games is elite control of the football. Gibbs carried the ground game against the Vikings, and that is part of Goff’s case rather than a reason to dismiss him: the quarterback keeps the offense balanced, organized and difficult to predict. The Lions have a genuine two-star engine, but Goff remains its conductor.

03
San Francisco 49ers • — HOLD

Brock Purdy

1,748 pass yds · 15 TD · 4 INT · 110.1 rating

Purdy held steady through the bye because the full body of work still demands respect. Fifteen touchdown passes, a 110.1 rating and a 6–1 record are not accidents. The Atlanta loss revealed what happens when the offense loses the turnover battle, so the response matters more than the week off. We are looking for him to control the game when his first read is taken away and the easy throws disappear. He remains close to the top because he has already done it consistently.

04
Seattle Seahawks • — HOLD

Jaxon Smith-Njigba

64 catches · 985 receiving yds · 9 receiving TD

The team lost, but JSN did not disappear. Six catches, 118 yards and a touchdown against Chicago pushed him to 985 yards and nine scores in eight games. That is outrageous production for a player every defense knows it must stop. We are not giving him an automatic quarterback-style MVP advantage, but we are refusing to erase his value because Seattle is 4–4. The Seahawks’ offense becomes dangerously one-dimensional without him. His individual case is stronger than the team record.

05
Cincinnati Bengals • NEW ENTRY

Joe Burrow

2,115 pass yds · NFL-best 20 pass TD · 6 INT · 111.6 rating

Burrow crashes the top five after a 462-yard, five-touchdown performance against Tennessee. He now leads the league with 20 passing touchdowns and is driving Cincinnati’s three-game winning streak. The three interceptions matter, and needing overtime against a one-win opponent is a fair criticism of the team. But an MVP discussion has to account for the quarterback who repeatedly rescues games with high-level creation. If the Bengals defense stabilizes, Burrow has the numbers to challenge the top three.

06
Jacksonville Jaguars • — HOLD

Trevor Lawrence

1,690 pass yds · 17 TD · 4 INT · 112.6 rating

Lawrence is playing the cleanest football of his career, and Jacksonville’s 42–7 demolition of Indianapolis gave him another statement result. His 112.6 rating is among the league’s best, and 17 touchdown passes in seven games explain why the Jaguars are running away from the AFC South. We are not ranking him purely on one blowout; we are rewarding an offense that can attack through him without becoming entirely dependent on his arm. A tougher opponent will test whether this level holds.

07
Kansas City Chiefs • ▼ -5

Kenneth Walker III

770 rush yds · 6 rush TD · 131 receiving yds · 2 receiving TD

Walker slides because Kansas City has lost two straight and his Week 8 rushing output was 69 yards. But 770 rushing yards through seven games still places him among the league’s leaders, and eight total touchdowns explain how much he changed the Chiefs’ offensive structure. We do not remove a running back from serious consideration because two defenses made him work. We do ask him to dominate the next marquee game, especially with the AFC West now controlled by Denver.

08
Chicago Bears • — HOLD

Caleb Williams

1,267 pass yds · 6 pass TD · 1 INT in 5 games · 104.6 rating

Caleb keeps his place after a composed Monday night road win. He completed 24 of 34 throws for 247 yards and a touchdown, then let D’Andre Swift finish drives on the ground. That is quarterbacking, not a lack of ambition. Chicago is 6–2, and Williams has played only five games after a hamstring absence, so the cumulative totals are lower than some rivals. His command of the offense and ability to avoid mistakes make the argument. He can climb if the Bears keep winning.

09
Detroit Lions • ▲ +1

Jahmyr Gibbs

773 rush yds · 8 rush TD · 285 receiving yds · 2 receiving TD

Gibbs earns the promotion after 153 rushing yards against Minnesota. He has more than 1,000 yards from scrimmage and ten total touchdowns in seven games, production that forces every defensive coordinator to adjust the front and coverage shell. We do not view him as a sidekick to Goff. He is the explosive element that makes Detroit’s offense so hard to defend. The MVP hill is steep for a running back sharing credit with an efficient quarterback, but his weekly impact belongs on this board.

10
Kansas City Chiefs • ▼ -3

Patrick Mahomes

1,795 pass yds · 12 TD · 4 INT · 99.7 rating

Mahomes stays in the ten because the Denver loss was not an individual collapse: he threw for 321 yards and three touchdowns. But we are not handing out reputation votes. Kansas City has lost two straight, and the quarterback of a five-win team cannot stay ahead of players driving stronger current momentum without better finishes. Mahomes has the ability to climb this list faster than almost anyone. The next statement must come in the win column.

ON THE OUTSIDE LOOKING IN

Bryce Young leaves the top ten after Carolina’s second straight defeat. The next wave includes players whose value is rising even when their teams do not dominate the standings. Every place on this board remains open to the next great performance.
