MVP Watch.

Our ballot weighs production, responsibility and team impact. It preserves the 4DK order while giving every challenger a clear case.

01 / MVP WATCH
Buffalo Bills · WEEK 8 #1 · HOLD

Josh Allen

2,027 pass yds · 12 pass TD · 6 INT · 285 rush yds · 9 rush TD

Buffalo's 7–1 start and another prime-time victory keep Allen atop the 4DK ballot. His candidacy is about control as much as fireworks: when defenses force patient football, the Bills still find a way to finish. The separation from the field depends on sustaining that efficiency against elite opponents.

The strongest argument for Allen is the combination of team position and the burden of creation. Buffalo is 7–1, and the quarterback's value includes decisions before the snap, off-schedule throws and rushing production that does not appear in a passing-yardage comparison. His 2,027 passing yards and 12 passing scores need to be read alongside 285 rushing yards and nine rushing touchdowns through Week 9. The six interceptions leave room for a cleaner stretch. We are keeping Allen first because the total offensive responsibility and the Bills' results remain persuasive, while acknowledging that other candidates have stronger pure passing totals.

02 / MVP WATCH
San Francisco 49ers · WEEK 8 #3 · ▲ 1

Brock Purdy

1,980 pass yds · 17 pass TD · 5 INT · 109.8 passer rating

Purdy's case rests on the 49ers' 7–1 record and an offense that has helped San Francisco recover from its first loss. He does not need to win every week through volume alone. The strongest argument is command of an attack that complements a turnover-hunting defense.

Purdy's 1,980 passing yards, 17 touchdowns and five interceptions illustrate why efficiency belongs beside volume in an MVP debate. San Francisco is 7–1, and the 38–17 win over Las Vegas reinforced the idea that the offense can rebound after a setback. His case grows when the 49ers are able to win in multiple game scripts rather than requiring a shootout. The counterargument is that he benefits from a strong overall roster; our response is that executing within a complete team is still a quarterback skill. A decisive performance against another contender would tighten his pursuit of No. 1.

03 / MVP WATCH
Detroit Lions · WEEK 8 #2 · ▼ 1

Jared Goff

2,134 pass yds · 15 pass TD · 2 INT · 106.4 passer rating

Detroit's 6–2 record puts Goff firmly in the conversation, even after a defensive-led win in Miami. MVP value also shows up in avoiding mistakes and keeping a talented team on schedule. He needs more signature offensive performances to pressure the top two.

Goff's 2,134 passing yards, 15 touchdowns and only two interceptions are a compelling argument for disciplined quarterback play. Detroit's 6–2 start has not been built on recklessness; protecting the football allows the defense and running game to remain meaningful parts of the formula. The 19–9 victory in Miami showed that the Lions do not always need a huge offensive number to win. What separates Goff from the top two today is the need for more defining victories in which his passing production is the clearest reason Detroit prevailed. His efficiency keeps him firmly in the race.

04 / MVP WATCH
Jacksonville Jaguars · WEEK 8 #6 · ▲ 2

Trevor Lawrence

1,932 pass yds · 18 pass TD · 4 INT · 111.7 passer rating

Jacksonville's 33–10 rout of Baltimore elevated Lawrence's profile. A 6–2 record and the team's commanding scoring differential provide real support, but voters will want to see that level sustained against contenders rather than treated as one exceptional night.

Lawrence's statistical case is difficult to dismiss: 1,932 passing yards, 18 touchdowns and four interceptions through Week 9. Jacksonville's 33–10 win over Baltimore supplied the kind of high-profile team performance that elevates an MVP candidacy beyond numbers. The Jaguars are 6–2 and have established a strong AFC South position, giving Lawrence a platform for a serious second-half push. The next test is how consistently he can create scoring opportunities when defenses remove the first read. If the Jaguars keep winning with this level of offensive efficiency, a move toward the top three becomes a real possibility.

05 / MVP WATCH
Seattle Seahawks · WEEK 8 #4 · ▼ 1

Jaxon Smith-Njigba

71 receptions · 1,036 receiving yds · 9 receiving TD

The highest-ranked non-quarterback on this ballot has a harder road to the trophy. Smith-Njigba's receiving impact must be judged against the positional bias that typically favors quarterbacks; his value lies in how much Seattle's offense depends on him.

A receiver entering Week 10 with 71 receptions, 1,036 yards and nine touchdowns has a legitimate claim to being one of the league's most productive offensive players. Smith-Njigba is not a novelty pick: his target volume and yardage show how much Seattle asks of him. The obstacle is structural. MVP voting historically favors quarterbacks, and the Seahawks' 5–4 record gives the position players on stronger teams an advantage in the team-success argument. Our No. 5 ranking recognizes extraordinary individual output while acknowledging the uphill path to the award itself.

06 / MVP WATCH
Kansas City Chiefs · WEEK 8 #10 · ▲ 4

Patrick Mahomes

2,037 pass yds · 14 pass TD · 4 INT · 101.8 passer rating

Kansas City is 6–2 and remains firmly in the AFC race. Mahomes has the platform to climb quickly, but the 4DK ballot rewards the season to date, not past trophies or reputation. His next statement opportunity will matter.

Mahomes' 2,037 passing yards, 14 touchdowns and four interceptions support a strong season, but 4DK is not awarding extra points for career achievements. Kansas City's 6–2 record and a 35–12 win over the Jets are important, yet the AFC West remains competitive with Denver in front by record. The Chiefs' offensive ceiling is obvious; the more interesting question is whether Mahomes can stack decisive performances against top defenses. He is sixth because the players ahead have built stronger combinations of current production and team argument, not because his ability is in doubt.

07 / MVP WATCH
Cincinnati Bengals · WEEK 8 #5 · ▼ 2

Joe Burrow

2,386 pass yds · 23 pass TD · 6 INT · 114.8 passer rating

Cincinnati's four-game winning streak and 34-point outing against Atlanta have moved Burrow's case forward. The Bengals are producing points and winning; the question is whether that surge continues against stronger defensive opposition.

Burrow's 2,386 passing yards and 23 touchdowns are elite counting numbers, and Cincinnati's 34–17 win over Atlanta added another useful result. The Bengals' 6–2 record means this is no empty-volume campaign. He has a real argument to be ranked higher on statistical production alone, particularly as the offense keeps finding the end zone. Our ballot still weighs how the entire season has unfolded, not just one passing category. The AFC North showdown with Pittsburgh offers an immediate opportunity to convert strong numbers into the kind of defining victory that reshapes the MVP order.

08 / MVP WATCH
Green Bay Packers · NEW SINCE WEEK 8

Jordan Love

2,618 pass yds · 19 pass TD · 6 INT · 97.9 passer rating

A 45-point performance at New England puts Love's offense in the spotlight. Green Bay has the explosive ceiling to make this candidacy interesting, but a 6–3 record leaves less margin for error than the candidates above him.

Love's 2,618 passing yards lead the league in the current Week 9 snapshot, while his 19 passing touchdowns make him more than a volume-only candidate. Green Bay's 45-point outing against New England demonstrated the offense's explosive potential. The issue is consistency: a 6–3 record leaves the Packers trailing Chicago in a tightly contested NFC North. For Love to move up, we want to see the same offensive control against a stronger defensive opponent and fewer games where Green Bay has to outscore its own mistakes. The statistical foundation is already there.

09 / MVP WATCH
Chicago Bears · WEEK 8 #8 · ▼ 1

Caleb Williams

1,546 pass yds · 8 pass TD · 5 INT · 95.4 passer rating

Chicago is 7–2, yet four interceptions in the win over Tampa Bay illustrate why Williams is ninth rather than higher. His ability to recover and win matters; his decision-making must become cleaner before he can challenge the leaders.

The Bears' 7–2 record is the strongest argument for Williams remaining on the ballot. But four interceptions against Tampa Bay are a serious warning, even in a 27–24 victory. A quarterback can demonstrate resilience by recovering from mistakes without those mistakes becoming evidence of excellence. Williams' 1,546 passing yards and eight passing touchdowns trail the top candidates' totals through Week 9. He belongs in the conversation because Chicago is winning and he has helped shape its offense; he cannot climb until the decision-making and production become more consistent.

10 / MVP WATCH
Buffalo Bills · NEW SINCE WEEK 8

James Cook

169 carries · 814 rush yds · 8 rush TD

Cook gives Buffalo a closing element that can turn competitive games into controlled wins. He is a genuine part of the Bills' identity, even if sharing the spotlight with Allen makes a running back MVP campaign especially difficult.

Cook's 814 rushing yards and eight rushing touchdowns are essential context for Buffalo's 7–1 start. The Bills are more difficult to defend when they can win early downs on the ground instead of asking Allen to manufacture every explosive play. That gives Cook an important role in closing games and controlling tempo. The difficulty for his MVP case is that he shares the same offensive stage as the No. 1 player on this ballot, while several quarterbacks elsewhere have bigger individual statistical portfolios. We are keeping him tenth because his impact is real, not because we expect a running back to win the trophy.
